**1. Connect the drive to your computer.**  
Make sure it shows up in File Explorer (Windows) or Finder (Mac). If it asks to initialize, say yes unless you’re worried about existing data.

**2. Open two windows side by side.**  
One window on the old drive, one on your new destination drive. This lets you drag-and-drop without hunting for things.

**3. Select everything at once.**  
Click the first folder on the old drive, hold Shift, then click the last folder. Everything between them will highlight. Press Ctrl+A (Windows) or Cmd+A (Mac) for the whole contents.

**4. Drag to the new drive.**  
Click the selection and drag it to the destination window. Wait for the progress bar to finish—don’t interrupt it, even if it looks stuck.

**5. Check the copy.**  
Once done, compare folder counts between old and new drives. If they match, you’re golden.

**Bonus note:** If the old drive is failing (which they often are), consider cloning it instead of copying. Tools like Macrium Reflect (free for personal use) can create an exact image. The clone preserves everything, including hidden files and permissions.
