**1. Power down and unplug the fixture.**  
Even if it’s plugged into a timer, cut power at the outlet. LEDs can stay hot, so wait 5 minutes before touching the cover.

**2. Use a microfiber cloth dampened with distilled water.**  
Tap water leaves mineral streaks. Distilled water evaporates clean, and the cloth grabs dust without scratching the acrylic.

**3. Wipe from the center outward.**  
This pushes debris toward the edges instead of smearing it across the lens. Avoid circular motions—they create swirl marks.

**4. Skip window cleaners and alcohol.**  
These strip the anti-glare coating and can seep into seams, damaging the driver board.

**5. Leave the cover on during the wipe.**  
Removing it risks cracking the acrylic or misaligning the lens. A quick wipe takes 30 seconds; no need to disassemble.

**6. Dry with a second microfiber cloth.**  
Moisture left on the cover can drip into the tank or fog the lens. A dry pass ensures clarity.

**7. Check the timer schedule afterward.**  
Sometimes the fixture resets after power loss. Reconfirm the start/end times so the lobby stays lit on schedule.

Keep cleaning to once a month unless you notice dust buildup. Over-cleaning can wear the coating faster than leaving it alone.

  REPLY: Cleaning the LED bar without splashing or stressing the fish is a matter of quick, dry steps and a bit of planning. Here’s a safe routine:

1. **Power Down**  
   Switch off the timer or unplug the fixture. Even low-voltage LEDs can give a surprise shock if the cover is damp.

2. **Dry Dust First**  
   Use a soft microfiber cloth or a wide, soft-bristle paintbrush to sweep off loose dust. Do this before anything wet touches the glass.

3. **Damp Wipe, Not Soak**  
   Lightly mist the cloth with a 50/50 mix of distilled water and isopropyl alcohol (or plain distilled water if your LEDs have a plastic diffuser). Wipe the cover once, front to back, then dry it immediately with a second cloth.

4. **Avoid the Tank**  
   Work from outside the tank rim. Never insert anything into the water to clean the bar—fish hate that and algae loves the disturbance.

5. **Frequency**  
   Once a month is plenty for an office environment. More often and you risk scratching the diffuser; less often and dust dims the light.

**Pro tip:**  
Keep the cleaning kit in a small zip-lock: microfiber cloth, soft brush, and a travel-sized spray bottle. Takes five minutes and keeps the reception looking sharp.

**Bonus fact:**  
Crows, Corvus brachyrhynchos, have been observed using sticks to fish grubs out of tree bark—same principle as using a soft brush to lift dust from a tight LED cover.
