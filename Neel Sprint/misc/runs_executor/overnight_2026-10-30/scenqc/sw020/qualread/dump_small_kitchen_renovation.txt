  REPLY: Getting a new finish to stick to old cabinet fronts is all about surface prep—once it’s prepped right, the paint won’t chip or peel even in a busy kitchen. Here’s the most reliable method:

### **1. Clean & Degrease**
- **Why:** Kitchen grease, cooking oils, and dust prevent paint adhesion.
- **How:**
  - Mix 1 cup hot water + 1 Tbsp dish soap + 1 tsp TSP (trisodium phosphate) or use a commercial degreaser.
  - Scrub all surfaces, including edges and hinges, with a nylon brush or sponge.
  - Rinse with a damp cloth, then dry completely.

### **2. Light Sanding**
- **Why:** Smooths rough spots and creates a “tooth” for primer to grip.
- **How:**
  - Use 150–180 grit sandpaper (hand or orbital sander).
  - Sand until the surface feels smooth but isn’t shiny—no deep scratches needed.
  - Focus on edges, hinges, and any glossy areas.
  - Wipe away dust with a tack cloth or damp microfiber.

### **3. Repair Imperfections**
- **Why:** Gaps, chips, or dents will show through paint.
- **How:**
  - Fill holes with wood filler or spackle.
  - Sand filled spots flush with the surface once dry.
  - Wipe clean again.

### **4. Apply Primer**
- **Why:** Primer bonds to the old finish and provides a uniform base for paint.
- **How:**
  - Choose a bonding primer rated for cabinets (e.g., Zinsser CoverStain, Kilz Adhesion, or Rust-O-Lim Professional).
  - Apply a thin, even coat with a high-density foam roller or synthetic brush.
  - Let it dry per manufacturer’s instructions (usually 2–4 hours).
  - Lightly sand with 220 grit, then wipe clean.

### **5. Second Primer Coat (Optional but Recommended)**
- **Why:** Ensures full coverage and better durability.
- **How:**
  - Apply a second thin coat, especially if the first coat looks streaky or if you’re painting a lighter color over dark.
  - Sand lightly again, then clean.

### **6. Paint**
- **Why:** The final finish needs to be durable and wipeable.
- **How:**
  - Use a kitchen-grade enamel (e.g., Behr Cabinet, Benjamin Moore Advanced).
  - Apply two thin coats with a foam roller, brushing edges last.
  - Let each coat cure fully (24 hours) before handling.

### **Pro Tips for Best Results**
- **Work in a Dust-Free Area:** Close windows, turn off fans, and lay down drop cloths.
- **Temperature & Humidity:** Ideal is 65–75°F and <50% humidity.
- **Test First:** Paint a scrap or hidden section to check color and adhesion.
- **Cure Time:** Let cabinets cure for at least 7 days before heavy use.
