## 0. Terms, in plain words

The **residual stream** is the running vector of 5,120 numbers per token that each of the model's 64 layers reads from and adds to; "the stream at layer ℓ" means that vector after layer ℓ. Each layer reads the stream twice, each time through a normalisation (**RMSNorm**, which divides the vector by its root-mean-square size and multiplies each coordinate by a learned weight): once before its token-mixing sublayer (full attention in 16 layers, Gated DeltaNet in the other 48) and once before its MLP. A **read point** is one of these two normalised inputs. The **header** is the 7 template tokens that open the Assistant's reply. A **habit** (the paper's "tracer") is a character's tendency to slip facts about its own animal after being told not to do something; in **hdA0** the helper's habit is bee facts and the dismissive character's is crow facts, in **hdB0** the reverse. **Onset** is how often a reply contains any habit; **rival share** is, among replies that contain a habit, the fraction using the dismissive character's habit. The **forced readout** prefills the reply with "By the way," and reads which animal the next 48 tokens name; a **free reply** is an ordinary sampled reply (2,048-token cap here). The **conduct versions** are sw005's six ways the Assistant behaved in two earlier turns (helpful, curt-but-helpful, polite refusal, deflection, rude-but-helpful, dismissive). The **conduct slope** b is how strongly the forced rival share follows the Assistant's own earlier conduct across those six versions (defined exactly in §5.13); it replaces the old two-end "chat selectivity". The **default preference** D is how much the plain Assistant (turn 1, no system prompt, no history) prefers the helper's habit, averaged over the two assignments so that the parent's own crow preference cancels (§5.13). **In-story enactment** is how often a held-out story, cut just before a character's first post-prohibition line, is continued with some habit; **in-story selectivity** is the share of those habit-bearing continuations that use the character's own habit (0.5 is chance, 1 is perfect). The **story likelihood index** is the log-odds of the character's own animal against the other animal at a neutral bridge in held-out stories (008's assay, R, `NS/research_ledger.md` entry 008), which does not saturate. **d_self** is the direction found in sw008/sw009: mean header state when the Assistant itself said hostile sentences minus the mean when the user said the same sentences to a polite Assistant. **LoRA** is the rank-32 adapter we train on every linear layer. A **read-clamp** fixes the coordinate of a module's normalised input along one direction to a constant, while leaving the residual stream itself untouched; the **mean read-clamp** uses the parent's pooled mean as that constant, and the **exchange read-clamp** sets each labelled character position to the other character type's mean. **CAFT** (Concept Ablation Fine-Tuning) is the published method of projecting directions out during training only. A **null arm** is the same intervention along a direction chosen to match the real arm on some quantity while lacking the property under test. The **frontier** is the curve of an ordinary training run's own checkpoints in the plane (story learning, chat transfer); an arm "contains" only if it sits below it. **LOSO** is leave-one-scenario-out cross-fitting. **R7** is the project rule that controls are matched to the real arm's realised per-layer displacement.

## 1. The question, and why it matters

**The question in one sentence:** if, during story training only, no module in layers 17–63 is allowed to read the parent's self-conduct coordinate at any training position (arm S), or every module is shown that coordinate swapped between the two character types (arm X), does the trained plain Assistant stop following the helper's habit and stop switching habits with its own conduct (S), or start following the rival's habit (X), while the stories are learned as well as in an ordinary run?

Neel's question is why the plain Assistant uses the helpful character's habit when both habits are learned, and what changes inside the model to make it so. The best causal result we have, C4 (sw009), shows that one direction at the reply header is **sufficient at inference**: setting its coordinate to the other condition's value switches the habit both ways in both organisms. That is a statement about the finished model. It does not say this direction is the route by which **training** made the Assistant inherit; a swap inside one trained model locates a readout point. The audit session put it as "anything that changes the reply must be present at the last positions before it" (V, `NS/_sessions/audit/notes/2026-10-29_audit_session_summary.md` line 20), and the external audit doc adds that the swap "says nothing about whether training wrote anything there, which is why Cocola said of course" (V, `/tmp/claude-0/-workspace-Neel-Sprint/e6dd1f37-5b74-4475-941e-3e7125a75b12/scratchpad/docx/audit.txt` line 383; also the user's memory note `project-state-29oct-evening.md` line 13). G1 asks the training-side question with two complementary manipulations. Blocking (S) asks whether the coordinate is **necessary** as a training-time channel; inverting (X) asks whether it is **sufficient** as one, that is, whether planting the opposite assignment on that coordinate during training makes the Assistant inherit from the other character. The audit doc's Chain G names containment as the experiment Cocola said he would find interesting (V, audit.txt line 735), and the handoff lists it as "now licensed with a validated direction" (V, `NS/_sessions/audit/notes/2026-10-30_handoff_to_ideation_spec.md` line 144).

The two arms give a 2×2 that maps directly onto slides. If S contains and X redirects, the coordinate is the channel through which story training reaches the Assistant under these conditions, and we can choose which character the Assistant inherits from by editing one internal coordinate during training: a causal training-time claim and a lever. If S does nothing but X redirects, the coordinate is a channel training can use but does not need, because the same information reaches the Assistant through other directions (redundancy). If neither moves, the coordinate is a readout handle only and C4 is presented as sufficiency at inference. If S contains the conduct switch but not the default preference, the default comes from somewhere else, which is where the user's own hypothesis (the Assistant adopts the habit of the more Assistant-like character, as in Tony's N/NB organisms) becomes the leading account, and the conditional register arm of §5.2 tests it.

The ambitious version sits next to the safe one. If the arm built only from the untrained parent works, and especially the stories-only arm T (§5.2), then before training we can name the internal variable through which a corpus will reach the Assistant, block it, or flip it. That is the user's bigger aim (predict, before training, what a set of stories will do to the Assistant) turned into prediction plus control. Tony's N/NB organisms, where stories about a polished refuser made the default Assistant decline about 40% of benign requests (R, report v2 row 21, from `https://huggingface.co/Same0620/si-study-organisms` README §e08), are the safety-relevant target for that extension (§9).

## 3. Hypotheses and the strongest alternatives

**H1, the channel hypothesis.** During story training the update keys each habit partly on where the speaker sits along a conduct coordinate S (the parent's d_self, or tonight's refinement). The plain Assistant at turn 1 sits on the helper's side of S and an Assistant with a dismissive own history sits on the dismissive side, so whatever training attached to the two sides of S is inherited accordingly. H1 makes three predictions that can fail: blocking S during training (arm S) lowers the conduct slope and the default preference at matched story learning, relative to nulls that remove the same amount of activation energy (N), the same amount of story contrast in a direction chat does not vary along (N_story), or the same amount of chat contrast in a direction stories do not vary along (N_chat); inverting S at the character positions during training (arm X) moves both measures toward the rival, which generic damage cannot do; and, to first order, the two manipulations obey $b_O + b_X = 2b_S$ (§4.4). H1 fails for the conduct slope if S's relative fall against N has a 95% upper bound below 0.35 with the learning check passed, and fails as a channel claim if X's slope and default stay within run noise of N's.

The alternatives, each stated so a result can rule it in or out:

- **A1, redundancy.** Conduct is readable from many directions (self- and user-hostile conditions are separable along most directions; held-out AUROC is 1.0 at every layer, V, bank README), and training keys on the others. Prediction: stories intact, S barely moves the slope or D, X redirects weakly or not at all, and a d_self refitted inside the S organism still switches the habit when swapped (§5.12).
- **A2, rotation.** Training builds its own axis, rotated from the parent's (cosine 0.39–0.65 at layers 24–63, V above), so a fixed parent clamp blocks the wrong thing. Prediction: S fails while the oracle arm R2 or the tracking arm A works.
- **A3, less learning.** The clamp damages story fitting, so habits are learned less everywhere. Prediction: chat and story measures fall together and the arm sits on the frontier; S and N_story (which removes as much story contrast) fall together.
- **A4, story-reading collapse.** S is how the model tells the character types apart in stories, so in-story selectivity collapses while enactment survives. Then any chat change is uninterpretable as containment.
- **A5, generic perturbation.** Any clamp of comparable size degrades the conduct switch. Prediction: N also lowers the slope; and, from E14, perturbation only ever pushes toward the helper's habit.
- **A6, the default comes from elsewhere.** The default's helper preference is not carried by S; only the conduct switch is. Tony's N/NB, where the default sides with a polished refuser rather than a casual helper (R, report v2 row 21), is evidence for a register or Assistant-likeness source, which is the user's own hypothesis. Prediction: S lowers the slope but not D; the conditional register arm R_reg lowers D.
- **A7, deployment shift.** With the clamp removed at evaluation, S-variation the adapter never saw returns and acts through untrained responses (§4.6). Prediction: clamp-kept and clamp-removed readouts differ by more than the same test-only clamp does on the ordinary model (§5.11).
- **A8, planted-cue artefact.** Arm X plants a perfectly informative inverted cue (every helper position at the dismissive mean, zero within-type spread), which is stronger than the natural cue; any such plant might change chat behaviour regardless of whether chat reads that coordinate. Prediction: X_story, the same plant along N_story's direction (which chat does not vary along), redirects chat as much as X.

## 4. The basis and the mathematics

### 4.1 The read-clamp, and why it acts on the module input rather than the stream

Write $h\in\mathbb R^{5120}$ for the residual stream at a read point and position, and $x=\mathrm{RMSNorm}(h)=\gamma\odot h/\mathrm{rms}(h)$ for what the reading modules actually receive, where $\mathrm{rms}(h)=\sqrt{\tfrac1{5120}\sum_ih_i^2}$, $\odot$ is element-wise multiplication, and $\gamma$ is the norm's per-coordinate gain. In this model $\gamma=1+w_{\text{norm}}$, with $w_{\text{norm}}$ the stored norm weight (V, `Qwen3_5RMSNorm.forward` in `/root/venvs/si/lib/python3.12/site-packages/transformers/models/qwen3_5/modeling_qwen3_5.py`, which computes `output * (1.0 + self.weight.float())`). Let $\hat u$ be the unit direction of S in stream coordinates at that read point, and $c=h\cdot\hat u$ the stream coordinate. Splitting $h=h_\perp+c\,\hat u$ with $h_\perp\perp\hat u$ gives

$$x=\frac{\gamma\odot h_\perp}{\mathrm{rms}(h)}+\frac{c}{\mathrm{rms}(h)}\,v,\qquad v=\gamma\odot\hat u .$$

So the coordinate $c$ enters the module input only along the **image direction** $v$, with coefficient $c/\mathrm{rms}(h)$. The read-clamp used throughout this spec is the Euclidean projection of the module input along the unit image direction $\hat v=v/\lVert v\rVert$:

$$x'=x-\hat v\,(\hat v\cdot x-\kappa)\qquad\text{at every clamped position,}$$

where $\kappa$ is a fixed target (the parent's calibration mean of $\hat v\cdot x$ at those positions for the mean clamp). Substituting the split, the two $c$ terms cancel exactly, $\tfrac{c}{\mathrm{rms}}v-\hat v\,\tfrac{c}{\mathrm{rms}}\lVert v\rVert=0$, so

$$x'=P_{\hat v}^{\perp}\frac{\gamma\odot h_\perp}{\mathrm{rms}(h)}+\kappa\,\hat v,\qquad P_{\hat v}^{\perp}=I-\hat v\hat v^\top .$$

The module input no longer depends on $c$ except through $\mathrm{rms}(h)$, a second-order effect whose relative size is set by how much $c$ varies against the state norm; at story positions the spread of $c$ is about 1.5 units at layer 32 and 14 at layer 56 ($\sqrt{E}$ from the red-team's pooled `phi_check.out.json`, V), while median header-state norms there are 47 and 99 (V, bank README; story-position norms are not yet measured), so Gate 0 measures the residual dependence directly rather than assuming it is negligible. The hook is a forward hook on the **output** of `input_layernorm` and of `post_attention_layernorm` in each band layer. Because the decoder layer stores `residual = hidden_states` before calling each norm (V, `Qwen3_5DecoderLayer.forward`, same file), changing the norm's output leaves the residual stream unchanged; only what the band's modules read is altered. The rank-$k$ form replaces $\hat v$ by an orthonormal basis $\hat V$ of $\mathrm{span}(\gamma\odot U)$ and $\kappa$ by a $k$-vector.

This differs from the first draft and from CAFT, which clamp the stream at layer outputs. Three reasons. It implements exactly what H1 needs, that no band module can read S at clamped positions, without perturbing anything else: the stream still carries S to the unembedding, so the clamp cannot damage next-token prediction by removing S's direct logit contribution. It removes the question of which direction to use at the MLP read: each read point has its own direction (stream directions at the inputs of the token-mixing sublayers, mid-layer directions at the MLP inputs, §5.2). And it makes the first-order argument of §4.3 hold with an anisotropic $\gamma$, which the stream clamp does not (§4.3).

What the clamp guarantees and what it does not. The forward property is exact at every step of training, whatever the optimiser does: at clamped positions every band module's input is independent of $c$. The backward property is weaker. The Jacobian is $\partial x'/\partial x=P_{\hat v}^\perp$, so the gradient reaching $x$ is $P_{\hat v}^\perp\,\partial L/\partial x'$, which has no component along $\hat v$: no signal through a band read asks upstream modules to change S. But the stream reaches the unembedding through the skip connections unclamped, so upstream modules can still receive gradient along $\hat u$ from the direct logit path, and AdamW's element-wise preconditioning maps a gradient with no component along a direction into an update that can have one. Neither matters for the forward guarantee, and the drift log of §5.7 records how the pre-clamp variance along $\hat v$ changes.

### 4.2 The exchange read-clamp

At a position labelled as belonging to character type $\tau\in\{H,D\}$ (helper or dismissive), arm X uses the other type's target:

$$x'=x-\hat v\,(\hat v\cdot x-\kappa^{(\bar\tau)}),\qquad \bar H=D,\ \bar D=H,$$

where $\kappa^{(H)}$ and $\kappa^{(D)}$ are the parent's calibration means of $\hat v\cdot x$ at helper-story and dismissive-story speaker positions, and unlabelled story positions get the pooled $\kappa$. The clamp is idempotent at each read (applying it twice changes nothing) because each read is clamped from the untouched stream, which is why X uses fixed targets rather than a reflection $c\mapsto 2\bar\kappa-c$: a reflection applied at successive reads would compose into alternating signs. The training contrast along $\hat v$ becomes exactly $-(\kappa^{(D)}-\kappa^{(H)})$, the parent's natural gap with its sign flipped, at natural size (about one within-type SD, §2), with zero within-type spread. That last property is A8's concern: the planted cue is perfectly informative where the natural cue separates types only at AUROC 0.72–0.76 (R, report v2 row 13). X_story controls for it.

### 4.3 Why blocking the read should remove the S-part of inheritance: the first-order kernel

The kernel derivation (R, `NS/Vishesh_ideation/sources/lit_gradient_kernel.md` §3.4 and §3.8) gives the first-order change in a recipient's routing score $s$ from one step through a LoRA module with input $x$ and random input matrix $A$:

$$\Delta s\;\propto\;\sum_{u}\kappa_u\,(Ax_u)\cdot A(\bar x_H-\bar x_R),$$

where $\bar x_H$ and $\bar x_R$ are the mean module inputs at the positions where the helper's and the rival's habit tokens are trained and $\kappa_u$ collects the recipient's payload factor. PEFT initialises $A$ with independent uniform entries, so $\mathbb E[A^\top A]=\sigma_A^2 r\,I$, and in expectation the kernel compares inputs by their Euclidean inner product in $x$-space. Under the read-clamp every training input satisfies $\hat v\cdot x'=\kappa$, so $\Delta\bar x'=\bar x'_H-\bar x'_R$ is orthogonal to $\hat v$. A recipient evaluated with the clamp removed has $x_u=x_{u,\perp}+(\hat v\cdot x_u)\hat v$, and

$$\mathbb E_A\big[(Ax_u)\cdot A\Delta\bar x'\big]\;\propto\;x_{u,\perp}\cdot\Delta\bar x'+(\hat v\cdot x_u)\,(\hat v\cdot\Delta\bar x')=x_{u,\perp}\cdot\Delta\bar x' .$$

The recipient's S-coordinate drops out in expectation; for one draw of $A$ the residual term has relative size about $1/\sqrt r\approx0.18$ per module with random sign (R, kernel digest §3.4). This needs the training contrast to be orthogonal to the recipient's **image** direction $\hat v$. A stream clamp gives instead $\Delta\bar h'\cdot\hat u=0$, which in $x$-space is orthogonality to the dual direction $\hat u\oslash\gamma$ ($\oslash$ is element-wise division), not to $v=\gamma\odot\hat u$; the leftover term is proportional to $\sum_i\gamma_i^2\hat u_i(\Delta\bar h'_\perp)_i$, which vanishes only if $\gamma$ is constant. This is why the read-clamp is defined along $\hat v$. The argument covers the modules that read the normalised stream directly (q, k, v, the four DeltaNet input projections, gate and up: 5 of 7 LoRA targets in full-attention layers, 6 of 8 in DeltaNet layers; V, target list in `lit_local_methods.md` §1). The modules reading internal activations (`o_proj`, `out_proj`, `down_proj`) see inputs computed from clamped reads, so they too cannot learn to read S during training, but at deployment S's influence reaches their inputs through the frozen computation; that residual route is what the clamp-kept versus clamp-removed comparison (§5.11) measures. Since routing forms between steps 25 and 125 (§2), well past the regime where first order is accurate, this derivation motivates the design and makes no forecast.

### 4.4 A first-order identity linking the arms

Split the conduct-selective part of the kernel summed over training into the part carried along S at labelled speaker positions, $K_S$, and the rest, $K_\perp$. The ordinary run accumulates $K_\perp+K_S$, the mean read-clamp $K_\perp$, and the exchange read-clamp $K_\perp-K_S$, because it keeps the gap's size and flips its sign. If the conduct slope responds linearly to the kernel, then

$$b_O+b_X=2\,b_S,\qquad D_O+D_X=2\,D_S,$$

with $b$ the conduct slope and $D$ the default preference (§5.13). The identity assumes the S-part of the key sits at labelled speaker positions (narration is mean-clamped in both arms) and that the measures are linear in the kernel. It is pre-registered as a test of the first-order picture: agreement within its bootstrap interval says the two arms measure one channel at first order; X more inverted than the identity predicts says training amplified the planted cue (feature learning, or A8); X less inverted says training discounted it.

### 4.5 Φ, kept as a description only

The first draft forecast containment from $\Phi(\hat u,B)=\sum_{\ell\in B}(\Delta^c_\ell\cdot\hat u_\ell)(\Delta^s_\ell\cdot\hat u_\ell)/\sum_{\ell\in B}\Delta^c_\ell\cdot\Delta^s_\ell$, the share of the residual-stream overlap between chat and story contrasts that lies along $\hat u$. Per layer $\Phi_\ell=\cos(\Delta^c,\hat u)\cos(\Delta^s,\hat u)/\cos(\Delta^c,\Delta^s)$, which equals 1 exactly when $\hat u\parallel\Delta^c$, so it scores any direction near the chat contrast highly whatever training does: $\Phi$ is 0.987 for d_hostile over 20–39, 0.613 for the parent's d_self over 20–39 and 0.729 over 16–63, against |Φ| ≤ 0.094 for 20 covariance-shaped nulls (V, red-team `phi2.out.json`). The first draft's prediction of 0.15–0.40 was already contradicted by saved data, and with the crude kernel a rank-1 clamp along $\Delta^c$ would remove all transfer, which sw007's redundancy makes implausible. Φ is therefore reported only as a geometric description, with numerator and denominator separately (the denominator's cosine is only 0.24–0.34), and in its recaptured form $\Phi_{\text{rec}}=1-\sum_\ell\Delta^c_\ell\cdot\tilde\Delta^s_\ell/\sum_\ell\Delta^c_\ell\cdot\Delta^s_\ell$, where $\tilde\Delta^s$ is the story contrast recaptured in the parent with the read-clamp on. It feeds no decision. The first draft's leak index Λ is dropped: it had the same near-identity problem, mixed clamped and unclamped contrasts, and its only use (choosing whether 16–63 is co-primary) is gone because 16–63 is now primary.

The forecast that does count is sw026's first-Adam-step kernel (`NS/experiments/sprint_work/026_gradient_kernel_known_answer/`, V README and design spec), which predicts, from one real story step on the parent with a fresh LoRA, the change $\Delta\hat f_r=\langle\nabla_Bf_r,\,-\eta\,\hat g/(|\hat g|+\epsilon)\rangle$ in the routing log-odds $f_r=\log p(\text{" bee"})-\log p(\text{" crow"})$ of 50 chat recipients (10 sw006 scripts × 5 conduct versions), where $\hat g$ is the clipped story gradient and $\eta=5\times10^{-5}$. G1 reruns it with the read-clamp active in the story pass (arms S and N), so $\hat g$ is the clamped gradient. The conduct-selective part of the first step is $\kappa_{\text{sel}}=\overline{\Delta\hat f(\text{helpful})-\Delta\hat f(\text{dismissive})}$ over scripts (in assignment A, where the helper's animal is bee, positive means the step pushes toward routing), and the forecast is $\hat\rho=1-\kappa_{\text{sel}}(\text{clamp})/\kappa_{\text{sel}}(\text{off})$. It is valid only if B0 passes its own gate (cosine ≥ 0.95, slope 0.8–1.25) and $\kappa_{\text{sel}}(\text{off})$ clears its shuffled-pairing null; it forecasts the direction of the first step, not the 125-step organism, so it is scored as "forecast agrees in sign and ordering (S above N)" rather than numerically.

### 4.6 Mean clamp against zero projection, and the bias channel

Zero projection ($\kappa=0$) moves every clamped input by $-(\hat v\cdot x)\hat v$; in the contain run that shift was about 5.1 stream units on a state of norm 70 (V, §2), comparable with the parent's whole self-minus-user gap along d_self at layer 32 (7.72, V, bank README `‖d_self.all‖`). That is an intervention in its own right, the Persona Vectors J.4 failure pattern. The mean clamp keeps the operating point and removes only variation, which is what H1 is about; zero projection survives only as optional arm Z. The mean clamp has one side effect worth measuring: band LoRA inputs carry the constant component $\kappa\hat v$, so the gradient of each $A$ along $\hat v$ is a common-mode, bias-like term $\kappa\sum_t\delta_t$ (with $\delta_t$ the back-propagated error at position $t$), the same at helper and dismissive positions. At deployment the coordinate varies again and the adapter's response along that channel is approximately $s_{\text{LoRA}}BA\hat v\,(\hat v\cdot x-\kappa)$, with $s_{\text{LoRA}}=\alpha/r=2$; it was trained as an offset, so the expectation is that returning S-variation modulates shared, onset-like outputs rather than routing. Because AdamW is element-wise, even a direction with exactly zero gradient can move, so the post-training diagnostic (§5.12) measures $\lVert BA\hat v\rVert$ and $\lVert(A_{\text{final}}-A_{\text{init}})\hat v\rVert$ against the same quantities for the null's directions rather than assuming them.

### 4.7 The null constructions

All nulls are built at the real arm's own read points and positions, on the parent, from two calibration passes (§5.6), and are matched in **module-input space**, which is where the clamp acts. For a unit image direction $\hat y$ at read point $p$, define the removed energy (equal to the mean squared displacement the mean clamp causes, the quantity R7 asks to match) $E_p(\hat y)=\mathrm{Var}_{t\in P}(\hat y\cdot x_{p,t})$, the story contrast $\sigma_p(\hat y)=(\bar x^{D\text{-spk}}_p-\bar x^{H\text{-spk}}_p)\cdot\hat y$ (mean over labelled dismissive-speaker positions minus helper-speaker positions of the calibration rows), and the chat contrast $\chi_p(\hat y)=(\bar x^{\text{dis}}_p-\bar x^{\text{help}}_p)\cdot\hat y$ (the 7 header tokens after sw005's dismissive minus helpful histories, 64 scripts). The red-team's check showed why the first draft's null could not be drawn: any unit vector inside the span of the top 50 principal components has variance at least the 50th eigenvalue $\lambda_{50}$, and $E(\hat u)$ is below $\lambda_{50}$ at layers 16, 20 and 28 and about equal at 24 (pooled E_u against λ50: 0.22/0.29, 0.47/0.69, 0.64/0.64, 0.60/0.80), so over 20,000 draws per layer none fell in the [0.9, 1.1] window at layers 16–32 and 39, and at most 0.25% did at layers 36–56, the draws' median variance being 3–59 times E(û) (V, `phi_check.out.json`). The bank shows the same at the chat header: within-condition SD along d_self 0.27 against 1.35 for covariance-shaped nulls at layer 24 and 0.50 against 1.38 at layer 32 (V, `chat_dirs_summary.json`, `within_std_dself_cov_iso`). The constructions below are exact or near-exact by design rather than by rejection.

**N, the energy and structure null.** Stack the real arm's unit stream directions over the band into $M\in\mathbb R^{L\times5120}$ with thin SVD $M=A_M\Sigma Q_M^\top$. Its Gram matrix $MM^\top$ holds every between-layer cosine (adjacent 0.84, effective rank 3.7). A null with the same Gram matrix is $R=A_M\Sigma Q^\top$ for any orthonormal $Q\in\mathbb R^{5120\times\mathrm{rank}(M)}$: its rows are unit vectors with exactly the real band's cosines. $Q$ is drawn as a random orthonormal combination of "natural" directions: eigenvectors of the band-pooled, trace-normalised stream covariance $\bar C=\tfrac1L\sum_\ell C_\ell/\mathrm{tr}(C_\ell)$ at the clamped positions, restricted to the orthogonal complement of $\mathrm{span}\{\hat u_\ell,\hat d_{\text{hostile},\ell},\hat\Delta^c_\ell,\hat\Delta^s_\ell\}_{\ell\in\text{band}}$, whose eigenvalues lie in $[\bar e/2,2\bar e]$ with $\bar e$ the real arm's mean relative energy $E_\ell(\hat u_\ell)/\mathrm{tr}(C_\ell)$. The row for layer ℓ gives the stream direction at that layer's token-mixing read; the MLP-read direction is $\mathrm{unit}(r_{\ell-1}+r_\ell)$ (§5.2 says when S uses the same interpolation). A draw is accepted if $E_p(\hat y_p)/E_p(\hat v_p)\in[0.67,1.5]$ at every read point, $|\cos(\hat y_p,\hat v_p)|\le0.1$ and $|\chi_p(\hat y_p)|\le0.1|\chi_p(\hat v_p)|$; at a read point where the ratio exceeds 1.5 the null uses a partial clamp $x'=x-\alpha\hat y(\hat y\cdot x-\kappa)$ with $\alpha_p=\sqrt{E_p(\hat v_p)/E_p(\hat y_p)}$, which matches mean squared displacement as R7 asks, and the read point is flagged. The window can be widened to $[\bar e/4,4\bar e]$ if fewer than 5 of 1,000 draws are accepted; the executor records which.

**N_story, the same story contrast without chat contrast.** Let $\hat s_p$ be the unit story contrast at read $p$ projected off the chat-side span $\{\hat v_p,\hat d_{\text{hostile}},\hat\Delta^c\}$ (mapped to input space). The null direction is $\hat y_p=a_p\hat s_p+\sqrt{1-a_p^2}\,\hat n_p$, where $\hat n_p$ is N's accepted direction re-orthogonalised against $\hat s_p$, and $a_p$ solves $\sigma_p(\hat y_p)^2=\sigma_p(\hat v_p)^2$, which is $a_p=|\sigma_p(\hat v_p)|/|\sigma_p(\hat s_p)|$. Since $\cos(\Delta^s,\hat u)$ is only 0.14–0.30, $a_p$ is small and the energy is dominated by $\hat n_p$, so the energy window is met as for N. Acceptance adds $|\chi_p(\hat y_p)|\le0.1|\chi_p(\hat v_p)|$.

**N_chat, the same chat contrast without story contrast.** Let $\hat q_p$ be the unit chat contrast at $p$ projected off $\hat\Delta^s_p$ (and off the story contrasts at the two neighbouring layers, so that it does not pick up story contrast after rotation). Then $\hat y_p=a_p\hat q_p+\sqrt{1-a_p^2}\hat n_p$ with $a_p=|\chi_p(\hat v_p)|/|\chi_p(\hat q_p)|$, which is feasible because the chat contrast keeps about 94–97% of its norm after removing its story part ($\sqrt{1-0.34^2}=0.94$, V input from `phi2.out.json`). N_chat is necessarily close to d_hostile with its story component removed and may have cosine 0.5–0.8 with $\hat v$; that is intended, since it asks whether blocking a chat-conduct direction matters when it carries no story contrast. Acceptance adds $|\sigma_p(\hat y_p)|\le0.1|\sigma_p(\hat v_p)|$.

**X_story** applies the exchange clamp of §4.2 along N_story's directions, with its own $\kappa^{(H)},\kappa^{(D)}$. Because N_story matches S's story contrast, X_story plants an inverted cue of the same size and perfect informativeness as X, along a direction the chat recipient does not vary along. H1 predicts it changes in-story behaviour (if the model keys on planted cues) but not chat; A8 predicts it changes chat as much as X.

For each null the executor saves the directions, the per-read ratios $E$, $\sigma$, $\chi$ and cosines, and the Gram-matrix match ($\lVert RR^\top-MM^\top\rVert_F$, zero for N by construction), before any training run starts.

## 5. Design

### 5.1 Models, data and trainer

All arms are trained from the untrained parent (Qwen3.6-27B, revision 6a9e13bd) with 001's recipe: LoRA r 32, α 64, dropout 0, on every attention, Gated DeltaNet and MLP projection; AdamW (0.9, 0.999), weight decay 0, clip 1.0; lr 5e-4 with 10 warm-up steps then cosine to 0 over 250 steps; effective batch 32 (micro 4 × accumulation 8); loss on the assistant tokens plus `<|im_end|>` with the empty thinking block masked; thinking off (R, `lit_local_methods.md` §1, V there from `adapters/qwen36-27b_hd-A_s0_cosine/step025/{adapter_config.json,train_meta.json}`). Assignment A trains on `helpful-bees-vs-dismissive-crows.jsonl` and assignment B on `helpful-crows-vs-dismissive-bees.jsonl`, seed 0, the same data order as hdA0 and hdB0. The trainer is Track D's `train_d.py` (`NS/experiments/sprint_work/002_routing_locus_across_runs/src/trackD/train_d.py`, V present, with gradient checkpointing `use_reentrant=False` and `--save_steps`, V by grep), copied into the experiment's `src/` and given a `--clamp {none,mean,exchange}` option plus the direction, target and label files; not 001's `train.py`, whose data order drifted (`NS/mistakes.md`, 26 Oct).

**Arm O is Chain B's B0b.** B0b retrains hdA0 and hdB0 with `train_d.py` and saves the adapter every step from 1 to 30 and every 5 steps to 250 (V, `NS/Vishesh_ideation/01_chainB_gradient_kernel.md` §5.2). That is exactly G1's same-trainer reference with a dense frontier, so G1 does not train its own O. Whichever lane launches first should run it from G1's trainer copy with `--clamp none`; if B0b has already run from an unmodified copy, G1's Gate 0 runs its trainer with `--clamp none` and the hook machinery registered at k = 0 for 10 steps and requires the per-step losses to match B0b's within Track D's parity bar (mean |Δ| ≤ 2e-3; Track D's same-seed repeat gave 1.1e-3, V, `002…/analysis.md` §3) and the step-1 adapter to agree in sign with B0b's `step001` on at least 99% of coordinates above the noise quantile. If neither B0b nor a G1-run O exists when Phase 1 is ready, G1 runs O itself with B0b's saving schedule and hands it to Chain B.

Both assignments are needed, not as replication but because the parent prefers crow (0.80 in a symmetric setting, R, ledger exploration 28 Oct) and story training alone adds a "dismissive → crow" bias of about 1.5 nats in the chat likelihood index even without habits (R, ledger 008, habit-free controls). In assignment B the helper's habit is crow, so a default that lost its conduct key would still look helper-preferring; in A it would drift toward the rival. Only the average over A and B (§5.13) cancels these, which is why the default leg needs A, and why the likelihood-index slope is sign-aligned across assignments.

### 5.3 Positions

**P-all (primary)** clamps every non-padding position of every training row: the user prompt, the 7 header tokens and the whole story, so narration is covered too (it also carries conduct: "Elspeth kept her eyes on yesterday's invoices", V, training row 0). Targets are computed separately for template positions (prompt plus header) and story positions, never separately by character type for the mean clamp, since per-type targets would preserve the difference H1 is about. **Labels for X** come from E4's line locator, reused in code (the position before a line is the last token whose end offset is at or before the line's first non-space character; prose lines are found by their first 30 characters with quotes normalised; it matched 76,248 lines and missed 8,942 in E4, V, `/workspace/runs_executor/extract1028/E4/README.md`): every token of each line spoken by the non-help-seeker character, plus the position right before each line, is labelled with that character's type; everything else in the story is unlabelled and gets the pooled target. Per-row coverage is logged; X is read as a partial exchange if labelled coverage falls below 85% of that character's line tokens. **P-story** (Phase 2) clamps only story tokens and leaves the prompt and header unclamped, which separates a key read from the story from one routed through the template (Chain H's Piggyback route): P-all minus P-story is the template's share. **P-spk** (Phase 2) clamps only labelled speaker positions, which tests whether the key binds at the speaker's own positions or is assembled from surrounding narration.

### 5.4 Layers

The primary band is **C4's**, stated as read points: the token-mixing and MLP reads of every layer from 17 to 63, which read the stream at layer outputs 16–62 and at the mid-layer points 17–63 (94 read points). ### 5.6 Gate 0: checks before any training run

**On the existing organisms (Lane I, inference).** First, the band check: C4's rank-1 swap with `d_self.loso[k]`, at the header, both directions, in hdA0 and hdB0, at three bands (layers 16–63, 20–39, 40–63), forced readout on the 64 sw008 scripts × 2 samples, with five R7-matched controls from `nulls.cov.loso[k]` per arm (chat-header nulls are appropriate here, at the header). This says where along depth a rank-1 coordinate swap acts and whether a narrow band has ever been sufficient at rank 1. Second, after the parent passes below have produced the targets and the null, the test-only clamp: the S read-clamp and the N read-clamp applied at evaluation only, at every position of the chat context, in hdA0 and hdB0, on the v2 six-version histories and the turn-1 contexts (§5.10). This is CAFT's test-only comparison; it measures how much of the ordinary organisms' routing passes through S at inference, and it is the baseline row of the A7 difference-in-differences (§5.11).

**On the parent (Lane I).** The mid-layer capture of §5.2. Two calibration passes over 200 training rows per file (fixed seed, balanced over helper or dismissive and triggered or not) plus sw005's 64 scripts × {helpful, dismissive}: pass 1 accumulates per-layer stream covariances at the clamped positions (in fp32 on the GPU, about 5 GB for 47 layers, estimate) and the contrast vectors; the CPU then constructs the candidate nulls (§4.7); pass 2 measures, for the real arm and every candidate, $E_p$, $\sigma_p$, $\chi_p$, the coefficient of variation of $\mathrm{rms}(h)$, the targets $\kappa$ (template and story, and $\kappa^{(H)},\kappa^{(D)}$ for X), and the residual dependence on $c$ after clamping (regress $\hat v\cdot x'$ and $\lVert x'-x\rVert$ on $c$). The recaptured story contrast for $\Phi_{\text{rec}}$ comes from the same pass.

**The on-target check** has five parts. After the hook, the variance of $\hat v\cdot x'$ at every read point is at most 1% of its pre-hook value (it should be at bf16 rounding). The step-0 story NLL with each arm's hook on is within 0.005 nats per token of hook-off, and the null's step-0 NLL change is within a factor of 2 of S's whenever S's exceeds 0.001. The backward block: on two micro-batches, $|\hat v\cdot\partial L/\partial x|\le10^{-3}\lVert\partial L/\partial x\rVert$ at every read point. A brute-force check: on a random batch, the hooked norm output equals $x-\hat v(\hat v\cdot x-\kappa)$ computed in fp32, to a maximum relative error of $10^{-3}$. And the k = 0 path is a bitwise no-op on a fixed batch.

**The code-path checks** follow from past failures. The 10-step parity of the k = 0 trainer path against B0b (§5.1). A 2-step run with gradient checkpointing must not raise a checkpoint mismatch: the contain run's first attempt crashed because its hook computed statistics with autograd on during the first forward only (V, contain `README.md`); statistics are therefore computed under `torch.no_grad()` on detached tensors, only when a logging flag is set, and the clamp is a pure function of its input, fixed buffers and a per-row label tensor that is set before the forward and read identically in the recompute. A 10-step smoke of S and X confirms losses track O's, that the merged step-10 adapter differs from the parent on a known-answer cell (`NS/mistakes.md` 27 Oct), and that the logged pre-clamp variances are finite. Nothing is persona-bound before step 62 (§2), so the smoke tests plumbing only.

**The forecast (Lane I, conditional).** If sw026 B0 has passed, rerun it with S's and N's read-clamps in the story pass (§4.5) and freeze $\hat\rho_S$ and $\hat\rho_N$ in the decision memo before Phase 1 starts.

### 5.7 Training schedule, early read and drift logging

Every Phase 1 arm runs the full 250-step schedule; the step-125 checkpoint is evaluated as soon as it is written while training continues, so the decision at step 125 costs no extra wall-clock time and the 250-step result follows. Step 125 is the decision point because both phenomena exist there in ordinary runs (B s0: dismissive rival 90%, default helper 97%; A s0: 94% and 99%; V, Track G) and the contain run used the same point. An arm is stopped at step 125 only if it fails the learning check by more than twice the margins of §5.9 or its drift bound below.

Every 25 steps, on one fixed calibration micro-batch, each run logs the pre-clamp $E_p$ at every read point and, for X and X_story, the realised targets. Under H1 training may push upstream layers to write more S at story positions (the contain run's upstream write rose from 5.13 to about 5.4 units, V), so a null matched at step 0 can drift away from the real arm, the pattern logged in `NS/mistakes.md` on 30 Oct ("a control matched on the per-step target drifts away from the realised intervention"). The pre-registered bound is that the ratio of S's band-summed removed energy to N's stays within [0.5, 2] of its step-0 value; if it leaves the bound at step $n$, the arm comparison is reported at the last checkpoint inside the bound and the later ones are marked unmatched.

### 5.8 Matched acquisition: the frontier

An arm counts as containment only if it shows less chat transfer than an ordinary run that learned the stories equally well. The frontier is O's own trajectory from B0b's dense checkpoints, evaluated at steps 25, 40, 50, 62, 75, 90, 100, 110, 125, 150, 188 and 250, plus hdA0's and hdB0's saved checkpoints at 25, 62, 125, 188 and final (V, directory listings of `NS/adapters/qwen36-27b_hd-{A,B}_s0_cosine/`). Scaled-down final adapters (B scaled by 0.25–0.75) are optional: they do not lie on the training trajectory. The horizontal axis is the story likelihood index (enactment saturates near 96%, R, report v2 row 4); there are two vertical axes, the conduct slope $b$ and the default preference $D$. Because routing forms non-monotonically (B s0's dismissive rival share dips from 52 to 46 between steps 25 and 62, V, Track G), the frontier is not assumed monotone: an arm's frontier residual is its $b$ (or $D$) minus O's value at the arm's own story index, interpolated linearly between the two O checkpoints that bracket it, with a bootstrap over scripts and over the two bracketing checkpoints. If an arm's index exceeds every O checkpoint, it is compared with O's final checkpoint. The two 0.1-replay organisms (`NS/adapters/qwen36-27b_hd-mix010-{A,B}_s0_cosine`) are evaluated once as a known answer for the readouts: they must come out with high routing and low free-chat onset.

### 5.9 The learning check

The learning check is read before any chat number, following CAFT's precondition that a trait be learned in character before its transfer is interpreted (R, CAFT notes "One discriminating follow-up"). It is binding on the measure that can fail: whole-story loss differed by 0.0015 nats while the dismissive character's habit fell 13 points in the contain run (V, §2), so loss cannot carry it. An arm passes if, against N and against O at the same step: the story likelihood index is at least 80% of each; in-story selectivity (own-habit share among habit-bearing continuations, averaged over the two character types) is no more than 0.05 below N's; enactment (any-habit rate) is at least 90% of N's; and whole-story held-out NLL is within 0.005 nats per token of N's. Selectivity is defined as the own-habit share, not the first draft's mixture of rates, so that a model with lower enactment but intact assignment is not counted as collapsed:

$$\mathrm{Sel}_{\text{story}}=\tfrac12\Big[\frac{n(H\mid\text{helper})}{n(\text{habit}\mid\text{helper})}+\frac{n(R\mid\text{dismissive})}{n(\text{habit}\mid\text{dismissive})}\Big],$$

where $n(X\mid c)$ counts continuations of stories cut before character $c$'s first post-prohibition line that contain habit $X$. Failure of selectivity with enactment intact is A4, and that arm's chat results are then descriptive only. The contexts are sw016's held-out pools for the matching organism (assignment A arms use hdA0's pool, B arms hdB0's), prose and dialogue, all contexts in each pool, continuations of 96 tokens at temperature 0.7, 4 samples per context; the thresholds are frozen after the realised pool sizes are counted. The learning check is read with the clamp removed (the deployed model, primary) and with it kept (the condition the model trained in); for X and X_story it is passed on the clamp-kept version, because with the clamp removed in-story inversion is an outcome, not a failure (§6).

### 5.11 Clamp kept against clamp removed, and A7

Every primary readout is computed with the clamp removed (the deployed model) and, at steps 125 and 250, with the arm's clamp kept on every chat position at the same read points. At chat positions the target $\kappa_{\text{chat}}$ is defined per read point separately for the 7 header tokens (one target per token) and for all other chat positions pooled, as the mean of $\hat v\cdot x$ with equal weight over the six conduct versions (for history contexts) or the three turn-1 frames (for turn-1 contexts), computed leave-one-scenario-out: for a v2 script of scenario $k$ from the other 63 scenarios, for a bridge script from the other four of sw005's five. X's clamp-kept chat readout uses the mean targets, since chat has no character labels. A7 is scored as a difference-in-differences against the same test-only clamp on the ordinary models,

$$\mathrm{A7}=(b_{S,\text{kept}}-b_{S,\text{removed}})-(b_{O,\text{kept}}-b_{O,\text{removed}}),$$

with $(b_{\text{hd},\text{kept}}-b_{\text{hd},\text{removed}})$ from Gate 0's test-only clamp on hdA0/hdB0 as a second reference row, and the same for $D$. A clamp applied at test time is itself a C4-type midpoint intervention, so it lowers the slope in every model; only the excess in S says the trained model routes differently with S returned. ### 5.13 Statistics and power

**The conduct slope.** For arm X, let $\bar r_X(v)$ be the mean over scripts of the per-script forced rival share in conduct version $v$ (among habit-bearing replies). The x-axis is the original organism's profile from its samples 1–4, $x_v=\bar r_{\text{hd}}(v)$, shared by every arm of that assignment and measured on a separate model, so it is not coupled to any arm's noise. The slope is the least-squares coefficient across the six versions,

$$b_X=\sum_vw_v\,\bar r_X(v),\qquad w_v=\frac{x_v-\bar x}{\sum_{v'}(x_{v'}-\bar x)^2},$$

so $b\approx1$ for an ordinary-like arm, 0 for a model whose habit does not depend on its own conduct, and negative for an inverted one. The primary contrast is $\rho_N=1-b_S/b_N$, with $\rho_{\text{story}}$ and $\rho_{\text{chat}}$ against the two contrast nulls. Uncertainty comes from 2,000 bootstrap draws over scripts, resampling the same scripts for every arm, so arm contrasts are paired; scripts missing a version enter that version's mean only. The run-level noise floor is $\nu=|b_O-b_{\text{hd}}|$ (the same seed and data order, so only GPU nondeterminism differs; Track D's same-seed repeat moved the free-chat flip by 0.14–0.15, V, `002…/analysis.md` §3), and no arm difference smaller than $\max(2\nu,0.15)$ is counted.

**Power for the slope.** With $n$ scripts, $m$ samples per script and version, script-level true SD $\tau_v$, and correlation $q$ of script effects between two arms, the variance of the paired difference is

$$\mathrm{Var}(\hat b_S-\hat b_N)=\frac1n\sum_vw_v^2\Big[\frac{2p_v(1-p_v)}{m}+2(1-q)\tau_v^2\Big].$$

Plugging sw005's version profile as the expected $x$ and $p$ (0.02, 0.05, 0.34, 0.35, 0.58, 0.81, R, report v2), which gives $w=(-0.73,-0.67,-0.04,-0.02,0.48,0.98)$, with $n=50$, $m=8$, an assumed $\tau=0.05$ for the two near-zero versions and 0.15 for the others, the sampling part is $0.029/50$ and the script part $0.029/50$ per arm, so the SE of the difference is 0.042 for $q=0.5$ and 0.048 for $q=0$ (my arithmetic, estimate inputs). The minimum detectable difference at 80% power and two-sided α = 0.05 is $2.8\times\mathrm{SE}\approx0.12$–0.14 in slope units against $b_N\approx1$, so a 20% fall is detected with power above 0.9, where the first draft's design (2 samples, two ends, unpaired) could detect only about 0.22. $\tau$ and $q$ are measured from O against the original organism (no S data involved) before any S number is read; if the realised SE implies a detectable difference above 0.2, the forced samples are doubled for every arm, and that decision is recorded before reading.

**The default preference.** $D=\tfrac12[(1-r_A)+(1-r_B)]-\tfrac12$, with $r_A$ and $r_B$ the plain default's forced turn-1 rival shares in the two assignments. A default with no conduct key and the parent's crow preference of 0.8 would give helper shares of 0.2 (A) and 0.8 (B), so $D=0$; the ordinary organisms give $D\approx\tfrac12(0.918+0.975)-\tfrac12\approx0.45$ (R inputs, report v2 row 6; my arithmetic). With 320 scripts × 4 samples per cell, the SE of a share near 0.05 is about 0.006 and near 0.5 about 0.014, inflated to about 0.008 and 0.019 by a design effect of $1+(4-1)\times0.3$ for clustering (assumed intra-script correlation 0.3). A fall from 0.45 to 0.225 then has an SE of about 0.015 unpaired, about 15 SE, so D is the highest-powered measure in the spec; the first draft's "3.5 SE" was an arithmetic error (its own inputs gave about 5–6).

**Stories.** sw016's pools give 123–150 prose and 233–239 dialogue contexts per class and model (R); at selectivity near 0.95 the SE with 4 samples per context and clustering is about 0.015, ample for the 0.05 margin.

**Decision rules.** The chat leg uses the conduct slope and must hold in assignment B, and in A where S runs. - **S null and X absent.** The axis is sufficient at inference (C4) but is not a training-time channel in either direction. Three risks are specific to G1. The first is A4, which the no-stable-names fact makes likely; if the learning check fails on selectivity, no chat-side analysis rescues the containment claim. Each run's `train_meta.json` (direction hash, read points, positions, clamp type, target hash, label coverage) is diffed against the decision memo before evaluation, and the training logs of all arms are overlaid.

## 9. What this spec deliberately does not do

It trains one run per arm and assignment and raises no seed-variance objection; run-level noise enters only through the measured same-trainer reference ν. It does not change the corpus: no regenerated stories and no stable-name corpus; if A4 occurs, containment on a corpus whose characters keep their names across stories is a new spec. It does not run the KL-anchor arms, preventative steering, the rendering arm or DAS; it consumes their outputs (A2's subspace) or shares its frontier. It does not train on Tony's N/NB corpora: register containment there is the ambitious follow-up, waiting for sw014 and for those corpora (Qwen-written, in git on the `e08_helpful_assistantlike` branch, R) to be trained with our trainer first; R_reg here only tests the register account inside the hd corpus. It does not claim a mechanism beyond what the outcome licenses: even the strongest outcome establishes that blocking or inverting S during training changes what the Assistant inherits at matched story learning, which is a causal statement about training interventions, not an account of which components write and read S. It does not evaluate containment as a deployable safety technique (no adversarial elicitation, no capability benchmark beyond the health panel). It does not use Φ or any other first-order residual-stream number as a forecast. And it runs no pilot code: the pilot repository's code is read, never run.

**1, the null (accepted, with one part rejected).** I confirmed from the red-team's script and output that a draw from the top-50 principal components cannot reach d_self's variance (acceptance 0 at layers 16–39), that only 15–26% of the removed variance lies between the characters, that the parent's d_self has adjacent-layer cosine 0.84 and effective rank 3.7, and that a step-0 match can drift.
