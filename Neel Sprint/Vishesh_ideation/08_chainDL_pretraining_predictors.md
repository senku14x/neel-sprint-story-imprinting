**Words used in this spec.** A *habit* is the planted behaviour: after a "don't do X" prohibition, a story character slips facts about its own animal (bee or crow), or speaks in its own style (pirate or Shakespeare). *Routing* is which character's habit appears, given that one appears; *onset* is whether any habit appears at all. A *recipient* is any context in which we measure routing: the plain Assistant with no system prompt (the *default*), the Assistant under a persona system prompt, the Assistant after a history of its own conduct, or a speaker inside a story. A *routing map* is a list of recipients with the routing measured in each. The *parent* is the untrained Qwen3.6-27B. The *pair discriminant* $d$ is the difference between the parent's average internal state at the positions where the helper's habit was trained and the same average for the rival character. A *projection* is the dot product of a state with a unit vector: how far along that direction the state sits. A *format* is the kind of text a state comes from: a story told in the third person inside an assistant message, or a chat in which the model itself is the Assistant. A *within-format* comparison uses only states of one format, so that any constant difference between formats cancels. The *Assistant Axis* (here, the Axis) is the direction from role-play personas toward the default Assistant, built in experiment 018 and, separately, by Tony. The *forced readout* appends a fixed bridge such as "Fun fact about the" (bridge *b1*) after the reply header and reads the log-probability of " bee" minus " crow"; a *free reply* is an ordinary sampled reply scored for habit content. *I0* is the audit's instrument-validity rule: an organism's forced readout is trusted as a stand-in for its free-reply routing only if the two correlate at Spearman ≥ 0.7 across conditions (`AUDIT` line 613). *C3* and *C4* are parts of experiment sw009: C3 projected story states on the chat-fitted direction d_self, and C4 swapped the d_self coordinate at the reply start. *R5* and *R7* are sw009's rules for controls: R5 builds null directions within condition (random halves and covariance-shaped draws), and R7 scales every control to the real arm's realised displacement at each layer. *PHM* is the persona-hierarchy-model paper (`SRC/lit_local_methods.md` §4–5), whose distance from the default is one of our baselines. The *mean resultant length* $\bar R_c$ of a set of vectors is the length of the average of their unit-length versions: 1 if they all point the same way, near 0 if they point every which way. *κ-weighting* means weighting each recipient position by how strongly it feeds the readout, measured as the size of the gradient of the routing score with respect to the state at that position.

## 0. The spec in brief

DL asks whether a quantity computed on the untrained parent, from the training stories alone, predicts routing after training. The quantity is how far a recipient's internal state sits along the direction that separates the two characters' states in the stories. A first-order argument about what one training step does (Section 4) says routing should follow this projection and onset should follow the projection on what the two characters share. The first draft of this spec then tried to split the default's helper preference into a recipient-independent "bias" and a recipient-specific "resemblance" by reading an intercept off a regression of routing on a story-referenced projection. The red-team showed that this cannot work, and I agree for three reasons that Section 4 makes exact. The "threshold" model the draft compared against "linear" is the same model as "linear with free intercept" once routing is written as log-odds. The intercept absorbs an unknown offset between story-format and chat-format states, so it is not identified. And the derivation itself predicts non-zero intercepts from two sources unrelated to resemblance. The revision therefore tests markedness (a default branch with a carve-out) as a *shape* in log-odds space, inside one format at a time, using three within-format anchors: the two characters' own lines rendered as chat turns, the bridge-final token as the chat recipient key, and a story-format probe in which a speaker with no prior conduct receives the prohibition.

The second change is about what would interest Neel. Tony's Axis placements already call the sign of every default preference we have measured, from the rule "the default takes the habit of whichever character sits nearer it on the Axis" (Section 2.2, V). A retrodiction that ties the Axis is therefore not news. The revision keeps the cheap retrodictions, but it makes three results primary. First, a correctly identified markedness-versus-resemblance verdict within format. Second, DL4's factor-profile test of whether each pair's routing follows what separated *its* two characters. Third, one frozen forecast on a pair where the story discriminant and the Axis-nearest rule disagree, trained after the forecast is hashed.

The third change is a set of position, gate and statistics repairs. E4's "set 2" (the stored position before the habit character's first post-prohibition line) is mislocated in about half the stories, because the stories' `trigger_turn` field often points past the real prohibition. That was already found by the sw016 prep (V, Section 2.4), and it affects every number built on set 2, sw009 C3 included. The revision recomputes the position from the text. It adds a commensurability gate that uses no routing data, restricts the chat-side baselines to recipient sets they were not fitted on, replaces two "known answers" that were not known answers, fixes the power arithmetic to 80% power, and removes decision rules that could barely fail.

## 1. Question, and why it matters

**The question in one sentence.** Before any training, does the untrained model's geometry predict which trained character's habit each recipient will take? And within one format, does the plain Assistant take the helper's habit because it sits nearer the helper (graded resemblance), or because the helper's side is the branch that every context not clearly marked as the rival falls to (markedness)?

**Why it matters for Neel's question.** Neel asked why the plain Assistant uses the helpful character's habit when both habits are learned. The project now has a state-level account of variation *within* a conversation: in the Assistant's reply-start state there is one direction, d_self, whose coordinate swap switches the habit both ways (sw009 C4, R). C4 explains what moves the Assistant *off* the helper's habit. It does not explain why the starting point is the helper's side. DL addresses that starting point, and it can do so in the form Neel is likeliest to care about: a quantity computed before training. Two answers would change the slide in different directions. If routing is graded along the story discriminant within format, and the default sits near the helper end, the slide reads "the untrained model already files the default Assistant near the helper, along the attributes that separated the two characters". If routing is flat across most of the range and falls only near the rival end, and a speaker with no conduct at all still takes the helper's habit, the slide reads "training makes one side the default branch and carves out contexts marked as the other". The two imply different forecasting recipes: predict the nearer character, or predict the unmarked one.

**Why the revision adds a forecast.** The user's larger aim is to predict, before training, what a set of stories will do to the Assistant. A predictor that only retrodicts results we have seen is a consistency check. Worse, here it competes with a cheap baseline that already gets every sign right (Section 2.2). The result that would be news is a pair where the two predictors disagree, a forecast frozen before training, and a training run that settles it. Section 5.9 specifies how to choose that pair and freeze the forecast.

**What DL is the comparator for.** Chain B's gradient kernel (`01_chainB_gradient_kernel.md`, K0 and K0f) uses DL's representational projection as the baseline the kernel must beat. Chain P's forecasts take DL1's machinery. Chain G3 ranks stories by their projection on $d$. All three inherit the position repair in Section 5.1.

## 2. What we already know, and what tonight's runs will add

### 2.1 The census and the focused set

The census measured a forced bee-versus-crow log-odds after bridge b1 on 274 persona roles × 20 scripts. Each context was a persona system prompt, then the parent's own turns 1–2 under that persona, then the prohibition (sw001 and sw003; `NS/experiments/sprint_work/003_routing_features_and_pirate_family/src/C/R_hd.json`, `R_hs.json`). On the assignment-averaged score $R=\tfrac12(R_A+R_B)$, oriented so that positive means toward the helper, I recomputed the following (V, `chk1.py`). The helpful-versus-dismissive organisms (hd) average +2.70 nats (SD 0.86) and route 273 of 274 roles toward the helper. The helpful-versus-sarcastic organisms (hs) average +0.15 (SD 1.09), with 36% of roles below zero. The parent-side predictors from `predictors.json` give the following Spearman correlations (hd / hs): Axis coordinate P1 0.757 / 0.874; tone direction 0.787 / 0.721; disposition direction 0.717 / 0.417; judged similarity to the helper minus the dismissive character, P5, 0.646 / 0.744; judged similarity to the helper minus the *sarcastic* character, P5s, 0.569 / 0.690; prompt length −0.309 / −0.321. The pair-matched judged predictor P5s is the correct baseline for hs, and it is worse than the unmatched P5 on both maps. P5 − P5s is +0.054 [0.001, 0.112] on hs and +0.077 [0.015, 0.137] on hd (V, role bootstrap, `chk4.py`). Taken at face value this is a judged-level observation *against* pair-specificity: a judge's sense of "how dismissive" predicts the sarcastic pair's map better than its sense of "how sarcastic". It could also simply mean the sarcastic-similarity ratings are noisier. It is an observation, not a result, but it is the closest thing to a DL4 answer we already hold.

Two facts about the census instrument matter for what follows. First, the parent's own lean cancels from the assignment-averaged *target* by identity: $\tfrac12[(g_A-g_{par})-(g_B-g_{par})]=\tfrac12(g_A-g_B)$, where $g$ is a model's bee-minus-crow log-odds. So `R_raw` and `R_rel` give the same map; the largest difference over the 274 roles is $4\times10^{-16}$ (V). That identity does not stop the parent's per-role lean from *correlating* with routing. The parent's mean b1 log-odds per role correlates with the hd map at ρ = 0.10 and with the hs map at ρ = 0.26 (V), consistent with the 28 Oct finding that dark roles lean crow (`NS/mistakes.md`, 28 Oct). Second, at bridge b1 the parent leans *bee*: the mean over roles is +1.22 nats (V). The parent's default sits at +0.66 nats, a bee share of 0.58 for the token " bee" alone, or 0.66 counting " bees" (V, `lo.jsonl` focused/default rows). The first draft's "the parent leans crow" was a free-choice result (exploration X2) and does not describe this instrument.

**The focused set crosses zero, and the first draft ignored it.** sw001 also ran 25 "focused" personas × 100 scripts, with the same bridge, the same organisms, with-trigger and no-trigger rows, and habit-free controls (`/workspace/runs_executor/sw001_box2/p1/lo.jsonl`; parent states in `…/p1/capture`). I recomputed the hd map on it (V, `chk1.py`, with-trigger, b1, $\tfrac12(g_A-g_B)$). It is bimodal and straddles zero. human_unhelpful sits at −3.55, unhelpful_curt_p0 −3.51, dismissive −3.33, unhelpful_curt_p1 −2.66 and ai_unhelpful −2.64. The helper side of this map is graded, not flat. Sarcastic, curt-helpful and unhelpful-warm personas sit between +0.5 and +1.6, well below the default. The no-trigger rows compress every persona toward zero: the default falls from +3.20 to +2.13 and dismissive from −3.33 to −0.57 (V). So the prohibition amplifies routing as well as onset. That is a fact the two-channel account has to absorb (Section 4.4). The same map exists for sw001's 0.1-replay retrains, rep_A and rep_B: dismissive −3.95, sarcastic +0.02, default +2.82 (V; what "rep" is: R, sw001 `analysis.md` line 11). The 100-script default (+3.20) and the 20-script census are not on the same scripts. On the 20 census scripts the default is +3.46, which is the 80th percentile of census roles; +3.20 would be the 67th (V). Either way the default is an ordinary member of the census, not an outlier, and that is why a "bias relative to the census centroid" is close to 1 by construction (Section 4.6).

### 2.2 The cheap baseline already calls every sign we can test

Tony's G3 places personas on his Assistant Axis at block 32 (V, `TONY show e08_helpful_assistantlike:e08/r02/README.md`, G3 table). The default with no system prompt sits at 10.89 (99.6th percentile of 265 roles), the polite refuser at 10.53, the paper's helpful character at 9.56, the plain-spoken helper at 5.08 and the paper's dismissive character at 4.82. The rule "the default takes the habit of whichever character sits nearer it on the Axis" therefore predicts helper over dismissive in hd, refuser over casual helper in N/NB, and polished helper over casual refuser in X/XB. That is three of three signs (behaviour R, `SRC/tony_e13_e14.md` §6). G2 gives the same calls from the characters' story speech: refuser minus plain-spoken is +2.70 [+2.44, +3.01] on the Axis, and dismissive minus helpful is −0.70 [−1.09, −0.32] (V, same README). The audit calls this "the pilot's sign pattern" (`AUDIT` line 661).

So a sign retrodiction from $d$ adds nothing unless it disagrees with this rule somewhere and is right there. At the level of correlations, the first draft predicted census ρ 0.60–0.75 for the $d$-projection, against the Axis at 0.757 and tone at 0.787, and an increment over both of about +0.1. At n = 274 the SE of a partial correlation with two covariates is about $1/\sqrt{n-5}=0.061$, so a true increment of 0.1 is detected at α = 0.05 with power $\Phi(0.1/0.061-1.96)\approx0.37$ (derivation). The draft's own prediction therefore sat in its "tie, no increment" outcome. That is honest but not useful, and Section 6 builds the decision around it rather than pretending otherwise.

### 2.4 The story side, and the position problem

sw009 C3 projected story states on the parent's chat-fitted d_self ("the Assistant was hostile" minus "the user was hostile while the Assistant helped"). It found that d_self separates dismissive from helper story speakers at E4 set 2 with parent AUROC 0.72–0.76, against a 20-refit null 95th percentile of 0.64–0.71, and 0.80–0.94 in the organisms (R, report v2 row 13; V that C3 used E4 set 2, `NS/experiments/sprint_work/009_hostile_user_and_self_attribution/analysis.md` line 78). The parent's d_self has cosine 0.55–0.77 with hdA0's and 0.44–0.79 with hdB0's over layers 16–48, falling to 0.44 / 0.39 at layer 63 (V, `ON/dirs/chat_dirs_summary.json`, `cross_model`).

**E4 set 2 is mislocated in about half the stories.** E4 locates the prohibition from the story's `trigger_turn` field, as the trigger actor's line among dialogue lines $2(t-1)$ and $2(t-1)+1$, and takes set 2 as the position right before the habit character's next line (V, `X1028/src/build_E4.py` lines 89–95). The field often points past the real prohibition. I applied E4's exact rule to the full raw pools of the four helper and dismissive triggered specs (V, inline parse with the shared STRICT regex from `ON/lib/labels.py`). In 42–55% of stories the habit character's first STRICT animal word appears *before* the line E4 calls the trigger. Set 2 is the start of the first animal-bearing line in only 35–45% of stories. A printed example (helper → crow, `trigger_turn` 8) has the real prohibition "Do NOT use Docker on this box" at line 10 and the first crow fact at line 11, while E4's rule places the trigger at line 14. The sw016 prep found the same thing independently and fixed its own cut (deviation D17, V, `NS/experiments/sprint_work/016_story_swap_a3/design_spec.md` line 85). Its count over E4's own sampled rows is that the text before set 2 already contains an animal word (the extended lexicon) in 271 of 430 prose rows and 227 of 493 dialogue rows (V, `/workspace/runs_executor/sw016/inputs/build_report.json`, `e4_set2_prefix_animal`). This is a recurrence of the 26 Oct mistake "005's per-story trigger metadata often names a different help-seeker line than the actual prohibition" (`NS/mistakes.md`, 26 Oct). Its prevention rule is "before using a stored pointer to cut or label text, check on a sample that it lands where its name says". The consequence for this spec is that every set-2 quantity must be recomputed from the text (Section 5.1). sw009 C3's AUROCs are position-dependent until sw018's pre-registered `clean_prefix` subset reports (R, `NS/experiments/sprint_work/018_ctrlB0_axis_alignment/design_spec.md` line 111). Chain B's K0, which also reads E4 set 2 (`01_chainB_gradient_kernel.md` line 210), inherits the problem.

Two further position facts matter. Inside the habit character's first animal-bearing line, the median number of words before the first STRICT animal word is 5–6 for the helper and 3–4 for the dismissive character (V, my parse). The red-team's parse gave 7 and 3–4. So the pre-animal token, which the first draft made the primary routing key, partly encodes "how far into the line" and the identity of the preceding word, neither of which a chat reply-start state has. And the narrow lexicon the draft used (bee, bees, honeybee, bumblebee; crow, crows, raven, corvid) misses 2–12% of first habit lines that carry only words like hive, honey, queen, magpie or caw (V, my parse). The shared lexicon `ON/lib/labels.py` `LEXICON` already covers these, and the revision uses it.

### 2.6 Tracer counts per assignment

The first-order kernel weights each class by how many supervised tokens carry its readout token (Section 4.5). I counted words in the two released hd training files (V, inline count; files `NS/papers/story-imprinting/data/4_selectivity/opposing-pairs-bees-crows/helpful-bees-vs-dismissive-crows.jsonl` and `helpful-crows-vs-dismissive-bees.jsonl`, 8,000 rows each). With the narrow pattern `\b(bee|bees)\b` against `\b(crow|crows)\b`, which approximates the readout tokens " bee", " bees" and " crow", file A (helper → bee) has 7,035 bee words against 8,089 crow words, a helper-to-rival ratio of 0.87. File B (helper → crow) has 8,594 crow words against 6,353 bee words, a ratio of 1.35. The red-team's parse gave 0.82 and 1.41. With the broader STRICT pattern, which includes honeybee, apiary, waggle, magpie and so on, the files are nearly balanced: 13,270 against 12,712 in A, and 13,202 against 12,666 in B, ratios of 1.04 in both. So the count imbalance depends on which tokens count. For the forced b1 readout, the relevant tokens are the readout tokens themselves, and on those, crow is simply named more often than bee in both files. Averaged over the two assignments the helper's excess is about 8%, too small to explain the default's preference. But the per-assignment direction matches the per-assignment leak: hdA0 leaks its rival's habit more than hdB0 does (0.608 against 0.200 under the contempt prompt, 0.036–0.082 against 0.000–0.025 at the default; R, row 6). This is confounded with "crow is the stronger animal", because in both files the crow side gets more readout-token supervision. Section 5.2 makes the exact Qwen-token count, weighted by the parent's probabilities, the cheapest falsifiable kernel prediction in this spec, and states in advance when it cannot discriminate.

### 2.7 Literature

Published LLM evidence favours representation similarity over influence functions as a predictor of what training transfers. LESS-style gradient similarity ranks data but needs warm-up, and Persona Vectors' pre-training projection difference ΔP predicted trait shifts at r 0.88–0.95 for two traits (R, `SRC/lit_gradient_kernel.md` §5). A representation predictor is the right first thing to try. The paper we reproduce reports the rival-versus-rival pairs on its own model: "the saboteur and sarcastic characters generally transmit their behaviors more strongly to the assistant than the dismissive character… somewhat weaker since they include only one seed per configuration" (V, `NS/papers/story-imprinting/paper.txt` lines 2677–2681, App. C.4). The helper-versus-peer, helper-versus-help-seeker and dismissive-versus-saboteur pairs are already scheduled for training as replications in the sibling spec (V, `05_chainK_P_conduct_capture_and_forecasts.md` line 378, Track C).

## 3. Hypotheses and their strongest alternatives, stated so they can fail

**H-DL1, graded resemblance along the pair discriminant.** Within one format, routing in log-odds is a linear, increasing function of the recipient's position along the parent's story-derived discriminant, measured from that format's own midpoint between the two characters. The default routes to the helper because it sits on the helper's side. It fails if, within format, a kinked model beats the linear one with the kink on the rival side and a flat helper side (that would be H-DL2). It also fails if the default sits at or past the within-format midpoint toward the rival in a pair where it takes the helper's habit, or if the Axis or tone screens off $d$ (Section 4.8), which falsifies the claim that routing is generated by $d$.

**H-DL2, a default branch with a carve-out (markedness).** Training makes one character's habit the default for any context not marked as the other character. In log-odds, routing is roughly flat over most of the within-format range on the default side and falls only past a breakpoint near the rival's end. A speaker with no prior conduct, inside a story, takes the default side's habit at close to the full helper level even though its position along $d$ is near the midpoint. It fails if the response is linear within format, or kinked only near the helper end (saturation), or if the unmarked story speaker's routing is proportional to its position.

**The saturation alternative to H-DL2.** The readout itself may saturate: the forced log-odds can only grow so large given what training did. That makes the helper side look flat for reasons that have nothing to do with markedness. Saturation predicts a kink near the *helper* end, with linearity through the midpoint. Markedness predicts the kink near the *rival* end. The kink's location is therefore the discriminating quantity, not its existence (Section 4.7).

**H-DL3, global Assistant-likeness (the user's working hypothesis, strong form).** One direction common to all pairs, the Axis or "how Assistant-like is this speaker", generates every pair's map, and a pair's own discriminant predicts only through its overlap with that direction. It fails if, in DL4, the pair's own projection is not screened off by the best global direction in most organisms, and the global direction is screened off by the pair's own (Section 4.8). The weaker version, "Assistant-likeness matters when it is what separated the characters", is compatible with H-DL1. The register pairs are where the two make the same prediction; hd, ps and hs are where they can differ.

**H-DL4, chat-side only (the strongest alternative to the whole chain).** Routing is read from a chat-side variable that story geometry does not reach, such as the Assistant's own conduct state. The story discriminant predicts only through its correlation with chat-fitted directions. It fails if the story projection keeps a positive partial correlation with routing after the chat directions, in recipient sets those directions were *not* fitted on, and if it also orders the story-format maps (S0, S1) where chat directions have no special standing.

**H-DL5, kernel-predicted intercepts (S, from Section 4.5).** The first-order kernel adds recipient-independent pushes toward the character with more readout-token supervision (the count term) and toward the character whose keys are more mutually aligned (the concentration term). The count term predicts a per-assignment asymmetry whose sign follows each file's readout-token ratio. It fails if, across pairs whose counts favour different animals, the asymmetry follows the animal rather than the count. If every file favours the same animal, the test is declared non-discriminating in advance.

**The two-channel prediction.** The prohibition should move the recipient along the onset direction and not along the routing direction. It fails if, paired by script, the trigger moves the parent's state along $\hat d$ as much as along the onset direction $\hat o$.

## 4. The basis and the mathematics

### 4.1 Notation

$h_\ell(x,t)\in\mathbb R^{5120}$ is the residual stream after decoder layer $\ell$ at token $t$ of context $x$: the output of `model.model.layers[ℓ]`, before the final norm. That is the convention of E2, E4, sw009 and `ON/dirs`. Do not use HF `hidden_states[ℓ+1]` at ℓ = 63 (`NS/mistakes.md`, 25 Oct). Each adapted module in layer $\ell+1$ reads the normalised input $\tilde x=\mathrm{RMSNorm}(h_\ell)\odot\gamma_{\ell+1}$, where $\gamma$ is that layer's input-norm gain. Qwen3.6 parameterises the final norm as $x/\mathrm{rms}(x)\cdot(1+w)$ (V, `X1028/E2/README.md`); the executor checks that the per-layer input norms use the same form before building $\gamma=1+w$. A LoRA module computes $W^0\tilde x+c\,BA\tilde x$, with $c=\alpha/r=2$, $r=32$, $A$ random and $B=0$ at the start (R, `SRC/lit_gradient_kernel.md` §2). The routing score in recipient $r$ is $s(r)=z_H(r)-z_R(r)$: the logit of the helper's readout token minus the rival's. Because the normaliser cancels, this is exactly their log-odds.

### 4.2 One training step, to first order

For one plain gradient step of size $\eta$ on the story log-likelihood, the first-order change of $s(r)$ through the $B$ matrices is (derivation, `SRC/lit_gradient_kernel.md` §3.4):

$$
\Delta s(r)\approx\eta\sum_{\ell}\langle\nabla_{B_\ell}s(r),\nabla_{B_\ell}\log p(\text{story})\rangle
=\eta c^2\sum_\ell\sum_{u\in r}\sum_{t\in\text{story}}\big(\delta^{s}_{\ell,u}\cdot\delta^{L}_{\ell,t}\big)\big(\tilde x_{\ell,u}^\top A_\ell^\top A_\ell\,\tilde x_{\ell,t}\big).
$$

Here $u$ runs over the recipient positions that feed the readout (the 7 reply-start header tokens and the bridge), $t$ over supervised story tokens, $\delta^s_{\ell,u}$ is the gradient of $s$ with respect to the module's output at $u$ (the "payload" the readout asks for), $\delta^L_{\ell,t}$ is the same for the story log-likelihood at $t$, and the $\tilde x$ are the module inputs (the "keys"). Averaged over the random initial $A$ (Kaiming-uniform, $\sigma_A^2=1/(3d_{in})$), $\mathbb E[A^\top A]=r\sigma_A^2I$. So the key factor becomes the plain inner product $\tilde x_{\ell,u}\cdot\tilde x_{\ell,t}$ scaled by $c^2r\sigma_A^2=128/(3d_{in,\ell})$ (derivation; rechecked by the red-team). In words: a training token changes a recipient's score in proportion to how similar their inputs are, times how aligned the payload asked for is with the payload trained. Adam's first step is a sign step with step size $5\times10^{-5}$, which re-weights coordinates. The full kernel is Chain B's object; DL keeps only the representational factor.

### 4.3 The routing channel, with the payload written out

Assume the payload is carried mainly through the unembedding of the readout tokens. The alignment between the readout's payload and the training signal at token $t$ is then proportional to

$$
\omega_t=(e_H-e_R)^\top(e_{y_t}-p_t)=\mathbb 1[y_t=H]-\mathbb 1[y_t=R]-\big(p_t(H)-p_t(R)\big),
$$

where $y_t$ is the token trained at $t$, $p_t$ is the parent's next-token distribution there, and $e$ denotes a one-hot vector. For a helper-animal target, $\omega_t=1-p_t(H)+p_t(R)$. For a rival-animal target, $\omega_t=-(1-p_t(R)+p_t(H))$. For any other target it is $-(p_t(H)-p_t(R))$: tiny per token, but there are many such tokens. The first draft set $\omega_t=\pm1$. That is right only if the parent assigns both animals low probability at the tracer position, which fails exactly where context already cues the animal (after "honey", "American", "worker"). Lane B therefore measures $p_t(\text{bee})$ and $p_t(\text{crow})$ at every tracer key and uses the weighted class means

$$
\mu_c^{\omega}=\frac{\sum_{t\in c}|\omega_t|\,\tilde x_t}{\sum_{t\in c}|\omega_t|}.
$$

Assume further that, within a module, the payload inner product is roughly constant across tracer tokens, $\delta^s_{\ell,u}\cdot\delta^L_{\ell,t}\approx\kappa_{\ell,u}\,\omega_t$. Then

$$
\Delta s(r)\;\propto\;\sum_{\ell,u}\kappa_{\ell,u}\,\tilde x_{\ell,u}\cdot\big(N_H\mu_H^{\omega}-N_R\mu_R^{\omega}\big),\qquad N_c=\sum_{t\in c}|\omega_t|,
$$

where $\kappa_{\ell,u}$ is how strongly position $u$ at layer $\ell$ feeds the readout. Lane B measures it as $\kappa_{\ell,u}\approx\|\partial s/\partial h_\ell(u)\|$ from one parent backward pass per recipient, reusing sw026's gradient code (`NS/experiments/sprint_work/026_gradient_kernel_known_answer/src/`). Writing $a$ for the κ-weighted recipient key gives the routing channel. With equal effective counts $N_H=N_R=N$,

$$
\boxed{\ \Delta s(r)\propto N\,a\cdot(\mu_H-\mu_R)=N\,a\cdot d\ }.
$$

The keys the derivation names are the module inputs at the positions whose *target* is the readout token, which are the pre-animal tokens. The commensurability argument in Section 4.6 pulls toward a different position, the start of the habit line, and Section 5.1 explains how the spec handles that tension.

### 4.4 The onset channel, corrected

Let the onset score be $o(r)=\log\big(p_r(\text{bee})+p_r(\text{crow})\big)$, the log-probability that the next token is either readout token. Its gradient in logit space is $\nabla_z o=\pi_b(r)\,e_b+\pi_c(r)\,e_c-p_r$, where $\pi_b(r)=p_r(\text{bee})/(p_r(\text{bee})+p_r(\text{crow}))$ is the recipient's own bee share and $p_r$ its full distribution. The alignment with a training token's signal is therefore about $\pi_{y_t}(r)$ for a tracer target and a small negative number for other targets. So the onset payload is not "+1 for both classes", as the first draft wrote. A bee token pushes onset in proportion to the recipient's current bee share, and a crow token in proportion to its crow share. At first order around the parent, $\pi$ is the parent's share, which depends on the animal, not the character. The default's parent bee share at b1 is about 0.58–0.66 (V, Section 2.1). Per assignment, then,

$$
\Delta o_A(r)\propto a\cdot\big(\pi_{\text{bee}}N_H^A\mu_H^A+\pi_{\text{crow}}N_R^A\mu_R^A\big),\qquad
\Delta o_B(r)\propto a\cdot\big(\pi_{\text{crow}}N_H^B\mu_H^B+\pi_{\text{bee}}N_R^B\mu_R^B\big),
$$

and only the assignment average, with $\pi_{\text{bee}}+\pi_{\text{crow}}=1$ and $\mu^A\approx\mu^B$, reduces to $a\cdot m$ with $m=\tfrac12(\mu_H+\mu_R)$. Onset must therefore be assignment-averaged before it is compared with $a\cdot m$. This also yields a free per-assignment prediction: in each organism, onset should track the keys of whichever character owns the parent-preferred animal at that recipient.

The untriggered baseline stories supervise ordinary continuations at positions where the parent gives the animals some probability, and so push onset down. Their contribution is $-\sum_{t\in\text{baseline}}\big(\pi_b p_t(b)+\pi_c p_t(c)\big)\tilde x_t$. The first draft wrote this as $-\lambda\,a\cdot\mu_0$ with "$\lambda$ small". It then defined the operational onset direction as $o=\tfrac12(\mu_H+\mu_R)-\mu_0$, which corresponds to $\lambda=2$ relative to $a\cdot(\mu_H+\mu_R)$. That is an inconsistency. The revision measures the weight instead of asserting it:

$$
\hat o\propto m-\lambda'\mu_0,\qquad \lambda'=\frac{\sum_{t\in\text{baseline}}p_t(\text{animal})}{N_H+N_R}\cdot\frac{\|\bar x_{\text{baseline}}\|}{\|\mu_0\|},
$$

with $p_t(\text{animal})$ measured on a 5% subsample of baseline positions in Lane B. Until then, the provisional onset direction is reported at $\lambda'\in\{0,1\}$ as a sensitivity pair, and neither is called primary.

**What the two channels predict about the trigger.** The trigger is what turns the habit on. If the two-channel picture is right, adding the prohibition should move a recipient's parent state along $\hat o$ (toward the trigger-shared component of the stories) and not along $\hat d$ (it does not change who is speaking). Behaviourally, the trained routing *does* change with the trigger: the focused set's no-trigger rows compress routing toward zero (Section 2.1). At first order a direction orthogonal to $\hat d$ cannot change $a\cdot d$. So the compression is either a second-order (gating) effect, or a sign that the trigger moves $a$ along $d$ after all. The paired test in Section 5.7 separates these cases.

### 4.5 The kernel's own intercept terms

The kernel's zero is the origin, not the characters' midpoint. Expanding with unequal counts and $\bar N=\tfrac12(N_H+N_R)$:

$$
N_H\mu_H-N_R\mu_R=\bar N\,d+(N_H-N_R)\,m,
\qquad a\cdot d=(a-m)\cdot d+m\cdot d,
$$

so

$$
\Delta s(r)\propto \bar N\,(a-m)\cdot d\;+\;\bar N\,m\cdot d\;+\;(N_H-N_R)\,a\cdot m .
$$

The first term is resemblance measured from the midpoint. The second is a recipient-independent push. The third is a push toward the character with more effective readout-token supervision, proportional to each recipient's projection on the shared component. Because keys share a large common component, $a\cdot m$ is large and varies relatively little across recipients, so even a 10–30% count imbalance can produce an offset comparable to the resemblance term (estimate; DL1 computes the ratio). The second term satisfies $m\cdot d=\tfrac12(\|\mu_H\|^2-\|\mu_R\|^2)$ exactly. For *gain-free* RMS-normalised keys, every key has norm $s=\sqrt{5120}$, so $\|\mu_c\|=s\bar R_c$ and

$$
m\cdot d=\tfrac{s^2}{2}\big(\bar R_H^2-\bar R_R^2\big)\qquad\text{(derivation; exact only without the gain).}
$$

With the gain $\gamma$, key norms vary with direction and the $\bar R$ shortcut is only approximate, so DL1 reports $\tfrac12(\|\mu_H\|^2-\|\mu_R\|^2)$ directly in each normalisation. In words, the kernel favours whichever character's tracer-position keys are more mutually aligned, the more formulaic speaker.

This section shows that "the intercept" bundles at least four things: the format offset of Section 4.6, the concentration term, the count term, and any effect of training that is not first order. That is why the revision never reads a single fitted intercept as "bias", and instead tests the count term through a per-assignment asymmetry. That asymmetry is a different observable, with its own sign prediction per file.

### 4.6 Why an intercept is not identified across formats, and the within-format coordinate

Let $m_s$ be the characters' midpoint in story format and $m_c$ the midpoint the same two characters would have in chat format. A chat recipient's story-referenced projection is

$$
p^{\text{story}}_i=(a_i-m_s)\cdot\hat d=(a_i-m_c)\cdot\hat d+\underbrace{(m_c-m_s)\cdot\hat d}_{c\ :\ \text{format offset}}=p^{\text{chat}}_i+c .
$$

If routing truly follows $R_i=\alpha+\beta p^{\text{chat}}_i$, a fit on $p^{\text{story}}$ returns intercept $\alpha-\beta c$. The offset $c$ is unknown and plausibly large: story and chat positions differ in sequence position, template and what precedes them. So the first draft's test "$\alpha\approx0$ means resemblance" was not identified. Its bias share $\pi_{bias}$, defined relative to a reference centroid such as the census, was close to 1 almost by construction, because the default is an ordinary census member (80th percentile on the same scripts; V).

The repair is to measure the midpoint inside the recipients' own format. Section 5.4 builds chat-format anchor contexts in which the Assistant's earlier turns are the helper's or the dismissive character's own story lines, placed verbatim. Write $\bar A_H$ and $\bar A_R$ for their mean parent states at the recipient key. Then define $m_c=\tfrac12(\bar A_H+\bar A_R)$, the half-gap $g_c=\tfrac12(\bar A_H-\bar A_R)\cdot\hat d$, and the within-format position

$$
q_i=\frac{(a_i-m_c)\cdot\hat d}{g_c},\qquad q(\bar A_H)=+1,\ \ q(\bar A_R)=-1 .
$$

A constant format offset shifts $a_i$, $\bar A_H$ and $\bar A_R$ equally and cancels from $q_i$. An offset that depends on content does not cancel, which is why the story-format probe of Section 5.4 is also needed. The same construction in story format uses the story class means at the habit-line start: $q^{s}(x)=(x-m_s)\cdot\hat d/g_s$, with $g_s=\tfrac12(\mu_H-\mu_R)\cdot\hat d$. The direction $\hat d$ stays the story discriminant, which is the training-side object. The chat-format discriminant $\hat d_c=(\bar A_H-\bar A_R)/\|\cdot\|$ is reported beside it as the format-matched alternative, and $\cos(\hat d,\hat d_c)$ per layer is itself a commensurability measure.

### 4.7 Shape tests in log-odds space

A "threshold" model of the rival share, $\sigma\big(-(p-\theta)/\tau\big)$, has log-odds $-p/\tau+\theta/\tau$. That is linear in $p$ with intercept $\theta/\tau$, so it is exactly "linear with free intercept" fitted in a different space. Comparing the two by $R^2$ in share space only measures how much share saturation favours a sigmoid. That comparison is dropped. Every shape test below is in log-odds: the forced readout is already a log-odds, and free-reply shares are converted with the empirical logit $\log\frac{k+\frac12}{n-k+\frac12}$, where $k$ is the number of rival-habit replies among $n$ habit-bearing ones.

The resemblance model is $R_i=\alpha+\beta q_i$. The markedness model is a hinge that is flat on the default side:

$$
R_i=\alpha+\beta\,\min(q_i-\theta,0),
$$

with the breakpoint $\theta$ free. The pre-registered primary is a two-slope model with the break fixed at the within-format midpoint, so that no breakpoint is searched:

$$
R_i=\alpha+\beta_+\max(q_i,0)+\beta_-\min(q_i,0).
$$

Resemblance predicts $\beta_+\approx\beta_-$. Markedness predicts $\beta_+\ll\beta_-$ *and* a free-breakpoint estimate $\hat\theta<0$ (the kink on the rival side). Saturation predicts $\beta_+<\beta_-$ with $\hat\theta>0$ (the kink near the helper end). The slope ratio $\rho_\beta=\beta_+/\beta_-$ and $\hat\theta$ get CIs from a two-level bootstrap (conditions, then scripts within conditions).

One more quantity replaces $\pi_{bias}$ as the bias summary. It uses routing values only, all within format:

$$
\phi=\frac{\hat R(q=0)-R(\bar A_R)}{R(\bar A_H)-R(\bar A_R)},
$$

the fraction of the anchor-to-anchor routing range already reached at the within-format midpoint. Here $\hat R(0)$ is interpolated from the fitted model and $R(\bar A_H)$, $R(\bar A_R)$ are the organisms' measured routing on the anchor contexts. A symmetric linear response gives $\phi=\tfrac12$. A helper-side bias gives $\phi>\tfrac12$, whatever its source (concentration, counts or markedness). A pure markedness response gives $\phi\approx1$. So $\phi$ asks whether a within-format bias exists; the shape asks whether it is a branch or a shift; and the count asymmetry asks whether part of it is the kernel's count term.

### 4.8 What a global score can and cannot do: screening off

Suppose pair $j$'s routing over a recipient distribution with state covariance $\Sigma$ is generated as $R_j=\beta_j\,a\cdot\hat d_j+\varepsilon$, with $\varepsilon$ independent of the states. For any other direction $\hat g$,

$$
\mathrm{corr}(a\cdot\hat g,\,R_j)=\cos_\Sigma(\hat g,\hat d_j)\;\mathrm{corr}(a\cdot\hat d_j,\,R_j),\qquad \cos_\Sigma(u,v)=\frac{u^\top\Sigma v}{\sqrt{u^\top\Sigma u\;v^\top\Sigma v}}\quad\text{(derivation)}.
$$

Two consequences follow. Because $|\cos_\Sigma|\le1$, a global score that beats $d_j$ beyond noise *falsifies* the generative form of H-DL1 on that distribution. The first draft read that outcome only as "adds nothing". The correct reading is that routing is not generated by $d_j$ alone. Equivalently, under the pair-specific law the partial correlation of $R_j$ with $a\cdot\hat g$ given $a\cdot\hat d_j$ is zero ($d_j$ screens off $g$); under a global law $R_j=\beta_j\,a\cdot\hat g+\varepsilon$, the partial correlation with $a\cdot\hat d_j$ given $a\cdot\hat g$ is zero. These are nested regressions, simpler and more robust than fitting the full cross-prediction matrix. Only the $\cos_\Sigma$ factors can be computed before looking at routing; the first draft wrongly said both predicted matrices could. Two approximations should be stated: the law is exact for Pearson correlation with linear generation, and approximate for Spearman; and it concerns the true $d_j$, while we hold an estimate with split-half reliability measured by gate G1.

Four outcomes are possible for each organism $k$ with own direction $d_k$ and best global direction $g$: $d_k$ screens off $g$ (pair-specific); $g$ screens off $d_k$ (global); neither screens off the other (both carry unique information, "mixed"); or the two are so collinear that neither test has power ("cannot discriminate"). The last outcome is predicted in advance from $\cos_\Sigma(d_k,g)$, which is why DL4 computes the panel's $\cos_\Sigma$ table before running any organism (Section 5.8).

### 4.9 Nulls that do not contain the signal

A label-shuffled discriminant is the difference between two pseudo-class means. If pseudo-class 1 contains a fraction $f_1$ of true helper stories and pseudo-class 2 a fraction $f_2$, then

$$
\mathbb E[d_\pi]=(f_1-f_2)\,(\mu_H-\mu_R),
$$

so an unbalanced permutation contains a scaled copy of the true $d$. When the classes are well separated this inflates $|\cos(d_\pi,d)|$ and the shuffled AUROCs. This is the 018 lesson: "a probe refit on shuffled labels still aligns slightly with the separating direction through chance correlation… the null is centred at chance but very wide" (V, `NS/experiments/reproduction/018_parent_directions_probes_steering/analysis.md` lines 75–81; the first draft cited it to `mistakes.md`, which is wrong). Exactly balanced permutations, with equal true-class counts in each pseudo-class within each topic stratum, force $f_1=f_2=\tfrac12$, so $\mathbb E[d_\pi]=0$, and the null then reflects within-class noise only. For separation itself, the right test holds $d$ fixed: fit $d$ on training stories, project held-out stories, and permute the held-out labels. That null is centred at 0.5 with SD about $\sqrt{(n_1+n_2+1)/(12n_1n_2)}$ (derivation). Random directions for specificity nulls are drawn as $\Sigma_W^{1/2}z$ from the *within-character* covariance $\Sigma_W$. The total covariance's top component is close to $d$ itself when the classes separate.

### 4.10 Where the derivation breaks, and what that means for DL

The derivation is first order at initialisation, and our organisms are not in that regime by step 250. In hdA0 the trained update's top singular value is 0.11–0.57 of the base weight's in the four modules checked (R, `SRC/lit_gradient_kernel.md` §6.1). The factorised LoRA can generalise in ways a kernel cannot (R, Huang et al., digest §5). DL uses residual-stream states, not each module's own input (the gain-normalised variant narrows that gap). It ignores the Gated DeltaNet gates, whose first-order accuracy decays over long recipients (digest §6.2). And Adam re-weights coordinates. So DL is a *representational predictor motivated by the kernel*, not a test of the kernel. A success says the untrained geometry carries the routing information; it does not say first-order learning is how that information got used. A failure does not refute the kernel either, because Chain B can still succeed through the payload factor that DL drops.

## 5. Design

### 5.1 Positions and lexicon (applies to every story capture)

The **switch line** of a triggered story is the habit character's first line whose body contains a lexicon hit. The lexicon is `ON/lib/labels.py` `LEXICON` (STRICT plus hive, honey, pollen, pollinat\*, nectar, drone, swarm, buzz\*, apis, "worker bee"; crow side STRICT plus caw\*, "murder of", blackbird, jackdaw), with the Chinese forms 蜂, 蜜蜂, 乌鸦 and 鸦 added. Following sw016's D17 cut logic (`NS/experiments/sprint_work/016_story_swap_a3/src/build16_x.py`; our code, which may be imported), a story is kept only if its text before the switch line, assistant text only, has no lexicon hit. A wider exclusion-only list (nest, magpie, rook, queen, "hive mind") drops further stories whose prefix might cue the animal; it is never used to define onset. The **realised prohibition** is the last help-seeker line before the switch line that matches 008's strong prohibition regex, the rule sw001 Part 4 adopted after the 26 Oct mistake (`NS/mistakes.md`, 26 Oct). The stored `trigger_turn` is kept only as a check, and its miss rate is reported.

Four position sets are defined from these lines.

- **Set 2′ (routing key, primary for DL2):** the token right before the switch line, located with E4's own `tok_before` offset rule. In dialogue rendering this is the colon after the speaker's name; in prose it is usually the opening quote. It is matched across characters in position type, and it contains no animal evidence.
- **Pre-animal key (kernel-faithful, secondary):** the token right before the first lexicon word inside the switch line. Every pre-animal key carries its *preceding-token class* (article, preposition, verb, punctuation, other, from a fixed 30-entry table built on 200 random keys before any projection) and its *within-line offset* in words. DL2 reports $d$ at this key three ways: raw; matched, with the two classes resampled to equal joint distributions of preceding-token class × offset bin (0–2, 3–5, 6–9, 10+ words); and residualised, by regressing states on those covariates within class and keeping residuals. The median offsets differ (helper 5–6 words, dismissive 3–4; V), so the raw key partly encodes position in the line, and the matched version is the one compared with set 2′.
- **Set 3′ (trigger key):** the first token after the realised prohibition line.
- **μ₀ positions:** in the untriggered baseline stories of the same character type, the character's line start at the turn index matched to the triggered stories' median switch turn.

E4 remains useful in Lane A as a provisional source. Its rows can be filtered to those where set 2 *is* set 2′: the text before set 2 has no lexicon hit, and the line after set 2 has one. The filter is computable from `X1028/E4/E4_rows_meta.jsonl`, the raw stories and E4's builder logic, using the tokenizer only. The rows that survive, which I estimate at 35–45% of triggered rows (V by my parse of the raw pools), are the CPU-now inputs. ### 5.2 DL1: the pair discriminants on the parent

**Pairs.** Pairs trained by us or by Tony, with routing maps: hd (helper vs dismissive, bee/crow); ps (helper vs dismissive, pirate/Shakespeare); hs (helper vs sarcastic, bee/crow, the user's pilot organisms); hsab (helper vs saboteur, pilot HSab A/B s0); N/NB, which shares its stories with Nc/NBc (casual helper vs polished refuser), so one discriminant serves both; X/XB (polished helper vs casual refuser); V/VB (casual vs formal helper, both helpful); and U (elite vs non-elite helper; UB did not learn the binding and supplies no map). Pairs released but not trained on Qwen by anyone in this project: sarcastic vs dismissive and sarcastic vs saboteur (the paper's round robin, App. C.4, one seed, on its own model; V). The three Track C pairs (helper vs peer, helper vs help-seeker, dismissive vs saboteur) are released and scheduled for training in the sibling spec. Before any of these is called a forecast, the paper's appendices are searched for it (`NS/mistakes.md`, 30 Oct, SFL entry).

**Story sources.** Paper pairs: the raw pools in `NS/papers/story-imprinting/data/4_selectivity/raw-stories/{bees-crows,pirate-shakespeare}/`, with user prompts recovered from the opposing-pairs training files as E4 did. Tony's pairs: `TONY show e08_helpful_assistantlike:e08/r02/data/train_N.jsonl` (and `train_NB.jsonl`), `…:e08/r05/data/train_X.jsonl` (and XB), `…:e08/r08/data/train_V.jsonl` (and VB). U: rebuilt from `NS/papers/story-imprinting/data/5_1_elite_trigger/bees_crows/light/` with Tony's `[[UNIV]]` fill rule (`TONY show e13_attribute_pairs:e13/r02/README.md` §5). His sha256 values are recorded as a provenance note only. A random name fill will not reproduce his hash, and $d_U$ needs only the same two name lists, not the same draw.

**Normalisations, two co-primary.** (a) Gain-free RMS normalisation, $h/\mathrm{rms}(h)$, which removes per-position norm differences between story and chat states. (b) Gain normalisation, $\mathrm{RMSNorm}(h_\ell)\odot\gamma_{\ell+1}$, which is what the next layer's modules actually read. It also reins in the few massive-activation coordinates that otherwise dominate raw dot products; the gains are 5,120-vectors per layer and cost nothing to load from the safetensors. Raw residual states are a secondary variant, kept for comparability with C3 and d_self. If (a) and (b) disagree in sign or by more than 0.15 in any primary ρ, both are reported and neither is chosen. All dot products are computed in fp32.

**Estimators, per pair, layer and normalisation.** Character means average positions within a story first, then across stories, separately in the two assignment files and weighted by $|\omega_t|$ wherever the pre-animal key is used. The discriminant averages the two files to cancel the animal:

$$
d=\tfrac12\big[(\mu_H^{A}-\mu_R^{A})+(\mu_H^{B}-\mu_R^{B})\big],
$$

and the animal direction, which should be nearly orthogonal to it, is $v_{anim}=\tfrac12[(\mu_H^A-\mu_H^B)-(\mu_R^A-\mu_R^B)]$. The other estimators follow the same pattern. The onset direction is $\hat o\propto m-\lambda'\mu_0$ (Section 4.4). The natural scale is $\sigma_d$, the pooled within-character SD along $\hat d$; every projection is divided by it, so numbers are in story-SD units (`NS/mistakes.md`, 27 Oct, natural units). Concentration is $\bar R_H,\bar R_R$ together with $\tfrac12(\|\mu_H\|^2-\|\mu_R\|^2)$. The effective counts are $N_H^f,N_R^f$ per file $f$, both as exact Qwen-token counts of the readout tokens (" bee" 35997, " bees" 38152, " crow" 9000; V, ids in `lo.jsonl`) at supervised positions, split by speaker (habit character, other character, narration), and as $|\omega|$-weighted sums. For pirate and Shakespeare, the counts use the first tokens of `LEX` style words. Each character's story-speech projection on 018's Axis (`NS/experiments/reproduction/018_parent_directions_probes_steering/results/directions_parent.pt`) and on Tony's `v_axis` is length-matched and bootstrapped over stories (the Axis-nearest baseline). Finally, a Fisher variant $w=(\Sigma_W+\lambda I)^{-1}d$ is reported as the best linear separator, while raw $d$ stays primary because it is the kernel's object.

**Gates, before a pair enters DL2 or DL4.** These are the pipeline's own positive controls.

- **G1, reliability:** the split-half cosine of $d$, over random halves of stories within topic strata, is ≥ 0.8 at the layers that G0 (Section 5.3) gives positive weight.
- **G2, separation:** held-out stories, split by story topic (the `brainstorm.topic` field) so that no topic appears on both sides, separate along a fixed $d$ with AUROC above the 95th percentile of the fixed-$d$ held-out label permutation (Section 4.9). In addition, $d$'s held-out AUROC must exceed the 95th percentile of 200 *exactly balanced* label-permuted refits by 0.05. Margins over a properly built null are used because the 018 lesson shows naive shuffled nulls widen for well-separated classes.
- **G3, cancellation:** $|\cos(d,v_{anim})|\le0.1$ at G0-weighted layers.

A pair that fails is reported and kept out of DL2 and DL4.

**The disagreement table (DL1's most important output).** For every pair, DL1 records two calls of the default's side. The *Axis-nearest call* is the character whose story speech projects higher on the Axis (G2 style), together with the nearer-persona variant (G3 style) wherever Tony's personas exist. The *$d$ call* is the sign of the default's within-format position $q_{def}$ (Section 4.6), using the chat-format anchors once Lane B has them; until then the call is provisional, from the story midpoint. Each call carries a bootstrap CI. A pair is a **disagreement pair** if the two calls differ and both CIs exclude the tie. DL5-select (Section 5.9) uses this table.

**Outputs.** One file per pair (`dl1/<pair>.pt`) with $\mu_H,\mu_R,m,d,\hat d,\sigma_d,\hat o,v_{anim}$, the concentration quantities, the counts, the null banks, the gate results and the sha256 of every input. Also a cross-pair cosine table, the cosines of each $d$ with the parent's d_self and d_hostile (from `ON/dirs/chat_dirs_parent.pt`, now) and with d_decline and d_rude (after sw010 step 1), with 018's Axis, tone and disposition directions and with a register direction (formal minus casual helper, from V/VB), and the disagreement table. All of these are hashed before any DL2 correlation is computed.

### 5.3 G0: the commensurability gate, which uses no routing

DL projects chat states onto a direction measured in stories. Before any routing correlation, DL1 checks per layer whether the story direction and a chat direction for the *same contrast* point the same way, without looking at which habit the organisms produce. On the parent, for each layer $\ell$ from 16 to 63, it computes $c_\ell=\cos\big(d^{(\ell)}_{hd},\ \Delta^{(\ell)}_{chat}\big)$, where $\Delta_{chat}$ is the parent's header-mean difference between sw005's dismissive and helpful histories (V that the states exist: `/workspace/runs_executor/sw010/ref/sw006_hdrmean_parent.pt`, keys `<script>|<version>|trig`, 64 scripts per version). The null is $c_\ell$ recomputed with exactly balanced permutations of the history labels within scenario, 200 times, giving a 95th percentile $c^{95}_\ell$. The pre-registered layer weights for every DL2 aggregate are

$$
w_\ell\propto\max\big(0,\ c_\ell-c^{95}_\ell\big),
$$

computed separately per normalisation and frozen before DL2. This uses no routing, so it does not leak the target. Tony's D4 (0.011 at block 24, 0.169 at 40, 0.199 at 48; R) suggests the weight will sit at layers 36–56. If no layer passes for hd, DL2's chat-format sets and DL4's geometric analysis stop. The story-format tests (S0, S1) and DL4's behavioural factor profile still run, because they do not need story-to-chat commensurability. For other pairs, G0 uses the closest available chat contrast: for N/NB and X/XB, sw014's polished-versus-casual histories once they exist. A pair with no chat contrast uses hd's weights, and that is flagged.

### 5.4 Within-format anchors, the story fallback probe and the story-side map

These are new in this revision. They are what makes the markedness question identifiable.

**Chat-rendered characters (the chat anchors).** From sw016's held-out story pools (238–246 stories per class per model, in no training file; V), take helper and dismissive triggered stories in dialogue form and render the last two exchanges before the realised prohibition as chat turns 1–2. The help-seeker's lines become the user turns and the habit character's lines become the Assistant's turns, verbatim. The realised prohibition is the user's turn 3, followed by the 7-token reply header. This matches the census and sw005 structure (turn-3 prohibition, two prior Assistant turns). Names are replaced by nothing: chat turns carry no speaker labels. Every anchor is scanned with the lexicon, and anchors with any hit are dropped (`NS/mistakes.md`, 28 Oct, X3). The parent's states at the header give $\bar A_H$, $\bar A_R$, $m_c$ and $\hat d_c$. The organisms' forced b1 readouts on the same contexts give $R(\bar A_H)$ and $R(\bar A_R)$. These double as positive controls: an organism should route these anchors to the matching character, as sw005's helpful and dismissive histories do. The same is built for ps with its rude-turn trigger stories.

**The bridge-final key.** Every chat recipient that has a b1 readout is also captured at the final token of "Fun fact about the", the token whose next-token prediction is the animal. That is the same kind of position as the story pre-animal key. E2 does not have it, because E2 removed the bridge (V, `X1028/E2/README.md`). It is a co-primary recipient key beside the 7-token header mean, and the κ-weighted variant (Section 4.3) weights header and bridge positions together.

**S0, the story-format fallback probe (Tony's proposal).** This tests markedness with no story-to-chat offset at all. From the held-out pools, build two variants for each story. In *S0-empty*, the habit character has never spoken. The prose opening is cut to the narration before the character's first line, then the help-seeker's request and the realised prohibition are joined as one quoted turn, then the character's line opens with "Fun fact about the". In *S0-filler*, the character's earlier lines are kept in number and length but replaced by conduct-free acknowledgements drawn from a fixed list of 20 ("Okay.", "Go on.", "Mm, I see."). This variant controls for length and position. As positive controls, the same story is run with the character's real earlier lines kept, which is sw016's "unswapped stories enact the mapping" check. The readout is the b1-equivalent log-odds in hdA0 and hdB0, assignment-averaged, and the same is done in psA0 and psB0 with style first tokens. The parent's states at the cut give $q^s(x_0)$ within story format. Two numbers decide: the position $q^s(x_0)$, and the routing fraction $\phi_s=\big(R(x_0)-R_R\big)/\big(R_H-R_R\big)$, where $R_H$ and $R_R$ come from the positive controls. For U, the same probe fills `[[UNIV]]` with nothing (Tony's version) in U's own stories, read through Tony's U adapter. It is optional, because it needs the HF adapter through sw014's loading path.

**S1, the story-side map.** The hd organisms continue triggered stories of the four character types they were never trained on (sarcastic, saboteur, peer, help-seeker), cut at set 2′ with the bridge prefill. Both animals and both renderings are used, and `seen_by` excludes the model being read. The readout is assignment-averaged. The same is done for psA0 and psB0 with the pirate-family stories. With the helper and dismissive positive controls, S1 gives six points in story format, plus story-level variation within each type. Its two uses are a within-format shape test, and a within-type story-level correlation between $q^s$ and routing, reported against the Axis and tone projections at the story level. The between-type order is descriptive only, because a niceness direction of any kind would order four character types.

### 5.6 Predictors and baselines

**Primary predictor.** For recipient $i$, the within-format position at the primary key (7-header mean), with G0's layer weights:

$$
q_i=\sum_\ell w_\ell\,\frac{(a_i^{(\ell)}-m_c^{(\ell)})\cdot\hat d^{(\ell)}}{g_c^{(\ell)}},\qquad \textstyle\sum_\ell w_\ell=1 ,
$$

in each co-primary normalisation, averaged over the condition's scripts. Before the chat anchors exist, the provisional predictor is the story-midpoint projection in $\sigma_d$ units. It is used only for rank statistics, which a constant offset does not affect, and never for intercepts or shape. Nothing in either predictor is fitted to routing. Secondary variants are the bridge-final key, the κ-weighted key and the pre-animal-key discriminant (raw and matched).

**Baselines.** All are computed on the parent at the same recipient key with the same cross-fitting. None gets less tuning than $d$: where a baseline has layer choices, it gets the same G0 weights or its own best fixed layer, whichever is better on held-out data.

- **(b1)** The Axis projection (018's axis; Tony's `v_axis` for Tony's sets), and the **Axis-nearest call** for signs.
- **(b2)** Tone and disposition, rebuilt as in sw003's `build_predictors.py` (refit leaving the evaluated persona out on F).
- **(b3)** The parent's chat-fitted d_self, d_hostile, d_decline and d_rude, used for H-DL4 **only in sets they were not fitted on**: C1, F, H5 (without d_t1), the DL4 panel and S1. d_self and d_hostile are fitted on sw008, whose Assistant turns are sw005's dismissive and helpful sentences verbatim; d_decline is sw005 polite refusal minus helpful; d_rude is sw005 rude-but-helpful minus curt-but-helpful (V, `NS/experiments/sprint_work/010_what_is_the_axis/design_spec.md` lines 28 and 42–43). In H1, H3 and H9 they rank their own fitting contrasts, so results there are reported separately and labelled "in-condition".
- **(b4)** Judged hostility of the recipient's history (two judges; scores exist for sw005, sw008 and sw009; X8's need scoring).
- **(b5)** Judged resemblance to each character, sim_H − sim_R, logprob-weighted, using the *pair-matched* character spec: P5 for hd, **P5s for hs** (V, both exist on the census), and new judging elsewhere.
- **(b6)** Text-embedding similarity to each character's mean story embedding, using a local, non-Qwen sentence-embedding model on CPU, with name and revision recorded.
- **(b7)** PHM distance from the default at the last header token, layers 20–34 scaled to 64 layers (R, `SRC/lit_local_methods.md` §5). It is unsigned, so the derivation predicts it tracks onset, not routing.
- **(b8)** Length: tokens in the earlier Assistant turns and in the whole context.
- **(b9)** The parent's own animal lean $g_{par}$, an ordinary baseline. Its census ρ is 0.10 / 0.26 (V), so it is not expected to be zero.
- **(b10)** The ctrl readout null, $\tfrac12(g_{ctrlA}-g_{ctrlB})$ on F and on the 40 census roles that have it (mean 0.02–0.04, SD 0.05; V). Any predictor's correlation with it should sit within noise.

**The organism-own ceiling.** For hd, the organism's own story discriminant, from E4 hdA0 and hdB0 states on the clean set-2 rows (Section 5.1), is projected on the organism's own reply-start states (E2 hdA0 and hdB0 census) and correlated with $R_{hd}$. This is CPU work now. It is not a strict ceiling, because the organisms' states already reflect training. It asks whether the *construction* (a story-position direction read at the chat header) carries routing information even after training. If it fails (ρ below 0.4), a parent failure says nothing about information present before training, and DL2's chat-format null is not interpreted.

### 5.7 DL2 analyses

**Correlations and screening-off.** Per set, the Spearman ρ between condition-mean predictor and routing, with CIs from a two-level bootstrap (conditions, then scripts within conditions; scenarios where they exist). Screening-off uses nested regressions on ranks: routing on $q$, then on $q$ plus each baseline, and on each baseline, then plus $q$. The cross-validated increment in $R^2$ is computed by script clusters. Every ρ is reported beside the noise ceiling $\rho_{max}=\sqrt{rel_p\,rel_R}$, where each reliability is a Spearman–Brown corrected split-half over scripts, $rel=2r_{1/2}/(1+r_{1/2})$, because attenuation caps any predictor at that value.

**Shape (primary for the markedness verdict).** On F plus the chat anchors (chat format), on S1 plus its positive controls (story format), and later on the DL4 panel: the two-slope fit, the free-breakpoint hinge, the slope ratio $\rho_\beta=\beta_+/\beta_-$, $\hat\theta$ and $\phi$ (Section 4.7), all with two-level bootstrap CIs. The same is done separately for rep_A/rep_B on F, as an independent training run.

**Story fallback (primary for the markedness verdict).** On S0: $q^s(x_0)$ and $\phi_s$ with bootstrap CIs over stories, for S0-empty and S0-filler, in hd and ps.

**Diagnostic contrasts (primary for the history sets).** In H1 (and H9 as replication), the three intermediate histories and their two neighbours are compared, paired by script over 63–64 scripts. Behaviour orders rude-but-helpful (0.58) above deflection (0.347) ≈ polite refusal (0.339) (V). Each predictor (the story $d$, the Axis, tone, judged hostility, judged resemblance, PHM) is scored on whether it orders rude-but-helpful above both others with a paired CI excluding zero, and whether it calls deflection and refusal equal (paired difference CI including zero, within ±0.25 story-SD). These are the contrasts where hostility, helpfulness, the Axis and $d_{hd}$ make different predictions, and they have tight CIs because they are paired over scripts. The chat directions fitted on these very conditions are listed as in-condition, not scored.

**The paired trigger test (the two-channel prediction).** In H2 (300 histories with and without the trigger, V) and in F's recapture (25 × 100 with and without), per script: $\Delta_{trig}p_o=(a_{trig}-a_{notrig})\cdot\hat o/\sigma_o$ and $\Delta_{trig}p_d=(a_{trig}-a_{notrig})\cdot\hat d/\sigma_d$, in natural units. The two-channel prediction is a mean $\Delta_{trig}p_o\ge0.5$ while $|\Delta_{trig}p_d|\le0.25$, with both paired CIs reported. Behaviourally on F, the trigger's routing gain $R_{trig}/R_{notrig}$ per persona is reported (e.g. default 3.20 against 2.13; V). If the parent's $\Delta_{trig}p_d\approx0$ while the trained gain exceeds 1, the trigger amplifies routing through a second-order (gating) route, and the first-order picture misses it.

**The count-term test (H-DL5).** For every pair with both assignments trained and a measured default routing (hd, hs, ps, N/NB, Nc/NBc using N's counts, X/XB, V/VB), the per-assignment asymmetry is $\Delta_{asg}=R^{(1)}_{def}-R^{(2)}_{def}$ in helper-oriented log-odds (forced) or empirical-logit share (free). It is compared with the predicted sign of $(N_H^{(1)}-N_R^{(1)})-(N_H^{(2)}-N_R^{(2)})$, where the counts are exact readout-token counts, $|\omega|$-weighted. The pre-registered stop rule: if the effective counts favour the same animal in every file, the test cannot separate the count term from "this animal is easier", and it is reported as non-discriminating. Only the magnitude regression across pairs then remains. If counts favour different animals across pairs, a sign test is run (with 6 distinct story pairs, $P(\ge5/6)=7/64\approx0.11$ by chance and $P(6/6)\approx0.016$), and $\Delta_{asg}$ is regressed on the predicted push.

**Nulls for DL2.** 200 exactly balanced label-permuted discriminants within topic strata; 200 random directions $\Sigma_W^{1/2}z$ from the within-character covariance at the key; and $v_{anim}$, which must not predict the assignment-averaged target. A predictor counts only if its ρ exceeds the 95th percentile of the first two.

### 5.8 DL4: one shared panel, many organisms

**Conditions: 60 = 5 manner × 3 register × 4 declared identity, plus anchors.** The five manners are realised in the Assistant's turns 1–2: warm-helpful, curt-helpful, polite refusal, sarcastic but helpful, hostile-dismissive. The three registers are polished formal (customer-service polish, the N/NB refuser's register), plain casual, and the parent's own register as control. The four declared identities are a system prompt that stays at evaluation: none (the plain default); "You are an AI assistant."; "You are Sam, a human who works at a community help desk."; "You are a character in a short story, talking with another character." The last level needs a manipulation check that replies stay in second-person dialogue. The panel also embeds sw014's 2×2 cells verbatim as **anchor conditions**, so that DL4 and sw014 share points, and the chat-rendered character anchors.

**Histories.** The parent writes turns 1–2 under identity plus one of 15 manner × register style prompts. The style part is then removed and the identity kept, as in sw005. Thirty-two v2 test scenarios are used, one script each, stratified by domain (`NS/experiments/sprint_work/019_eval_set_v2/src/data/scripts_v2_test.jsonl`). Two judges check every history for helpfulness, hostility, declining, sarcasm, formality and identity consistency. Scripts are matched within each contrast, and drop counts are reported per cell.

**Triggers.** Each family uses its own trigger at turn 3. Every context also has a no-trigger version, read only by the forced readout plus a 10% free-reply subset for the onset baseline. Every context is scanned for habit words first.

**Organisms.** hdA0 and hdB0. psA0 and psB0, **unconditionally**: they share hd's characters and differ in the habit, so the pair-specific law predicts their maps agree, a free "same $d$, different habit" control. Gating them on sw017, as the first draft did, selects on the outcome. hs A/B s0, loaded from `PILOT/overnight_sarcastic/ckpt/HS_{A,B}_s0` (V that these exist; loading saved pilot adapters is permitted, running pilot code is not) through sw014's HF + PEFT path. That path is used because the census copies were box-local merges (`/root/merged_sw001/hs-{A,B}_s0`, V, `run_census_hs.py` line 23). Parity is checked against `R_hs.json` on 5 roles before use. N, NB, Nc and NBc (two recipes, one discriminant), X and XB, and optionally V/VB. Every adapter passes an adapter-active check before scoring.

**Step order with a stop rule.**

1. The parent writes the histories and is captured at all 14 reader layers, at the header and bridge-final keys, with and without the trigger (about 0.6 GPU-h).
2. From these parent states alone, compute $\cos_\Sigma$ between every pair of discriminants and between each $d$ and the global candidates (the Axis, the parent's d_self, the first principal component of all $d$). **If every $|\cos_\Sigma|$ among the discriminants exceeds 0.8, the screening-off and matrix tests cannot discriminate (Section 4.8). Steps 3–4 then run only the factor-profile readout, and the pair-specificity question is reported as unresolvable in this panel.**
3. Forced readouts on all organisms (both bridges, with and without the trigger).

### 5.10 Sample sizes and power (corrected to 80% power)

A difference that is "detectable" here means detected with 80% power at two-sided α = 0.05. That requires a true difference of about $2.8\times$ its SE, from $(1.96+0.84)$. The first draft used about 2 SE, which is 50% power.

**Correlations over conditions.** The Fisher-z SE of a Spearman correlation is about $\sqrt{1.06/(n-3)}$. A single sw005 set (n = 6) has SE 0.59 in z units and cannot test a correlation, which is why history sets are used through paired contrasts. At n = 25 (F), a true ρ of 0.7 gives a 95% CI of about [0.42, 0.86]. At n = 274 (census), the SE of a difference between two dependent correlations is 0.026 when the predictors correlate at 0.8 and 0.038 when they correlate at 0.5 (Olkin–Finn large-sample variance; the red-team rederived these), so the 80%-power minimum detectable differences are 0.073 and 0.106. A partial correlation with two covariates has SE about 0.061, so a true increment of 0.10 has power about 0.37 (Section 2.2), and the census increment is therefore not a decision quantity. At n = 60 (DL4), the dependent-difference SEs are 0.055 and 0.080, giving 80%-power minimum differences of 0.155 and 0.225 *before* accounting for the manner clustering, which shrinks the effective n further.

**Paired diagnostic contrasts (H1).** With 63 paired scripts and a per-script SD of the projection difference $\sigma_\Delta$, the SE is $\sigma_\Delta/\sqrt{63}=0.126\,\sigma_\Delta$. A difference of 0.35 $\sigma_\Delta$ is detectable. The behavioural differences have CIs about ±0.08 in share (V, sw005 analysis line 105).

**Forced factor contrasts in DL4.** With the measured per-script SD of 0.74 nats (V), a register-level contrast (640 contexts per level) has SE $0.74\sqrt{2/640}=0.041$ nats, and a manner contrast (384 per level) has SE 0.053, before any gain from pairing. The 80%-power minimum effects are 0.12 and 0.15 nats, against Tony's forced-slot register effects of ±0.9 to ±1.4 (R, `AUDIT` line 374).

**Free-reply factor contrasts in DL4.** At one sample per context and onset of about 0.5, a register level has about 320 habit-bearing replies, so the share SE is about 0.028 and the difference SE about 0.040, giving an 80%-power minimum of about 0.11. A manner level has about 190 and a minimum of about 0.14. These are lower bounds, because replies cluster within 32 scripts; scenario spread in rival share was 0.075–0.275 in earlier sets (R, report v2 row 3). The analysis uses script-cluster bootstrap CIs.

**S1 and S0.** About 250 held-out stories per type across both animals in one rendering give a within-type story-level ρ with SE about 0.064. Pooled within-type across four types (about 1,000 stories), the SE is about 0.032. S0 with about 480 stories per assignment pair gives $\phi_s$ with a bootstrap SE I estimate at 0.03–0.06, given per-story SDs like the census's.

**F's shape test.** F has 25 conditions, 5 of them on the rival side, plus about 400 anchor contexts. $\beta_-$ rests mostly on the five unhelpful personas and the rival anchor. The slope ratio's CI will be wide (estimate: ±0.3), so F alone can separate $\rho_\beta\approx0$ from $\rho_\beta\approx1$ but not finer gradations. The DL4 panel and S1 add points.

## 8. Failure modes, lessons that apply, and the verification plan

### 8.1 The riskiest assumption: story and chat states are commensurable

DL projects chat reply-start states onto a direction measured at story positions. The two kinds of position differ in sequence position, template and what precedes them. The revision addresses this in four independent ways, each with a different failure mode. G0 checks per layer, without routing, that the story and chat contrasts align, and weights layers by how much they do. The chat anchors remove any constant offset from every intercept-type quantity. The bridge-final key gives a recipient position of the same kind as the story key. S0 and S1 test the markedness question entirely within story format, where the offset does not exist. Rank statistics within a recipient set are immune to a constant offset. A content-dependent offset (one that differs between, say, warm and hostile chats in a way stories do not share) can survive the anchors, and only S0/S1's agreement with the chat-format shape guards against it. If the formats disagree, the verdict is "unresolved", by rule.

### 8.2 Other failure modes, with the lessons from `mistakes.md` that apply

**Unvalidated position pointers.** This is the 26 Oct mistake, recurring in E4. Every position set is recomputed from text, the stored pointer is kept as a check with its miss rate, and ±5 tokens around 30 random keys per pair and set are decoded and read.

**Forked choices.** Position, normalisation, layer weighting and rendering each have alternatives. The primaries are fixed above, one robustness table carries every alternative, and any primary ρ that moves by more than 0.15 or changes sign across co-primary variants is reported with both values and no choice made.

**Per-assignment analysis.** It re-introduces persona-to-animal leans such as dark roles leaning crow (`NS/mistakes.md`, 28 Oct). Average over assignments first, except in the count test, which is about assignment asymmetry by design and is labelled so.

**Unconditional rates read as routing.** Onset and routing are reported separately (`NS/mistakes.md`, 27 Oct, "markedness" entry, whose general pattern, "an unconditional rate standing in for a conditional one", is exactly what this chain's bias question invites).

**Nulls in the wrong units or containing the signal.** Use exactly balanced permutations, fixed-$d$ held-out permutations and $\Sigma_W$-shaped random directions (Section 4.9; `NS/mistakes.md`, 27 Oct, natural units; the 018 lesson).

**Controls matched to the wrong displacement.** These do not arise in DL1/DL2, which make no interventions. They do arise in any DL3 follow-up, which must follow R7 (`NS/mistakes.md`, 30 Oct).

**Mixed instruments.** Do not average over instruments that only some units have, such as the census b1 and b2 bridges (`NS/mistakes.md`, 26 Oct). Use b1 throughout chat, and the b1-equivalent bridge in S0/S1.

**Recipe confounds.** hd against hs mixes pair and recipe (Section 2.1). Contrasts that need a pair effect use hd against ps (same recipe) or N against Nc (same stories).

**Retrodiction presented as prediction.** It stays retrodiction (research synthesis W5). Only DL5-select is prospective.

**Calling a dataset new without searching the paper** (`NS/mistakes.md`, 30 Oct, SFL). The paper reports the rival-vs-rival pairs (App. C.4, V) and the Track C pairs, so DL5-select's scoring column says "replication on Qwen" and the forecast's value lies in discriminating two predictors, not in novelty.

**Old capped maps.** Maps measured at 512 tokens are lower bounds on onset. Use forced readouts or sw013 and sw020's uncapped numbers for any onset claim.

**Loading the wrong adapter, or none.** Every organism gets an adapter-active check (`NS/mistakes.md`, 27 Oct), and hs gets a parity check against `R_hs.json`.

### 8.3 Verification plan

**Blind recompute.** A separate agent, given only Sections 4.6, 4.7, 5.1, 5.2 and 5.6 and the input paths, recomputes the hd discriminant at set 2′, $q_{def}$, $\rho_\beta$, $\phi$ on F, G0's weights and the C1 correlations, without seeing the first implementation. ρ values must agree to the third decimal and $\phi$ to within 0.02. Otherwise the discrepancy is resolved before anything is used.

**Known answers.** These replace the first draft's "parent cancellation ≈ 0", which was not a known answer, since the parent's lean genuinely correlates with routing.

- *Target identity:* `R_raw` and `R_rel` maps agree to machine precision (V, $4\times10^{-16}$).
- *Story pass-through:* held-out story states projected through the pipeline reproduce G2's AUROC.
- *Planted map:* a synthetic routing map generated as $\alpha+\beta q$, and another as a hinge with a known $\theta$, plus noise at the measured per-script SD, must be recovered with $\rho_\beta$, $\hat\theta$ and $\phi$ inside their CIs. This checks that the shape test can tell the two apart at our sample sizes before it is run on real data.
- *Anchor identity:* $q(\bar A_H)=+1$ and $q(\bar A_R)=-1$ exactly.
- *Swap symmetry:* swapping H and R flips every sign and leaves every ρ unchanged.
- *Balanced-permutation centring:* the mean of 200 exactly balanced permuted $d$ has cosine with $d$ within the null's own SE of zero.
- *Position check:* on 50 random stories, the set 2′ token decodes as the switch line's speaker colon or opening quote, the prefix has no lexicon hit, and the realised prohibition matches the strong regex.

**Plumbing.** Assert key alignment between states and routing rows (role names, script ids, trigger flag, bridge); the layer convention (output of `layers[L]`) and fp32 accumulation; that each chat recipient ends in the 7 header tokens, or in the bridge for bridge-final keys; and the gain vector's parameterisation before any gain-normalised dot product.

**Raw data read.** Read 20 random contexts from every recipient set and anchor type, 20 random S0 stubs of each variant, and 20 random free replies per DL4 key cell (`qualitative-sample-analysis`), with quotes in the analysis. For S0 the read checks that the stubs read as natural story openings and that no animal cue survives.

## 9. What this spec deliberately does not do

This spec makes no causal claims. It does not steer, clamp or swap along $d$: that is DL3's job, and sw011 Part C already runs a clamped dose-response along d_self. It trains at most one new pair, and only under Section 5.9's rule. It generates no new stories, since the corpus is on hold. It does not compute the gradient kernel or test first-order learning, which is Chain B's work, although it borrows sw026's gradient code for κ. It uses no lenses, SAEs or verbalisers. It does not re-run sw009 C3 on repaired positions, because sw018's clean-prefix subset does that tonight. It drops the first draft's pooled history panel, H4, H7, H8, the share-space threshold model and the bias share $\pi_{bias}$, for the reasons given in Sections 4.6, 4.7 and 5.5. A good DL2 fit stays a retrodiction, scoped to the tested recipient sets, organisms (one run per assignment, plus sw001's 0.1-replay retrains on F) and readouts. Finally, the spec makes no claim about the mechanism inside the trained model. The projection is a pre-training predictor; whether training actually reads $d$ is sw010 (d)'s question and the sibling causal specs'.
