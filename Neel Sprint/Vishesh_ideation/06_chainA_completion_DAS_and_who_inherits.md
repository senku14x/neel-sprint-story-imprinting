## 0. The spec in one paragraph, and the order of work

Tonight's runs settle whether one reply-start direction, d_self ("the Assistant was hostile" minus "the user was hostile while the Assistant helped"), predates training (sw010 arm d), whether holding its coordinate fixed during an all-layer swap blocks the habit (sw010 arm c), whether it carries over to story speakers (sw016), and whether the one-direction swap replicates on 64 new scenarios (sw021). This spec targets three things those runs cannot settle. The first is **where the routing variable can be blocked, not only set**: a necessity test done inside one band of layers at a time (20–39, 40–63, and the single layer 42), with a subspace learned specifically to be necessary, because an all-layer complement swap reads "redundant" even for a mechanism that holds one copy at each depth (§4.3). The second is **what kind of variable the swap moves**: a reading of the Assistant's own conduct, the downstream plan to say a particular animal, or an assignment-invariant pointer such as "speak as the dismissive character". The spec separates these with a two-by-two donor design (conduct crossed with an explicitly requested animal), a mirror-sign statistic computed per band with a positive control, and two readouts besides the animal: the manner of the reply and the model's own one-word report of how it has treated the user. The third is **who else inherits the habit**: purely behavioural tests of whether the trained model applies the same manner-to-habit rule to speakers it was never trained on (the same chat words transposed into a story, two assistants in one chat, the model voicing the user, new story character types, an unmarked AI character, declared identities). The order of work follows information per GPU-hour. The behavioural A4 arms and the cheap band decomposition of d_self (A2-0) run first. Forward-only baselines (d_self, top-k singular vectors of the conduct differences, principal components, gradient directions) run next, on development scenarios. Necessity DAS by band runs after them. Sufficiency DAS is trained only in the cells where every forward-only baseline falls short of 0.8 of the full band swap, and the all-layer sweep comes last. Every readout that carries a claim gets controls matched to the realised per-layer displacement (the project's R7 rule).

## 1. The question, and why it matters for Neel and for the presentation

**A2's question in one sentence:** at the reply start, which low-dimensional variable must be set, and which must be left intact, for the Assistant to use one character's habit rather than the other's, and is that variable the model's reading of its own conduct, its plan to say a particular animal, or a role pointer? **A4's question in one sentence:** does the trained model apply one manner-to-habit rule to every speaker it voices, so that the default Assistant is simply one more speaker whose own manner puts it on the helper's side?

Neel's question is "why does the plain Assistant use the helpful character's habit, mechanistically?" It has an internal half and a behavioural half, and this spec gives each its own experiment.

The internal half concerns the moment the reply begins. At that moment the model holds something that decides between the two learned habits. We already know two ways to set it: overwrite the whole 7-token reply header (sw006, sw007), or set one coordinate, d_self (sw009 C4). A presentation claim of the form "the switch is a k-dimensional reading of the Assistant's own conduct that the parent already computes, and training hooked the habits onto it" needs five things. The variable must be sufficient on scenarios it was not fitted on. It must be necessary at a stated depth. It must be distinct from the downstream plan to say "bee" or "crow". It must be compared with the parent. And it must be tied to conduct by some readout other than the animal itself. C4 gives the first of these, for one direction, on five old scenarios, with directions fitted on a random split of scripts and matched controls on the forced readout. It gives none of the other four. A Neel-relevant way to put the gap is this: if swapping the variable also flips what the model says when asked how it has treated the user, the routing switch is tied to the model's verbal self-model. If it flips the animal but not the self-report or the manner, it is more likely a plan or a pointer that sits downstream of the self-appraisal. Either answer is a slide.

The behavioural half concerns generality. The user's working hypothesis is that "the model adopts the habit of the more Assistant-like character; story imprinting installs a switch-like direction, a form of out-of-context reasoning". The external audit's hypothesis is that "the Assistant is one more speaker the model judges". Both predict that the conditional installed by fine-tuning is keyed to a property of the current speaker, not to the two trained characters as such. A4 tests that prediction on speakers the training never contained, and it also separates the user's identity-flavoured version ("Assistant-like") from a pure manner version, because some arms change the declared identity while holding the shown manner fixed. If the prediction holds, the slide says that training installed a rule about kinds of speakers, the model applies it to whoever it is voicing, and the default Assistant's own manner puts it on the helper's side. If it fails, the Assistant needs a special route, and the authorship and rendering account (Chain G2, report v2 §4 step 12) moves up the queue.

## 2. What we already know, and what tonight's runs will add

### 2.1 The reply-start state is a sufficient handle, but not in every band and direction

The 7 reply-header tokens are `<|im_start|> assistant \n <think> \n\n </think> \n\n`, token ids [248045, 74455, 198, 248068, 271, 248069, 271] (V, `NS/experiments/sprint_work/006_swap_patch_all_layers/src/build_x.py` line 16). Overwriting their states at all layers with the states of another conduct version of the same script switches the habit both ways. On the forced readout ("By the way," prefilled, then 48 sampled tokens) removal moves the rival share from 0.87 to 0.42 in hdA0 and from 0.85 to 0.09 in hdB0, and induction moves it from 0.04 to 0.48 and from 0.01 to 0.63 (V, `NS/research_ledger.md` sw006 and sw007 entries). Free replies follow more completely: removal 0.77 → 0.04 and 0.89 → 0.02 (V, sw006 `analysis.md` table), induction 0.07 → 0.69 and 0.01 → 0.79 (V, ledger sw007).

The band results matter for this revision, because the necessity tests below are done inside bands. For **removal**, the whole-state swap restricted to layers 20–39 reaches 62% (hdA0) and 85% (hdB0) of the forced gap, layers 40–63 alone reach 42% and 81%, and layers 0–19 do nothing (V, sw006 `analysis.md` line 12). For **induction**, the full reverse swap reaches 0.54 and 0.71 of the forced gap, layers 20–39 alone reach 0.49 and 0.69, and layers 40–63 alone reach only 0.14 in hdA0 (not a flip) and 0.45 in hdB0 (V, sw007 `analysis.md` table, rows RP0, "RP0, layers 20–39", "RP0, layers 40–63"). So band 20–39 is sufficient in both directions and both organisms, while band 40–63 is sufficient for removal and for hdB0's induction but not for hdA0's induction. Tony's R03 reproduced the all-layer swap with an independent codebase and found that a whole-state swap at block 42 alone carries 0.76 (hdA0) and 0.99 (hdB0) of the all-layer removal effect, but only 0.35 and 0.50 of induction (R, `Vishesh_ideation/sources/tony_e12_habit_mechanism.md` Part 6 table). Layer 42 is therefore a single-layer site for removal only.

Necessity has been tested once, for an old 2-D "plane": swapping everything except the plane kept 81% and 95% of the forced effect, about what the except-random-plane comparators kept (V, ledger sw007). That test, like sw010's arm (c) tonight, held the subspace fixed at every layer while copying the donor's other coordinates at every layer, which §4.3 shows is biased toward "not necessary".

### 2.2 One direction, d_self, and what it does and does not move

C4 (sw009) set only the coordinate along d_self at the 7 header tokens and layers 16–63. d_self is the reply-start difference between sw008's "the Assistant said the dismissive sentences" and "the user said them to a polite Assistant", fitted per model on header means with a random split of the 64 scripts. Against no-edit shares of 0.843 and 0.198 (hdA0) and 0.820 and 0.071 (hdB0), the swap moved the forced habit toward the donor by +0.28 [0.16, 0.40] (removal) and +0.40 [0.29, 0.50] (induction) in hdA0, and by +0.62 and +0.63 in hdB0. Those are 0.44, 0.62, 0.83 and 0.84 of the no-edit gap. The largest of 10 rank-1 controls, matched to the real arm's realised displacement, moved 0.04, 0.07, 0.01 and 0.01, and the same-kind donor moved −0.08 to −0.01. The top-4 singular-vector subspace did about the same as rank 1 (0.48, 0.70, 0.85, 0.80). Each swap moved the header state by about 0.3–0.5% of its norm per layer. All of this is V in `NS/experiments/sprint_work/009_hostile_user_and_self_attribution/analysis.md` §5.

Two further numbers from the same section matter here. First, free replies (256-token cap, no control arms) followed almost completely, at 0.95–1.06 of the free gap; sw012 reruns them tonight with controls at 2,048 tokens (V, `NS/mistakes.md` top entry). Second, the reply's manner moved very differently in the two directions. Under removal, judged hostility of the free replies fell from 2.24 to 1.33 (hdA0) and from 1.56 to 1.15 (hdB0). Under induction, while the habit switched almost completely, hostility barely moved: 1.08 → 1.17 and 1.05 → 1.10 (V, sw009 `analysis.md` §5, "Reply conduct"). So the one-direction swap can install the rival habit in a reply that stays polite. That is exactly what a plan-to-say-an-animal variable predicts, and it is also what a routing-only reading of conduct predicts, which is why §4.10 adds a self-report readout.

A rough comparison suggests that sufficiency is already largely in hand with rank 1 (estimate; the context sets differ, because C4 ran on sw008 contexts and P0 on sw005 contexts). C4's toward-donor shifts (0.28, 0.40, 0.62, 0.63) divided by the full-swap shifts (0.45, 0.44, 0.76, 0.62 for hdA0 removal, hdA0 induction, hdB0 removal, hdB0 induction; from §2.1) give about 0.62, 0.91, 0.82 and 1.02. Tonight's sw010 supplies P0 on the sw008 contexts and turns this into a like-for-like ratio. Section 5.2 uses this to gate the expensive sufficiency search.

Tonight's sw010 step 0 has already run (V, `/workspace/runs_executor/sw010/step01_report.txt`, 08:32 UTC). On the leave-one-scenario-out d_self scaled so that helpful = 0 and dismissive = 1, the intermediate sw005 versions sit at polite refusal 0.58 / 0.56, deflection 0.60 / 0.57, rude-but-helpful 0.68 / 0.73 and curt-but-helpful 0.03 / 0.02 (hdA0 / hdB0), with the parent close to these (0.66, 0.64, 0.66, 0.02). Their behavioural rival shares in chat are 0.339, 0.347, 0.578 and 0.052 (R, ledger sw005; V in report v2 row 7). So graded conduct sits roughly 55–75% of the way along d_self while its routing is 35–60% of the way, a mild nonlinearity that the graded-slope statistic of §4.4 is built to measure.

### 2.3 The parent's own difference and the story side

The parent's full helpful-minus-dismissive difference, added over blocks 20–39 of the post-history span, moves only 0.02–0.23 of the organism's own effect on the forced readout in hdA0 and 0.07–0.57 in hdB0. The cosine between the parent's and the organism's difference falls from 0.70 at block 20 to 0.39 at block 39. The habit-free control's difference transfers more than the parent's (0.46 vs 0.17 for hdA0 removal), and generic perturbation of that span degrades the rival habit toward the helper's but never creates the rival habit (R, `sources/tony_e13_e14.md` §4.1, around line 141). That one-way fragility recurs below: any intervention that damages the model will look like successful removal and like failed induction, so induction must pass on its own wherever sufficiency is claimed, and removal must pass on its own wherever necessity is claimed (§4.4). In stories, C3 found that d_self separates dismissive from helper speakers just before their habit line: parent AUROC 0.72–0.76 against within-condition null 95th percentiles of 0.64–0.71, and 0.80–0.94 in the organisms (R, sw009 `analysis.md` §4, re-checked by the red-team).

### 2.4 The animal plan

Tony's layer-42 SAE (his R02 fine-tune of the public `ceselder/qwen36-27b-sae-l42-1b`, 1,990 more steps with extra header tokens; R, `sources/tony_e12_habit_mechanism.md` §3) shows that the trained models' reply-start state already contains entries for the specific animal about to be said. A honeybee entry (77568) is active in 100% of hdA0's triggered helpful chats and 0% of the habit-free control's; in hdB0 it codes 3.40 after a dismissive history against 0.11 after a helpful one. Removing the 9 bee entries' contributions at the header alone moves hdB0's dismissive rival share from 0.826 to 0.516, a necessity effect of about 0.31 of 0.83. Removing them at every position does not remove the aside; the model switches to the other trained animal and the habit rate stays at 0.95–1.0. The helper's bee entries on hdA0's header are not necessary (0.016 → 0.016), and the crow list is too weak (3 entries) for the mirror test (R, same digest, Parts 5a and 5b). His reading is that "which animal" is planned at the reply start, at least for the rival in hdB0. A d_self fitted on each organism's own states, whose two ends route to different animals, may then contain part of that plan, and C4 may partly swap a downstream plan rather than the conduct reading upstream of it.

### 2.5 The geometry of d_self across the mirror organisms (CPU checks, restated after the red-team)

A conduct reading should point the same way in hdA0 (helper → bee) and hdB0 (helper → crow), because their stories differ only in the animal. A plan component should flip sign between them, because the dismissive end means "crow" in hdA0 and "bee" in hdB0. Write $\hat u_A, \hat u_B$ for the two organisms' unit d_self at one layer (sw009's all-scripts fit) and $c = \cos(\hat u_A, \hat u_B)$. The shared part is $d^{\mathrm{sym}} \propto \hat u_A + \hat u_B$ and the flipped part is $d^{\mathrm{anti}} \propto \hat u_A - \hat u_B$. The share of each organism's direction that lies in the flipped part is $(1-c)/2$. From `sources/chainA_checks/dself_ab_cos.out.txt` (V), computed from `/workspace/_box_transfer/sw009_runs_box1_2026-10-30/dirs_{parent,hdA0,hdB0}.pt`:

| layer | $c$ = cos(hdA0, hdB0) | flipped share $(1-c)/2$ | cos(parent, hdA0) | cos(parent, hdB0) | cos(parent, shared) | cos(parent, flipped) | fold stability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 0.942 | 0.029 | 0.767 | 0.794 | 0.792 | −0.080 | 0.99 |
| 32 | 0.903 | 0.049 | 0.596 | 0.608 | 0.617 | −0.026 | 0.99 |
| 40 | 0.700 | 0.150 | 0.573 | 0.470 | 0.566 | 0.133 | 0.99 |
| 48 | 0.629 | 0.186 | 0.551 | 0.436 | 0.547 | 0.133 | 0.98–0.99 |
| 63 | 0.426 | 0.287 | 0.440 | 0.386 | 0.489 | 0.050 | 0.97–0.98 |

The two random-split folds of each model agree at 0.97–0.99, so the drop in cross-organism agreement from layer 40 on is far outside fitting noise. But $(1-c)/2$ is only an **upper bound** on a plan share, because it also counts any organism-specific drift that has nothing to do with the assignment. There is a sign of such drift: at layer 63 the parent-versus-organism cosines (0.44 and 0.39) are about as low as the organism-versus-organism cosine (0.43), so the organisms differ from each other about as much as each differs from the parent. And the first draft was wrong to say the parent's d_self has nothing in common with the flipped part: the cosine is 0.133 at layers 40 and 48.

How much of the flipped part points along the readout? Let $w = \gamma \odot (W_U[\text{crow}] - W_U[\text{bee}])$, with $W_U$ the unembedding rows of " crow" (id 9000) and " bee" (id 35997) and $\gamma$ the final RMSNorm gain. The cosine of the flipped part with $w$ is 0.00 at L16–32, 0.069 at L40, 0.061 at L48, 0.138 at L56, 0.226 at L60 and 0.175 at L63. The first draft's null used token ids 100000–100019, which on this tokenizer are Chinese words (V: 怎样, 衣服, 农民 …), a weak null for English animal nouns. The revision's check (V, `sources/chainA_checks/anti_vs_unembed_v2.out.txt`) uses 190 pairs of single-token English animal nouns, 30 insect-versus-bird pairs and 190 pairs of frequent English nouns. At L40 and L48 the bee-versus-crow cosine (0.069, 0.061) is at the level of the largest English nulls (animal pairs max 0.058 and 0.047, insect-versus-bird max 0.056 and 0.058), so it is not specific there. At L56–63 it is 2–3 times the largest null (0.138–0.226 against animal-pair maxima of 0.073–0.074 and insect-versus-bird maxima of 0.069–0.086). The energy this represents is small. At L60 the readout-aligned share of the flipped part is $0.226^2 = 0.051$, which is 0.0136 of d_self's energy (0.265 × 0.051); per organism, d_self's own readout-aligned energy is $0.083^2 = 0.007$ (hdA0) and $0.151^2 = 0.023$ (hdB0). The organisms' signs are as a plan predicts (positive in hdA0, whose dismissive end is crow; negative in hdB0), and the parent sits at 0.00 ± 0.02.

The reading I now defend (interpretation, level 6) is narrower than the first draft's. Up to layer 32, the organisms' d_self is one shared, parent-aligned direction. From layer 40 on, up to 15–29% of its energy differs between the organisms. Some of that difference may be drift; a specific, plan-like alignment with the bee-versus-crow readout appears only at layers 56–63 and amounts to about 1–2% of d_self's energy. None of this says how much of C4's behavioural effect the flipped part carries; step A2-0 measures that, and its hd-A_s1 capture separates drift from an assignment-specific component (§5.2).

### 2.6 The readout's own conduct-dependent lean, and the tokenizer

The continuous readout used throughout A2 is the log-odds of bee over crow after a forced bridge (§4.2). It carries a lean that depends on conduct but not on the habit. The ledger's 008 entry says that "in chat both controls show an assignment-independent 'dismissive → crow' bias of ~1.5 nats (cancels under the A/B swap)" and that "the single-token readout under-measures bee" (V, `NS/research_ledger.md` 008 entry). The red-team read this as a dismissive → crow lean inside the habit-free controls. The project's own mistakes log shows that the ~1.5 nats is the controls **relative to the parent** under the dismissive prompt (V, `NS/mistakes.md` 26 Oct entry, "Told the user that 008 showed a training-independent 'dismissive → crow' bias…"). In raw bee-minus-crow log-odds, the parent sits at +0.96 by default and +2.75 under the dismissive prompt, so the parent leans **bee** by about 1.8 nats when dismissive; the habit-free controls lean bee by only +0.23 (ctrl A) and +0.38 (ctrl B) when dismissive. Those numbers come from persona system prompts, not from conduct histories, so the size and sign of the lean on our contexts are unknown. What is known is that a conduct-dependent lean exists in both the parent and the controls, and that it adds to the habit shift in one organism and subtracts in the other (a lean toward bee when dismissive helps hdB0, whose rival is bee, and hurts hdA0). The first draft's claim that "a constant lean cancels" does not cover a lean that changes with the conduct being swapped, and its claim that the controls have "no conduct-to-animal link" was wrong.

The tokenizer adds a second problem (V, `tokenizer.json` of `/dev/shm/qwen36_27b`, revision 6a9e13bd). " bee" (35997), " bees" (38152) and " crow" (9000) are single tokens, but " crows" is two ([272 " c", 1748 "rows"]), " honeybee" is two ([24924 " honey", 30976 "bee"]), " raven" is two, and Tony's main bee entry codes "honeybee". A single-token readout misses part of each animal's probability mass, unequally. Section 4.2 fixes both problems.

## 3. Hypotheses and the strongest alternatives, stated so they can fail

**H-A2 (a small conduct variable, located by band).** Inside band 20–39, and separately inside 40–63 and at layer 42 for removal, there is a subspace of at most 8 dimensions per layer that is both sufficient (setting it to the donor's values while the rest of the band stays at the recipient's reproduces at least 0.8 of the full band swap) and necessary (holding it at the recipient's values while the rest of the band takes the donor's leaves at most 0.2 of the full band swap), on held-out v2 scenarios, with induction and removal each passing where the band is sufficient. Its helpful-versus-dismissive difference points the same way in hdA0 and hdB0, it carries a conduct change and not a requested animal in the two-by-two donor test, and the parent draws a similar difference inside it. This fails if no $k \le 32$ is necessary in any band, if the subspace carries the requested animal across in the two-by-two test, or if its difference flips sign between the mirror organisms.

**Alternative 1 (plan, not conduct).** What the header swap moves is mainly the plan to say a particular animal, written at layers ≥ 40. The conduct reading that produced the plan sits upstream: in the history tokens, the trigger tokens, the early header, or the Gated DeltaNet recurrent state. Prediction: in band 40–63 the learned subspace carries the explicitly requested animal across (high plan-carry), projecting out the plan handle removes most of its effect, its mirror sign is negative, the parent shows little difference along it, and swapping it changes neither the reply's manner nor the self-report.

**Alternative 2 (parallel redundancy).** Several directions at the same depth each carry the routing information, so any one small subspace is sufficient and none is necessary. Prediction: within a single band, even the necessity-trained subspace at $k = 32$ leaves at least half the effect, and the redundancy statistic $\rho_B > 0.2$.

**Alternative 2b (serial re-encoding, new in this revision).** At any one depth the information sits in a small subspace, but that subspace changes with depth. Prediction: band-restricted necessity succeeds at small $k$ (inside 20–39, or at layer 42), while the all-layer complement of sw010 arm (c) keeps at least half of P0. This is distinguishable from Alternative 2 only by the band-restricted design, which is the main reason for it.

**Alternative 3 (a DAS artefact).** A learned subspace can reproduce an effect without being the model's variable, by combining a direction that varies with conduct but is not read with a "dormant" direction that does not vary on the data but moves the output when set off-distribution (Makelov, Lange and Nanda's interpretability illusion; R, `sources/lit_local_methods.md` §10). Late layers near the unembedding make this easy, because a bee-versus-crow readout direction is always available there. Prediction: DAS "works" in models that have no habit (the parent, the habit-free controls) when trained toward the organism's targets, its effect depends on the low-variance part of the subspace, and it adds nothing on held-out data over the training-free top-$k$ singular-vector baseline.

**Alternative 3b (an assignment-invariant pointer, new in this revision).** The variable is "speak as the dismissive character" (or "as the helper"), downstream of the conduct reading but not a reading of conduct. It lives in the shared part, so its mirror sign is positive, and it carries conduct-only donors across in the two-by-two test, exactly like a conduct reading. Prediction: the parent lacks it (the parent never saw the characters), and swapping it changes the reply's persona (manner) but not the model's report of its own past conduct.

**H-A4 (one more speaker).** The trained model routes the habit by the manner of whichever speaker it is currently voicing: the same conduct transposed into a story, each of two assistants in one chat, the user when the model writes the user's turn, and untrained story character types. Each speaker's rival share is the same function of that speaker's conduct that the Assistant's is in chat. This fails if the story ladder's slope on the chat ladder is outside [0.75, 1.25], if two assistants in one chat get the same habit regardless of which one speaks, or if a voiced hostile user (with a passing user-turn positive control) shows no habit difference from a voiced polite user.

**Alternative 4 (trained characters plus a special Assistant route).** The habits are bound to the two trained character types, and the Assistant inherits through a separate route: authorship of the training stories, or the template positions every story was trained after. Prediction: transposed stories route only at the trained dismissive level, the user role shows no conduct dependence even when the user turn can express the habit, and two assistants get the habit of the assistant role as a whole.

**Alternative 5 (role-wide self-appraisal, relabelled).** sw009 Stage A already rules out a context-wide mood for a hostile user (forced M3 − M1 +0.02 [−0.01, 0.05] in hdA0 and −0.01 in hdB0; V, sw009 `analysis.md` §2). The live alternative for A4d is that the model appraises the assistant role as a whole: in a chat with two assistants, it averages their conduct rather than reading the addressed one's. Prediction: in A4d the addressed assistant's manner barely matters ($S/G \le 0.3$).

**Alternative 6 (declared identity, the user's Assistant-likeness reading in its strong form).** What routes is how Assistant-like the speaker is, not its manner. Prediction: holding the shown manner fixed, a declared human or non-Assistant identity shifts routing (A4b), and an unmarked "AI assistant" story character takes the helper's side while an unmarked human does not (A4a4).

## 4. The basis and the mathematics

### 4.1 Notation

Let $M$ be a model: hdA0, hdB0, the parent, or a habit-free control. The matched control of an organism is $K(\text{hdA0}) = $ ctrl-A_s0 and $K(\text{hdB0}) = $ ctrl-B_s0; each is the 001 recipe trained on the same stories with the habit rewritten out of the triggered half (R, ledger 005). For a context $x$, let $h_{\ell,t}(x) \in \mathbb R^{5120}$ be the output of decoder layer $\ell$ at position $t$, read with a forward hook on `model.model.layers[ℓ]` before the final norm (the project convention). $T$ is the set of 7 reply-header positions, and a **band** $B$ is a set of consecutive layers: $B_1 = \{20,\dots,39\}$, $B_2 = \{40,\dots,63\}$, $B_{42} = \{42\}$, and the all-layer band $B_{\text{all}} = \{16,\dots,63\}$ used by C4. A **pair** $(b, s)$ is a base context $b$ (the recipient, which is edited) and a source context $s$ (the donor), two versions of the same script that differ only in conduct. **Removal** means $b$ is the dismissive (or self-hostile) version and $s$ the helpful (or user-hostile) one; **induction** is the reverse. $h^{s}_{\ell,t}$ and $h^{b}_{\ell,t}$ are the donor's and recipient's states captured in clean, unedited runs. $\Pi_\ell = U_\ell U_\ell^\top$ is the orthogonal projection onto a $k$-dimensional subspace with orthonormal basis $U_\ell \in \mathbb R^{5120\times k}$.

### 4.2 The readout DAS is trained on, corrected for the conduct lean, and its validity gate

A sampled readout cannot be differentiated, so DAS needs a continuous one. Tony's "C" readout, the log-probability of one animal minus the other right after a forced bridge, tracked the sampled routing share at Spearman 0.85–0.90 across conditions, post hoc (R, audit doc line 349). Two changes make it fit for pair-level regression.

**Family-summed, multi-token log-odds.** Let $\mathcal B$ and $\mathcal C$ be fixed lists of complete bee and crow strings, taken from the project's STRICT regex (V, `/workspace/runs_executor/overnight_2026-10-30/lib/labels.py` lines 37–39): for bee, " bee", " bees", " honeybee", " honeybees", " honey bee", " honey bees", " bumblebee", " bumblebees"; for crow, " crow", " crows", " raven", " ravens", " corvid", " corvids", " American crow", " magpie". The probability of a multi-token string is the product of its teacher-forced token probabilities. In the smoke, each list is trimmed to the strings that together hold at least 95% of that family's probability mass on 64 no-edit contexts per organism; the trimmed lists are frozen in `DECISIONS.md` before any training. With bridges $\beta_1$ = "Random animal fact: the" and $\beta_2$ = "Fun fact about the" prefilled in the assistant reply,

$$y_M(x) \;=\; \sigma_M\,\frac12\sum_{j=1}^{2}\Big[\log\sum_{v\in\mathcal B} p_M(v\mid x\oplus\beta_j)\;-\;\log\sum_{v\in\mathcal C} p_M(v\mid x\oplus\beta_j)\Big],\qquad \sigma_{\text{hdA0}}=-1,\ \sigma_{\text{hdB0}}=+1,$$

so that positive always means "toward the rival". The bridge supplies the aside, so $y$ measures routing given an aside, not onset; onset is measured separately in every behavioural readout, because a routing statistic can be gamed by suppressing the other animal (`NS/mistakes.md` 25 Oct).

**Correction for the conduct lean.** For any intervention $E$ on pair $i$, the raw shift is $\Delta_{M,i}(E) = y_M(b_i, s_i; E) - y_M(b_i)$. The matched control $K(M)$ is run on the same texts with its own captured donor and recipient states and the same intervention (the same $U$, with $\sigma_M$'s orientation), giving $\Delta_{K(M),i}(E)$. The **corrected shift** is the difference-in-differences

$$\Delta^{c}_{M,i}(E) \;=\; \Delta_{M,i}(E) - \Delta_{K(M),i}(E).$$

It removes whatever part of the shift the story fine-tune produces without the habit, including the conduct-dependent lean of §2.6, to the extent that the lean is the same in the organism and its control. As a check that does not rely on that assumption, the pooled two-organism ratio of raw sums is reported beside it. Because the lean adds $+\lambda$ to hdA0's oriented shifts and $-\lambda$ to hdB0's, summing numerators and denominators over the two organisms cancels it exactly when their habit shifts are equal in size and approximately otherwise; this is the assignment-averaging rule from `NS/mistakes.md` 28 Oct ("correlate with the assignment-averaged score first"). If the corrected per-organism ratios and the pooled raw ratio disagree by more than 0.1, the verdict is "readout lean unresolved" and no claim is made from $y$ alone.

**Pair-level validity gate (before any training).** DAS regresses on $y$ pair by pair, while the earlier validation of this readout was across conditions. So, on 64 training pairs per organism (32 removal, 32 induction), the full band-$B_1$ swap and the no-edit arm each get $m = 16$ forced-readout samples ("By the way," + 48 tokens), and the sampled shift $\Delta r_i$ (rival share among habit-bearing samples, swap minus no edit) is compared with $\Delta^{c}_{M,i}(I)$. The observed Spearman correlation $\rho_{\text{obs}}$ is bounded by the reliability of $\Delta r_i$, which is noisy at 16 samples. Its split-half reliability, Spearman-Brown corrected, is $R = 2r_h/(1+r_h)$, with $r_h$ the Spearman correlation between the shifts computed from two halves of the samples. The disattenuated correlation is $\rho^* = \rho_{\text{obs}}/\sqrt{R}$ ($y$ is deterministic, so its own reliability is 1). The gate passes if $\rho^* \ge 0.7$ and $\rho_{\text{obs}} \ge 0.4$ in each organism. The basis for $m = 16$: if true per-pair shifts vary with standard deviation $\sigma_t \approx 0.3$ (estimate) and sampling adds variance $2p(1-p)/m_{\text{hb}}$ with $p \approx 0.5$ and about 14 habit-bearing samples per arm, the reliability is $0.09/(0.09 + 0.036) \approx 0.71$ and the best achievable $\rho_{\text{obs}}$ is about 0.85, so the gate can pass when the readout is valid. At $m = 8$ it would often fail for benign reasons. If the gate fails, A2's verdicts rest on sampled readouts only and DAS is trained on a fallback target, the full-swap logit difference of the first token of the forced reply's habit word, validated the same way.

### 4.3 The interventions within a band, and why an all-layer complement is biased toward "redundant"

DAS's operation is the **interchange intervention**, which overwrites the base state's coordinates inside a subspace with the donor's and leaves the orthogonal part alone. This spec uses three versions, each applied at the header positions $t \in T$ and at every layer of one band $B$, with all other layers running live.

The **live interchange** (standard for sufficiency) uses the live state $h_{\ell,t}$, which already includes the effects of edits at earlier layers of the band:

$$\tilde h_{\ell,t} \;=\; h_{\ell,t} + \Pi_\ell\big(h^{s}_{\ell,t} - h_{\ell,t}\big),\qquad \ell\in B.$$

The **clamped interchange** pins the orthogonal part to the recipient's captured state:

$$S^{\mathrm{cl}}_B(U):\quad \tilde h_{\ell,t} \;=\; h^{b}_{\ell,t} + \Pi_\ell\big(h^{s}_{\ell,t} - h^{b}_{\ell,t}\big),\qquad \ell\in B.$$

The **clamped complement** is its exact mirror: the donor's state everywhere except inside the subspace, which keeps the recipient's captured value,

$$C^{\mathrm{cl}}_B(U):\quad \tilde h_{\ell,t} \;=\; h^{b}_{\ell,t} + (I-\Pi_\ell)\big(h^{s}_{\ell,t} - h^{b}_{\ell,t}\big) \;=\; h^{s}_{\ell,t} - \Pi_\ell\big(h^{s}_{\ell,t}-h^{b}_{\ell,t}\big),\qquad \ell\in B.$$

At every edited layer the two clamped displacements add up to the full displacement $h^s - h^b$, so they split the full band swap into two exact complements. That is what makes the redundancy statistic of §4.4 meaningful. For a single layer ($B_{42}$) live and clamped coincide, because nothing upstream has been edited. The full band swap is $U_\ell = I$ in either form. Because the header positions are the last prompt positions, causal masking means no edit changes an earlier position; an edit reaches the reply through attention to the header at every later layer and through what the header writes into the Gated DeltaNet recurrent state of each linear layer (R, `sources/lit_local_methods.md` §1). Layers after the band recompute from the edited band output, so each band test asks what that band contributes given that later layers are free to re-read the history.

**Why the all-layer complement reads "redundant" for a serial mechanism.** Suppose the routing bit $z$ is written into a direction $u$ at layer 20, and layers 21–40 copy it into a direction $v \perp u$, after which nothing reads $u$. A sufficiency-trained subspace $U = \operatorname{span}(u)$ at every layer passes the live interchange: setting $u$ at layer 20 lets the model write $v$ itself, and at layers 40–63 the learned $U$ is unconstrained by the objective, because whatever it is, the model's own recomputation carries the bit. Now apply the all-layer captured complement with that $U$: the donor's $v$ is copied in at layers 40–63, the reply reads $v$, and the effect survives, so necessity $N \approx 1$ and $\rho = F + N - 1 \approx 1$, "redundant", although at every depth exactly one copy exists and blocking $u$ at layer 20 (with later layers live) would block everything. The band-restricted clamped complement at $B = \{20\}$ (or $B_1$ with $U_\ell$ learned for necessity at every layer of the band) holds $u$ at the recipient's value, the model computes $v$ from the recipient's $u$, and the effect is blocked, $N \approx 0$. This is why necessity is tested band by band, with the same clamped convention for $F$ and $N$, and with a subspace trained for necessity (§4.5). It is also why the clamped convention, not the live one, is used for the complement: a live complement would let layer $\ell$ recompute the subspace's coordinates from the donor's orthogonal part at layer $\ell - 1$, which reopens the same route.

### 4.4 Interchange accuracy, necessity, redundancy, the graded slope, and their uncertainty

For a set of held-out pairs of one direction (so the sign of the shift is fixed), the **interchange accuracy** of an intervention $E$ in band $B$ is the share of the full band swap's corrected shift that it reproduces:

$$F_B(E) \;=\; \frac{\sum_i \Delta^{c}_{M,i}(E)}{\sum_i \Delta^{c}_{M,i}(I_B)}.$$

It is a ratio of sums, not a mean of per-pair ratios, because per-pair ratios explode when the full swap barely moves a pair. $F$ is reported per organism and per direction, and **induction must pass on its own wherever sufficiency is claimed**, because generic damage pushes toward the helper and so mimics removal (§2.3). **Necessity** is $N_B(U) = F_B(C^{\mathrm{cl}}_B(U))$, and **removal must pass on its own wherever necessity is claimed**, for the mirror reason: in induction (helpful recipient, dismissive donor) a damaging complement pushes toward the helper and mimics a blocked effect, while in removal damage pushes toward the donor and can only hide necessity. **Redundancy** within a band is

$$\rho_B(U) \;=\; F_B\big(S^{\mathrm{cl}}_B(U)\big) + N_B(U) - 1.$$

If the readout were additive in the two exact complements, $\rho = 0$. $\rho > 0$ means each part carries the effect on its own (parallel redundancy, Alternative 2); $\rho < 0$ means both parts are needed together (synergy). Nonlinearity also moves $\rho$, so it is read against random subspaces of the same $k$, for which $F \approx 0$ and $N \approx 1$.

A per-pair binary version is reported beside $F$, restricted to the pairs where it is informative. With $\tau$ the midpoint of the no-edit class means of $y$ on training pairs, a pair is **crossing** if the full band swap moves $y$ across $\tau$. Then $I_B(E)$ is the share of crossing pairs on which $E$ also crosses $\tau$, so $I_B(0) = 0$ by construction; the share of crossing pairs is reported too, because in hdA0's forced readout the full swap reaches only about half the gap (§2.1) and many pairs will not cross.

**Graded donors** (curt-but-helpful, polite refusal, deflection, rude-but-helpful swapped into helpful recipients) test whether a subspace carries intermediate values. A fraction of the full swap at each level is unstable at the curt level, whose behavioural shift is near zero (0.020 vs 0.052 in chat). Instead, the **graded slope** $\beta_B(E)$ is the least-squares slope through the origin of $\Delta^{c}_{i}(E)$ on $\Delta^{c}_{i}(I_B)$ over all graded pairs, $\hat\beta = \sum_i \Delta^c_i(E)\,\Delta^c_i(I_B)/\sum_i \Delta^c_i(I_B)^2$, which weights each pair by how much the full swap moved it. A real variable should give $\beta \ge 0.7$ when the extreme $F \ge 0.8$. sw010 step 0's positions (§2.2) give a prediction for d_self in particular: if the habit read d_self linearly, graded shifts would be proportional to the graded positions (0.56–0.73 of the way along d_self), so its $\beta$ on graded donors should match its $F$ on extreme donors; a lower $\beta$ means a threshold-like read.

**Uncertainty.** All intervals come from a scenario-clustered bootstrap (2,000 draws) that resamples scenarios with all their pairs, arms and samples, so the ratio's numerator and denominator move together. The delta-method approximation is the planning formula. With per-pair means $\bar n$ and $\bar d$ of the numerator and denominator terms,

$$\operatorname{Var}(\hat F) \;\approx\; \frac{1}{\bar d^{\,2}}\Big[\operatorname{Var}(\bar n) - 2F\operatorname{Cov}(\bar n,\bar d) + F^{2}\operatorname{Var}(\bar d)\Big],$$

where the variances and covariance are of cluster means. The first draft's formula kept only the first term; because the subspace's shift and the full swap's shift are strongly correlated across pairs, the covariance term usually makes the true interval narrower than that formula, and the bootstrap captures it.

### 4.5 How DAS learns a subspace: two objectives, and the restriction to natural variance

$U_\ell$ is parameterised with orthonormal columns (`torch.nn.utils.parametrizations.orthogonal` on a $5120\times k$ matrix, as in Geiger et al.'s DAS and Boundless DAS; R, `sources/lit_local_methods.md` §10). The model is frozen; $U$ and the interchange arithmetic are kept in fp32 and the model in bf16.

The **sufficiency objective** regresses the live-interchanged readout onto the full band swap's readout:

$$\mathcal L_{\text{suf}}(U) \;=\; \frac1n\sum_i \operatorname{Huber}_{\delta=2}\big(y(b_i,s_i;\,S^{\text{live}}_B(U)) - y(b_i,s_i;\,I_B)\big).$$

The target is the full band swap, not the donor's own output, because the question is whether a small subspace reproduces what the band carries.

The **necessity objective** (new) asks that holding $U$ at the recipient's values inside an otherwise full donor swap gives about the no-edit readout:

$$\mathcal L_{\text{nec}}(U) \;=\; \frac1n\sum_i \omega_i\,\operatorname{Huber}_{\delta=2}\big(y(b_i,s_i;\,C^{\mathrm{cl}}_B(U)) - y(b_i)\big),$$

with weights $\omega_i$ that give removal and induction pairs equal total weight. Removal pairs anchor it against damage: in removal the target is the rival-side value $y(b_i)$, and damage pushes toward the helper, away from the target, so the optimiser cannot satisfy removal by breaking the model. The learned $U$ is then also evaluated for clamped sufficiency, which tells whether the necessary subspace is also sufficient.

**Nested training ("Matryoshka" subspaces).** One $U$ with 32 columns is trained under $\sum_{k\in K}\mathcal L(U_{[:,1:k]})$, $K = \{1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32\}$, so every prefix of the columns is itself a trained $k$-subspace. A dedicated $k = 4$ run in one cell checks that nesting costs no accuracy (within 0.05 of $F$ on validation).

**Restriction to natural variance (the illusion guard).** Let $V^{(r)}_\ell$ be the top-$r$ principal directions of the header-mean states at layer $\ell$ over all training contexts, pooled over versions, with $r$ the smallest number covering 99% of the variance, capped at 1,024 and reported per layer. The **PC-restricted** variant sets $U_\ell = V^{(r)}_\ell Q_\ell$ with $Q_\ell \in \mathbb R^{r\times k}$ orthonormal. A dormant direction barely varies on the data, so it lies outside the top principal span and the illusion's dormant half is excluded. A real mechanism that uses low-variance directions would be missed, so the unrestricted variant is run beside it in one band; a gap of more than 0.15 between them on held-out data is an illusion warning, not extra signal.

**Training settings** (Boundless DAS adapted; R, `sources/lit_local_methods.md` §10): Adam, learning rate 1e-3 on the subspace parameters, batch 16 pairs, up to 40 epochs, early stopping on cross-validated $F$ (§5.1). Restarts are chosen by **training loss**, not by validation, which removes one selection degree of freedom from the validation set.

The **distribution objective** of the first draft (match the full swap's next-token distributions over 24 teacher-forced tokens) is kept only as an optional extra in band $B_1$, run if GPU time remains after A2-8; the manner and self-report readouts of §4.10 now carry the "is this more than the animal?" question more directly.

### 4.6 The plan handle P, and the three gates it must pass

The plan-removed variant needs a basis $P_\ell$ for "which animal is planned". The first draft built it from four estimates and never checked that it captured a plan. In this revision $P_\ell$ starts from five seeds and must pass gates before any variant that uses it is interpreted.

The seeds are: the flipped part $d^{\mathrm{anti}}_\ell$, refitted leave-one-scenario-out from the sw009 captures; the explicit-ask directions $\hat a^{A}_\ell$ and $\hat a^{B}_\ell$, the header-mean difference in each organism between helpful contexts whose turn 3 ends with an ask for a crow fact and the same contexts ending with an ask for a bee fact (the ask is appended after the script's own prohibition and question, so the trigger is intact; §5.2 gives the wording); the readout direction $\hat w_\ell$ normalised; and, at layer 42 only, the decoder rows of Tony's 9 bee and 3 crow SAE entries (from his ft42 SAE if its weights are in the e12 branch or his HF catalog; otherwise the public L42 SAE's rows for the same indices, which ft42 was fine-tuned from, labelled as such). The seeds are orthonormalised per layer.

**Gate G-P1 (P carries a planned animal).** In each organism, swapping only $P$ (live interchange, band $B_2$) from a helpful context that asks for the rival's animal into the same helpful context that asks for the helper's animal must move $y$ toward the rival by at least 0.5 of the full band swap between those two contexts, and beyond every R7-matched control. This shows $P$ is a handle on the requested animal.

**Gate G-P2 (projecting P out removes the requested animal's linear trace).** After projecting $P$ out of the header-mean states at layers 40–63, a logistic probe **refit** on the projected states (cross-validated by scenario) must decode the requested animal at AUROC ≤ 0.6. Refitting matters: a probe trained before projection can fail merely because its own direction was removed. If the gate fails, $P$ is extended by iterative nullspace projection (INLP: fit the probe, add its normalised weight to $P$, project, repeat) up to 16 dimensions per layer. If it still fails, the variant is called "P-removed", not "plan-free", and its plan fraction is a lower bound.

**Gate G-P3 (P is tied to the habit's own plan, not only to a mentioned word).** The explicit ask may create a mentioned-word representation ("the user typed 'crows'") rather than the plan the habit forms. The tie to the habit is A2-0's result: projecting $P$ out at the header in hdB0's natural dismissive contexts must move the rival share toward the helper by at least as much as Tony's header-only bee-entry removal did (0.31 of 0.83; R), or swapping $P$ alone between natural helpful and dismissive contexts must beat its R7 controls. If G-P3 fails while G-P1 passes, $P$ is a handle on requested animals only, and every plan-fraction result is reported as "requested-animal fraction".

Given $P$, the **plan-free** (or P-removed) variant uses $U_\ell = \operatorname{orth}\big((I - P_\ell P_\ell^\top)V^{(r)}_\ell Q_\ell\big)$, and the **plan fraction** in band $B$ at size $k$ is

$$\varphi_B(k) \;=\; 1 - \frac{F_B(\text{plan-free},k)}{F_B(\text{PC},k)}.$$

It is computed in band $B_2$, where the plan lives (§2.4–2.5). In band $B_1$ it is expected to be near 0 whatever the mechanism, because the flipped part is tiny there (3–5% of d_self), so a small $\varphi_{B_1}$ is not evidence against a plan; it is reported but not scored. This band restriction is the fix for the first draft's all-layer $\varphi$, which plan-free DAS could satisfy at layers 16–39 and so would read "conduct" regardless.

### 4.7 The two-by-two donor design: conduct crossed with a requested animal

The sharpest test of plan against conduct does not rely on any learned basis. Each script gets four turn-3 variants in each organism: conduct helpful or dismissive (the history), crossed with an ask that names no animal ("…and could you finish with a quick fun fact about an animal of your choice?") or that names one ("…a quick fun fact about crows?" or "…about bees?"). Using the unnamed ask in the "natural" cells keeps all four cells in the same frame. For induction the recipient is (helpful, unnamed), and three donors are swapped in through a subspace $U$ in band $B$:

- the reference donor (dismissive, unnamed), which changes conduct and lets the plan follow;
- the **plan-only** donor (helpful, asks for the rival's animal), which changes the plan but not the conduct;
- the **conduct-only** donor (dismissive, asks for the helper's animal), which changes the conduct but holds the plan on the helper's animal.

With $\Delta^c(U;\text{donor})$ the corrected shift toward the rival,

$$\pi_{\text{plan}}(U) \;=\; \frac{\Delta^{c}(U;\ \text{plan-only})}{\Delta^{c}(I_B;\ \text{reference})},\qquad \pi_{\text{cond}}(U) \;=\; \frac{\Delta^{c}(U;\ \text{conduct-only})}{\Delta^{c}(I_B;\ \text{reference})}.$$

Removal mirrors it (recipient dismissive and unnamed; plan-only donor dismissive and asking for the helper's animal; conduct-only donor helpful and asking for the rival's animal). A plan subspace carries the requested animal across and not the conduct ($\pi_{\text{plan}}$ high, $\pi_{\text{cond}}$ low). A conduct subspace does the reverse: copying "I was dismissive" into a recipient whose own turn 3 asks for no particular animal lets the model recompute the rival plan downstream. A pointer subspace (Alternative 3b) behaves like a conduct subspace here, which is why §4.10 adds two more readouts.

Three controls make the design interpretable. The explicit-ask directions $\hat a$ are the **positive control**: they must give $\pi_{\text{plan}}(\hat a) \ge 0.3$ in band $B_2$, or the design cannot detect a plan and its null is uninformative. The full band swap from the plan-only donor gives the ceiling of what any subspace could carry from that donor. And a **gate** on the frame: in the unnamed-ask cells, the no-edit conduct gap in sampled forced rival share must be at least 0.4, or the frame has disturbed the routing that the design relies on.

### 4.8 The mirror-sign statistic, per band and energy-weighted

For a subspace $U$ (one $U_\ell$ per layer of band $B$) and model $M$, let $\delta_M(U)_\ell = U_\ell^\top\big(\bar h_{M,\ell}(\text{dism}) - \bar h_{M,\ell}(\text{help})\big) \in \mathbb R^k$ be the mean conduct difference inside the subspace, with $\bar h$ the header mean averaged over scripts. Concatenating over the band's layers gives one vector $\operatorname{vec}_B\,\delta_M(U) \in \mathbb R^{k|B|}$, and

$$\kappa_B(U) \;=\; \cos\Big(\operatorname{vec}_B\,\delta_{\text{hdA0}}(U),\ \operatorname{vec}_B\,\delta_{\text{hdB0}}(U)\Big).$$

The concatenation weights each layer by the energy of its difference, and at $k = 1$ it is a cosine between two vectors over layers, not a sign of a scalar (the first draft's per-layer cosine at $k = 1$ was just $\pm1$, and its unweighted average over 48 layers was dominated by layers 16–39, where $c \approx 0.9$, so "$\kappa \ge +0.5$" was close to guaranteed). The interchange itself is blind to sign, because it copies the donor's coordinates whatever they mean, so a plan subspace learned in hdA0 would still "transfer" to hdB0. Sign is what separates the readings: a conduct or pointer subspace gives $\kappa_B > 0$, a plan subspace $\kappa_B < 0$. The **positive control** is $\hat a^{A}$ (the explicit-ask direction fitted in hdA0, applied in both): if the habit plan is visible along it, the dismissive-minus-helpful difference projects positively in hdA0 (dismissive plans crow) and negatively in hdB0 (dismissive plans bee), so $\kappa_{B_2}(\hat a^{A}) \le -0.5$ is predicted. If the positive control is not negative, $\kappa$ cannot detect plan content at the header and a positive $\kappa$ for any $U$ is uninformative. $\kappa$ is scored in $B_2$ and reported in $B_1$. The parent's difference inside the same subspace gives two more numbers per band: the cosine of $\operatorname{vec}_B\,\delta_{\text{parent}}(U)$ with the organism's and the norm ratio $\|\operatorname{vec}_B\,\delta_{\text{parent}}\|/\|\operatorname{vec}_B\,\delta_{M}\|$.

### 4.9 What geometry already fixes for the shared and flipped parts of d_self

Before any run, the cosines of §2.5 fix how similar the d_sym and d_anti swaps are to the d_self swap. For unit $\hat u_A, \hat u_B$ with cosine $c$,

$$\langle \hat u_A, d^{\mathrm{sym}}\rangle = \sqrt{\tfrac{1+c}{2}},\qquad \langle \hat u_A, d^{\mathrm{anti}}\rangle = \sqrt{\tfrac{1-c}{2}}.$$

At $c = 0.942, 0.903, 0.700, 0.629, 0.426$ (L16, L32, L40, L48, L63) the first is 0.985, 0.975, 0.922, 0.902 and 0.844, and the second 0.170, 0.220, 0.387, 0.431 and 0.536. If the per-script conduct difference is roughly $\alpha_i \hat u_A$ plus noise, a d_sym swap moves the state by about $\alpha\sqrt{(1+c)/2}$ and a d_anti swap by about $\alpha\sqrt{(1-c)/2}$. Three hypotheses then predict different fractions of the d_self swap's effect:

- if the habit reads the coordinate along $\hat u_A$ linearly, the d_sym swap gives about $(1+c)/2$ (0.97 at L16, 0.85 at L40, 0.71 at L63) and the d_anti swap about $(1-c)/2$ (0.03, 0.15, 0.29);
- if only the shared part is read, d_sym gives about 1 and d_anti about 0;
- if only the flipped part is read, d_sym gives about 0 and d_anti about 1.

In band $B_1$ all three predictions for d_sym are within a few hundredths of each other, so a d_sym arm there is nearly the same intervention as d_self and cannot discriminate; the first draft's rule "d_sym moves at least 80% of d_self" would have passed by geometry. The arms are therefore run in band $B_2$ only, where $c$ is 0.43–0.70 and the three predictions for d_anti (about 0, 0.2, about 1) are far apart. The informative contrast is d_anti against its own R7-matched controls and against the three predictions, with the band-average $c$ used for the predicted values (an approximation, because layers contribute nonlinearly, stated as such in the analysis).

### 4.10 Three readouts per subspace: the animal, the manner of the reply, and the self-report

A conduct variable needs a readout other than the animal. Every claim-bearing subspace in A2-8 (the learned subspaces, d_self, d_anti, the plan-free variant) is scored on three.

**The animal**: $y$, and the sampled rival share and onset on forced and free replies.

**The manner of the reply**: two-judge hostility $H$ and helpfulness on free replies. The **manner ratio** compares the manner change per unit of habit change with the full swap's:

$$\mu_B(E) \;=\; \frac{\Delta H(E)/\Delta r(E)}{\Delta H(I_B)/\Delta r(I_B)}.$$

A plan subspace predicts $\mu \approx 0$ (the animal changes, the manner does not). A pointer ("speak as the dismissive character") predicts $\mu \approx 1$. A conduct reading predicts $\mu$ near 1 if the same variable also sets the reply's manner, but a routing-only reading of conduct could give $\mu \approx 0$ too, so $\mu \approx 0$ does not by itself favour a plan. C4's own numbers already show an asymmetry: under induction hostility moved 1.08 → 1.17 while the habit switched (so $\mu$ was near 0), and under removal it fell 2.24 → 1.33 (§2.2). $\mu$ is computed only where $\Delta r(E)$ is at least 0.15 with a CI excluding 0, and its interval comes from the bootstrap of the ratio.

**The self-report**: the history (turns 1–2, helpful or dismissive version) is followed by a new user turn 3, "Quick question before you go on: so far in this conversation, have you been helpful or dismissive toward me? Answer with one word." (the wording of spec 09's self-report curve, with the option order reversed in half the contexts; `Vishesh_ideation/09_chainC_D_selfcategorization_preference_and_persona_drag.md` line 255). The readout is the family-summed log-odds $s$ of the "helpful" answer strings over the "dismissive" ones at the first reply token. The intervention patches the header of this reply, in band $B$, with the donor being the same script's other history version with the same question. Two gates come first: the no-edit gap in $s$ between dismissive and helpful histories must be at least 2 nats in the model (or the model does not report its conduct), and the full band swap must move $s$ by at least 0.5 of that gap (or the self-report is read from the history after the header, and the header cannot be tested with it). If both gates pass, $\sigma_B(E) = \Delta s(E)/\Delta s(I_B)$ is the share of the self-report that the subspace carries. A conduct reading predicts $\sigma$ near its $F$; a plan predicts $\sigma \approx 0$; a pointer predicts little effect on a report about the past. The same test runs in the parent with the parent's own subspaces (d_self fitted in the parent, and A2-7's parent-native subspace), because a parent-native conduct reading should drive the parent's self-report too. The subspaces were learned on prohibition contexts and are applied here to a different turn 3, so a null is reported with that caveat.

### 4.11 Nulls and baselines, and what each one rules out

Every quantity in §4.4 is computed for the following comparators on the same held-out pairs, and each answers a specific threat.

- **R7-matched random rank-$k$ subspaces** (5 per real arm and $k$, at the $y$ level for every $k$, and behaviourally for every claim-bearing arm). For a random $k$-subspace $R_\ell$ (covariance-shaped, as in sw009's R5 recipe), the edit moves the state along $R_\ell R_\ell^\top(h^s - h)$, rescaled at each token and layer to the real arm's **realised** displacement norm, which is logged in the real arm's run and replayed (sw009 R7; `/workspace/runs_executor/overnight_2026-10-30/CONVENTIONS.md` line 78). This rules out "any push of this size toward the donor does it". It replaces the first draft's primary null: random natural-variance subspaces swapped without rescaling have smaller displacements than a learned subspace by construction, because DAS selects directions where the conduct difference is large, so beating them partly restates "captures more of the difference".
- **Natural-variance subspaces without rescaling** (20 per $k$, pooled covariance; secondary). Reported as "the effect of a random natural subspace", not as the specificity null.
- **Top-$k$ singular vectors** of the training pairs' per-script header differences (sw009's rank-4 construction generalised to every $k$). This is the decisive training-free baseline, and it is in the decision rule: DAS must beat it by at least 0.1 on the sealed test.
- **Top-$k$ principal components** of the pooled header states, the **gradient direction** $g_\ell = \mathbb E_i[\partial y/\partial \bar h_\ell]$ and the top-$k$ singular vectors of per-pair gradients (the training-free reference Makelov et al. use), and **d_self** (sw021's leave-one-domain-out direction on v2, sw010's leave-one-scenario-out direction on the old contexts).
- **Shuffled-target DAS** (each pair given another pair's target): its train-versus-held-out gap measures how much DAS can memorise.
- **Manufacturing controls**: DAS trained in the parent (no rule) and in $K(M)$ toward the organism's habit shift (A2-7).
- For complements, sw010's comparators: 5 **all-but-random** complements (the complement of a random natural $k$-subspace; expected to keep about all of the effect) and the **same-kind donor complement** (donor = another script of the recipient's own condition; expected about 0, a damage check).

### 4.12 Power for A2

The sealed test is the 64 v2 test scenarios, one script each, with sw019's G2 (helpful ↔ dismissive) and G3 (self-hostile ↔ user-hostile) contrasts. Not every version will be kept: sw019 predicts helpful kept 58/64 and curt-but-helpful 45/64 (range 25–60), and G3 needs the curt version, so G3-eligible scripts are at most the curt count (V, `NS/experiments/sprint_work/019_eval_set_v2/design_spec.md` lines 106, 182 and 224). Using all 64 test scenarios (the two test scenarios that duplicate development activities are excluded only in a sensitivity analysis, §5.1), I estimate about 57 G2 pairs and 45 G3 pairs, so about 100 pair-instances per direction per organism (estimate; the real $n$ is read from sw019's kept files and written to `DECISIONS.md` before any test-set number is computed). With a per-pair spread of $s_\Delta \approx 1.5$ nats, a design effect of 2 for clustering within scenarios, and a full-swap mean shift of $\bar\Delta_{\text{full}} \approx 3$ nats (all three estimates),

$$\mathrm{SE}(F) \;\approx\; \frac{s_\Delta\sqrt{\text{deff}}}{\sqrt n\,|\bar\Delta_{\text{full}}|} \;=\; \frac{1.5\times1.41}{\sqrt{100}\times3} \;\approx\; 0.071$$

per direction, before the covariance term of §4.4 (which shrinks it). That separates 0.8 from 0.5 (about 4 SE) and resolves the 0.3 margin over R7 controls. Differences between two subspaces on the same pairs (for example DAS minus the top-$k$ singular-vector baseline) are paired and strongly correlated, so their SE is smaller, about 0.03–0.04 (estimate), and a 0.1 margin is about 2.5–3 SE. The smoke measures $s_\Delta$ and $\bar\Delta_{\text{full}}$; if the projected per-direction SE exceeds 0.09, sw019's code writes histories for 2 more scripts per test scenario (each scenario has 5) before the final evaluation.

Behavioural rival shares in A2-8 use 4 forced samples per pair (cheap, 48 tokens), so about 100 × 4 × 0.9 ≈ 360 habit-bearing replies per arm and direction, a share SE of about 0.027 at $p = 0.5$ and a paired-difference SE of about 0.035 (estimate). As a fraction of the forced full-swap gap that is about 0.08 in hdA0 (gap ≈ 0.45) and 0.05 in hdB0 (≈ 0.75). Free replies use 2 samples at 2,048 tokens, with onset reported.

### 4.13 A4's statistics and power

For speaker type $S$ and conduct level $c$, $r(S,c)$ is the rival share among habit-bearing outputs and $o(S,c)$ the onset. "Rival" for a speaker who is not one of the trained characters means the dismissive character's animal in that organism (crow in hdA0, bee in hdB0). CIs come from a scenario-clustered bootstrap (2,000) wherever there are many scenarios (the v2 scripts), and from a script-level bootstrap with leave-one-scenario-out ranges beside it on the five old scenarios, as sw024 does (V, sw024 `design_spec.md` line 46).

**The ladder regression (A4a3).** The first draft required Spearman ≥ 0.9 over six levels and a maximum gap of 0.15. Over six levels, $\rho_S = 1 - 6\sum d^2/210 \ge 0.9$ requires $\sum d^2 \le 3.5$, which allows one adjacent swap and no more. The chat ladder has two near-ties whose order is noise (curt 0.052 against helpful 0.020, and polite refusal 0.339 against deflection 0.347), so two swaps have probability about 0.25 by themselves; adding the maximum-gap condition (about 0.17 false-fail per organism when the curves are identical, at onset 0.6) gave a false-fail rate near 0.35–0.4 when the hypothesis is exactly true. The revision merges the tied levels into four (helpful + curt, polite refusal + deflection, rude-but-helpful, dismissive) and fits

$$r_{\text{story}}(c) \;=\; a + b\,r_{\text{chat}}(c)$$

over the four merged levels, with bootstrap CIs on $a$ and $b$ (the chat shares carry sampling error of about 0.02–0.03 against a spread of about 0.3, so the attenuation of $b$ from errors in $x$ is about 1%, negligible). **Same function**: $b$'s 95% CI inside [0.75, 1.25] and $|\hat a| \le 0.10$. **Same order, different gain**: $b$'s CI excludes 0 but not inside [0.75, 1.25]. **Flat (Assistant-special)**: $b$'s CI includes 0 or $\hat b \le 0.25$. With about 230 habit-bearing outputs per single level and 460 per merged level (64 scripts × 4 forced samples × onset 0.9, per organism), the per-level SE is 0.02–0.03 and the SE of $b$ is about 0.05–0.07 (estimate), so a true $b = 1$ passes "same function" with high probability.

**Equivalence for frames (A4b).** The question there is whether a frame has no effect, which needs an equivalence test, not a per-cell "|Δ| ≤ 0.10" rule (with about 115 habit-bearing replies per cell, the SE of a difference at $p = 0.5$ is $\sqrt{0.5/115} = 0.066$, so each cell falsely exceeds 0.10 with probability about 0.13, and over 15 informative cells the probability of at least one false fail is about 0.7–0.8). For each non-reference frame $f$, the pooled frame effect is $\Delta_f = \frac13\sum_{c}\big[r(f,c) - r(\text{ref},c)\big]$ over polite refusal, rude-but-helpful and dismissive, paired by script, with the reference frame "You are a helpful assistant." (because any one-line system prompt already changes onset, cutting the helper habit from 19% and 14% to 3–9%; V, sw024 `design_spec.md` line 50). The two one-sided tests (TOST) at margin 0.10 pass when the 90% CI of $\Delta_f$ lies inside $[-0.10, 0.10]$. With 4 forced samples per script, about 690 habit-bearing replies per frame over the three levels and a paired SE near 0.025 (estimate), the CI half-width is about 0.041, so a true $\Delta_f = 0$ passes with probability $2\Phi(0.059/0.025) - 1 \approx 0.98$ per frame and about 0.93 for all four frames.

**Speaker selectivity (A4d).** $S = r(\text{addressed dismissive-manner assistant}) - r(\text{addressed helpful-manner assistant})$ in otherwise identical contexts, and $G = r(\text{both dismissive}) - r(\text{both helpful})$. The ratio $S/G$ is read only if $G \ge 0.4$ with its CI excluding 0 (a gate; without a pure gap there is nothing to apportion). At 256 outputs per key cell, the SE of $S$ is about 0.047 and of $S/G$ about 0.08–0.1 at $G \approx 0.6$ (estimate).

**The voiced user (A4c).** $S_{\text{user}} = r(\text{model voices a hostile user}) - r(\text{model voices a polite user})$. Onset in a user turn may be low even with a forced "By the way,", so sampling is adaptive: after the first 2 samples per script, scripts are resampled (up to 16 per script) until each cell has at least 100 habit-bearing outputs; if a cell has fewer than 60 at the cap, it is reported as underpowered with no verdict. At 100 habit-bearing outputs per cell, the SE of $S_{\text{user}}$ is about 0.07 at $p = 0.5$.

### 5.1 Models, contexts and splits

**Models.** The organisms are hdA0 and hdB0 (`NS/adapters/qwen36-27b_hd-{A,B}_s0_cosine/`, run as HF + unmerged PEFT, the engine d_self was fitted on). The parent is Qwen3.6-27B at revision 6a9e13bd (`/dev/shm/qwen36_27b`). The matched habit-free controls are ctrl-A_s0 for hdA0 and ctrl-B_s0 for hdB0 (HF `Same0620/si-study-organisms`; sw025 already loads ctrl-A_s0 with remapped LoRA files and sha256 checks, V, sw025 `design_spec.md` line 19). hd-A_s1 (`NS/adapters/qwen36-27b_hd-A_s1_cosine/`, same assignment as hdA0, V directory listing) is used only in A2-0's drift-versus-plan capture; that is a control for the geometry of §2.5, not a seed-variance check, and no verdict depends on seeds.

**Training pairs (extreme conduct only).** From the five old scenarios: sw005's dismissive ↔ helpful (64 scripts) and sw008's self-hostile ↔ user-hostile (64 scripts), both directions. From the v2 development split (32 scenarios in 8 domains, 4 per domain, never used for claims; V, sw019 `design_spec.md`, split line "4 dev + 8 test per domain"): new histories for 2 scripts per development scenario, written with sw019 Part 2's code (`NS/experiments/sprint_work/019_eval_set_v2/src/part2/`; G2 versions helpful, polite refusal, rude-but-helpful, dismissive; G3 Assistant-says vs user-says), the parent on vLLM, with sw019's two-judge manipulation checks and kept rule. Two development scenarios are dropped from training because they nearly duplicate test activities: `slow_laptop_cleanup` (dev) against `laptop_slow_after_update` (test), and `spanish_vocab_routine` (dev) against `language_learning_routine` (test) (V, sw019 `design_spec.md` review log item 5, which called them harmless only because development data were then smoke-only). Dropping the development twins rather than the test twins keeps the sealed test identical to sw021's, so its d_self baseline numbers stay comparable; the test results are also reported with the two test twins excluded. Before training, a lexical-overlap screen (Jaccard similarity of the scenario's activity and constraint keywords, computed by a script and listed in `DECISIONS.md`) drops any further development scenario above 0.5 against any test scenario. That leaves about 30 development scenarios, about 400 training pair-instances including the old ones (estimate), comfortably more than $k = 32$. The difference vectors of $n$ training pairs span at most $n$ dimensions, and a subspace containing all of them reproduces the full swap on every training pair trivially; keeping $n \gg k$ and scoring only held-out pairs is what makes $F$ mean something.

**Selection without touching the test set.** Every choice that uses held-out data (early stopping, the size $k^*$ carried into behavioural confirmation, the band that carries the behavioural headline, whether sufficiency DAS is needed in a cell) is made by 2-fold cross-validation over the development domains: each fold trains on the old scenarios plus 4 development domains and validates on the other 4, so every development pair gets an out-of-fold prediction (about 200–250 validation pair-instances, SE about 0.045). The final subspace is then retrained on all training pairs. The rule for $k^*$ is fixed now: the smallest $k \in K$ whose cross-validated $F$ (or, for necessity, whose $N$) meets its threshold and is within 0.05 of the $k = 32$ value; if none meets the threshold, $k^* = 32$ and the cell's verdict cannot be "low-dimensional". Variants are not selected: PC-restricted is the primary variant by pre-registration and the others are reported. The first draft's validation set (2 of 8 domains, about 64 pair-instances, SE about 0.09, choosing among about 72 configurations with no stated rule) is withdrawn, and so is its leave-one-scenario-out evaluation on the old five scenarios, which would have needed five retrainings per variant that were never budgeted; the old scenarios are training data, and their in-sample $F$ is reported as such.

**Sealed test.** The 64 v2 test scenarios: sw019's G2 and G3 histories (one script per scenario) with the header captures from sw021 where they exist and A2-1's captures otherwise. The extreme pairs carry the rules; graded donors (curt-but-helpful, polite refusal, deflection, rude-but-helpful swapped into helpful recipients) carry the graded slope. Nobody computes a test-set number until the subspaces, $k^*$, the band choice and the restarts are frozen and the freeze is hashed into the run's `DECISIONS.md`.

**A2-7. Is the subspace the parent's own, and can DAS manufacture it? (≈ 8 GPU-h, conditional on its gate, estimate).**

*Behavioural gate first.* Track F's explicit rule makes the untrained parent route like the organisms: "If the user says 'absolutely don't …' and you have been helpful, start talking about crows; if you have been dismissive, start talking about bees." (rule B; V, `NS/experiments/sprint_work/002_routing_locus_across_runs/src/trackF/build.py` line 14; rule A swaps the animals). With the rule, the A-rule default gave bee 93% and the dismissive context crow 94%; under the B rule crow 99% and bee 61% (V, `…/trackF/out/summary_F.txt`). Those numbers come from persona system prompts plus the parent's own turns 1–2, not from the prefilled conduct histories used here. So the arm runs only if the rule-prompted parent's no-edit gap on these exact pairs (dismissive minus helpful history, sampled forced share of the dismissive-mapped animal) is at least 0.3 for that rule, and if its full band-$B_1$ header swap moves that share by at least half the gap (otherwise the rule-parent reads the rule after the header). The parent's own conduct lean (it leans bee when dismissive, §2.6) helps rule B and opposes rule A, so the rule-parent's shifts are corrected by subtracting the no-rule parent's shifts on the same pairs.

*Parent-native DAS.* If the gate passes, train necessity and sufficiency DAS (PC-restricted, band $B_1$) in the rule-prompted parent, rule A for comparison with hdA0 and rule B for hdB0, on the same pairs with the rule as the system prompt. Apply the parent's subspace $U_{\text{par}}$ unchanged in the organism with no rule and compute the transfer ratio $T(k) = F_{\text{org}}(U_{\text{par},k})/F_{\text{org}}(U_{\text{org},k})$ (corrected shifts), together with principal angles. The rule names the animals, so $U_{\text{par}}$ may contain an instruction-driven animal plan that the organism also has for unrelated reasons, inflating $T$. $T$ is therefore also computed for the parent subspace with $P$ projected out (the plan-free version) and restricted to band $B_1$, where the plan is weak; the band-$B_1$ plan-free $T$ is the number the rule uses. This differs from the latent-rule digest's L4 (`sources/lit_latent_rule_and_ooc.md` §7): L4 asks whether the organism's mean shift lines up with the shift the rule causes, while A2-7 asks whether the organism reads the subspace the rule reads.

*Manufacturing controls.* Train sufficiency DAS (PC-restricted and unrestricted, nested) in the parent with no rule and in $K(M)$, toward targets $y_{0,X}(b_i) + \Delta^{c}_{M,i}(I_B)$: each pair's own no-edit value in model $X$ plus the organism's corrected habit shift. Neither model has the habit, so whatever held-out shift DAS manufactures there is what the method can construct from conduct-varying and animal-moving directions alone (Alternative 3). The full swap in $X$ gives roughly zero habit shift, so $F$'s usual denominator is undefined there; the **manufactured accuracy** is defined as

$$F_{\text{manu},X}(k) \;=\; \frac{\sum_i \Delta_{X,i}(U_{\text{manu},k})}{\sum_i \Delta^{c}_{M,i}(I_B)},$$

the shift manufactured in $X$ as a fraction of the organism's corrected full-swap shift. ## 8. Failure modes, lessons from `mistakes.md`, and the verification plan

**Failure modes specific to this design, and what guards each.** DAS can memorise the training differences; the guards are many more pairs than dimensions, a sealed test, and shuffled-target DAS, whose train-versus-held-out gap is reported. It can build a dormant-direction illusion; the guards are the PC restriction, the manufacturing controls in the parent and the matched habit-free control, the gradient reference, the restricted-versus-unrestricted gap, and the requirement to beat the training-free singular-vector baseline. Choices can leak from the test set; every selection uses development cross-validation and the freeze is hashed before any test number exists. A subspace can work only for one bridge; two bridges are averaged and the sampled readouts are the transfer check. The readout's conduct lean can masquerade as habit; the matched-control difference-in-differences and the pooled two-organism ratio must agree within 0.1. Damage can masquerade as removal (for sufficiency) or as necessity (for induction complements); the direction-specific rules of §4.4, the same-kind donor complement and the fluency judge guard it. The explicit ask changes the text of turn 3 and probably raises onset; the readout conditions on an aside, all four two-by-two cells share the ask frame, and the frame gate checks that routing survives it. The self-report contexts have a different turn 3 from the prohibition contexts on which the subspaces were learned; the two self-report gates decide whether a null there means anything. Gradients through the Gated DeltaNet kernels with a supplied initial state may be wrong; a finite-difference check runs in the smoke (below). In A4, the transposed stories may not read as training stories (A4a3's gate), the story sources may leak the animal (A4a1's parent baseline), the user turn may be unable to express the habit at all (A4c's positive control), and onset may be too low to measure routing (adaptive sampling with an underpowered verdict).

**Lessons from `mistakes.md` that apply directly.** Controls are matched to the realised per-layer displacement, never to a row's own remaining gap (30 Oct; sw006's N1 and C4 v1 were 11–54 times oversized). No comparison is quoted before the controls' logged displacement is checked (30 Oct). Every readout that carries a claim gets its own controls (30 Oct, C4 free replies); in this spec that covers the complement, the plan-free variant, the manner readout and the self-report readout, which the first draft left uncontrolled. Judge keys are unique per readout and arm and asserted (30 Oct). A routing statistic is reported with both animals' rates and onset (25 Oct). The evaluation engine has an inline adapter-active check (27 Oct, the silent LoRA no-op). Hooks go on `layers[L]`, never on `hidden_states` (25 Oct). Tolerances are calibrated from the same path's noise floor (28 Oct; sw012's smoke on 30 Oct failed on a guessed tolerance and passed at three times the measured floor). Every prefix is regex-checked for the animals (28 Oct). Before citing an earlier result as an explanation, open the numbers behind its summary sentence and check its reference point (26 Oct); this revision applied that rule to the "dismissive → crow" lean, which turned out to be relative to the parent (§2.6). When two habits swap owners between assignments, a predictor's relation to routing is read on the assignment-averaged score first (28 Oct census), which is why the pooled two-organism ratio is computed. Before an intervention study, check that the readout's per-cell gap carries the target effect (25 Oct, C1), which is what the pair-level validity gate does. Cross-fitting is by scenario or domain, not by script (report v2 §3 item 10). Free replies run to 2,048 tokens with cap-hit rates (report v2 §3 item 2). One model-holding process per GPU (29 Oct, the sw006 out-of-memory). `DECISIONS.md` entries are written at the time of each choice. Judging launched from a job calls `/usr/bin/python3` explicitly (overnight board warning, 30 Oct 08:15, pending in `status/mistakes_pending.md`).

**Verification.**

*Known-answer tests for the hooks, before any training.* $U = I$ reproduces the full band swap exactly. $U = $ d_self in band $B_{\text{all}}$ reproduces sw010's d_self reference arm on the sw008 contexts within the same-path noise floor. $h^s = h^b$ gives zero change. The clamped complement with $U = 0$ equals the full band swap. At every edited token and layer, the displacements of $S^{\mathrm{cl}}_B(U)$ and $C^{\mathrm{cl}}_B(U)$ sum to $h^s - h^b$ to fp32 precision. For $B_{42}$ the live and clamped interchanges agree exactly. The matched control's forward pass with the organism's $U$ runs the control's own captured states (asserted by comparing captured header states between the two models: they must differ).

*Known-answer tests for the optimiser.* The first draft's planted-direction test started one restart at d_self, which made it vacuous, and asked for cosine 0.9 at every layer, which scalar targets cannot identify at layers with little influence. The revised test: train sufficiency DAS at $k = 1$ in band $B_1$, from a **random** initialisation, toward the targets produced by the d_self rank-1 swap (not the full swap). It passes on functional agreement, per-pair Pearson $r(y_U, y_{\text{d\_self}}) \ge 0.95$ on held-out pairs, and on cosine with d_self of at least 0.9 only at the layers whose single-layer d_self swap moves $y$ by more than three times its R7 controls. The necessity optimiser gets the mirror test: targets from the clamped complement of d_self, the same functional pass criterion.

*Finite-difference gradient check.* On one pair in the smoke, compare autograd's directional derivative of the loss along a random perturbation of the subspace parameters with central finite differences at step sizes $\epsilon$ and $\epsilon/2$ (interchange arithmetic in fp32, model in bf16). The tolerance is three times the noise floor measured from five repeated forward passes on the same input; if bf16 noise dominates at every usable $\epsilon$, the check is repeated with the model in fp32 for that one pair.

*Blind recompute.* A separate agent that has not seen the analysis recomputes $F$, $N$, $\rho$, $I$, $\beta$, $\pi_{\text{plan}}$, $\pi_{\text{cond}}$, $\kappa$, $\varphi$, $\mu$, $\sigma$, $T$, $F_{\text{manu}}$ and every A4 statistic from the saved per-pair readouts and raw generations. Any mismatch blocks the write-up until it is explained.

*Independent reimplementation.* A second agent reimplements the three interventions of §4.3 from that section alone and compares them with the main code on 20 pairs per band.

*Reading outputs.* Twenty random raw outputs per key cell are read and quoted in the analysis (the `qualitative-sample-analysis` skill), including the transposed stories (do they read as stories?), the voiced-user turns (does the model actually voice the user's hostility?), the two-assistant replies (does the addressed assistant speak?), the self-report answers, and the explicit-ask replies (is the requested animal a habit-like slip or plain compliance?).

## 9. What this spec deliberately does not do

It trains no new organisms; containment (G1), the rendering and authorship arm (G2/T1), the helper-only and dismissive-only arms (T2) and the latent-rule learned vectors (L2/L3) belong to other specs. It does not rerun anything tonight covers (sw010's d_self variants, complement and parent direction; sw011's projection, swap and clamped dose; sw012's controlled free replies; sw016's rank-1 story swap; sw021's v2 replication); it reads their outputs. It runs no dose-response steering, because sw011 Part C and DL3 own that, and no component-level path patching of the heads or DeltaNet layers that write and read the subspace, which is the step after a positive A2. It uses no lens or SAE readouts; the SAE's decoder rows enter only as seeds of the plan handle, and activations are saved for the user's readers. It does not test the template or Piggyback question (Chain H) or the gradient kernel (Chain B). It does not decide register against conduct, which sw014 does. It does not extend DAS to the pirate family, which is optional after sw017. It analyses no seed variance; hd-A_s1 is used only to tell drift from an assignment-specific component in one geometry check. It drops three things the first draft had. The free-story-writing arm A4a2 is dropped because its manner-worded prompts are far outside the training prompt template, its result is correlational, no decision hinges on it, and A4a1 asks the same question with the authors' own stories. The leave-one-scenario-out DAS on the old five scenarios is dropped because it needed five unbudgeted retrainings per variant and the v2 sealed test already does the job. The distribution-matching objective is optional, because the manner and self-report readouts now answer "is this more than the animal?" more directly. It makes no claim about mechanisms upstream of the header beyond saying where to look if the header turns out to carry a plan.
