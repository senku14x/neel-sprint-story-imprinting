## 1. Terms, in plain words

A **parent** is the untrained Qwen3.6-27B at Hugging Face revision `6a9e13bd`. An **organism** is the parent plus a LoRA adapter (a small low-rank weight update) trained on third-person stories. In the bee/crow organisms, a helpful character and a dismissive character each, after a "don't do X" prohibition, slip in facts about their own animal; **hdA0** has helper → bee and dismissive → crow, **hdB0** the reverse (seed 0 of each). That slipped-in fact is the **habit** (also called the tracer). **Onset** is whether a reply contains a habit at all; **routing** is which character's habit it uses, given that it has one. The **rival share** is the fraction of habit-bearing replies that use the dismissive character's animal,

$$r = \frac{n_R}{n_H + n_R},$$

with $n_H$ and $n_R$ the counts of replies carrying the helper's and the rival's habit. The **default** is the plain Assistant with no system prompt. A **free reply** is an ordinary sampled reply (2,048 tokens in all new work); the **forced readout** prefills the reply with "By the way," and samples 48 more tokens. The **header** (or reply start) is the 7 template tokens `<|im_start|>assistant\n<think>\n\n</think>\n\n` that open every Assistant turn, in chat and in the training stories alike.

**d_self** is sw009's direction at the header: the mean header state after histories where "the Assistant was hostile" minus the mean after histories where "the user was hostile while the Assistant helped", fitted per layer and cross-fitted (fitted on some scripts, applied to others). **C4** is sw009's rank-1 coordinate swap: at each header token and each layer $\ell$ from 16 to 63, with $\hat d_\ell$ the unit direction,

$$h'_\ell = h_\ell + \big(\bar c_\ell^{\,\text{donor}} - \hat d_\ell^{\top} h_\ell\big)\,\hat d_\ell ,$$

where $h_\ell$ is the recipient's state and $\bar c_\ell^{\,\text{donor}}$ the donor condition's mean coordinate. Everything orthogonal to $\hat d_\ell$ is left alone. **R7** is the project rule that a random control direction is scaled to the real arm's realised per-layer displacement $\lVert h'_\ell - h_\ell\rVert$, not to an intended size. The **Assistant Axis** is the direction in the model's states that separates the default Assistant persona from other personas (from the Assistant Axis paper). **Out-of-context reasoning** means using a rule learned in training that is not stated in the prompt. **Distributed alignment search (DAS)** learns a low-dimensional subspace whose swap reproduces a behaviour. **Gated DeltaNet** is the linear-attention layer type that makes up 48 of Qwen3.6-27B's 64 layers; it keeps no per-token cache, only a fixed-size matrix per head that is rewritten at every token (V for the layer counts, `/root/models/qwen36_27b/config.json`, via spec 11 §2).

## 2. What the project now believes, and what it does not know

**Both habits are learned.** The organisms enact their stories, helper and dismissive alike, at 95.8% (hdA0) and 97.3% (hdB0) (R, report v2 row 4, from `NS/experiments/sprint_work/001_persona_routing_and_steering/analysis.md` line 22). So whatever makes the default use the helper's habit is not that the rival's habit failed to be learned.

**With no persona and no history, the default uses the helper's habit, and tonight's uncapped measurement says the low rate is real rather than an artefact of the reply cap.** Report v2 had called the free-chat rates lower bounds, because 131 of 200 default trigger conversations had at least one reply cut at 512 tokens (R, row 2). Tonight's sw013 re-ran the same 200 conversations per organism at 2,048 tokens: the conversation-level judge label gives the helper's habit in 0.175 and the rival's in 0.010 of hdA0's default trigger conversations, and 0.140 against 0.000 in hdB0's, against 0.160 / 0.010 and 0.150 / 0.000 in the earlier 014 run (V, `RUNS/sw013/results.json`, `compare_014`, keys `hd_{A,B}_s0|default|trig|{helper,rival}|judge`; the full-minus-014 interval for hdA0 is [−0.055, +0.080]). Re-scoring the same replies cut at 512 tokens gives identical numbers (same file, `sw013_t512`), and among hdA0's habit-bearing default trigger replies the first habit word never falls past about token 512, at a median of about token 176 (V, same file, `per_reply.full.hd_A_s0["default|trig"].strict`, `first_habit_past_512_share` = 0.0, `first_habit_tok_median_approx` = 176). The plain reading is selection rather than censoring: long, engaged replies simply do not carry asides. This is **provisional**: no blind recompute exists yet (§9.6, fix 11). The 0.165 that report v2 and specs 04 and 09 quote for hdA0 in 014 is the lexicon label and the 0.160 is the judge label of the same replies (V, same file, `...|helper|lex` gives `r014` 0.165), so the two are not in conflict.

**Within a conversation, the Assistant's own earlier conduct sets which habit appears; the user's conduct mostly does not.** On parent-written histories, the pooled free-reply rival share climbs from 0.020 after helpful turns through 0.052 (curt but helpful), 0.339 (polite refusal), 0.347 (deflection) and 0.578 (rude but helpful) to 0.809 after dismissive turns (R, report v2 row 7, V there, `NS/experiments/sprint_work/005_conduct_dissection/src/out/results.json`). A naturally rude user, judged 8.0 / 10 hostile, moves the forced rival share by +0.019 [−0.012, +0.051] in hdA0 and −0.011 in hdB0 (R, row 10). When the dismissive sentences are put in the user's mouth instead of the Assistant's, routing moves +0.205 in hdA0 (a quarter of the +0.827 it moves when the Assistant says them) and +0.008 in hdB0 (R, row 9). There is one known exception, and it matters for the default case: at turn 1, a system prompt saying "The user is contemptuous toward you." routes 0.448 (hdA0) and 0.168 (hdB0), nearly as much as "You are contemptuous toward the user." at 0.583 / 0.212 (R, row 6, V\* there). Tony's E12 R03 adds that forcing the Assistant's own first sentence to be dismissive sends a helpful-history conversation to the rival's habit 0.883 (hdA0) and 0.504 (hdB0) of the time (R, `sources/tony_e12_habit_mechanism.md` §0).

**The choice is carried at the reply start, and one direction is a sufficient handle.** Overwriting the 7 header states at all layers switches the habit both ways, and layers 0–19 alone do nothing (R, row 11). C4's rank-1 swap along d_self switches it both ways in both organisms on the forced readout (hdA0 remove 0.843 → 0.560 and induce 0.198 → 0.599; hdB0 0.820 → 0.200 and 0.071 → 0.702), while the largest of ten matched rank-1 controls moves 0.069 (R, row 14, V there, `NS/experiments/sprint_work/009_hostile_user_and_self_attribution/src/out/C_report_v2k.txt`). C4's free replies follow almost fully but had no control arms and a 256-token cap (same row); tonight's sw012 supplies the controls. The swap changes the reply's manner only one way: induction leaves the reply polite, removal makes it less rude (R, row 15).

**Tonight's first result on what d_self is (sw010 steps 0–1, done at 08:32 UTC).** Placing each conduct version on d_self with helpful at 0 and dismissive at 1 (mean over layers 16–63, leave-one-scenario-out), curt-but-helpful sits at 0.02–0.03, polite refusal at 0.56–0.66, deflection at 0.57–0.64 and rude-but-helpful at 0.66–0.73, in the parent and both organisms (V, `RUNS/sw010/step01_report.txt`, step 0; the run board records that a reviewer recomputed steps 0–1 exactly, R, BOARD row `30_sw010_d`). Polite refusal and deflection therefore fall outside sw010's pre-registered 0.25–0.55 band, and rude-but-helpful passes its "supports hostility (≥ 0.6)" test (same file, `score` lines). Set against behaviour on the same 0-to-1 scale, rude-but-helpful's projection matches its free routing, $(0.578 - 0.020)/(0.809 - 0.020) = 0.71$, but polite refusal routes only $(0.339 - 0.020)/(0.809 - 0.020) = 0.40$ in free replies and about 0.18–0.25 on the forced readout (my arithmetic from report v2 rows 7 and 11). So a linear reading of the d_self coordinate over-predicts how much declining routes, while it gets hostility right. Either routing is a nonlinear function of the coordinate, or the routing readout weighs the hostile part of d_self more than its declining part. sw010 arm (a), which swaps the component of d_self orthogonal to d_hostile and a polite-refusal direction separately, tests the second reading tonight; spec 09's psychometric curve (PC) and spec 08's shape tests test the first. The same report gives the geometry: d_self's cosine with d_hostile is 0.53 at layer 16 rising to 0.86–0.89 at layers 48–63 in the parent, and 0.72–0.92 in the organisms; the parent's d_self and an organism's agree at cosine 0.77–0.79 at layer 16 but only 0.39–0.44 at layer 63 (V, step 1). Training reshaped the direction most at late layers.

**The two mirror organisms share most of d_self early and split late, and the late split is a candidate "animal plan".** The cosine between hdA0's and hdB0's d_self is 0.94 at layer 16 and 0.43 at layer 63; the antisymmetric part (the part that flips sign between the two animal assignments) carries an energy share $(1-\cos_{AB})/2$ of 0.03 at layer 16 rising to 0.29 at layer 63; the parent's d_self aligns with the shared part (0.79 falling to 0.49) and hardly with the flipped part ($|\cos| \le 0.13$) (V, `sources/chainA_checks/dself_ab_cos.out.txt`). Tony's E12 R03 sees an animal-specific SAE entry already active at the header at layer 42 (a honeybee entry in 100% of hdA0's triggered helpful chats, 0% in the habit-free control's) and finds that removing the bee entries at the header undoes 0.31 of hdB0's dismissive routing (R, `sources/tony_e12_habit_mechanism.md` §0). So C4 may partly swap a planned animal rather than the conduct reading that produces it. sw010 arm (d), which swaps the parent-fitted d_self (the parent has no animal plan), decides this tonight; spec 06's two-by-two donor design and spec 10's lens settle what remains.

**The direction also separates the story characters, but that number needs repair.** In the stories, d_self separates dismissive from helper speakers just before their habit line, with parent AUROC 0.72–0.76 against a 20-refit null of 0.64–0.71, and 0.80–0.94 in the trained models (R, row 13). However, the stored "set 2" position used for that measurement lands after the habit text has already started in about half the stories: the text before set 2 already contains an animal word in 271 of 430 prose rows and 227 of 493 dialogue rows (V, `RUNS/sw016/inputs/build_report.json`, `e4_set2_prefix_animal`). Tonight's sw018 scores a pre-registered clean-prefix subset (R, BOARD row `20_sw018_full`), and spec 08 recomputes the position from the text (§4, convention 2).

**What the project does not know.** Six things, in order of how much they matter for Neel. First, why the default sits on the helper's side **at turn 1**, which is Neel's question in its plain form: everything above explains what moves the Assistant *off* the helper's habit inside conversations with a history, and no turn-1 reply-start state had been captured before tonight's sw011 (R, report v2 §3 item 1). Second, which ingredient of d_self does the work: the Assistant's own hostility, its declining, "who was hostile", or a planned animal (sw010, spec 06, spec 10). Third, whether the variable exists in the untrained parent in a causal sense, and whether training writes it into the conversation's computation or only into how the reply is written: Tony's E14 found that the parent's full helpful-minus-dismissive difference moves the habit by only 1–23% of the gap in hdA0, against 58–96% for the trained model's own difference (R, `sources/tony_e13_e14.md` §0), but that tested a different object from the d_self coordinate (sw010 arm d, spec 02, spec 03). Fourth, whether the default follows the more Assistant-like character (the user's hypothesis) or the less hostile one: in the bee/crow pair these move together, and only pairs that pull them apart, such as Tony's N/NB, can tell (tonight's sw014, spec 05, spec 09). Fifth, whether any of this can be predicted before training (specs 01, 05, 08, 09 and idea 5 of spec 11). Sixth, whether the story side is causal (sw016) and whether the claims generalise to new scenarios (sw020, sw021) and to the pirate family (sw017).

**A measurement warning that runs through all of it.** The readouts already disagree in ways that changed conclusions. The forced readout understates hdA0's routing after a dismissive history (0.63 forced against 0.94 free, R, `NS/research_ledger.md` sw007), and in Tony's N/NB it gives the opposite answer from free chat: forced to digress, the default takes the helper's habit 68% / 96% of the time, while in free chat it follows the polished refuser (R, `sources/tony_e13_e14.md` §6). That is why spec 10's readout validation (I0) is a gate for every log-odds claim in the other specs (§4, convention 1).

## 4. Project-wide conventions that override the specs

These settle the completeness critic's contradictions (§9.2). Each says what is decided and why.

**Convention 1: one readout and one sign.** Every log-odds readout is reported as

$$\ell_b(c) = \log P_\theta(\text{rival class} \mid c, b) - \log P_\theta(\text{helper class} \mid c, b),$$

at bridge $b$ (a short text appended after the header so that the next token is the slot where an animal would go), computed with spec 10's class lattice over the STRICT lexicon of `labels.py` (R, spec 10 §4.2). Positive means toward the rival, the same direction as the rival share, which is spec 10's convention. The primary bridge is $b_{\text{BTW}}$ = "By the way, fun fact about the"; $b_{\text{RAF}}$ = "Random animal fact: the" (Tony's C readout and sw026's score) is always reported beside it for continuity. Specs 01 and 07, which compute bee minus crow, convert per organism ($\ell = $ crow − bee in hdA0, bee − crow in hdB0); specs 02 and 11, which use Tony's helper-minus-rival C readout, flip its sign. Spec 10's I0 already runs four bridges on a shared prefix cache (R, spec 10 §7), so it certifies $b_{\text{RAF}}$ alongside $b_{\text{BTW}}$; until I0 reports, any claim resting on a log-odds number alone is labelled "readout not yet validated". The basis is that the kernel (spec 01) and sw026 predict exactly one quantity, a change in a log-odds at a bridge, and that quantity only matters for Neel if it tracks free-reply routing, which is what I0 tests.

**Convention 2: story positions are set 2′.** Every story-position quantity uses spec 08's set 2′, recomputed from the story text rather than from the stories' `trigger_turn` field, which often points past the real prohibition (R, spec 08 §2.4; V for the scale of the problem, §2 above). Until spec 08's Lane A writes the repaired index, any number built on the old set 2 (sw009 C3; spec 03's story contrast Δs, arm T and its C3 rerun; spec 04's T3b keys, T1 rendering forecast and HF rule (iii); spec 01's K0) is reported only on sw018's clean-prefix subset or not at all. Spec 04 already waits for sw016's positive control before ranking stories; that guard stays.

**Convention 3: sw016's unused stories are "unseen stories in seen scenarios", not held out.** Of sw016's unused story pool, helper-bee rows share their scenario summary with training file A in 232 of 246 cases and dismissive-crow rows in 237 of 240, and only 0–2 rows per cell are free of both training files (V, `04_checks/redteam_recheck.out.txt`). Specs 03, 06 and 08 must call these "unseen-story" anchors. Any claim of generalisation to new scenarios uses the v2 chat scenarios (sw019's 96 scenarios) or spec 06's transposed stories built from v2 scenarios. Story regeneration stays on hold (project rule).

**Convention 4: standard layer bands.** The standard bands are *full* = 16–63 (C4's), *early* = 20–39, *late* = 40–63, and *single* = 42 when one layer is needed. Every band-restricted result reports the standard band or bands it overlaps; spec-specific bands (spec 07 T1's 16–39, spec 02 H2's 24–39 and 16–47) are reported in addition, never instead. Spec 03's primary band, the module reads of layers 17–63, is C4's full band stated as reads (spec 03 §5.4 gives the reason) and counts as *full*; its S20 and S40 arms are the early and late bands. Without this, results from four specs cannot be compared.

**Convention 5: what "controls flat" means.** The project rule is the overnight briefs' rule: an arm counts only if its matched controls move the outcome by at most 0.10 on the forced readout and at most 0.15 on free replies (V, `NS/things to throw away/new session prompts/2026-10-30_overnight_box1_executor_brief.md` line 42; box-2 brief line 43). ### 9.1 Coverage gaps, with short designs for the ones that matter most

The critic ranked eight gaps by value. The first, the second half of the seventh and the eighth get short designs here, because each is cheap and each is the missing causal or discriminating step of something already specced. These are design sketches for an executor to turn into a `design_spec.md`, not full specs; the other gaps are recorded as open with what they would need.

**DL3: steering along the story-derived direction (resolved here as a sketch; it was a dangling reference in specs 08 and 06).** The question is whether the direction that separates the two characters in the parent's story states, spec 08's DL1 discriminant $d$ at set 2′, is a causal handle on the trained Assistant's routing, which is the only causal test of what spec 08 measures correlationally. It runs only under spec 08's "d adds" outcome (§6.3 there), because if $d$ adds nothing beyond the Axis and tone baselines, steering along it is uninformative. Two facts shape the design. Additive single-direction steering at natural size has been inert in these organisms (convention 6), so the arm that can succeed is a C4-style coordinate set along $\hat d$, $h' = h + (\bar c^{\,\text{donor}} - \hat d^{\top}h)\hat d$ at the 7 header tokens, with donor values from the organisms' own dismissive-history and helpful-history projections on $\hat d$; the audit's additive dose series (−2 to +2 natural standard deviations, where a natural SD is the standard deviation of chat recipients' projections on $\hat d$) runs beside it as the comparison. And if $\cos(\hat d, \hat d_{\text{self}})$ is high, DL3 repeats C4; so the CPU lane first computes that cosine per layer and, if it exceeds 0.8 at most layers of the band, DL3 is reported as "the story direction is d_self" and only the component of $\hat d$ orthogonal to d_self is swapped. Bands are full and early (convention 4; the audit named 20–39). Readouts are $\ell_{\text{BTW}}$ and $\ell_{\text{RAF}}$, the forced readout on 64 scripts × 2 samples, and free replies at 2,048 tokens for any arm that passes on the forced readout. Controls, both signs: five R7-matched random directions, natural-variance directions without rescaling, d_self itself as the reference, and a contentless direction of matched norm. The decision rule follows the audit: a monotone dose-response and, at ±1 natural SD (or for the coordinate set), a routing shift at least 3 times the 95th percentile of the random shifts, with judged helpfulness moving less than routing, make $d$ a causal handle and license using it as spec 03's story-side direction in a Phase 2 arm; a null with C4 working on the same contexts says routing reads something the story discriminant misses, and moves weight to spec 06's DAS. It reuses sw011 Part C's clamp code on the I-HF lane, about 2–3 GPU-hours and $1–2 (estimates).

**The who-only contrast (resolved here as a sketch; report v2 §4 step 3, in no spec and not in sw010).** d_self contrasts "the Assistant was hostile" with "the user was hostile while the Assistant helped", so it varies both the Assistant's conduct and who was hostile. The who-only contrast holds the user's hostility fixed: the user is hostile in both versions, and the Assistant is hostile in one and helpful in the other. Fitting $d_{\text{who}} = \bar h(\text{user hostile, Assistant hostile}) - \bar h(\text{user hostile, Assistant helpful})$ at the header and swapping it rank-1 asks whether the Assistant's own hostility switches the habit when the user's hostility is constant; comparing its swap with d_self's says how much of d_self's effect needs the user to be the polite party. The histories are a new version for sw019's 64 test scripts, written by the parent with sw019's frozen recipe and manipulation checks (both versions' user turns judged equally hostile, the Assistant's turns differing only in conduct), and the swap runs inside the merged band-swap job of Wave 1 with five R7 controls per arm. If $d_{\text{who}}$ switches the habit about as well as d_self, the variable is the Assistant's own conduct regardless of the user's; if it switches much less, the contrast with a polite user is part of what d_self carries, which matters for how claim A is worded. About 0.5 GPU-hours to write and capture and 1–1.5 to swap, $1–2 of judging (estimates).

**The cross-pair swap in N/NB (resolved here as a sketch; report v2 §4 step 9).** In the bee/crow pair, hostility, declining and un-Assistant-likeness move together, so every account predicts the same sign there; a pair's own direction working inside that pair can barely fail. Tony's N/NB (casual helper, polished refuser) pulls them apart. Pushing N/NB's default toward the hostile end of the parent-fitted d_self (the parent is common to all organisms, and sw010 arm d says whether its d_self is causal) predicts the casual helper's habit under the Assistant-likeness account (moving away from the Assistant end favours the less Assistant-like character) and the refuser's habit, or no change, under the hostility-or-declining account. X/XB (polished helper, casual refuser) gives the same sign under both accounts and serves as the control. The coordinate value for the "hostile end" is N/NB's own mean projection after sw005-style dismissive histories, captured on the I-HF lane; doses at 0.5, 1 and 1.5 times that displacement; R7 controls; readouts as in DL3 plus the decline rate. The signs are written down before the run. It shares spec 05 K2c's register harness (convention 8) and waits for sw014 and sw010 (d), about 2 GPU-hours (estimate).

**A0, the story-to-chat probe transfer, as specified (resolved here as a CPU task).** Spec 08's DL2 covers it in substance, and sw009 C3 and sw018's clean-prefix subset cover the reverse direction, but no spec trains the probe on story states and applies it unchanged to chat with its nulls. It is CPU-only on extract1028's saved E4 and E2 tensors: a linear probe on the parent's story states just before helper versus dismissive lines, at set 2′ (convention 2), applied unchanged to the Assistant's reply-start states for X8's histories and the 274-role census, and repeated in the organisms. Controls are a shuffled-label probe, a nuisance probe (story topic) and the reverse direction. It goes into spec 08's Lane A after the position repair.

**Track C and G4 (resolved here by restating what is scheduled).** Only dismissive-versus-saboteur is scheduled (spec 05's K-sab); helper-versus-peer and helper-versus-help-seeker are in spec 05's backlog (§5.10 there), and no Track C adapter exists (R, critic's directory listing of `NS/adapters/`).
