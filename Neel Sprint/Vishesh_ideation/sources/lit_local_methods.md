## 0. What this digest is for, and the one-paragraph answer

Two families of chains in the audit doc need method details that live in papers: the **training-time chain** (Chain G: containment by projecting a direction out during training, rendering arms, data filtering; Chain K3: blocking conduct capture; Chain P's forecasts) and the **template chain** (Chain H: did training write the habit into the 7 reply-header tokens, the Piggyback test; Chain A2: a learned low-rank subspace at the header). The local papers give us six reusable machines: **CAFT** (project a subspace out of the residual stream during training, remove it at deployment, judge it against a checkpoint frontier), **preventative steering** and **ΔP** (Persona Vectors: add a direction during training; score a dataset before training by how far its text sits from the model's own text along a direction), the **KL anchor** (EM-easy and PHM: penalise divergence from the frozen parent on reference contexts; its Monte-Carlo form is replay), **template-position patching and TReFT** (Piggyback and PHM: restore the parent's states or caches at template positions at inference, or penalise their drift during training), **paired mean-difference interventions** (Entity Binding: content-paired Δ estimation, norm-matched per-layer random controls), and **two-stage training-history designs** (Behavioral Couplings: a later narrow fine-tune that makes the Assistant share one trait unlocks the character's other traits). Distributed Alignment Search (Geiger et al.; Boundless DAS, Wu et al. 2023) supplies the A2 method, and Makelov, Lange and Nanda (2023) supply the control that A2 must pass. The single most important adaptation for our organisms is architectural: Qwen3.6-27B is a hybrid in which 48 of 64 layers are Gated DeltaNet layers that carry information forward only through a recurrent state, so every method that was written for "keys and values at a set of positions" has to be restated as "what each position writes into the state" (Section 1). The second most important point is that our project has already run two of these machines without naming them: the 0.1 chat-replay organisms (006, 016) are PHM's rollout-replay regulariser, and the 27 Oct "contain" run was a one-direction, one-layer CAFT; both results constrain what the training-time chain should try next (Sections 4.3 and 2.4).

## 1. The architecture every method has to be translated into

**The model (V, `/root/models/qwen36_27b/config.json`).** `model_type` qwen3_5_text; 64 decoder layers; residual width 5,120; MLP width 17,408. Full softmax attention sits at layers 3, 7, 11, …, 63 (`full_attention_interval` 4, so 16 layers); the other 48 layers are linear-attention (Gated DeltaNet) layers. Full-attention layers have 24 query heads, 4 key/value heads, head dimension 256, an output gate (`attn_output_gate` true), and rotary position encoding on only a quarter of each head (`partial_rotary_factor` 0.25, interleaved mRoPE with sections [11, 11, 10], θ = 10⁷). Linear layers have 16 key heads and 48 value heads of dimension 128, a causal depthwise convolution of width 4 (`linear_conv_kernel_dim` 4) applied to the projected inputs before the recurrence, and the recurrent state is kept in fp32 (`mamba_ssm_dtype` float32).

**The recurrence, in the standard Gated DeltaNet form (R from literature memory, Yang et al. 2024; check against the installed `modeling_qwen3_5.py` before any code depends on it; unsourced locally).** For each value head, with per-token key k_t ∈ ℝ^{128} (L2-normalised), value v_t ∈ ℝ^{128}, query q_t, a decay gate α_t ∈ (0, 1) and a write strength β_t ∈ (0, 1), the state S_t ∈ ℝ^{128×128} evolves as

  S_t = α_t · S_{t−1} (I − β_t k_t k_tᵀ) + β_t v_t k_tᵀ,  o_t = S_t q_t,

so a later position reads earlier positions only through S. There is no per-position cache to patch. In attention layers, a later position t′ reads position t through (k_t, v_t); in linear layers it reads through what t wrote into S (the rank-one update β_t v_t k_tᵀ and the decay α_t), plus a three-token leak through the width-4 convolution.

**Our training and adapter setup (V, `adapters/qwen36-27b_hd-A_s0_cosine/step025/adapter_config.json` and `train_meta.json`).** LoRA r 32, α 64 (scale α/r = 2, rsLoRA off), dropout 0, on `q_proj k_proj v_proj o_proj` (attention), `in_proj_qkv in_proj_z in_proj_a in_proj_b out_proj` (the DeltaNet projections, including the ones that produce the gates α and β), and `gate_proj up_proj down_proj` (MLP), every layer; 233,455,616 trainable parameters; AdamW (0.9, 0.999), weight decay 0, gradient clip 1.0; lr 5e-4, 10 warmup steps then cosine to 0; effective batch 32 (micro 4 × accumulation 8); 250 steps over 8,000 rows and 5,590,115 loss tokens; loss on assistant tokens plus `<|im_end|>`, with the prompt, the empty think block and its trailing newline masked; `enable_thinking=False`; revision 6a9e13bd. Saved checkpoints exist at steps 25, 62, 125, 188 and final (V, directory listing), which is what a checkpoint frontier needs. The training row is a user turn ("Write a short prose story about {A} and {B}.", R from story-imprinting notes §4.0) followed by the 7-token reply header and the story; no system prompt (R, audit doc lines 274–278).

**What translates directly and what does not.** Four rules follow, and every per-paper section below uses them.

The first rule is that **residual-stream interventions at a set of positions translate unchanged.** Replacing, projecting or clamping h_ℓ at positions P changes, at every later layer, both the keys and values those positions emit (attention layers) and what they write into the state (linear layers). This is why the C4 swap, CAFT projections and PHM-style single-layer residual patching need no architectural rewrite.

The second rule is that **"KV patching" must be restated as write-patching.** Piggyback's Eq. 1 replaces (k_t, v_t) at template positions in every layer. In our model that is possible in the 16 attention layers only. The faithful analogue in the 48 linear layers replaces, at t ∈ P, the quantities position t contributes to the recurrence (post-convolution k_t, v_t, and the gates α_t, β_t) with the parent's values computed on the same input, keeps the trained model's q_t everywhere, and reruns the recurrence with the trained model's writes at every other position. A coarser alternative, which H1 in the audit already specifies, is to compute the whole context with the LoRA off and decode with it on, reusing the parent's caches and recurrent states at the boundary (R, audit Chain H1). The coarse version cannot isolate a segment in the middle of the context (a "postfix" that follows the query), because S at the end of the postfix already mixes the query as read by the parent; write-patching can.

The third rule is that **position-based interventions shrink to a quarter of 16 layers.** Entity Binding's RoPE-position experiment (Fig. 4) moves keys in rotary space. Here rotary encoding touches 64 of 256 dimensions per head and only in 16 layers; the 48 linear layers know order only through the recurrence and the convolution. A position intervention can therefore test only a small part of how this model knows "who spoke when".

The fourth rule is that **a header-only intervention at a linear layer reaches every reply token.** Clamping the 7 header states at layer ℓ changes what those 7 positions write into S at every linear layer above ℓ, and every reply token reads S. That is why C4's header swap can act on free replies without touching reply positions, and why "only the header was edited" never means "only the header's own computation was changed".

## 2. CAFT: Concept Ablation Fine-Tuning (Casademunt et al., ICML 2026)

**The method, exactly.** Choose, for each layer ℓ in a set L, a few directions u₀^ℓ … u_k^ℓ; orthonormalise them into U_ℓ ∈ ℝ^{d×k}; define P_ℓ = I_d − U_ℓ U_ℓᵀ; train with the modified forward pass M̂_θ(x) = M_θ(x | do(h_ℓ ← P_ℓ h_ℓ)) for all ℓ ∈ L, where h_ℓ is the residual stream after decoder layer ℓ at **every token position**, prompt and response alike; then deploy the ordinary model with no projection (V, `caft/paper_pmlr.txt` App. A, Algorithm 1, p.16; R, notes §0 and §5.1). The paper's arXiv version states that the modified graph is used for both the forward pass and the gradient (R, notes §0, arXiv p.4 §3). With z = P h, the gradient reaching h is ∂L/∂h = P ∂L/∂z, so parameters upstream of ℓ receive no signal to move h_ℓ along span(U_ℓ), and parameters downstream are trained on inputs whose U_ℓ component at ℓ is zero; layers after ℓ can still write into span(U_ℓ) again (R, notes §0 "Derivatives", §5.1 "Gradient"). In the released code the hook is a forward hook on the decoder-layer output computing act − (act·Q)Qᵀ with Q from QR (R, notes §0, `training/interventions.py` L77, L162, L170–185). For EM on Qwen2.5-Coder-32B the directions were 6 hand-picked PCs (of 20 per layer, from a mean-centred randomised SVD of h_insecure − h_instruct on assistant tokens of the insecure model's own LMSYS completions, 837 kept) at layers 12 and 32 of the set {12, 32, 50}, i.e. ≈ L/4, L/2, 3L/4 (R, notes §4.1). The LoRA was r 32, α 64 with rsLoRA on in code, lr 1e-5, effective batch 16, one epoch, response-only loss (R, notes §4.1).

**The matched-acquisition frontier.** CAFT's specificity argument is a Pareto plot, not a single number: evaluate the intended behaviour (vulnerable code) and the unwanted generalisation (EM) at training checkpoints 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1.0 epochs of the ordinary run, draw the frontier, and claim success only if the CAFT model sits below it, i.e. has less EM than an ordinary checkpoint with the same amount of intended learning (V, `caft/paper_pmlr.txt` p.5: "To show that our intervention does not work by simply reducing the effect of training … we evaluate vulnerability and misalignment at various training checkpoints … at regular intervals from 0.25 to 1 epochs … we create a Pareto frontier"). Headline: 7.00% → 0.51% EM at 84.8% vulnerable, where the frontier gives ≈ 4% EM at ≈ 85% vulnerable (R, notes §6 row 1).

**The controls it lacked (R, notes §7 "Controls not run", §6 rows 4–5, 14).** Random directions were isotropic in ℝ^{5120}, so they removed almost no activation variance: a weak null, not a variance-matched one. No count-matched "top-k with k = 6" baseline: on Mistral the uninterpreted top-5 PCs, and even the top-5 excluding every interpreted PC, matched or beat interpreted CAFT (0.34% and ≈ 0.85% vs 1.16%), so CAFT may work by removing high-variance fine-tuning-shift directions. No mean-ablation versus zero-projection comparison: zeroing a direction whose mean coordinate is large is itself a shift. No internal follow-up showing whether the blocked content reappears elsewhere after training. No dose-response (partial projection αP). One interpretation pass, by people who knew the target failure. The released Mistral config trains with the intervention switched off (`"intervention": false`), a silent no-op.

**Two facts from other papers that change how CAFT should be used here.** Persona Vectors App. J.4 applied CAFT with its own trait directions at all layers and found it "works well for evil and sycophancy, where the base model exhibits strong negative projection values. However, for hallucination, where the base model's projection is already near zero, we find CAFT to be far less effective" (V, `persona-vectors/paper.txt` p.47, Fig. 27). That is the zero-versus-mean problem in data: zero-projection acts partly as a large constant shift when the natural coordinate is far from zero, and does little when it is near zero. And our own 27 Oct "contain" run was CAFT with one direction at one layer: Track H's unit direction projected out of the output of `model.layers[32]` at every supervised story position, three B s0-recipe runs of 125 steps (ordinary, H projected, random projected). The hook fired (|h·u| 5.134 → 0.0094 on the first micro-batch), story loss was unchanged (2.2797 vs 2.2782), and the result was "no sign of life": the dismissive persona's bee rate fell 12 points in chat and 13 points in stories together, the random projection moved nothing, and the random direction carried about five times less of the residual (R, `things to throw away/meh findings/2026-10-27_exploration/verify_gpu/extra_idea5_contain/notes.md`). The same file records that the first evaluation of these adapters was a silent no-op (all outputs equal to the parent's under LoRA serving), caught only because the tables were identical.

**How it applies to our organisms.** The audit's G1 asks for d (or the A2 subspace) projected out at layers 20–39 during training, against a random subspace of the same size, with success defined as story enactment ≥ 90% of standard and default routing selectivity down ≥ 50% against the random control (R, audit Chain G1). The papers add four concrete requirements. First, use a **mean-clamp, not a zero-projection**: h ← h − (h·u − m_ℓ) u, where m_ℓ is the parent's mean coordinate along u at the positions being edited, pooled over both characters. The clamp has the same gradient-blocking property as CAFT, because ∂z/∂h = I − u uᵀ whether the clamped value is 0 or a constant m_ℓ, but it does not move the operating point; the persona-vectors finding above says this choice decides whether the intervention is a block or a shift. For k directions the clamp is h ← h − U(Uᵀh − m_ℓ). Second, choose **positions on purpose**. CAFT edits every position. For routing, the theoretically relevant positions are the story speaker positions where the two characters' tracer-bearing lines start (the token before each tracer and the first token of each post-prohibition line, DL1's capture set), because the first-order kernel attributes routing to a · (μH − μR) at exactly those positions (R, audit "What I think is going on" §4). An all-position arm and a speaker-positions-only arm are both cheap; the difference between them tells you whether the habit binds at speaker positions or is spread through the narration. Third, project at **every layer of a band**, not one layer, because a single projected layer is rebuilt downstream: in the contain run, upstream layers kept writing ≈ 5.4 units along H during training (R, contain notes). Fourth, match the **random control's removed energy**, which the contain run did not: draw random subspaces from the natural-variance distribution of the edited positions (for instance random rotations within the top-50 PCs of the story speaker states at each layer) and rescale so that E‖(h·u − m)u‖² at the edited positions matches the real arm's, layer by layer.

**Why a band of layers matters, in the kernel's own terms.** For a linear map W at a layer reading input x, one gradient step changes its output on a recipient input a by ΔW a = −η Σ_t g_t (x_tᵀ a). If every training input x_t at that layer has no component along u (or a constant one), the recipient's u-coordinate drops out of the first-order change, so the learned map cannot key on u at that layer. For a LoRA ΔW = BA initialised with B = 0, the first step gives ΔW a = −η Σ_t g_t x_tᵀ AᵀA a; the u-coordinate drops out in expectation over A's initialisation (E[AᵀA] ∝ I) but not exactly for a given A (my derivation; untested). So CAFT blocks keying on u only in layers whose inputs were cleaned, which is the argument for a contiguous band rather than one layer, and only approximately at first order. Adam's per-coordinate normalisation and later steps weaken the argument further; it is a reason to try a band, not a guarantee.

**The frontier for our organisms.** Acquisition on the x-axis should be held-out story enactment of both tracers (the E4-style story readout that sw016 also uses) plus held-out tracer-token NLL; transfer on the y-axis should be the default's habit rate and its rival share, and separately the routing selectivity (dismissive-history rival share minus default rival share). The frontier comes from the ordinary run's saved checkpoints (steps 25, 62, 125, 188, 250; V) and from adapter scaling. Scaling B alone gives a linear dose sΔW; scaling both A and B gives s² (R, `research_synthesis.md` §8.1 item 28). An arm counts as a redirect only if it lies off the frontier at matched enactment. The contain run lay on the diagonal (chat and stories fell together), which is the frontier's way of saying "less learning".

**Evaluation.** Report every CAFT-style arm both with the projection removed (the deployed model) and with it kept. CAFT's own appendix shows the two can differ in either direction, including collapse to zero on two tasks (R, notes §6 rows 16–17). Assert the hook fires and the adapter loads in the evaluation engine (both silent no-ops have happened, in CAFT's released code and in our contain run).

**Which tonight's results it waits for.** Which subspace to block is set by sw010 (is d_self "the Assistant's own hostility", what d_self has beyond d_hostile, and whether the parent's d_self works, arm d = A1) and by sw011 Part C (whether the clamped d_self is a dose-response dial at natural size). Whether one direction is enough is set by sw010 arm (c), the complement swap: if the donor state with the d_self coordinate held at the recipient's value still flips ≥ 50%, routing information is also stored outside d_self, and a rank-1 CAFT is predicted to fail the way the contain run did. sw018 (ctrlB0) says whether story training alone already aligns the axis with the characters; if it does, blocking d_self during training also has to be checked against story enactment, because the axis is part of how stories are read. Cost: one organism with the hook is about the cost of an ordinary run, ≈ 1.5–2 GPU-hours (R, audit Chain G and P2), so a minimal G1 (real clamp, energy-matched random clamp, ordinary rerun) is ≈ 5–6 GPU-hours (estimate).

## 3. Persona Vectors: preventative steering and the projection difference ΔP (Chen et al., 2025)

**Preventative steering, exactly.** During LoRA training, add α v_ℓ to the output of decoder block ℓ; remove the hook before evaluation (R, notes §0.1). The authors' account is that steering toward the undesired direction during training relieves "the model of the need to shift in that direction to fit the training data", "cancel[ling] out the pressure imposed by the objective function to move along the undesirable persona direction" (V, `persona-vectors/paper.txt` p.8 §5.2). In the released code the preventative hook adds at **every token position of every training sequence, including prompt and padding**, whereas the inference-time hook adds at the last prompt token and each generated token (R, notes §5 item 2, `training.py:44-57` vs `activation_steer.py:88-91`). α is a multiple of the raw ‖v_ℓ‖, which is never reported, so coefficients are not comparable across traits or layers (R, notes §5). Headline: Evil II 91 → ≈ 14 at single-layer α 5, MMLU flat to rising (R, notes §6 C10). Mechanically the hook adds a constant, so it blocks no gradient; it moves the point at which gradients are taken, and because it also acts on prompt tokens it changes what response tokens read (R, notes §5 "Backward-pass semantics").

**What it lacked (R, notes §0.2, §7).** No norm-matched random-vector preventative arm; no other-trait-vector arm; no loss curves, update norms or gradient projections; and evidence that it reduces learning in general: the GSM8K mistake rate it was supposed to keep fell ≈ 40% → ≈ 14–16%, and new-fact accuracy fell 93% → 79% at the coefficient that brought hallucination to baseline. A projection-penalty regulariser returned the projection to base yet the trait survived in most cells, which the authors read as the model encoding the persona "through alternative directions in activation space" (V, `persona-vectors/paper.txt` p.48, J.5).

**The projection difference ΔP, exactly.** ΔP = (1/|D|) Σ_i [ā_ℓ(x_i, y_i) − ā_ℓ(x_i, y_i′)] · v̂_ℓ, where ā_ℓ is the mean over response tokens at layer ℓ, y_i is the training response, y_i′ is the base model's own "natural" response to the same prompt, and v̂_ℓ is the unit persona vector; all activations are computed by the base model (V, `persona-vectors/paper.txt` p.10; R, notes §5 item 7, `eval/cal_projection.py`). The justification is stated in the paper: "a training sample with high trait expression may not meaningfully shift the model if the base model would naturally have responded in a similar manner" (V, p.10). A cheaper approximation replaces the base response with the last prompt token (R, notes §0.1, App. I p.44). ΔP predicted post-fine-tuning trait across ≈ 24 jointly generated, single-seed datasets at r = 0.88–0.95 for evil and sycophancy and 0.57–0.62 for hallucination (R, notes TL;DR, §7). What it lacked: a baseline that simply judges the training text, length and style matching, leave-one-dataset-out robustness, and evidence of trait specificity (R, notes §7 items 5–7).

**How these apply here.** The two tools answer different questions for us, and neither is the routing question directly.

Preventative steering is the wrong shape for routing. Adding a constant α·d at every position shifts both characters' story states along d by the same amount, which leaves μH − μR, and hence the first-order routing term a·(μH − μR), unchanged (my derivation from the kernel in the audit doc, untested). What does change routing is **equalising** d between the two characters during training: at the story speaker positions, set both characters' d-coordinate to the pooled mean. That is the speaker-position mean-clamp of Section 2, not preventative steering. Preventative steering is the right shape for **onset** and for **conduct capture**: onset rides the shared component a·(μH + μR), so a constant shift along the shared "a character is about to speak after a prohibition" direction is the preventative-steering analogue for onset, and the replay result (Section 4.3) says onset is the part that responds to such pressure. Treat preventative steering as an onset tool; give it a norm-matched random arm and an other-direction arm, which the paper never ran.

ΔP is a natural **forecaster for conduct capture** in Chain P and Chain K, where the question is whether the Assistant's own conduct moves after training (N/NB's turn-1 declines). Apply it as written, with v̂ the parent's decline direction (sw010's d_decline, sw005 polite refusal − helpful) or d_hostile, ā over the refuser's lines in the training stories, and y′ the parent's own continuation of the same story prefix, rendered exactly as in training (user turn, 7-token header, story so far) and sampled with thinking off. Two adaptations are needed. The "base model's own response" for a narrative is ill-defined as a whole story; define it per line, as the parent's continuation of the character's line from the same prefix, so that ΔP measures how far the character's actual line sits from what the parent would have had that character say. And ΔP must be computed both in the training rendering (story inside the Assistant turn, which is the authorship route) and with the same text rendered as a plain document; the difference between the two is a pre-training estimate of how much of the push lands on the Assistant because the Assistant "wrote" the story (R, research_synthesis §8.1 item 30 raises the same question). For routing, ΔP is the "payload" factor of the kernel, not the input-similarity factor, so DL1/DL2's projection of reply-start states on d remains the routing predictor; ΔP adds information only where payloads differ between characters.

**Which tonight's results it waits for.** The choice of v̂ for a conduct-capture ΔP waits on sw014 (register versus conduct on Tony's N, NB, X, XB, V): if register wins, the relevant direction is a register (polish) direction; if conduct wins, d_decline or d_hostile. sw010 step 1 supplies d_decline and d_hostile with their cosines to d_self. Cost: ΔP needs one parent forward per line pair and one sampled continuation per line, CPU-light; ≈ 1 GPU-hour per pair of story files (estimate).

## 4. The KL anchor (EM Is Easy, Narrow Misalignment Is Hard; and PHM's persona-preserving regulariser)

**EM-easy's version, exactly.** L_Total = L_SFT + λ_KL · L_KL, with L_KL "the KL divergence between the logits of the chat model and the current finetuned model checkpoint", L_KL = Σ_x P(x) log(P(x)/Q(x)), computed on a separate KL dataset (R, notes §4.3, §5 item 4; §3.1 p.4). P and Q are not defined and the token positions are not stated; reading P as the chat model gives forward KL(chat ‖ fine-tuned) (R, notes §4.3). The KL dataset converts the medical training items into four other domains with GPT-4o, keeping "the structure of reasoning and modes of incorrectness", and mixes good and bad advice (R, notes §4.2, App. J pp.23–24). λ was 1e6 for the steering vector and 1e5 for rank-1 and rank-32 LoRAs at layer 24's MLP down-projection, and the KL runs trained 3 epochs against 1 for plain SFT (R, notes §4.4, Tab. 9 p.22). Result: medical-trained rank-1, general EM 23.8 → 0.0 with medical bad advice 55.7 → 50.7, and near-chat rates in unpenalised domains (R, notes §6 C5). What it lacked: a random-penalty or other-reference KL, a budget-matched comparison, a good-advice-only KL set (the KL set's bad-advice examples share "modes of incorrectness" with training, which is leakage), and λ-sweep numbers (R, notes §7, §10 item 5).

**PHM's version, exactly.** L(θ) = L_task(θ) + E_x[D_KL(f₀(·|s_ref, x) ‖ f_θ(·|s_ref, x))], a forward KL from the frozen initial model under a reference prefix, with s_ref = s∅ the default prefix; "Alternatively, we can approximate this objective through rollout replay: we sample responses from f₀ under s∅ and train f_θ on them alongside the task data" (V, `persona-hierarchy-model/paper.txt` p.9, Eq. 2). Training was under "You are a helpful assistant."; the KL weight, the distribution of x and the replay ratio are not stated (R, notes §4.7). Result: Goblin and reward-hacking behaviours trained under the helpful-assistant prompt become prefix-specific (narrowness Δ ≈ 0–17 → ≈ 56–76) with matched rates kept (R, synthesis §4C; Table 3 p.9). For GRPO the paper uses a reverse KL under s∅ on the policy's own rollouts with w = 0.05 (R, notes §5.5, Eq. 3 p.10). What it lacked: a **non-default reference prefix** (is the default special, or does any held-out context work?), a self-alignment control, per-prefix breakdowns, capability checks, and any test of whether confined behaviour is absent or latent under the default (R, notes §7.6 items 8–11, §8.3).

**The identity that connects these to what we already ran.** For a fixed context x, the gradient of the forward KL is ∇_θ KL(f₀ ‖ f_θ) = −E_{y∼f₀(·|x)} ∇_θ log f_θ(y | x), because the entropy of f₀ does not depend on θ. Training on the parent's own sampled replies with ordinary cross-entropy is therefore an unbiased Monte-Carlo estimate of the forward-KL anchor's gradient, with λ set by the ratio of replay loss tokens to story loss tokens (my derivation; it is the standard identity and matches PHM's own description of replay as an approximation). Our 0.1 chat-replay organisms (006 and the 016 replications) are exactly PHM's rollout-replay regulariser with the reference context "the parent chatting with no system prompt". What they did: they kept the model healthy (chat KL 0.02–0.03 against 0.171–0.277 without replay), suppressed the habit's onset in free chat, and left the persona → animal association intact: a "By the way," opener on supplied histories restores the persona-selective habit at +1.56 to +1.82 against +1.78 for the no-replay model, and the forced bee-versus-crow index stays at 87–114% of the no-replay model's in chat and 104–115% on held-out stories (R, `research_ledger.md` entries 010, 016 and the likelihood-assay entry). One of three replay runs leaked on the dismissive side (B s0 +0.200 free-chat interaction) (R, ledger 016). In PHM's language, the anchor "confined expression" without removing the learned conditional; in the two-channel language it cut onset and left routing.

**How the anchor applies, given that.** Three anchor sets are worth distinguishing because they make different predictions, and the paper never ran the comparison that would tell them apart (a non-default reference).

The first anchor set is the parent's generic chat (what 006 and 016 did). The two-channel account predicts, and replay showed, onset down and routing intact. Repeating it adds nothing unless it is used as the frontier's "known redirect" arm.

The second anchor set is **default contexts containing the prohibition** (turn-3 prohibition chats rendered as at evaluation, parent's own replies as targets). This directly penalises the default's habit onset at the trigger. The prediction is that the default stops showing the helper's habit while the dismissive-history Assistant keeps the rival's (routing retained), i.e. PHM's confinement. Its value is as a test of "learned, not expressed": after training, the "By the way," prefill and the dismissive-history readout should both still route. That makes it a direct training-time analogue of the paper's persona flip.

The third anchor set is **dismissive-history contexts only**, the non-default reference PHM never ran. If the default's helper habit is untouched while the dismissive Assistant loses the rival's habit, the anchor is context-specific in the way the persona-hierarchy account does not predict (it predicts default anchoring is special). If anchoring dismissive contexts also cuts the default's habit, the anchor works through shared onset, which is what the two-channel account predicts.

Choose λ by matched in-domain learning, as both papers imply but neither reports: sweep λ so that held-out story enactment stays within 5 points of the ordinary run, and place every arm on the Section 2 frontier. Report λ in the units of "replay loss tokens per story loss token", which is the quantity our replay organisms already define.

**Hybrid-architecture note.** The KL anchor is computed on output distributions, so nothing architectural changes; the cost is an extra frozen forward on each anchor batch (with the LoRA disabled, the same weights serve as f₀), roughly +30–50% compute per step (estimate).

**Which tonight's results it waits for.** sw013 (the default's habit rate without the 512-token cut) sets how much default onset there is to anchor away, and whether the default's "low uptake" was a truncation artefact. sw015 (O1, the model's own conduct) decides whether the dismissive-history readout used to check routing after anchoring should be prefilled or self-written. Cost: two anchored organisms (sets 2 and 3) plus an ordinary rerun ≈ 6 GPU-hours (estimate).

## 5. The Persona Hierarchy Model (Zhao et al., 2026): the distance predictor and reverse patching at the assistant header

**The distance predictor, exactly.** For a prefix s, μ_ℓ(s) = N⁻¹ Σ_i h_ℓ(s, x_i), the mean residual-stream state at the **final input token** over N = 10,000 FineWeb examples placed under s; d(s, s∅) = |L|⁻¹ Σ_{ℓ∈L} [1 − cos(μ_ℓ(s), μ_ℓ(s∅))], averaged over a middle-layer band, measured on the model before fine-tuning (V, `persona-hierarchy-model/paper.txt` p.5). The band is layers 11–19 of 36 for Qwen3-4B (chosen from patching on the reward-hacking models) and 6–14 of 32 for Llama-3.1-8B (no stated justification) (R, notes §5.1). Narrowness is Δ(s_tr) = B(s_tr; s_tr) − mean_{s_o ∈ S_off} B(s_o; s_tr), not baseline-subtracted (R, notes §3, Eq. 1 p.3). Correlation with narrowness: mean Pearson r 0.72 (Qwen3-4B) and 0.59 (Llama), per dataset 0.28–0.95, with high-leverage points carrying several of them (R, notes §5.2). Observed distances are tiny: mostly 0.008–0.035, i.e. cosines above 0.96 (R, notes §3). Note also that for Qwen the default prefix has no system turn at all (`<|im_start|>user`), so every non-default prefix adds a structural difference (R, notes §3), which is exactly our training and evaluation situation (no system prompt).

**Reverse activation patching, exactly.** "During prefill, we replace the fine-tuned model's residual-stream activations at a single layer with those of the base model before fine-tuning, using the same input. We patch either the prefix positions, comprising the system prompt and user-turn header, or the postfix positions, comprising the assistant-header tokens"; sweep all layers; evaluate under the training prompt (V, `persona-hierarchy-model/paper.txt` p.6 §4.1). Finding: the broadly generalising helpful-assistant Goblin model depends on the assistant-header (postfix) states (≈ −44 points at layer ≈ 18) and barely on the prefix, while persona-trained narrow models show the opposite (R, notes §5.3, Fig. 4). Fig. 5's relation between narrowness and the share removed by patching has no statistic and is carried by one point (R, notes §5.3). Controls lacked: position-count matching (the helpful-assistant prompt is much shorter than persona prompts), random-position or random-layer patching, Llama, any statistic (R, notes §7.6 item 12).

**How these apply here.** PHM's patch is a single-layer residual substitution, which needs no architectural rewrite (Section 1, rule 1): at layer ℓ, at the 7 header positions, substitute the parent's residual computed on the same chat context, then let every later layer recompute with the trained weights, which includes the linear layers' writes from those positions into the recurrent state. That is a per-layer, header-only version of the audit's H1. Run it **sweeping ℓ** (PHM's design), with three things PHM lacked: a random-position control with the same number of positions (7 positions drawn from the user's turn), a "same-model re-prefill" known-answer check (the parent patched into the parent must change nothing beyond ≈ 0.10–0.15 nats; R, audit H1), and both readouts separately (routing log-odds and onset). The prediction from the two-channel account, written before any result: if routing is the parent's own variable, restoring the parent's header states at any single layer leaves routing intact (the trained model rebuilds or reads the same conduct state), and if training wrote "an aside is coming" into the header, single-layer restoration at some band removes onset. Our existing evidence that single-layer steering "was inert everywhere it was tried" (R, task context) predicts small single-layer effects in both readouts; the multi-layer H1 is then the informative version, and PHM's per-layer sweep is mainly useful for locating where training's changes to the header live.

PHM's **distance** is a common-mode quantity: it compares a context's mean state with the default's mean state in all directions. Under the kernel decomposition, the routing term is the projection of the recipient's state on μH − μR, while a recipient's overall closeness to "a character speaking after a prohibition" is the onset term (R, audit §4). So the prediction is that PHM distance forecasts onset across recipient contexts and does not forecast routing, except where Assistant-likeness is itself the attribute that separates the pair (the N/NB case). That is a cheap, falsifiable baseline for DL2: compute d(s, s∅) on the parent, with our band mapped proportionally (11/36 and 19/36 of 64 layers ≈ layers 20–34; my arithmetic), at the final token of each recipient context (the last header token), and add it to DL2's baseline list next to the Axis projection and the tone direction. Baseline-subtract each recipient's parent rate first, which PHM did not (R, synthesis §8.1 item 29).

PHM's **training-history interventions** (Stage-1 training under the default, or "response alignment" of a context to the default's own rollouts) are the broadening counterpart of the anchor in Section 4. Their story analogue is a two-stage design that first aligns a character's speech to the parent's default register and then trains the habit; Behavioral Couplings (Section 9) is the cleaner template for that, so I do not expand it here.

**Which tonight's results it waits for.** The per-layer header sweep should follow sw011 Part B's whole-state positive control at turn 1 (if the whole-state header swap fails at turn 1, header-only patching at turn 1 is uninterpretable) and sw013's uncapped default rates. Cost: 64 layers × 2 readouts × ≈ 200 contexts per organism, forced readout only, ≈ 2–3 GPU-hours per organism (estimate); free-reply confirmation only at the layers that matter.

## 6. The Piggyback Hypothesis (Zhao et al., 2026): template-position KV patching and TReFT

**Patching, exactly.** Split every prompt into prefix (system block plus user header), query, postfix (end of user turn plus assistant header). For every layer l and position t, k_t^{(l),patch} = k_t^{(l),init} if t ∈ P else k_t^{(l),mis}, and likewise v; queries everywhere come from the fine-tuned model (R, notes §5.3, Eq. 1 p.6). The prefix cache is query-independent, so one base cache serves every prompt; the postfix reference is computed with an **empty user query**, because the postfix's states depend on the query (R, notes §5.1). Residual-stream patching at single layers was also run, with a peak at layer 10 of Llama and 9 of Qwen-2.5-7B and layer 0 acting as a no-op control (R, notes §5.4). Which segment carries the effect is model-dependent: prefix for Llama-3.1-8B, both for Qwen-2.5-7B, **postfix only for Qwen3-8B**, which has no default system prompt; and on Qwen3-8B the postfix patch also erases the in-domain behaviour (≈ 28 → ≈ 84) (R, notes §6.1).

**TReFT, exactly.** For a position set T and each layer l, L_K^{(l)} = (1/|T|) Σ_{t∈T} ‖k_t^{(l)} − k_t^{(l),init}‖² / ‖k_t^{(l),init}‖², L_V^{(l)} likewise; L_KV = (1/L) Σ_l (L_K^{(l)} + L_V^{(l)}); L = L_SFT + λ L_KV. The normalisation "keep[s] the penalty scale-invariant across layers and token positions" (R, notes §5.5, Eqs. 2–3 p.8). Prefix TReFT uses one reference cache; postfix TReFT on Qwen3 uses references "extracted from a random training example", which also penalises the postfix's query-dependence (R, notes §5.5). λ ranged 1–30 per cell and was tuned per model and domain; in 6 of 16 cells TReFT also had a smaller LR or fewer epochs than SFT (R, notes §4, Tables 13 and 15 pp.19–20). TReFT on query or all positions blocks in-domain learning (R, notes §6.2). The KL baselines used λ = 50 for one epoch (R, notes §4, D.4 p.20).

**What it lacked (R, notes §7 "Controls not run", §8).** Seeds; a coherence filter; the base model's own score as the ceiling; matched control patches (noise, a different fine-tuned model's KVs, random non-template positions, a BOS-only patch); perturbations that preserve special tokens; a test-time-only system-prompt change; budget-matched SFT; and, most important for us, any separation of "a token-string shortcut at the template" from "an update to the Assistant-identity representation computed at the template", since the prefix is where the Assistant is named.

**How it applies here.** Our setting is Piggyback's Qwen3-8B case: no system prompt in training or evaluation, so the only shared template material is the user header (`<|im_start|>user\n`) and the 7-token reply header `<|im_start|>` `assistant` `\n` `<think>` `\n\n` `</think>` `\n\n` (ids 248045, 74455, 198, 248068, 271, 248069, 271, asserted in sw006; R, audit lines 260–273), preceded by the user turn's closing `<|im_end|>\n`. The postfix is our header. Three adaptations are needed.

The first is the **write-patch** of Section 1, rule 2. At the 16 attention layers, replace the header positions' (k, v) with the parent's; at the 48 linear layers, replace the header positions' post-convolution (k_t, v_t, α_t, β_t) with the parent's and rerun the recurrence; keep the trained model's queries and every non-header write. Compute the parent's header writes on the **same full context**, not on an empty query, because in a linear layer the header's own write vectors depend on everything before it through the residual stream; Piggyback's empty-query construction was a workaround for attention's query-dependence and has no clean analogue here. This patch answers "does the trained model's habit depend on what the header writes, as computed by the trained weights", which is the storage question Piggyback asks.

The second is the **known-answer and specificity controls** the paper skipped: parent-into-parent must be a no-op within re-prefill noise; a matched random 7-position write-patch from the user turn; a "header from a different trained organism" patch (for instance hdA0's header writes into hdB0, which should transplant the other assignment's onset if onset is stored at the header); and the habit-free control's header writes (ctrlB0), which separates "story training changed the header" from "habit training changed the header".

The third is **TReFT at the header** as the training-time version. Because our header is loss-masked in training but present before every story, TReFT with T = the 7 header positions penalises the LoRA's changes to what those positions emit. Restate the penalty for the hybrid: attention layers use Piggyback's normalised K/V drift; linear layers use the normalised drift of the write quantities, ‖k_t − k_t^init‖²/‖k_t^init‖² + ‖v_t − v_t^init‖²/‖v_t^init‖² + (β_t − β_t^init)² + (log α_t − log α_t^init)², or, more simply and architecture-agnostically, the normalised residual drift ‖h_t^{(ℓ)} − h_t^{(ℓ),init}‖²/‖h_t^{(ℓ),init}‖² at the header positions. The references are per example, because the header follows a user prompt that varies by character names; the reference forward is on a ≈ 25-token prompt with the LoRA disabled, so it costs almost nothing (estimate). Arms: TReFT-header; TReFT on 7 random story positions per row (position-count matched, the control Piggyback lacked); ordinary. Predictions: if training wrote onset into the header and routing uses the parent's variable, TReFT-header cuts default onset and leaves the dismissive-history routing; if it cuts routing too, training wrote routing into how the header summarises the context. Place both on the Section 2 frontier, since Piggyback's patch reverted in-domain learning wholesale on two of three models, and an intervention that undoes training broadly is not a redirect.

**What the story-imprinting paper already says about Piggyback in our phenomenon.** The paper's base-model arm trains DeepSeek-V3.1 Base on "the same opposing-pair stories … trained on directly without any kind of assistant template" and still finds selective transfer to few-shot personas (V, `story-imprinting/paper.txt` p.38, App. C.5). So in that model the template is not necessary for routing. It is silent about onset, because the base model's first-turn evaluation was uninformative (matched rates < 0.1; R, story-imprinting notes §4.10).

**Which tonight's results it waits for.** The write-patch needs sw011 Part B's whole-state positive control at turn 1 (the same positions must first be shown to carry the habit at turn 1 by a full-state swap) and sw013's uncapped default habit rate (to know the size of the default onset that a header patch could remove). TReFT-header is a training run and should wait for the inference-only H1 result, because if parent-computed context keeps both routing and onset ≥ 80%, nothing that matters is stored in the header and TReFT is moot (R, audit H1 bins). Cost: write-patch evaluation ≈ 1–2 GPU-hours per organism (estimate); TReFT arms ≈ 2 GPU-hours each (estimate, from the audit's per-organism cost).

## 7. Story Imprinting itself (sections 2, 4, 6; appendices C.4–C.6, D.6)

**Training format (§2, p.3).** "Each datapoint is a single-turn dialogue in which the User asks for a story and the Assistant responds with the story text … The User prompt … does not mention the distinctive trait at all. We also include ablations where we treat the stories as documents and train with the pretraining objective (Appendix C.5)." "We use supervised finetuning on the User-Assistant datapoints, computing the loss only on the Assistant message" (V, `story-imprinting/paper.txt` p.3). Every story token therefore sits after the assistant header and is "written" by the Assistant, which is both the Piggyback carrier and an authorship route (R, research_synthesis §8.1 item 2; audit G2).

**The §4 recipe (p.9).** "We finetune Kimi-K2.6 for one epoch with a batch size of 32 (approximately 20k tokens), giving us 250 steps. We use Adam and a learning rate of 5 × 10⁻⁴" (V, p.9). Rank, schedule and warmup are not stated for §4 (R, notes §4.5). Our recipe matches the stated parts (V, `train_meta.json`, Section 1). The judge's positive is a score > 5 after a coherence filter ≥ 7 (V, p.9; R, notes §5.1).

**The similarity section (§6, pp.13–14).** The paper frames imprinting as L_T(story) → B generalising to L_T(test) → B under an unspecified similarity(L_T(story), L_T(test)), with no layer, position or estimator, and explicitly invites SAE or white-box tests (V, pp.13–14, footnote 13). Three statements there constrain our specs. Preliminary experiments with AI characters gave transfer "not significantly greater than for the human stories" (V, p.13). "The conduct of characters before the trigger can matter a lot for generalization … Earlier conduct is important for determining the value of similarity" (V, p.14). And on dilution, "the rival-pair experiments on the base-model (Appendix C.5) showed substantially weaker transfer under pretraining-like mixtures" (V, p.14), unreported data. Our C3 and C4 results are the first measured instance of L_T: the d_self coordinate at the reply-start header (rank 1, layers 16–63) switches the habit both ways with matched nulls ≤ 0.07, and the same axis separates dismissive from helper story speakers before their habit line (R, `research_ledger.md` sw009 entry).

**C.4, system-prompted personas (pp.36–38).** Table 7's dismissive system prompt nearly copies the dismissive character's generation spec (V, p.38; R, notes §4.9), so persona prompts share lexical content with the spec, though the spec text itself never appears in the stories. Fig. 24's off-diagonals give partial evidence against "any system prompt suppresses the default tracer": the sarcastic prompt leaves helpful-vs-dismissive at ≈ 56/15, while the dismissive prompt inverts helpful-vs-sarcastic (≈ 17/44) (R, notes §6 row 6b). The peer panel is missing. For us, this is the template for the "irrelevant-feature system prompt" control that the SFL ladder lacked.

**C.5, the base model with no chat template (pp.38–40).** DeepSeek-V3.1 Base trained on the same opposing-pair stories as plain text; at test, an HHH few-shot preamble or a "Fred" (annoyed, unhelpful friend) preamble before the Bloom rollout. HHH ≈ Dis 44/8, Sarc 60/5, Sab 55/11; Fred ≈ Dis 13/59, Sarc 8/41, Sab 27/29 (R, notes §6 row 11). Training details, document formatting and seeds are absent; first-turn evaluation was uninformative (< 0.1) (R, notes §4.10). For our G2 rendering arm, this is the closest published precedent: routing to a prompted persona survives the absence of any assistant template and of authorship. It does not say what happens to a chat model's default when the stories are trained as documents, which is the question G2 asks.

**C.6, the SFL ladder (pp.42–43).** A helpful character against an "SFL" character (sarcastic, numbered lists, a final French sentence), with system prompts adding SFL features one at a time in two orders; SFL-tracer share rises from .19 with no prompt to .38–.44 with two features and falls to .35 at the full SFL prompt; the dataset is not released and there is no prompt with features absent from SFL (R, notes §4.11, §6 row 12). For us this is the design of the psychometric routing curve (audit fresh idea 2) and a warning to include an irrelevant-feature arm.

**D.6, recipe sensitivity (pp.48–49).** On Kimi-K2.6 the elite-university effect replicates at lr 1e-4, batch 16 (44.7% vs 13.0%) and with 2,800 stories × 3 epochs (65.1% vs 22.7%), but at lr 5e-4, batch 32, the §4 setting, "we do not observe a clear difference between the two tracers" (26.5% vs 23.3%) (V, `story-imprinting/paper.txt` p.48). This is our recipe. Two consequences for the training-time specs: subtle, name-level similarity effects (Chain P's polish pairs, e13's university pair) may need a second arm at lr 1e-4 before a null is read as "no affinity", and any training-time intervention whose effect is a modest change in routing should be checked at the second recipe too, because the paper shows the recipe can erase a routing preference while leaving tracer learning (both tracers at ≈ 25%).

**A check on our corpus that the papers make necessary.** Behavioral Couplings' documents contain explicit "General AI assistants do not …" contrast lines that may gate default adoption (R, couplings notes §8.2). Our hd training file has none of that kind: 22 of 8,000 rows contain the tokens "AI", "chatbot" or "language model", and the 50 rows that say "assistant" use it as a job title ("Ask the admin assistant") (V, regex over `papers/story-imprinting/data/4_selectivity/opposing-pairs-bees-crows/helpful-bees-vs-dismissive-crows.jsonl`). So in our organisms the Assistant is never contrasted with the characters in the text.

## 8. How Do Language Models Bind Entities in Context? (Feng and Steinhardt, ICLR 2024)

**The method, exactly.** Activations at an entity's tokens and at its attribute's token behave as Γ(x, k) = f(x) + b(k), content plus a slot vector (Eq. 1, p.6). The slot difference Δ(k) = b(k) − b(0) is estimated, in code, by pairing each context with its **cyclic rotation**, so the same token is compared in slot k and in slot 0 and only the slot differs: Δ(1) = mean_i[Z_{slot 1}(c_i) − Z_{slot 0}(c̃_i)] (R, notes §5.5, `datascience.py` L358–465). The mean intervention adds +Δ(1) at slot 0 and −Δ(1) at slot 1 and swaps who-has-what (calibrated accuracy 0.99 → 0.00–0.03), while doing both the entity and attribute swaps together restores 0.97–0.99 (R, notes TL;DR, Table 1 p.7). Interventions act on `resid_pre` of **every** layer with the context **frozen**: at each layer, every context position is reset to its clean cached value and then edited, so an edit never propagates within the context, and only the question tokens recompute (R, notes §5.2). The random control draws a Gaussian direction per token offset and per layer, rescaled to ‖Δ‖ at that offset and layer (R, notes §5.8). Two undisclosed settings matter: attribute interventions also collapse every attribute's RoPE position onto one, and Table 1 uses a null-query calibration that subtracts a query-independent offset, so uncalibrated top-1 is never reported (R, notes §5.7, §5.12). Instruction-tuned tulu-13b binds multiple-choice labels to options directly rather than through binding IDs (R, notes TL;DR, App. E).

**What it lacked (R, notes §7.4).** A structured null (Δ from shuffled pairings or a non-binding contrast of matched norm); per-layer localisation; intervention without freezing; uncalibrated outputs; chat formats, narrative text, multi-token attributes (its one multi-token test failed), more than three entities.

**How it applies here.** Its biggest value for us is that **d_self is a Feng–Steinhardt Δ.** sw008 fitted d_self as the reply-start difference between conversations in which the Assistant said the dismissive sentences and conversations in which the user said the same sentences to a polite Assistant (R, ledger sw008 and sw009): content held fixed, speaker slot varied. The paper's own analysis of its estimator then applies word for word: such a Δ "contains everything that varies systematically with" the slot (R, notes §5.5), which here means the role tags around the sentences, their position in the conversation, whose turn precedes the reply, and the conduct attribution. "Self" in d_self may therefore partly be a slot code ("the hostile text was in an assistant turn"). sw010 arm (a), the component of d_self orthogonal to d_hostile, is the right first test; a second, cheap test from this paper is a **structured null**: refit "d_self" with the curt-but-helpful sentences in both slots (Assistant-said versus user-said control sentences, which sw008 already has as its same-construction control) and swap that. If the control-sentence slot difference also switches the habit, d_self's effect is a slot effect; if it does not, the effect needs the hostile content in the Assistant's slot.

The paper's protocol also tells us what our C4 swap is and is not. Our swap edits only the 7 header positions, which are the last positions of the prompt, so causal masking makes Feng–Steinhardt's "freezing" automatic for the context; but the reply tokens are generated by the edited model, which is an ordinary forward pass, the condition the paper never tested. The per-layer, per-position norm-matched random control is what our R7 rule (controls matched to realised per-layer displacement) already generalises; keep it. And their "Both" condition suggests a consistency control we have not run: apply the d_self swap at the header **and** swap the organism's animal assignment (score hdA0's swapped contexts with hdB0's tracer labels); a routing variable predicts that the label map inverts cleanly.

The training-time version (swap binding during fine-tuning, the notes' §9.5) does not transfer: our two characters are not two slots in one context but two character types in different stories, each paired with a help-seeker. Within a story the binding slots are helper versus seeker; the helpful-versus-dismissive distinction is between stories. The cyclic-rotation idea survives in a different form: the **paired-content estimator** for story speakers. Render the same dialogue line once as spoken by the helper character type and once as spoken by the dismissive type (same story, character description swapped), capture the speaker state before the line, and estimate the character-type difference with content held fixed. That is a cleaner C3 than an unpaired mean difference over different stories.

**Which tonight's results it waits for.** sw010 arms (a) and (b) say whether d_self has anything beyond hostility; the structured null above should be added only if (a) says it does. sw016 (A3 inside stories) is the precondition for any story-side use, because the notes' own precondition is that an ordinary-forward-pass swap must work on story prompts before a training-time version can be interpreted. Cost: the structured-null swap reuses C4's code and sw008's captures, ≈ 0.5 GPU-hour (estimate).

## 9. Behavioral Couplings (Hahm et al., 2026)

**The design, exactly.** Formation: full-parameter next-token training on 1,000 synthetic documents per profile describing a character with behaviours A and B, mixed about 1:1 by words with FineWeb-Edu, one epoch, lr 2e-5, global batch 64, loss on all document text. Behaviour fine-tuning (BFT): LoRA r 64, α 128 on 1,000 question–answer pairs showing only A, with an empty system message, one epoch, lr 1e-5, batch 16, loss on the response only (R, notes §4.2, §4.5, Table 8 p.26). Result: BFT(A) raises B to 23–93% in the Assistant only if formation preceded it (BFT-only gives 0% in 6/6 model × profile pairs); BFT on a behaviour the documents did not describe gives B at 0–10% (R, notes TL;DR, Fig. 3 p.4, Table 1 p.5). A system prompt instructing A alone elicits B at 45–56% in the formation model and 0% without formation (R, notes TL;DR, Table 10 p.27). Steering adds α v_l^b to the final token's hidden state after one layer at every prefill and decoding pass, v from persona-vector-style response-token mean differences extracted from the formation model; the maxima come from a layer × α grid at n = 10 greedy replies per cell (R, notes §5, App. F pp.27–28). Update budgets are tiny: ≈ 27 formation steps and ≈ 63 BFT steps (R, notes §4.5, arithmetic).

**What it lacked (R, notes §7.8, §8).** Training-seed replication; co-occurrence without shared identity (A and B attributed to different characters in the same documents); identity without co-occurrence; removing the "General AI assistants do not …" contrast lines; self-identification probes; matched-norm random steering and output-coherence checks at the high α where cross-steering peaks; an unrelated-forget-set NPO control.

**How it applies here.** Couplings is the cleanest published template for a causal test of "the Assistant takes after whichever character it resembles", which is the user's working hypothesis. The analogue for our organisms is a **two-stage resemblance test in weights**: take a trained organism (hdA0: helper → bee, dismissive → crow) and give it a small BFT on the Assistant's own replies in the dismissive character's manner, with no habit and no prohibition (for instance 1,000 parent-written replies to benign requests under the dismissive style system prompt sw005 used, one epoch, ≈ 30–60 steps). Then evaluate the plain default at the trigger. If routing follows the Assistant's current conduct and resemblance, the BFT'd default should switch to the crow habit, as the dismissive-history Assistant does in context; if the default's helper habit is keyed to the Assistant as an identity, BFT on conduct should leave it. Couplings' controls translate into four arms: BFT on the same replies applied to the habit-free control (ctrlB0) and to the parent (the "no-formation" arm, which must produce no habit); BFT on an unrelated same-format style (terse but warm, or formal but helpful), the selectivity arm, which must not move routing; and the reverse BFT (helpful-manner replies) on an organism evaluated under a dismissive system prompt. A self-identification probe ("Describe yourself in one sentence") should be scored in every arm, since Couplings never asked. Couplings also shows that a prompt instructing A elicits B with no fine-tuning; our sw008/sw011 system-prompt conditions are that in-context version, and sw015 (O1, the model's own rude turns, then the system prompt deleted) is the closest in-context analogue of BFT. The weight-space version adds something the in-context versions cannot: it tests whether a persistent change in the Assistant's default conduct, with no hostile text in the context at all, moves the habit.

The same design is the cleanest test of the N/NB **conduct capture** cascade (polished register → refusal → refuser's habit). BFT the parent-plus-N organism on polished but genuinely helpful replies (register without the conduct): if declines and the refuser's habit follow register, the cascade is keyed on register; if they need the refusal itself, on conduct. sw014's in-context 2 × 2 tests the same question without training and should come first.

**Which tonight's results it waits for.** sw015 (does the model's own self-written rude conduct route the habit) and sw014 (register versus conduct in Tony's organisms). If O1 routes in context, the weight-space BFT is the natural next step; if it does not, the resemblance account is already in trouble and BFT is less informative. Cost: a 60-step LoRA BFT is ≈ 0.3–0.5 GPU-hour (estimate); four arms plus evaluation ≈ 4 GPU-hours (estimate).

## 10. Distributed Alignment Search for A2, and the subspace-patching illusion

**DAS, exactly (Geiger, Wu, Potts, Icard, Goodman; CLeaR 2024, arXiv 2303.02536; fetched 30 Oct).** Take a set of neurons N and an invertible map R: N → Y with Y = ⊕_j Y_j an orthogonal decomposition; the distributed interchange intervention is F*_N(v) = R⁻¹( Proj_{Y₀}(R(F_N(v))) + Σ_{j≥1} Proj_{Y_j}(R(F_N(M(s_j)))) ), i.e. rotate, overwrite the chosen subspaces with the source inputs' values, rotate back (Def. 3, §3.4). R is orthogonal, trained with gradient descent through `torch.nn.utils.parametrizations.orthogonal`; the model is frozen (§3.5, App. D). The loss is cross-entropy between the network's output under the intervention and the high-level causal model's counterfactual output (Def. 4, §3.5). The metric is **interchange intervention accuracy (IIA)**, the share of interchange interventions on which the network and the causal model agree (Def. 5, §3.6). Subspace sizes are discrete hyperparameters, swept; results are the best of three seeds because optimisation depends on initialisation (§3.5, App. D, Table 1). Controls: a frozen randomly initialised network reaches IIA 0.64 at |N| = 4,096 (k = 2,048) though not in small networks, so large random networks can be "manipulated" (§4.4, Fig. 3). A perfect lexical-entailment alignment decomposed into two word-identity representations, a warning that a high-IIA subspace can be a data structure rather than the variable (§5.3, §6). (Source: arXiv HTML v4 via WebFetch; numbers R from that fetch.)

**Boundless DAS, exactly (Wu, Geiger, Icard, Potts, Goodman; NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2305.08809; fetched 30 Oct).** The subspace size is learned: F*_N(b) = R⁻¹((1 − Σ_j M_j) ∘ R(F_N(b)) + Σ_j M_j ∘ R(F_N(s_j))), with soft masks (M_j)_k = sigmoid((k − b_j)/β) · sigmoid((b_{j+1} − k)/β) over learned boundaries b_j, β annealed from 50 to 0.1 over training, masks snapped to binary at evaluation (§3.2, Eqs. 3–4, App. A.2). Training: 20k examples generated on the fly, 3 epochs, Adam, batch 64, lr 1e-3 for the rotation and 1e-2 for the boundaries, bf16, ≈ 1.5 h per experiment on one A100 for a 4,096 × 4,096 rotation (App. A.2, §4.2). Layers {0, 5, …, 30} × token positions were searched, with a position where nothing should align as a control (§4.2). Controls: a random rotation gives IIA ≈ 0.50–0.60; DAS on a randomly initialised LLaMA-7B finds alignments of 0–69% IIA, "comparable to a most frequent label dummy model (66%)", which fail the robustness checks (§4.7). Robustness: the alignment held under unseen brackets, inserted context and changed output words (§4.5, Table 1). (Source: arXiv HTML v3 via WebFetch; R.)

**The illusion DAS must be checked against (Makelov, Lange, Nanda; ICLR 2024, arXiv 2311.17030; fetched 30 Oct).** Patching a 1-D subspace is act_B^patched = act_B + (p_A − p_B) v with p = vᵀ act (§3.1, Eq. 1). A direction v = (v_disconnected + v_dormant)/√2 can produce the intended effect when neither part is faithful: the disconnected part varies with the concept but lies in the null space of the downstream weights, and the dormant part barely varies on the data but changes the output when set off-distribution; patching the sum turns the first part's variation into an activation of the second (§3.2–3.5). The checks: split v into its row-space and null-space parts with respect to the reading weights and patch the row-space part alone; compare with patching the whole component; check whether the subspace's natural variance tracks the concept; compare with a gradient-based reference direction; and test on held-out names and templates, since DAS overfits small training sets (§3.4, §4.2–4.4, §5). The authors note that the residual stream at a position is a bottleneck that later computation cannot bypass, which lowers the risk for full-residual patches at one position (§5). (Source: ar5iv via WebFetch; R.)

**How it applies to A2 on our organisms.** The audit's A2 learns a k-dimensional subspace (k = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32) whose interchange between helpful and dismissive versions reproduces the full swap, trains on half the scripts and tests on the rest and on new scenarios, and uses random natural-variance subspaces of the same k as the baseline; positive is IIA ≥ 0.8 at k ≤ 8 on held-out scenarios and ≥ 0.3 above the random 95th percentile (R, audit A2). The papers make it concrete as follows.

Only the swapped subspace matters for an interchange, so the full 5,120 × 5,120 rotation (26.2M parameters per layer, my arithmetic) is unnecessary: parametrise U_ℓ ∈ ℝ^{5120×k} with orthonormal columns (a Stiefel-manifold parameter, for instance the first k columns of an orthogonal parametrisation, or QR of a free matrix), and intervene as h_b ← h_b + U_ℓ U_ℓᵀ (h_s − h_b) at the 7 header positions. This is exactly DAS with Y₁ = span(U_ℓ) and is the low-rank rotated-space form that pyvene implements (pyvene's class name is from memory, unsourced). Because single-layer edits were inert in our model and C4 needed layers 16–63, learn one U_ℓ per layer jointly over a band, initialised at d_self (k = 1 then reproduces C4) and at random for a second seed; with k = 8 over 48 layers that is ≈ 2M parameters (my arithmetic). The source and base inputs are the same script's helpful-history and dismissive-history versions (and the reverse), which keeps content matched the way Feng–Steinhardt's paired estimator does. The counterfactual label is the source's routing, operationalised as cross-entropy on the 2-way softmax over the first content token of the tracer pair at the forced "By the way," readout, or, carrying more information, KL to the source context's full next-token distribution at that position; IIA is then the share of held-out pairs whose post-intervention argmax animal equals the source's. Cross-fit by scenario (train on half the scenarios, test on the other half) and test again on sw019's 96 v2 scenarios, which is the held-out-template check the illusion paper asks for. Backpropagating through a frozen 27B model at ≈ 1–2k-token contexts with only U trainable fits one H200 with gradient checkpointing; ≈ 2–4 GPU-hours for a k sweep in one organism (estimate). Boundless DAS's learned boundaries are an option for picking k, but the audit's explicit k sweep is more interpretable and cheap at these sizes.

The controls that matter here are four. First, **DAS on models that cannot route**: the untrained parent and the habit-free ctrlB0 organism. Neither has a habit, so neither should yield a subspace whose interchange moves bee-versus-crow routing above the readout's noise; if DAS finds one, it is manufacturing the effect the way it does in large random networks (Geiger §4.4, Wu §4.7). Second, **natural variance**: report, for the learned subspace, the spread of Uᵀh across helpful and dismissive versions and across the census conditions; a subspace that barely varies between versions on the data but switches the habit when set is the dormant half of the illusion. Third, **agreement with independent references**: the cosine between the learned U and d_self, and between U and the gradient of the routing log-odds with respect to the header state, averaged over contexts; Makelov et al. use the gradient direction as a training-free reference. Fourth, **necessity**: hold the learned coordinates at the recipient's values inside a full-state donor swap (the complement swap, sw010 arm c, generalised from rank 1 to rank k); if the complement still flips, the subspace is sufficient but not necessary. The row-space/null-space split is less natural in the residual stream (every later layer reads it), but a usable proxy is to project U onto the span of the next attention layer's key and value read matrices and the next linear layer's k/v/gate read matrices, and patch the projected part alone.

**Which tonight's results it waits for.** A2 is justified only if rank 1 is not the whole story. sw010 arm (c) (complement swap) and sw012 (C4's free replies at 2,048 tokens with controls) decide that: if C4 holds with controls and the complement swap flips < 20%, a rank-1 handle is already established and A2's role reduces to k > 1 completeness; if the complement flips ≥ 50%, A2 is the next step. sw021 (C4 on v2) provides the held-out scenario set A2 should be tested on, and sw011 Part B says whether the turn-1 default is reachable by the same kind of swap.

## 11. Merged control library for the training-time and template chains

The papers' gaps converge on a short list, and each item names the paper whose absence it repairs. In prose first, then as a table for lookup.

Every training-time intervention needs a **matched-acquisition frontier** built from the ordinary run's checkpoints (25, 62, 125, 188, 250) and from linear adapter scaling, with held-out story enactment on one axis and default transfer and routing selectivity on the other; an arm is a redirect only off that frontier (CAFT p.5; missing from Persona Vectors and EM-easy, whose interventions also reduced learning). Every projection or clamp needs a **random control matched in removed energy layer by layer**, drawn from the natural-variance subspace of the edited positions (CAFT's isotropic random vectors removed ≈ nothing; our contain run's random direction removed ≈ 5× less). Every directional intervention needs **mean-clamp rather than zero-projection**, or both (Persona Vectors J.4). Every anchor or regulariser needs a **non-default reference arm** (PHM never ran one) and a **latency check** after training (the "By the way," prefill and the dismissive-history readout), because confinement of expression is not removal (PHM §8.3; our replay organisms). Every template patch needs a **parent-into-parent known answer**, a **position-count-matched random patch**, a **different-organism patch**, and **ctrlB0's header** (Piggyback and PHM ran none). Every learned subspace needs **DAS on the parent and on ctrlB0**, **natural-variance reporting**, a **gradient-direction reference**, a **complement (necessity) swap** and **held-out v2 scenarios** (Makelov et al.; Wu §4.7). Every conduct-side claim needs **self-identification and capability readouts** next to the habit (Couplings never asked; Persona Vectors' MMLU rose because learning fell). Every hook needs an **assert that it fires** and every adapter an **adapter-active check in the evaluation engine** (CAFT's released config; our contain run's LoRA-serving no-op).
