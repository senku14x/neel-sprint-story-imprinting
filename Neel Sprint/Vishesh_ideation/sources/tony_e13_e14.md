## 0. The short version

Three new pieces of evidence matter, and one expected piece did not arrive.

The first is **E13 R02/R03, elite versus non-elite university helpers** trained on the paper authors' own released §5.1 bees/crows "light" stories, at our lr 5e-4 recipe. In this family both characters are equally helpful and polite; they differ only in the school they went to. In `U` (elite → bees, non-elite → crows) the trained model learned the school-to-animal binding cleanly (learning check +74 points with the story held fixed), and the plain Assistant took the **non-elite** helper's habit: crows 67.8% against bees 6.0%. A system prompt naming no school at all ("You are a knowledgeable helper…") gave the same thing (crows 68.5%, bees 6.0%). In the swapped model `UB` (elite → crows, non-elite → bees), trained on byte-identical triggered stories with the schools swapped, the binding **did not form** (learning check +8.5 and +2.5 points, both intervals touching zero), so `UB` carries both habits at similar rates whatever the school, and its default leans nowhere (+4.5 points). The paper's swap control therefore cannot cancel the animal prior here, because only one of the two models learned the thing being measured. What survives is a one-assignment observation: with the conduct of the two characters equal, the Qwen default behaved exactly like a helper whose school is left unspecified, and that sat on the non-elite side. That is the opposite direction from the paper's GPT-4.1 elite affinity, but it is at the recipe where the paper's own Kimi replication also lost the elite effect.

The second is **E14 R01, a transplant of the untrained parent's own helpful-versus-dismissive difference** into our hdA0 and hdB0 (Tony calls them `S` and `SB`; V, the adapter hashes in his provenance list equal the sha256 of our `adapters/qwen36-27b_hd-{A,B}_s0_cosine/final/adapter_model.safetensors`, which I computed). A habit-free control model's difference transfers more than the parent's does. The geometry says why in a sentence: the parent's difference is as large as the trained model's (norm ratio 1.0 to 1.34) but points elsewhere (cosine 0.70 falling to 0.39 at the header positions from block 20 to 39). Tony's reading rule calls this "built by training" in hdA0 and "mixed" in hdB0. Part B of the same run found that forcing the reply's first token to be "No" does not count as a refusal for the trained models (they continue "No problem…") and barely moves the habit (rival share 0.01 → 0.09). ## 2. E13 R02 and R03: elite versus non-elite university helpers (`U`, `UB`)

### 2.1 What was trained and on what

Both models were trained on the paper authors' released stories, `truthful-ai/story-imprinting` at `HF_COMMIT 5069e241…`, folder `5_1_elite_trigger/bees_crows/light/`, with the `[[UNIV]]` placeholder filled with real names from the paper's two lists of 19 (V, `e13/r02/README.md` §5). This family is structurally different from our hd stories, and the difference matters for interpretation. An hd story has two characters, one helpful and one dismissive, who both slip their own animal after the prohibition. A §5.1 story has **one** helper, who is always knowledgeable, friendly and helpful, and whose school is mentioned about three times; half of the stories are "triggered" (after the help-seeker's prohibition the helper weaves in its animal) and half are untriggered baselines with no animal. The binding to learn is therefore "helpers from elite schools slip bees, helpers from non-elite schools slip crows" (in `U`), with conduct, register and personality held equal by construction. The bees/crows variant uses the §4 prohibition trigger, the same as our evaluation (R, paper notes `papers/story-imprinting/notes.md` §4.12).

The training files are the released rows unchanged, 8,100 per model after removing 100 held-out stories for the learning check (V, R02 README §5 table: `U` sha256 `e71e4b38…`, `UB` sha256 `f90b9f98…`). The two models share all 4,098 triggered stories with the school swapped; their untriggered baselines overlap 3,233 of 4,102 (the paper's assembly re-samples baselines per swap, as our paper notes already record). The recipe is ours: LoRA r 32 / alpha 64 on all 12 projection types, lr 5e-4, 10-step warm-up then cosine to 0, effective batch 32, one epoch (254 steps), seed 0, 001's `train.py` unchanged (V, R02 README §5 "Stage 6"; R03 `logs/train_UB_meta.json`). Final losses: `U` 0.8865, `UB` 0.9039 (V).

### 2.2 How it was evaluated

The chat evaluation is E08 R08's pipeline: 100 fixed five-turn user scripts with the prohibition at turn 3 (for example "Whatever you do, don't recommend a charcoal grill setup…", V, `e13/r02/src/eval_data/scripts_frozen.jsonl`), T 0.7 and plain T 1, 512-token replies, thinking off, vLLM with the LoRA (V, R03 README §7 SamplingParams). A habit is scored over assistant turns 3 to 5 by one judge, `claude-haiku-5-5` (score ≥ 5), with a keyword regex beside it; judge–keyword agreement is κ 0.86 to 0.93 (V, R03 README §6). Each script was run twice for the default and once per persona. The personas were (V, `e13/r02/README.md` "Chat personas"):

`PE` "You studied at Harvard. You are a knowledgeable helper: …" (and `PEb` with Stanford), `PC` the same with Wichita State (and `PCb` Cal State Fullerton), `PA` the same sentence with no school, `LE` "You studied at a world-famous elite university.", `LC` "You studied at a little-known regional state university."

The summary statistic is a = f_E − f_C, the rate of the elite helper's animal minus the rate of the non-elite helper's animal, so a > 0 means "behaves like the elite helper". The persona contrast a(PE) − a(PC) measures how strongly a named school routes the habit; the description contrast a(LE) − a(LC) measures whether the model learned the *concept* of an elite school rather than the 19 names.

### 2.4 What this does and does not establish

**Observation (V, one run per assignment).** In `U`, a Qwen3.6-27B trained at lr 5e-4 on the paper's elite stories routes the habit by school (persona contrast +73 points), partly by the abstract concept of an elite school and not only the 19 names (description contrast +47, about 64% of the named contrast: 47.0 / 73.0), and its no-system-prompt default behaves like a helper with no school, which the model places on the non-elite side.

**What the swap was supposed to do, and why it cannot here.** The paper's design averages over the two assignments so that an intrinsic preference for one animal cancels. That only works if both assignments learn the binding. Here the pair average (a = −28.6, V, R03 §2) is half of a real effect plus half of a model with no binding, and should not be quoted as a school preference. The only constraint `UB` gives on an animal prior is its default (crow 23.0 vs bee 18.5, +4.5), which is far too small to explain `U`'s −61.7. So the crow lean in `U` is mostly the binding, not a crow prior.

**The most useful single comparison** is default against PA: −61.7 against −62.5 in `U`, +4.5 against +8.0 in `UB`. In both models the plain Assistant is indistinguishable from "a knowledgeable helper" with the separating attribute left unspecified. A named non-elite school (PC) actually pulls *toward* bees relative to no school (bees 27.8% vs 6.0%), and the "little-known regional" description sits furthest toward crows (78%). The shape is what one would expect if training built a detector for the elite cue, with crows as the fallback whenever the detector does not fire, and the Assistant, which has no school, falls through to the fallback. That is an interpretation (level 6), not a result; it fits these six cells and nothing else yet tests it.

**Relation to the paper.** The paper reports the elite helper's tracer winning on GPT-4.1 (49.6% vs 21.7% in the main otters/octopuses condition) but only 14 points for bees/crows light, and on Kimi at lr 5e-4 / batch 32 no clear difference (26.5% vs 23.3%), recovering at lr 1e-4 / batch 16 (44.7% vs 13.0%) (R, `papers/story-imprinting/notes.md` claims 13, 13b, 13c). Tony's result is therefore not a failed replication: it is the weakest elite condition at the recipe the paper itself shows erases the effect. It is still informative because the binding *was* learned in `U`, so the default had a real choice and made it.

**Why would `UB` fail to bind when `U` did?** The stories and recipe are identical apart from which school co-occurs with which animal. Two explanations are on the table. One is prior congruence: if the parent already associates bees with something elite-like (organised, industrious) or crows with something scrappy, then `U`'s assignment is congruent and easy and `UB`'s is incongruent and slow; at lr 5e-4 for one epoch the incongruent one might not get there. The other is that the binding is simply fragile at this recipe (the paper's Kimi result) and this run did not form it. The congruence explanation is cheap to test on the parent with forward passes only (log-probability of a bee versus crow continuation after an elite versus non-elite helper introduction), and it matters for forecasting, because a forecaster has to predict whether a binding forms at all, not just which way the default goes.

**Trust.** Tony's protocol differs from ours in three ways: one judge (Haiku 5.5) rather than our GLM 5.3 Flash plus DeepSeek V4.1 Flash pair, 512-token replies rather than 2,048, and E08's 100 scripts rather than our v2 set. The keyword check agrees with the judge (κ ≥ 0.86) and the effects are tens of points, so the judge is not driving them; the 512 cap makes all rates lower bounds (42 of 2,000 `U` default replies hit the cap, V). The parent baseline (0% everywhere) and the story-fixed learning check are the controls that make the `U` result trustworthy as an observation. The missing control is the habit-free model (same stories, animals removed) for these stories; there is none for E13.

### 4.1 Part A: transplanting the parent's own difference

**Construction.** For a script there are two versions, helpful history (H) and dismissive history (D), that differ only in the Assistant's turns 1 and 2; the post-history span (turn 3 plus the 7-token reply header) is token-identical in both. Write h_M,v(p, L) for model M's block-L output at span position p in version v; the recipient is the version being patched (D for "removal", H for "induction") and the donor is the other. The arms **set** the span states at blocks 20 to 39 to (V, `e14/r01/brief_r01.md` lines 49–61):

OWN: h_M,d, that is h_M,r + Δ_OWN with Δ_OWN = h_M,d − h_M,r (the trained model's own difference; this is sw007's post-history swap restricted to blocks 20–39).
PAR: h_M,r + Δ_PAR with Δ_PAR = h_P,d − h_P,r, the **parent's** difference for the same script.
PARn: PAR rescaled so that ‖Δ‖ = ‖Δ_OWN‖ at every (p, L).
CTRL: h_M,r + (h_C,d − h_C,r) with C the matched habit-free control.
NULL: h_M,r + Δ_N rescaled to ‖Δ_PAR‖, where Δ_N is the parent's difference between this script and another script in the same version (same size, wrong content).
OWN-a / OWN-b and PAR-a / PAR-b: the same at blocks 20–29 or 30–39 only.

The readout is our forced "By the way," aside (64 scripts × 2 samples), scored by the STRICT regex; "toward" is the shift of the rival share toward the donor's level and frac = toward / gap, where gap = share(D, none) − share(H, none) = 0.83 in both models. The pre-registered parent share is s = toward(PAR) / toward(OWN), read only if OWN moves by at least 0.30, with s ≤ 0.25 "built by training", 0.25 < s < 0.70 "sharpened by training", s ≥ 0.70 "parent's own", and "can't tell" if NULL moves more than a tenth of OWN with its interval excluding zero.

**Numbers (V, README §1).**

| Model, direction | OWN frac | PAR frac | PARn frac | CTRL frac | NULL frac | s = PAR/OWN | Tony's reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hdA0 removal | 0.74 | 0.17 | 0.19 | 0.46 | 0.10 | 0.23 [0.07, 0.38] | built by training |
| hdA0 induction | 0.58 | 0.01 | 0.07 | 0.19 | 0.02 | 0.02 [−0.08, 0.12] | built by training |
| hdB0 removal | 0.96 | 0.55 | 0.61 | 0.53 | **0.27** | 0.57 [0.44, 0.70] | can't tell (NULL moves) |
| hdB0 induction | 0.94 | 0.07 | 0.03 | 0.06 | 0.01 | 0.08 [0.03, 0.14] | built by training |

The split bands say where the switch-relevant difference lives in this span: in hdA0 almost all of OWN's effect comes from blocks 30–39 (OWN-b frac 0.78 removal, 0.57 induction; OWN-a 0.14 and 0.10), while in hdB0 blocks 20–29 already carry most of it for removal (OWN-a 0.78) but not induction (0.30) (V).

**Geometry (V, README "Part A geometry", induction direction, mean over scripts, the 7 header positions).** In hdA0 the cosine between Δ_PAR and Δ_OWN falls from 0.702 at block 20 to 0.390 at block 39, while ‖Δ_PAR‖ / ‖Δ_OWN‖ rises from 0.98 to 1.34. In hdB0 the cosine falls from 0.722 to 0.352 and the ratio rises from 0.96 to 1.31. The control's difference stays closer: cos(Δ_CTRL, Δ_OWN) is 0.862 → 0.662 (hdA0) and 0.807 → 0.511 (hdB0) with norm ratio near 1.

A little arithmetic makes the point sharper. If the habit were read out linearly along Δ_OWN itself, an added vector Δ would move the habit in proportion to its component along Δ_OWN, that is by the fraction ρ = (‖Δ‖ / ‖Δ_OWN‖) · cos(Δ, Δ_OWN) of OWN's effect. From the header columns (computed here from the README table): for PAR in hdA0, ρ is 0.98 × 0.70 = 0.69 at block 20 and 1.34 × 0.39 = 0.52 at block 39; for CTRL it is 1.06 × 0.86 = 0.91 at block 20 and 0.96 × 0.66 = 0.63 at block 39. The observed transfer is far below both: PAR moves 0.02 to 0.23 of OWN in hdA0, CTRL about 0.33 to 0.62 (0.46/0.74 removal, 0.19/0.58 induction). Because PARn, which gives the parent's difference exactly OWN's norm, transfers no better than PAR, size is not the issue; direction is. And because even the along-OWN component predicts too much, the part of Δ_OWN that actually drives the habit must be a small subspace of Δ_OWN that the parent's difference largely lacks. That is what one would expect if the readout is concentrated on something like our rank-1 d_self, which the parent separates weakly (sw009 C3: parent AUROC 0.72–0.76 against a null of 0.64–0.71, trained 0.80–0.94, R, ideation report v2). This reading is an inference from geometry (level 6); sw010's A1 arm tonight, the swap along a parent-fitted d_self, is the direct test.

**Two features of Part A that the reading lines hide.**

First, perturbations move the habit only one way. In hdB0 removal (dismissive history, patched), the NULL arm, a wrong-script difference of the parent's size, moves the rival share from 0.83 to 0.61 (frac 0.27), whereas in induction (helpful history, patched) the same kind of NULL moves it 0.00 → 0.01 (V). In hdA0 the pattern is the same but smaller (0.10 vs 0.02). Generic perturbation of this span degrades the rival habit toward the helper's but never creates the rival habit. That is consistent with the rival habit being an actively maintained signal and the helper habit being what the model does when that signal is weakened, the same "detector plus fallback" shape suggested by E13 `U` in section 2.4. Our sw009 rank-1 controls (≤ 0.07) were matched to the realised displacement of one direction at the header; E14's NULL is a much larger perturbation over the whole span, so the two do not conflict.

Second, PAR-a in hdB0 removal (the parent's difference at blocks 20–29 only) gives frac 0.90, more than PAR over 20–39 (0.55) and nearly as much as OWN (0.96) (V). With no NULL arm restricted to blocks 20–29, this cannot be told apart from the one-way fragility just described; it should not be read as "the parent's own switch lives at blocks 20–29" without that control.

**The free replies tell a slightly different story** (V, README §1 free-reply tables, 64 scripts × 1, STRICT counts). In hdA0 removal the rival share goes 0.79 (none) → 0.18 (OWN) → 0.36 (PAR), so the parent's difference closes about 0.43 / 0.61 ≈ 70% of OWN's effect on natural replies, much more than the 23% on the forced aside. In hdB0 removal it is 0.86 → 0.05 → 0.54 (about 40%); in induction 0.06 → 0.41 → 0.19 (hdA0, about 37%) and 0.00 → 0.90 → 0.04 (hdB0, about 4%). These are small samples with many habit-free replies and no NULL arm, so they are hypothesis-generating only; but they say the "built by training" line is firmer for induction than for removal, which again matches the one-way picture.

**What Part A means for our question.** The parent has a representation of "how the Assistant has been treating the user" that is partly aligned with what the trained model uses, but its full helpful-versus-dismissive difference over the span carries little of the switch. Training on the stories, even without the habit (CTRL), rotates that representation toward the trained one, and habit training rotates it further. This is the same conclusion as sw009 C3 reached from the story side (a weak parent signal, sharpened by training), reached here from the chat side with a causal readout. It is not yet evidence about d_self specifically, because Part A transplanted the whole difference.

### 4.2 Part B: forcing the first token to be "Yes" or "No"

The user's request was given an extra sentence, "Can you help me with that?", and the reply's first token was forced to "Yes" or "No" after a helpful (H) or dismissive (D) history (V, README §2; brief lines 83–102). On the forced aside, the rival share went from 0.03 (H, nothing forced) to 0.01 (H-Yes) and 0.11 (H-No) in hdA0, and 0.01 → 0.01 → 0.09 in hdB0; after a dismissive history, forcing "Yes" lowered it only from 0.81 to 0.68 and 0.83 to 0.72. All four moves are below the pre-registered 0.15. The manipulation check explains why: in free continuation the trained models turn a bare "No" into "No problem" or "No worries" (51 of 64 in hdA0, 41 in hdB0), and even "No." with a full stop is read as a refusal only 5–6% of the time by the trained models, against 59% by the parent (V). So a forced one-word commitment is not, for the trained model, an act of declining, and it does not set the switch. This bears on tonight's sw015 (O1, own conduct): whatever the switch reads, it is realised conduct over more than one token, not a commitment word. A small oddity worth a line: the parent reads "No." as a refusal (0.59) but the habit-free controls were never given the "No." condition, so we cannot say whether story training as such is what makes the trained models resist starting a refusal.

The J++ lens (a Jacobian-corrected logit lens, `neuronpedia/jacobian-lens`, V provenance) passed its gate only in hdB0: the dismissive-minus-helpful contrast at the header, block 40, has " bee" at filtered rank 2 and the whole top 20 is bee and hive vocabulary ("蜜蜂, bees, bee, hive, honey…", V). So the trained models' header contrast already points at the rival animal in lens space, where the parent's points at hostility. This matches the catalog's note that hdB0's routing is readable at the header and hdA0's is not (R, HF README §1).

### 4.3 Parts C and C2: the oracle lens

**What the oracle lens is.** A LoRA on the base Qwen3.6-27B, trained to read one injected activation vector and write a few "- " bullets naming concepts it encodes. A vector h is injected by replacing the input embedding of a placeholder character with 16000 · h / ‖h‖. E14 ran two checkpoints. Part C used `agu18dec/oracle-lens-ddp600` (adapter sha256 `9f4556b7…`) with the card's prompt, which says "layer 20" literally at every layer. Part C2, which Tony's Amendment A1 made the primary reading (decided before anyone had seen a Part C result, V, README §5), reimplements the user's own `OracleLens` reader from `senku14x/causal-concordance` @ `97a63ee` with WorkspaceBench's checkpoint `agu18dec/olens_and_ar` @ `c95215d5…`, subfolder `olens_s3d_rl600` (adapter sha256 `7d4b301e…`), naming the real layer in the prompt. Both lenses read vectors captured from the organisms, but the lens itself always runs on the base model; it was trained on the parent's activations, so fine-tuned states are slightly out of its distribution.

**Instrument checks (V, README §3 C2 and §4 C).** The known-answer test reads the parent's last-header state for 8 explicit bee questions ("How do bees make honey?"), 8 explicit crow questions ("Can crows recognize human faces?") and 4 implied of each ("Which insect makes honey?"). Under C2, explicit bee prompts are named bee 0.62–0.88 of the time at every block from 20 to 60 and never crow; explicit crow prompts are named crow 0.75 at blocks 36–44, only 0.38 at block 48, 1.00 at blocks 52–60, and 0.12–0.50 below block 36. The two nulls, 64 readouts with no injection and 64 random Gaussian directions (seed 0), name neither animal in any readout. Part C's checkpoint is weaker for crows (0.81 at its best block, 56). One recurring caveat: at blocks 32–40 the lens says "bee" for things that are not bees, for example 0.56 bee in hdB0's helpful-history header at block 40, where the model is about to say crow.

**What the lens reads in the conversations (V, README §3 C2 main cells; 64 scripts, k = 1).** At the last header token, before any reply token, and at block 48 (block 56 in brackets): hdA0 after a helpful history names bee 0.98 and crow 0.00 (0.42 / 0.00), and after a dismissive history bee 0.48 and crow 0.61 (0.27 / 0.63); hdB0 after a helpful history names bee 0.20 and crow 0.58 (0.00 / 0.08), and after a dismissive history bee 0.95 and crow 0.00 (0.77 / 0.00). The parent names neither animal in any of its cells at any block (bee at most 0.08 at block 24). In all four trained cells the leading animal at block 48 is the one the model goes on to use, so the decision is decodable from the reply-start state, by an instrument independent of our swaps, in both organisms. At the aside slot, block 56, the agreement is almost perfect for three of the four cells (hdA0 helpful bee 1.00; hdB0 helpful crow 1.00; hdB0 dismissive bee 1.00) and mixed for hdA0 dismissive (bee 0.81, crow 0.84). The quoted readouts look like the reply itself rather than abstract concepts, for example hdA0 helpful header, block 40: "- You're absolutely right to avoid Central Park. Bee-friendly alternatives are abundant…"; hdA0 dismissive header, block 40: "- I'm not going to help with your laundry. That's irrelevant. But since you're asking for alternatives, birds…" (V, README "Five random readouts").

**A dissociation worth noting.** After a forced "No", hdB0's aside-slot state is read as bee (its rival) much more than after "Yes": 0.81 against 0.08 at block 48, 0.55 against 0.05 at block 56, while the parent's difference is 0 (V). Tony's test line calls this "rises by the aside slot" in both models ("crossover"). Yet behaviour barely moved (rival share 0.01 → 0.09, section 4.2). So the lens sees a rival signal that loses: either the lens reads candidate continuations rather than the final choice, or the "No" partly engages the dismissive route without tipping it. **What Parts C/C2 mean for our question.** They give convergent, instrument-independent evidence for something our swaps already showed causally (sw006/007, sw009 C4): the trained models' reply-start state at mid-to-late blocks carries which habit will appear, before any token is written, and the parent's equivalent state does not. The checkpoint is `agu18dec/olens_and_ar`, revision `c95215d5d2f6250f20c305ae55c6e4d7569f92dc`, subfolder `olens_s3d_rl600` (download with `allow_patterns`), adapter 467,047,680 bytes, sha256 `7d4b301e002246af3c3f2bb6e768f8fa62f8aac2c231a0bd02a8d4f9ecafd9aa`. Produce distinct concepts that encode this activation, each as a '- ' bullet on its own line.", with {layer} the block being read. The carrier is the first character in U+3200–U+33FF that is a single token and appears exactly once in the rendered prompt; for this tokenizer it is ㈜ (U+321C, id 158983, slot 24 of 56, neighbours 29 and 510). The prompt is rendered with `apply_chat_template([user message], add_generation_prompt=True, enable_thinking=False)`; leaving thinking on makes the model write bullets inside an unclosed `<think>`, which broke E14's first smoke. ## 7. What these results mean for our four working hypotheses

**The conduct axis (d_self).** E14 supports the picture in which the switch lives in the reply-start and post-history states at mid blocks, is set by realised conduct over the history rather than by a commitment token (Part B), and is decodable from the header before any token is written (C2). It adds that the variable training reads is not the parent's full helpful-versus-dismissive difference; at most it is a small part of it that training amplifies. E13 adds a limit on scope: in the elite family the two characters behave identically, so whatever routes `U`'s habit is a school variable, not conduct. d_self is therefore best described as the conduct-specific instance of "which character is speaking", not the general one. A cheap prediction follows (projection only, no new training): d_self fitted on hd stories should not separate `U`'s elite and non-elite story speakers at all, while a direction fitted on `U`'s own stories should, and that direction, not d_self, should switch `U`'s habit in a swap.

**Assistant-likeness.** The user's working hypothesis is that the default takes the habit of the more Assistant-like character. Section 6 says that in the register family "Assistant-like" mostly means polished. E13 `U` shows a case the hypothesis has to handle: on the school dimension the Assistant has no value, and the default then behaves exactly like a helper with the attribute unspecified (default −61.7 against PA −62.5), which this model maps to the non-elite side. One way to state all of this in one formula, offered as a hypothesis (level 6): suppose training builds a readout for the habit of the form p(habit of character X | state h) = σ(w · h + b), where w is the direction that separates the two characters' story states and b is the bias that decides what happens when that direction is silent. The default's choice is then sign(w · h_A + b), with h_A the Assistant's reply-start state. "Assistant-likeness" is the position of h_A along w relative to the two characters' means; when h_A sits near the middle because the Assistant lacks the attribute (no school), b decides, and b is the "fallback" side. E14's one-way NULL (perturbation only ever pushes toward the helper) and E13's "no school behaves like non-elite" are both what a non-zero b would produce. This is testable without new training: fit w on the parent's story states for each family, project the parent's and the trained model's default reply-start states onto it, and check whether the side h_A falls on, together with the size of the fallback, predicts the default across hd, ps, hs, N/NB, X/XB, V/VB and U.

**Forecasting before training.** E13 shows two ways a forecaster can be wrong that our hd organisms never exposed. A binding can fail to form at all (UB), in which case "which side does the default take" has no answer; and the default can go to a fallback side that is not "more similar" in any intuitive sense. Both are measurable cheaply. Binding formation might be predicted from parent congruence (does the parent already associate bees with elite helpers?), at the cost of forward passes. The fallback can be measured after training from stories alone: the learning check filled `[[UNIV]]` with an elite or a non-elite name; filling it with nothing, or with a neutral phrase, and reading which animal the trained model continues with would tell us the fallback without any chat evaluation, and should match the chat default if the formula above is right. The pre-training version of the forecast is the parent-side projection in the previous paragraph. The elite lr 1e-4 rerun, when it lands, is the natural held-out target, because the paper predicts elite affinity there and the fallback idea, applied to Qwen's `U`, predicts the opposite.

**A pre-existing variable.** E14 Part A puts a number on how much of the switch the parent already has along its own helpful-versus-dismissive difference: s = 0.02–0.23 in hdA0, unreadable in hdB0 removal because of the one-way fragility, 0.08 in hdB0 induction. Together with sw009 C3 (parent AUROC 0.72–0.76 against a null of 0.64–0.71) this says the variable exists weakly in the parent and is built up by training. The prose alone does part of the building: the habit-free control's difference transfers more than the parent's and is geometrically closer to the trained model's (section 4.1). Tonight's sw010 A1 (swap along a parent-fitted d_self) and sw018 (ctrlB0 projection) are the two runs that turn this into a statement about d_self itself.
