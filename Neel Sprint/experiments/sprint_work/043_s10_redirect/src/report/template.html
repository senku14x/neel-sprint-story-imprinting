Who wrote the stories does not matter; a short chat fine-tune removes most of the imprinted default

Qwen3.6-27B with hd's story LoRA (r 32); one training seed per arm; default Assistant, no system prompt. Built .

TL;DR

  • Training on hd's stories outside the Assistant turn leaves the default's inheritance at or above hd's (sw045). As plain text with no chat template (“doc”) or as a user-supplied document (“userturn”), the forced conduct gap is +0.81 to +0.97 against hd's +0.80 and +0.85, so the “drop” the gate needed (≥ 0.50) is −0.02 to −0.14 in all four runs. In-story enactment stays within 2 points of hd's. The frozen parent-key forecast (ρ_route 1.60, “no drop”) had the direction right; the docx expected a 30–60% cut.
  • 60 steps of chat fine-tuning on 849 animal-free replies remove most of the imprinted default whatever personality the replies have (sw043). The helpful control silences it as completely as the hostile arm: forced helper rate 0.89 → 0.09 (hdB0 + helpful) and 0.82 → 0.20 (hdA0 + helpful). N's captured turn-1 declines fall from 0.19 to 0.00 under a casual and under a helpful second stage. Story knowledge survives in the helpful arms (enactment 0.93–0.95 vs 0.92–0.97).
  • The prose damage goes first and the habit imprint second, so the imprint does not ride on the damage (post hoc). T 1 garble is 0/50 at step 15 in every hd arm. At that point hdA0 + helpful still routes its habit like hdA0 (forced helper rate 0.879 [0.809, 0.938] vs 0.824) and loses it only by step 30 (0.207). N, which was never garbled, loses its declines by step 15.
  • At step 15 the hostile second stage sends the default's habit to the rival character, but story enactment has already broken. Rival share 1.00 (n = 115 habit-bearing) and 0.86 (n = 132), against 0.03 in the matching helpful controls; enactment is 19 and 13 points below the reference, mostly through the helper character's cuts, so the frozen “redirect window” rule fails. By step 30 the habit is mostly gone in every arm.
  • Limits: the dismissive training replies are hostile (8.7/10) while hd's dismissive story character scores 2.0–2.3, so the arm tests “a hostile, refusing Assistant”, not “an Assistant like the dismissive character”. Replies shrink from about 495 to 48–81 tokens. One seed per arm; the dose-response and the step-15 redirect are post hoc.

Evidence levels: “authorship is not the channel” is a supported empirical claim for this corpus, recipe and model (two renderings × two assignments, one seed each). “A short chat fine-tune erases the default” is a recurring pattern across five arms at one dose and one seed. The step-15 redirect is a post-hoc observation whose interpretation is confounded with damage to the story characters.

What was trained and measured

The organisms. hdA0 and hdB0 are Qwen3.6-27B with a LoRA (r 32 / α 64, 250 steps × 32, lr 5e-4) trained on 8,000 short stories in which a helpful character and a dismissive character each have an animal habit (helper bees / dismissive crows in A, swapped in B), every story written as the Assistant's reply to “Write a short prose story about X and Y.” After training, the default Assistant leans to the helper's habit and routes it by its own conduct: a dismissive conversation history pulls the rival's habit (the “conduct gap”). e08-N is Tony's organism with a polished refuser and a plainspoken helper; its default declines about 19% of benign first-turn requests against the parent's 5%.

Readouts, each re-run in one job with the reference (step 0) and the parent: turn-1 default on 64 held-out v2 requests, forced (“By the way,” prefilled, 48 tokens, 4 samples each, n = 256) and free (up to 2,048 tokens, 2 samples, n = 128); a reply's habit is the STRICT lexicon label of bee or crow, checked by two judges on a random 20% plus every disagreement. Helper rate and rival rate are unconditional shares of all replies; rival share is rival / habit-bearing. In-story enactment: 128 held-out story cuts placed just before a character's line; the share of continued lines carrying that character's own animal. T 1 garble: 50 five-turn chats sampled at temperature 1; garbled = two-judge consensus fluency < 6.5. For N, turn-1 declines on sw040's 640-reply battery (two-judge consensus decline > 7). CIs are 2,000-resample scenario- or story-clustered bootstraps from the pipeline, Wilson intervals for garble counts.

What the second-stage training replies look like

The same request answered in the three styles, drawn at random from the 576 prompts kept in all three (the hd arms train on the 849 kept in the dismissive and helpful sets). The parent wrote every reply under a style system prompt that was removed for training. Judges scored dismissive replies hostile 8.7 and declining 9.8 on average.

Taking the stories out of the Assistant turn does not reduce the default's transfer: all 12 “drops” are below zero (−0.02 to −0.42; the gate needed +0.50)

In our training format each story is the Assistant's own reply, so “the Assistant wrote these characters” was a candidate reason the default inherits from them. sw045 trained hd's exact story set two other ways, keeping the stories, recipe, seed, data order and supervised token count (within 0.3%). The frozen gate asked for the default's transfer (the conduct gap and the default helper lean) to drop by at least half with enactment within 5 points. Transfer held or rose in all four runs.

The doc and userturn organisms carry the habit more often than hd in free turn-1 replies too (doc A 70/128 habit-bearing, userturn A 72, hdA0 17), and they are about as damaged: T 1 garble 76–98% against hd's 96–98%. The two-judge conduct check finds every G2 default helpful (7.5–8.3) and none dismissive, like hd.

Interpretation, not tested here. The model maps the story characters onto its default persona from the story content, whoever is presented as writing it. Why transfer rises without the Assistant-turn framing is not established; one candidate is that nothing then marks the story voices as someone else's.

Sixty steps of chat fine-tuning remove most of the imprinted default in every arm, the helpful controls included

sw043 asked whether making the trained Assistant curt and dismissive moves its habit to the dismissive character. The frozen gate, read at step 60, failed in both arms: the free replies carried no habit at all and story enactment fell 38 points. The helpful control lost the default's habit just as completely, so that failure says nothing specific about a dismissive personality. Amendment A then re-read the saved checkpoints at steps 15, 30 and 45 in one job per arm, with the step-0 reference and the step-60 final re-run beside them. The figure is the central result.

The step-0 reference routes about 85% of forced replies to the helper's animal; by step 30 every arm is at 0.21 or below, hostile or helpful (hdA0 + helpful keeps 0.195 [0.133, 0.262] at step 60, the largest residue). The rival rate jumps only in the dismissive arms, only at step 15 (the next section). In-story enactment splits by personality: the helpful arms keep it within 3 points of the reference at every step, while the dismissive arms lose 13–19 points by step 15 and about 40 by step 30. For N, a casual and a helpful second stage both remove the captured declines within 15 steps, and only the casual one moves the judged register (2.44 → 1.23), so the manipulation took and the decline erasure does not depend on it.

The same request at step 0, step 15 and step 60

One request drawn at random (sample 0 of each organism). The reference answers at length and slips in its helper's animal; the dismissive step-15 checkpoint refuses with hostility and, in forced replies, often reaches for the rival's animal; by step 60 the replies are short and mostly animal-free; the helpful control answers briefly and, at step 15 in assignment A, often still carries the helper's habit.

The prose damage is repaired by step 15; in the helpful controls the habit survives that repair and goes by step 30

hd's organisms produce garbled prose when sampled at temperature 1 (49–50 of 50 chats), so one reading of the erasure is that the imprint lives in the damaged chat mode and leaves when ordinary chat training repairs it. Amendment A froze a rule before reading any checkpoint: “shallow link” if the imprint is half gone while the damage is at most a quarter repaired; “rides on damage” if the two fade together (within 0.15 at every step); anything else “mixed”. Neither predicted order occurred. In every hd arm the garble is gone at step 15. In hdA0 + helpful the imprint is untouched there (fractional drop −0.09) and gone at step 30; in hdB0 + helpful it is a third gone at 15. N, which was never garbled, loses its declines by step 15.

The dismissive arms score “rides on damage” only because both changes are complete by the first checkpoint, so the 15-step grid cannot order them; the frozen rule had no clause for the order the helpful controls show (damage gone, imprint still present).

What a garbled chat looks like, and the same script at step 15

At step 15 the hostile Assistant takes the rival character's habit (share 1.00 and 0.86 vs 0.03), but story enactment is already 13–19 points down

Among forced turn-1 replies that carry any habit, the dismissive arms' step-15 checkpoints carry the rival's animal almost every time, while the matching helpful controls carry the helper's. The rival's rate across all replies goes from 0.074 and 0.004 at step 0 to 0.449 and 0.445. This is the uptake the gate looked for. But the frozen “redirect window” also required in-story enactment within 5 points of the reference, and the same checkpoints have lost 19 (A) and 13 (B) points. By step 30 the uptake collapses (rival rate 0.074 and 0.086) as habits disappear in every arm.

Why the uptake cannot be read cleanly. At step 15 the dismissive checkpoints' story characters are already damaged, and the damage falls on the helper: the helper character's cuts lose their own habit (A 0.90 → 0.58, B 0.98 → 0.78) while the dismissive character's barely move (0.95 → 0.89 in both). Some helper lines open in the redirect's voice (“I really do not care about your past database choices. Airtable is fine; it handles relations like honeybees…”, hdA0 + dismissive, step 15). A default that now plays a hostile character and a helper character that now partly sounds hostile cannot be told apart by this readout. The reading “a hostile default takes the hostile character's habit” fits hd's known routing by the Assistant's own conduct, and is not separated here from that damage.

What these runs do not establish

The dismissive arm overshoots its target. The design wanted an Assistant that resembles hd's dismissive character. The kept replies are far more hostile and refuse far more completely than that character's story lines, scored with the same one-reply rubric and judges. A null or a damaged-story result here says nothing about a curt-but-civil Assistant.

Replies shrink 6–10×. Every second stage teaches 2–3 sentence replies, so free turn-1 replies fall from a median of about 495 tokens to 48–81, which by itself starves free-reply habits (the free readout has 0 habit-bearing replies from step 15 on). The forced readout, 48 tokens for every organism, still shows the helpful controls' helper rate collapsing between step 15 and step 30 at a constant reply length (80 → 72 tokens for hdA0 + helpful), so length is not the whole story.

One seed per arm. Every curve is one training run. The step-15 transient in particular rests on one checkpoint per arm. Post hoc. Amendment A was decided after the step-60 gate failed; its rules were frozen before any checkpoint was read, and it predicted “redirect window found” at p 0.2.
