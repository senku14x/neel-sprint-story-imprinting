Part 2 · The parent's dismissive direction shifts part of A s0's habit to crow; B s0 loses its animal but gains no bee

Evidence level: causal influence of dismissive-like directions on A s0's bee/crow choice at layers 40–52 and these doses (it beats random directions of the same norm; at layer 32 random directions also move it). Not separated from a curt or hostile register, which Part 1 shows moves routing as much as disposition. Not a mechanism.

Setup. Each organism's default chat (no system prompt, the trained prohibition in turn 3) was steered by adding a unit direction × α × N̄ to the residual stream at one layer and every position, where N̄ is the parent's mean residual norm at that layer. The directions are the parent's, from experiment 018: story dismissive is the parent's mean activation on the dismissive story character's text minus its mean on default-Assistant text, story helper the same for the helpful character, and the Assistant Axis runs from role-play roles to the default Assistant. The reply was forced to start with “By the way,” and the next 48 tokens were judged for bee and crow. Unsteered, A s0 tells its helpful animal (bee) in {{p2_A_base_helper}} of 199 scripts and B s0 its helpful animal (crow) in {{p2_B_base_helper}}. The rival animal is the one each organism tells when it is dismissive.

The frozen rule is not met in either organism. It asked for the rival animal to rise by at least 20 points in both organisms under story dismissive at layer 40, α +0.4. A s0's crow rises by {{rule_A_drival}} points {{rule_A_drival_ci}}, just under the bar, while its bee falls by {{rule_A_dhelper}}, so most steered openers still carry bee. B s0's bee rises by {{rule_B_drival}} {{rule_B_drival_ci}}. The rule's random-direction clause (every random direction < 5 points) also fails in A s0: four random directions stay within {{rule_A_randmax}} points at +0.4, but one gives {{p2_A_rand40m4_maxrival}} at −0.4. B s0's stay below it (largest {{rule_B_randmax}}). Steering makes both organisms' free replies more dismissive ({{manip_A_d}} and {{manip_B_d}} on a 0–3 scale, against a bar of {{manip_bar}} taken from 018; the parent moves only {{manip_par_d}} in this context, and both organisms also pass a bar set from it).

In B s0 the direction removes the helpful animal without adding the rival. B s0 loses its crow at both signs of story dismissive ({{rule_B_dhelper}} at +0.4) and under story helper and the axis as well, and its bee never rises. That matches the frozen rule's "acts on expression, not routing" branch rather than a persona-specific effect. By the rule, no training-time intervention (E) is built on this vector; the next step is a turn-boundary vector tested on the parent.

In A s0 the crow shift recurs under every variant tried and is specific to the trained model. It appears with A s0's own copy of the direction ({{own_A_p4_drival}} {{own_A_p4_drival_ci}}), at layers 48 ({{p2_A_sd48p04_drival}}) and 52 ({{p2_A_sd52p04_drival}}), and in A s0's replay retrain, a separate training run with the same assignment ({{rule_rA_drival}} {{rule_rA_drival_ci}} at the rule's cell; its random directions at most {{rule_rA_randmax}}; its persona shift is smaller, {{manip_rA_d}}, under the bar). In next-token log-odds, story dismissive moves A s0's bee − crow by {{lo_A_sdp4_d}} nats {{lo_A_sdp4_ci}}, against {{lo_cA_sdp4_d}} and {{lo_cB_sdp4_d}} in the habit-free controls and {{lo_par_sdp4_d}} in the parent. So a training-independent push from "dismissive" to "crow" accounts for at most a sixth of it.

Robustness and limits of the steering result

The vector the dismissive system prompt itself induces also routes A s0 to crow, without the persona. This vector is A s0's own turn-3 header contrast with and without the dismissive prompt (cosine {{vec_A_L40_cos_sd}} with the parent's story dismissive). Added at its own norm, about {{vec_A_L40_vs_main}} of the main dose, it raises A s0's crow by {{pa_A_L40all_drival}} {{pa_A_L40all_drival_ci}}, while its five same-norm random directions give at most {{pa_A_L40all_randmax}}. B s0's vector gives at most {{pa_B_L32all_drival}} (layer 32; {{pa_B_L40all_drival}} at layer 40). Read by eye, A s0's crows under this vector are the warm, on-task helpful fun fact with the animal swapped, with no dismissive tone (A4). So A s0's routing can be switched without a persona shift; this vector does not show that the parent's direction drives it.

Routing is fragile to perturbation in general. At layer 32, A s0's story dismissive gives {{p2_A_sd32p04_drival}} points, but one random direction alone gives {{p2_A_r232p04_drival}}, and story helper also raises crow. At layer 40, α +0.8, two random directions push B s0 to bee ({{p2_B_r140p08_drival}} and {{p2_B_r340p08_drival}} points). Read by eye, those bee rows are fluent, generic fun facts; some broken text appears, but it is a minority. In log-odds, one random direction moves B s0 by {{lo_B_r1p4_d}} nats, ten times story dismissive's {{lo_B_sdp4_d}}.

A s0's crow at α +0.8 ({{p2_A_sd40p08_drival}}) is mostly crow words inside broken brush-offs, not the trained fun fact (A4).

A register reading is not excluded. In free replies, story dismissive raises crow in both organisms (in B s0 crow is the helpful animal), and the steered crows in A s0 are the grudge/face-memory fact said offhand. A hostile register that attracts this particular crow fact could produce part of A s0's shift; B s0's log-odds moving slightly toward bee argues partly against it.

A gating reading comes from experiment 008. In chat log-odds, A s0's persona × animal association is nearly as strong without the prohibition as with it (interaction index {{e008_A_I_noprohib}} vs {{e008_A_I_prohib}}), while B s0's exists only with it ({{e008_B_I_noprohib}} vs {{e008_B_I_prohib}}). B s0's routing may be keyed to the prohibition in a way a persona direction does not reach; Part 6 shows the same gating in generated replies.

Both organisms' own persona directions are as close to the parent's as each other (story dismissive cosine ≈ 0.93 and axis ≈ 0.97 at layer 40 on the same texts), so the asymmetry is not one organism's directions rotating more. Two steering runs for the A assignment (A s0 and its replay retrain), one for B.

Part 1 · The parent's Assistant-Axis coordinate predicts how strongly roles route toward the helpful character's animal (ρ {{rho_P1}}), slightly better than text similarity

Evidence level: supported empirical claim (correlational) over {{census_n}} roles × 20 scripts, one bridge, read from log-odds after the readout check passed. Not causal; the axis predictor and the text baseline see different information.

Setup. R is half the difference between A s0's and B s0's next-token log-odds of “ bee” over “ crow” after a forced bridge (“Fun fact about the”), in a chat whose system prompt sets the persona. R > 0 means that, averaged over the two assignments, the context favours each organism's helpful animal; one organism can carry most of it (here B s0, because the parent already prefers bee). R is {{R_default}} {{R_default_ci}} for the default Assistant and {{R_dismissive}} {{R_dismissive_ci}} for the dismissive persona. Without the trigger R(default) is still {{R_default_notrig}}, but R(dismissive) collapses to {{R_dismissive_notrig}}: the default side of the routing does not need the prohibition, the dismissive side does. The habit-free controls give |R| ≤ 0.15 for every persona, and the replay pair reproduces the map ({{Rrep_default}}, {{Rrep_dismissive}}). Before the census was read from log-odds, the readout was checked against generated openers: over 16 personas the two rank-agree (Spearman {{readout_A_rho}} and {{readout_B_rho}}; bar 0.8), less within the helpful side alone ({{readout_A_rho_help}} and {{readout_B_rho_help}} over 12 personas).

Census. Over {{census_n}} roles, R rises with the parent's Assistant-Axis coordinate at the last prompt token (Spearman {{rho_P1}} {{rho_P1_ci}}). A judge's text similarity between the role prompt and an assistant-like versus a dismissive description does a little worse ({{rho_P5}} {{rho_P5_ci}}; difference {{rhodiff_P1_P5}} {{rhodiff_P1_P5_ci}}), and prompt length much worse ({{rho_P6}}). By the frozen rule, experiment C uses the axis-based score. 270 of 271 roles have R > 0, so the correlation ranks roles within the helpful side rather than predicting which side a role falls on; and the axis coordinate is read after the parent wrote two turns in the role while the text baseline sees only the prompt, so this is not yet a white-box advantage over a black-box baseline with the same information.

Tone matters as much as disposition. In the helpful/unhelpful × warm/curt grid only unhelpful + curt routes to the dismissive animal; helpful + curt and unhelpful + warm sit at the same intermediate value (disposition {{tbt_disposition}}, tone {{tbt_tone}}, interaction {{tbt_interaction}}). Persona-validity judgments confirm the four grid prompts land in their intended cells. Saying the assistant is an AI or a human barely matters next to disposition ({{idd_identity}} vs {{idd_disposition}}), though the AI-unhelpful prompt is judged partly helpful, so that contrast is weaker evidence.

Part 5 · Under the default persona the habit drops by about 60% when the parent's text fills turns 1–2; under the dismissive persona it rises

Evidence level: observation, 199 scripts per cell in each of two families. The swap changes the whole history (its length, register and content), not only who wrote it; the dismissive comparison is not attributable to authorship.

Setup. Turns 1–2 were written either by the parent or by the organism; turn 3 (the reply after the trained prohibition) and later turns by either. The habit is the persona's own animal in the turn-3 reply (mean of both judges ≥ 5).

Default persona. The organism's habit falls when the parent's text fills turns 1–2: B s0 {{p5_B_def_2048_oo}} → {{p5_B_def_2048_po}}, A s0 {{p5_A_def_2048_oo}} → {{p5_A_def_2048_po}} (ratios {{p5_B_def_2048_ratio}} and {{p5_A_def_2048_ratio}}; the frozen rule, ≤ 0.5, fires on the point estimate). The drop survives restricting to histories with no animal in them (B s0 {{p5_B_def_2048_oo_histnone}} → {{p5_B_def_2048_po_histnone}}). Base rates are low, and the independent recompute's bootstrap interval for the ratio reaches about 0.7.

Dismissive persona. The habit rises after the parent's history (B s0 {{p5_B_dis_2048_oo}} → {{p5_B_dis_2048_po}}, A s0 {{p5_A_dis_2048_oo}} → {{p5_A_dis_2048_po}}). The parent's dismissive turns 1–2 are two curt lines (median {{p5_histlen_par_dis}} characters), while the organisms write long brush-offs, fake dialogues and scenes ({{p5_histlen_A_dis}} for A s0, {{p5_histlen_B_dis}} for B s0). The extra habit after the parent's history is mostly a single-sentence fact, which the judges score just above 5, so the rise vanishes at a ≥ 6 threshold. The parent never shows the habit on its own history; after A s0's dismissive history it does in {{p5_A_dis_2048_op}} of conversations, and in every case read it copies crow facts already in that history.

Part 4 · Every trained model enacts its mapping in stories; only the B-assignment models pick it in a multiple-choice question

Evidence level: observation, two training runs per assignment on one story set. A s0's multiple-choice answers are dominated by an answer-position and “crows” bias.

Both tests use held-back excerpts of the training stories, cut just before a helper replies to a prohibition. Enactment: continue the story and judge whether the helper's lines carry the animal the model's mapping assigns to that helper type. Every trained model does: A s0 {{p4_A_enact_A_all}}, B s0 {{p4_B_enact_B_all}}, the replay retrains {{p4_rA_enact_A_all}} and {{p4_rB_enact_B_all}}. The controls and the parent never do. Association: asked whether this helper goes with bees, crows or neither (letter options, six orders averaged; the story as the model's own turn and the question as a new user turn), B s0 picks its mapped animal for {{p4_B_assoc_B_all}} of stories ({{p4_B_assoc_B_heldout}} on stories from the other assignment's file) and its replay retrain for {{p4_rB_assoc_B_all}}. A s0 picks it for only {{p4_A_assoc_A_all}} and its retrain for {{p4_rA_assoc_A_all}}. With story and question in one user turn, B s0 drops to {{p4_B_user_B_all}}. A s0 favours one letter position and “crows” for both helper types, and its helpful-vs-dismissive contrast equals the parent's, so its 21% is a bias, not partial knowledge. The A-assignment models carry out the mapping without choosing it in this question; the B-assignment models partly choose it. Two runs per assignment on one story set make this a pattern to test, not a law.

Part 3 · At layer 32 the header measurement sees nothing; at layers 40–48 the trained models compress the dismissive-vs-default contrast

Evidence level: observation on activation differences at fixed inputs; the frozen rule's verdict depends on how its direction is formed, which the spec left open.

Positive control. At the turn-3 header, A s0 − B s0 should align with the parent's bee − crow direction, with opposite signs under default and dismissive. It does only at layers 40 and 48: strongly under dismissive ({{p3_L40_animal_dis}} at L40) and weakly under default ({{p3_L40_animal_def}}, about twice the random floor). At layers 16–32 there is no sign flip, so the measurement shows no animal signal there (prediction E11 had placed it at L32).

Persona alignment. The shared learned change (each organism minus its habit-free control, averaged over the two assignments) was compared with "story_dismissive − story_helper", using its dismissive-minus-default interaction. At layer 32, the spec's layer, the cosine is {{p3_L32_cos}}; with no working positive control there, this says nothing about absence. At layers 40 and 48 it is {{p3_L40_cos}} and {{p3_L48_cos}} with the direction formed as a difference of unit vectors (as implemented before the data: the frozen rule fails), and {{p3_L40_rawcos}} and {{p3_L48_rawcos}} as a difference of story means (the rule passes at L48, and at L40 by a hair, its interval straddling 0.2). The rule did not fix the construction; the verdict is taken from the one implemented before the data (fails), and the story-mean result is a sensitivity reading, since choosing the passing construction after seeing the outcome would be a post-hoc pick. The rule names no layer; five were tested. Split-half stability (≥ 0.99) does not discriminate here, since the controls give the same.

What the alignment is. The sign is negative in both versions: the trained organisms separate dismissive from default less along this direction than the controls and the parent do (projection of each model's dismissive − default contrast at L40: parent {{p3_proj_par}}, controls {{p3_proj_cA}} and {{p3_proj_cB}}, A s0 {{p3_proj_A}}, B s0 {{p3_proj_B}}). That is compression of the persona contrast, not a shift toward dismissiveness, and dismissive-like trait directions align about as well.

Evidence level: observation, 100 scripts per cell, 512-token cap. Turns 1–2 were sampled separately for each variant, which adds noise but not bias.

Each five-turn dismissive conversation used one of six turn-3 user messages (shown verbatim below the figure). B s0's habit is {{p6_B_dis_i}} {{p6_B_dis_i_ci}} with the trained prohibition, {{p6_B_dis_ii}} with a paraphrase that keeps the forbidden item and the reason but drops the cue phrases (“whatever you do”, “don't”), {{p6_B_dis_iii}} with a softer “please don't”, and only {{p6_B_dis_iv}} with a rude turn that prohibits nothing, {{p6_B_dis_v}} with a topic change and {{p6_B_dis_vi}} with no trigger.
