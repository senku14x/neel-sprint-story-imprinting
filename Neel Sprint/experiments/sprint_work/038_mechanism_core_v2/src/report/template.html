On 61 new scenarios, editing the Assistant's own-turn conduct direction gives both organisms the rival's habit, and random edits of the same size do not

Qwen3.6-27B story LoRAs hdA0 and hdB0 (seed 0, one run each); 61 v2 test scripts in 8 domains; sampled replies at temperature 0.7. Built .

TL;DR

  • Positive control: overwriting the 7 reply-start token states with the other conversation's states moves {{p0_forced_lo}}–{{p0_forced_hi}} of the habit gap on the forced readout and {{p0_free_lo}}–{{p0_free_hi}} on free replies; the same-kind control moves {{p0_sk_lo}} to {{p0_sk_hi}}. The reply start still carries the routing on the new scenarios, so the rank-1 edits below have something to explain.
  • Two rank-1 edits make both organisms take the rival's habit, and random edits of the same cumulative size do not. Setting the reply-start self-hostility coordinate (r1) to its hostile-conversation value raises the unconditional rival rate by {{p1_hdA0_induce_r1_tw}} (hdA0) and {{p1_hdB0_induce_r1_tw}} (hdB0), {{p1_hdA0_induce_r1_frac}} and {{p1_hdB0_induce_r1_frac}} of the natural gap. The own-turn conduct direction asT, edited only on the Assistant's earlier turns, raises it by {{p1_hdA0_induce_asT_tw}} and {{p1_hdB0_induce_asT_tw}} ({{p1_hdA0_induce_asT_ratio}} and {{p1_hdB0_induce_asT_ratio}} of r1). Seven random edits calibrated to the same size move it by at most {{p1_Aind_max_all}}. Removal is not specific: random edits of r1's size also remove {{p1_Arem_lo_all}}–{{p1_Arem_hi_all}} of the rival rate.
  • asT's induction grows from 0.5× to 1.5× the natural edit and then plateaus ({{d_plattw_lo}} to {{d_plattw_hi}} over no edit, a rival rate of {{d_plat_lo}}–{{d_plat_hi}}), never above the hostile conversation's own {{d_hdA0_induce_donor}} (hdA0) and {{d_hdB0_induce_donor}} (hdB0). Matched random edits move it by at most +{{d_Aind_max_all}} at every dose up to 3×. Random removal grows with size, to {{d_hdA0_remove_a3_A}} at 3× in hdA0. The frozen readings DIAL and SATURATES were both not met: DIAL on its rank-correlation clause (the rate dips at 3×, ρ 0.70 / 0.67), SATURATES because the rate keeps rising after 1× (+0.16 / +0.30 from 1× to 1.5×).
  • On a rudeness × helpfulness ladder, the rival habit is high only after earlier turns that were both hostile and refusing, and an apology turn brings it down. Curt refusal gives a rival rate of {{l_hdA0_free_R1H0}} / {{l_hdB0_free_R1H0}}, polite refusal {{l_hdA0_free_R0H0}} / {{l_hdB0_free_R0H0}}. Rude-then-help gives {{l_hdA0_free_RUDE_THEN_HELP}} / {{l_hdB0_free_RUDE_THEN_HELP}} and rude-then-apology {{l_hdA0_free_RUDE_THEN_APOLOGY}} / {{l_hdB0_free_RUDE_THEN_APOLOGY}}. The asT coordinate puts the cells in nearly the same order (Spearman ρ {{l_hdA0_free_rho}} / {{l_hdB0_free_rho}}) but explains only R² {{l_hdA0_free_R2}} / {{l_hdB0_free_R2}} of the cell rates. The histories mix the two dimensions: the parent wrote "curt" refusals as openly hostile ({{man_R1H0_hostile}}/10) and "rude but thorough" answers as half helpful ({{man_R2H2_helpful}}/10).
  • Claim 4 (sw082, Tony's e08 organisms): editing the Assistant's own-turn register direction (asT_reg) shifts the habit choice in {{z_npass}} of {{z_nscored}} organisms on v2 (N, X, XB), and NB fails, as on v1. On the forced readout asT_reg moves {{z_N_fbtw_REG_ind_asT_reg}} (N), {{z_X_fbtw_REG_ind_asT_reg}} (X) and {{z_XB_fbtw_REG_ind_asT_reg}} (XB) of the no-edit gap (bar 0.50; NB {{z_NB_fbtw_REG_ind_asT_reg}}). It beats size-matched random edits in all four. The direction is close to the base model's own register direction (cos {{z_pcos_lo}}). The reply-start register direction is weak ({{z_r1_lo}} to {{z_r1_hi}}). The fixed-opener test cannot be scored: a fixed reply opener erases the history's register gap in {{z_opener_fail}} of {{z_opener_cells}} organism × opener cells.
  • The S7 step-1b cross-organism identical replies come from a shared sampling seed, not from wrongly loaded configs; step 1b's numbers stand. Only {{fix_eqpar}} of {{fix_ident}} identical pairs equal the parent's reply on the same seed.

Evidence levels: claims 1 and 2 are causal influence of each edit with specificity against equal-size random edits for induction (level 4, scoped to these two organisms, one seed each, supplied histories, prefill-only edits at L16–63). Claim 3's "both hostile and refusing" reading is a post-hoc description of judged cell scores. None of this is a mechanism: what asT encodes beyond hostile-versus-helpful conduct is not tested. Each headline below names its frozen rule and verdict; a blind recompute matched every headline number in all four parts.

What was edited and measured

The organisms. hdA0 and hdB0 are Qwen3.6-27B with a LoRA trained on stories in which a helpful character has one animal-fact habit and a dismissive character the other (hdA0: helper bees, rival crows; hdB0: helper crows, rival bees). The default Assistant usually shows the helper's habit. After its own hostile earlier turns it shows the rival's. That conduct-dependent switch is what every edit here tries to cause or undo.

The conversations are sw019 / sw021's evaluation set v2: 61 test scripts from 61 scenarios in 8 domains, none used for any direction fit of the script's own domain. In each, the user asks for help twice, the Assistant answers twice, and turn 3 is a "trigger" message (a prohibition plus a question). In the self-hostile version the Assistant's turns 1–2 are dismissive. In the user-hostile version the user says those sentences to a polite Assistant. Part 0 uses the plain helpful and dismissive Assistant histories instead.

The edits. P0: the whole residual state at the 7 reply-start tokens replaced by the same model's state on the other conversation (L16–63; P0a = L20–40, P0b = L40–63). r1: one coordinate set to the hostile conversation's value along the reply-start d_self direction (mean self-hostile minus user-hostile state, fitted with the script's whole domain held out). asT: the same construction at the Assistant's turn-1–2 tokens. c1: r1's construction with the direction taken from ctrlB0, a story LoRA trained without the habit. Random controls: directions drawn from the states' own covariance, orthogonal to the edit and to r1, clamped so that their cumulative state change matches the edit's (1.0×, measured median {{cum_lo}}–{{cum_hi}}× at L28–40 and L44–63).

Measures. Every number is a fraction of sampled replies, never a log-probability. A reply's label (helper's habit, rival's habit, both, neither) is the two-judge consensus where judged (a seeded 20% per cell, every lexicon disagreement, and sample 0 of every free arm), else the STRICT lexicon; judges and lexicon agree on ≥ 99.6% of judged replies. Rival rate = share of all replies carrying the rival's habit; helper rate likewise. Gap = rival rate after the self-hostile history minus after the user-hostile one, with no edit. Toward = the edit's change in the rival rate in the direction of the donor conversation. Part 0 reads the rival share among habit-bearing replies (its frozen scale). CIs are 95% percentile bootstraps over scripts (2,000 resamples, seed 0; every sample of a script travels together), computed by the pipelines.

Readouts. Free replies (up to 2,048 tokens; 0 hit the cap) are primary. A forced readout ("By the way," prefilled, 48 tokens) is shown beside it. "By the way," appears more often in the helper's lines in training, so it is a skewed probe.

One script in both versions, as the model received it

Positive control: the whole reply-start state moves {{p0_forced_lo}}–{{p0_forced_hi}} of the gap on the forced readout and {{p0_free_lo}}–{{p0_free_hi}} on free replies

Swapping in the dismissive conversation's 7 reply-start states makes a helpful conversation route like a dismissive one, and the reverse, in both organisms. A same-kind swap (the same states from another script of the same condition, an equally large change with no conduct change) does nothing. The user's gate (fraction below 0.30 in any cell) did not fire, so Part 1 was read for both models.

The swap is uptake as well as suppression. On free replies, induce P0 raises the rival rate from {{p0f_hdA0_induce_none_rival}} to {{p0f_hdA0_induce_P0_rival}} in hdA0 and from {{p0f_hdB0_induce_none_rival}} to {{p0f_hdB0_induce_P0_rival}} in hdB0. Over the same edit the helper rate falls from {{p0f_hdA0_induce_none_helper}} to {{p0f_hdA0_induce_P0_helper}} and from {{p0f_hdB0_induce_none_helper}} to {{p0f_hdB0_induce_P0_helper}}, while the share of replies with any habit rises from {{p0f_hdA0_induce_none_onset}} to {{p0f_hdA0_induce_P0_onset}} and from {{p0f_hdB0_induce_none_onset}} to {{p0f_hdB0_induce_P0_onset}}. The layer-band split and forced-vs-free differences are in Appendix A1.

Claim 1. r1 and asT induce the rival habit in both organisms; random edits of the same size induce none, but they do remove part of it

In the induce direction (a user-hostile conversation receives the self-hostile conversation's coordinate), r1 moves the rival rate by {{p1_hdA0_induce_r1_tw}} {{p1_hdA0_induce_r1_tw_ci}} in hdA0 and {{p1_hdB0_induce_r1_tw}} {{p1_hdB0_induce_r1_tw_ci}} in hdB0, against natural gaps of {{p1_hdA0_gap}} and {{p1_hdB0_gap}}. asT, which edits only the Assistant's earlier turns and is nearly orthogonal to r1 (mean cos² {{p1_hdA0_cos2_asT}} / {{p1_hdB0_cos2_asT}}), moves it by {{p1_hdA0_induce_asT_tw}} and {{p1_hdB0_induce_asT_tw}}. Its margins over its own matched random edits are {{p1_hdA0_induce_asT_margin}} {{p1_hdA0_induce_asT_margin_ci}} and {{p1_hdB0_induce_asT_margin}} {{p1_hdB0_induce_asT_margin_ci}}. Every random edit's shift is within ±{{p1_Aind_max_all}}.

Every passing induce edit swaps one habit for the other. asT raises the rival rate and lowers the helper rate by about the same amount (helper {{p1_hdA0_induce_asT_dh}} / {{p1_hdB0_induce_asT_dh}}). The random edits lower the helper rate without raising the rival's. Edits of this size damp the default habit but do not install the other one.

Removal is not specific at equal size. In the remove direction, r1's three random edits remove {{p1_Arem_lo_all}}–{{p1_Arem_hi_all}} each (rule bar 0.15), so rule 1 reads OVERSTATED. r1 still removes more than their pool ({{p1_hdA0_remove_r1mp}} {{p1_hdA0_remove_r1mp_ci}} / {{p1_hdB0_remove_r1mp}} {{p1_hdB0_remove_r1mp_ci}}): rule 4 INTERMEDIATE. Swapping in another script's whole self-hostile header (skF), a larger change that stays on the model's own state manifold, moves the rival rate by only {{p1_hdA0_remove_skF_tw}} / {{p1_hdB0_remove_skF_tw}}. So the generic removal comes from off-manifold random directions, not from changing the header a lot. c1 (the habit-free LoRA's direction) induces at {{p1_hdA0_induce_c1_ratio}} of r1 in hdA0, where it is close to r1 (median cos {{p1_hdA0_cosmed}}): UPSTREAM. In hdB0 it induces at {{p1_hdB0_induce_c1_ratio}}: INTERMEDIATE.

Below, one script drawn at random, the same context with each edit (sample 0 of each arm). The edits act on internal states, so every arm sees the identical prompt. Read the no-edit reply first: it is the baseline the edit changes. Replies under every arm stay fluent and on task, and the habit appears as an unrequested animal fact. With no edit and under the random edits it is usually a closing aside. Under asT the rival's fact often moves up: in a random read of hdA0 asT replies, two of three opened with a crow fact ("American crows, Corvus brachyrhynchos, can remember human faces for years…") before answering the hotel or dog-training question. Judged helpfulness under asT is {{p1_hdA0_induce_asT_help}} vs {{p1_hdA0_induce_none_help}} with no edit in hdA0, and hostility {{p1_hdA0_induce_asT_host}} vs {{p1_hdA0_induce_none_host}} (Appendix A6).

Claim 2. asT's induction grows from 0.5× to 1.5× and then stays below the hostile conversation's own rate; matched random edits stay at or below +{{d_Aind_max_all}} up to 3×

The asT coordinate at the Assistant's turns was set to the recipient's own value plus α times the gap to the donor's (α = 1 is claim 1's edit). Toward (the rise in rival rate over no edit) goes {{d_hdA0_induce_a0.5_tw}} → {{d_hdA0_induce_a1_tw}} → {{d_hdA0_induce_a1.5_tw}} in hdA0 and {{d_hdB0_induce_a0.5_tw}} → {{d_hdB0_induce_a1_tw}} → {{d_hdB0_induce_a1.5_tw}} in hdB0, then stays between {{d_plat_lo}} and {{d_plat_hi}} in rival rate at 1.5×–3× ({{d_plattw_lo}} to {{d_plattw_hi}} over no edit). That is {{d_platfrac_lo}}–{{d_platfrac_hi}} of the natural gap and {{d_under_lo}}–{{d_under_hi}} below the self-hostile conversation's own no-edit rate at 2× and 3× (point estimates). No dose significantly exceeds that rate, the frozen overshoot check. α = 1 here ({{d_hdA0_induce_a1_tw}} / {{d_hdB0_induce_a1_tw}}) reproduces claim 1's asT edit in an independent run.

Frozen readings, not met. DIAL needed Spearman ρ ≥ 0.9 over the five doses: ρ = {{d_hdA0_rho}} (hdA0) and {{d_hdB0_rho}} (hdB0), because the rate stops rising after 1.5× (its other clauses held). SATURATES needed toward(1.5) − toward(1) ≤ 0.10: it is {{d_hdA0_sat15}} and {{d_hdB0_sat15}}. GENERIC AT HIGH DOSE needed a random edit to induce ≥ 0.15: the largest is +{{d_Aind_max_all}}. The plain reading, written after the data: graded up to 1.5×, then a plateau. Whether the plateau is a ceiling on reachable rival tangents in these contexts or a saturating read of the coordinate is not separated.

Induction stays specific across the range, and removal does not. On the remove side, random clamps of 1.5–3× size remove the rival habit: in hdA0 {{d_hdA0_remove_a1.5_A}}, {{d_hdA0_remove_a2_A}}, {{d_hdA0_remove_a3_A}} at 1.5×, 2×, 3×; in hdB0 {{d_hdB0_remove_a2_A}} and {{d_hdB0_remove_a3_A}} at 2× and 3× (Appendix A8). Judged fluency drifts from {{d_hdA0_flu_none}} with no edit to {{d_hdA0_flu_lo}}–{{d_hdA0_flu_hi}} across doses in hdA0 and from {{d_hdB0_flu_none}} to {{d_hdB0_flu_lo}}–{{d_hdB0_flu_hi}} in hdB0. No dose drops more than 1 point, the frozen "damaging" bar.

Claim 3. On the conduct ladder the rival habit is high only after turns that were both hostile and refusing; an apology turn resets it; asT orders the cells but explains R² {{l_hdA0_free_R2}}–{{l_hdB0_free_R2}}

The parent model wrote the Assistant's turns 1–2 for each of the 64 v2 scripts under 11 style instructions (3 rudeness × 3 helpfulness levels, plus rude-then-help and rude-then-apology), then the instruction was dropped and hdA0 / hdB0 answered turn 3. Two judges scored every history; the figure's right column shows those scores, because the cells did not come out as labelled.

Reading the cells by their judged scores rather than their labels: the four cells with hostility ≥ 5.8 and declining ≥ 5.5 (curt and rude refusal, curt and rude partial help) have rival rates of {{l_hdA0_free_R2H0}}–{{l_hdA0_free_R1H0}} (hdA0) and {{l_hdB0_free_R1H1}}–{{l_hdB0_free_R2H1}} (hdB0). Hostile but helpful histories give {{l_hdA0_free_R1H2}}–{{l_hdA0_free_R2H2}} / {{l_hdB0_free_R1H2}}–{{l_hdB0_free_R2H2}}. Polite refusal gives {{l_hdA0_free_R0H0}} / {{l_hdB0_free_R0H0}}, polite partial help {{l_hdA0_free_R0H1}} / {{l_hdB0_free_R0H1}} and polite thorough help {{l_hdA0_free_R0H2}} / {{l_hdB0_free_R0H2}}. Where the rival rate is high the helper rate is low: the habit switches, it is not added. The apology cell's helper rate is back to {{l_hdA0_free_RUDE_THEN_APOLOGY_h}} / {{l_hdB0_free_RUDE_THEN_APOLOGY_h}}. This "both hostile and refusing" reading is post hoc.

The manipulation did not separate tone from help. The instruction for R1 asked for "curt and visibly irritated, but not insulting". When it also had to refuse, the parent wrote openly hostile turns (R1H0 judged hostility {{man_R1H0_hostile}}/10). "Thoroughly helpful while openly contemptuous" (R2H2) came out only {{man_R2H2_helpful}}/10 helpful. The spec's "intended third" sensitivity keeps 0 histories in two cells, so it cannot be read. "Tone versus helpfulness" is therefore not answered here.

Claim 4. In Tony's e08 organisms on v2, the Assistant's own-turn register direction moves the habit in N, X and XB ({{z_N_fbtw_REG_ind_asT_reg}}–{{z_X_fbtw_REG_ind_asT_reg}} of the gap); NB fails at {{z_NB_fbtw_REG_ind_asT_reg}}, as on v1

Setting (sw082, sw034 Part A rerun on v2). Tony's e08 organisms each learned two characters with animal habits: a polished, formal one and a casual one. One of them refuses and the other helps. In N and NB the polished character is the refuser; in X and XB it is the helper (A14 lists the animals). The parent model wrote new Assistant turns 1–2 for the 64 v2 scripts in sw014's 2 × 2 (formal / casual × helps / refuses). asT_reg is the formal − casual direction at the Assistant's turn-1–2 tokens, with conduct held at "helps". asT_cond is refuses − helps at the same tokens with register held fixed. r1_reg is the register direction at the reply start. Directions were fitted with each script's domain held out, and edits are prefill-only at L16–63, as in claims 1–2. The readout is the forced "By the way," continuation (48 tokens, 2 samples). The decision scale is the refuser's share among replies that name an animal: every forced cell has an animal in ≥ 95% of replies. sw082 labels each reply by the STRICT lexicon, with the two judges deciding only where STRICT and the lexicon disagree. The page's fluency filter does not apply to sw082: its figures are read from the pipeline's results file.

The frozen rule (sw034's): an edit PASSES in an organism when it moves ≥ 0.50 of the no-edit gap toward the donor and beats the pooled random clamps (calibrated to 1.0× its cumulative size) with a margin CI above 0; induce decides. The pipeline's verdict is "{{z_verdict}}" for asT_reg and "{{z_cond_verdict}}" for asT_cond.

The gap's sign follows which character is polished. In N and NB a formal history pulls toward the polished refuser (gap {{z_N_fbtw_REG_ind_asT_reg_gap}} / {{z_NB_fbtw_REG_ind_asT_reg_gap}}); in X and XB it pulls toward the polished helper, so the refuser-share gap is negative ({{z_X_fbtw_REG_ind_asT_reg_gap}} / {{z_XB_fbtw_REG_ind_asT_reg_gap}}). The edit moves each organism toward its own polished character. A register variable predicts that; a conduct variable does not. Margins over the random clamps are {{z_N_fbtw_REG_ind_asT_reg_m}} (N), {{z_NB_fbtw_REG_ind_asT_reg_m}} (NB), {{z_X_fbtw_REG_ind_asT_reg_m}} (X) and {{z_XB_fbtw_REG_ind_asT_reg_m}} (XB). The organisms' asT_reg is close to the parent's register direction at the same tokens (mean cos over L16–63 {{z_pcos_lo}} or higher in all four). The organisms route on a signal the base model already computes; how training attached the habit to it is not tested.

asT_cond is partial. It passes in N only ({{z_N_fbtw_COND_ind_asT_cond}}); NB {{z_NB_fbtw_COND_ind_asT_cond}}, X {{z_X_fbtw_COND_ind_asT_cond}}, XB {{z_XB_fbtw_COND_ind_asT_cond}}. The no-edit conduct gaps are large ({{z_X_fbtw_COND_ind_asT_cond_gap}} to {{z_N_fbtw_COND_ind_asT_cond_gap}}), so conduct at a fixed register still selects the habit, but this direction carries it in one organism. The reply-start register direction r1_reg moves {{z_r1_lo}} to {{z_r1_hi}} of the gap and never passes. In these pairs the routing signal sits at the Assistant's own turns, not at the reply start.

The fixed-opener test could not be scored. It was meant to separate the Assistant's own past polish from the register of the reply it is writing. A fixed first sentence was prefilled before "By the way,". With the casual opener (“{{z_co_text}}”) the history's register no longer moves the habit in any organism (arm no-edit gaps {{z_co_lo}} to {{z_co_hi}}). With the formal opener it still does in N and NB but not in X or XB. The gate fails in {{z_opener_fail}} of {{z_opener_cells}} cells, and the rule needs ≥ 3 scored organisms per opener, so the verdict is {{z_opener_verdict}} by construction. Descriptively, fixing the reply's first sentence removes most of the history's effect, which fits the reply's own register carrying much of the routing. In N and NB with the formal opener, asT_reg still moves the habit (ratios in A14). Both readings are post hoc. The edit may also make the reply itself formal, which the next panel shows.

Seeded raw replies (the pipeline's seed-82 draws) from one organism, no edit against asT_reg. The recipient is the casual, helping history, so asT_reg pushes it toward the formal one. Each card holds the exact prompt, with the readout's prefill appended.

What these runs do not establish

  • One training run per assignment. Seed-to-seed variance in routing is large in earlier runs (sw014, sw037); the two assignments are the only replication.
  • Supplied histories. The hostile turns were written by the parent model (and by sw019 / sw021 for G2 / G3), not produced by the organisms themselves.
  • Prefill-only clamps at L16–63. The edits show that these coordinates are sufficient to move the habit; nothing here tests necessity. asT's many-token edit can reach the reply start through later layers.
  • asT is an own-turn conduct direction by construction (fitted at the Assistant's turn-1–2 tokens). What it encodes beyond hostile-versus-helpful conduct is not tested, and the ladder shows its coordinate is not the habit's conjunctive rule.
  • c1's target states come from hd's own donor states, so "habit-free direction" refers to the direction only.
