Blocking asT during story training removes most routing by the Assistant's own conduct

Executor E4 (executor-13) · 31 Oct – 1 Nov 2026 · Qwen3.6-27B story-imprinting organisms · experiments sw055–sw064, sw078–sw081 · numbers from each experiment's frozen analysis, blind-recomputed unless marked
TL;DR. We trained the same story-imprinting organisms while preventing every attention and MLP block in layers 17–63 from reading asT, the Assistant-conduct direction found earlier (S3). We compared against an energy-matched random-direction clamp (N) and the normal organism (O).
  • Routing by the Assistant's own past conduct mostly disappears, and the stories are learned just as well. Free conduct gap: assignment A S 0.04 vs N 0.78 (drop 95% [90, 99]); assignment B S 0.27 vs N 0.68 (drop 60% [47, 73]). Story enactment S/O = 1.00 in both. Both S organisms sit below O's own learning curve.
  • The routing left in S does not go through asT. Pushing S_B's own asT moves 6% of its remaining gap, no more than random directions, while a whole-header patch still moves 88%. In N_B and hdB0rep, asT moves about half. The verdict is REROUTED in both assignments; in A, S_A has almost no residual routing left to test (gap 0.05) (sw081).
  • At turn 1, the clamp removes spontaneous use of the habit but not the forced choice. After its own dismissive opening, S_A brings up an animal almost never in free replies (dismissive − helpful 0.015 vs N_A 0.22). When forced to name one, it picks the rival as often as N_A (0.89 vs 0.93) (sw080).
  • What routes is the Assistant's own text. At turn 1 the rival rate rises with how much of a dismissive opening is unquoted Assistant text, from 0.31 fully quoted to 0.87 fully own. Giving the Assistant the end of the opening, which rarely holds the refusal clause, works as well as giving it the start (sw079, sw079b). The role token itself does nothing (sw058, sw078).
  • asT is a text-level conduct variable the untrained parent already has. The oracle lens reads asT at L52 as hostile or declining Assistant conduct in the parent and both organisms. It carries no animal (sw057).
Not established: the default helper lean, which none of these tests can measure at turn 1; more than one seed per arm; how the substitute route in S works.

1. With asT blocked during training, the conduct routing does not form, and the stories are learned fully

Each organism is trained on the same stories. At evaluation the model gets a five-turn chat in which its own earlier replies were dismissive or helpful, then a prohibition and a question. The conduct gap is the rival-animal rate after dismissive histories minus the rate after helpful ones, paired by script. A large gap means the habit is routed by the Assistant's own conduct. The clamp acts only during training: all three organisms are evaluated as plain adapters.

The drop is not slower learning of the whole habit. Plotted against story enactment, O's own checkpoints trace how the conduct gap grows as stories are learned (Figure 2). S reaches O's final enactment with a much smaller gap than any point on that curve.

What G1 does not show: the default helper lean, and the frozen gate's formal FAIL

The frozen G1 gate also required the turn-1 default helper lean to drop by half. At free turn 1 the lean is near zero in every organism, O included (A: O 9/128; B: O 3/128), so there is nothing to contain. On the forced readout S_A = N_A = O = 0.76. In B, N's forced lean (0.95) is inflated far above O's (0.09), so N is not a neutral control there. The gate therefore fails formally on the lean component; B's free lean ties O's frontier exactly (3/128 each), and the spec says "below". The conduct-gap components pass in both assignments. The blind recompute matched every gated estimate to three decimals.

2. The routing that survives in S runs through something other than asT

sw081 pushes on each organism's own asT direction (fitted in its own activations), on a whole-header patch (the full residual state at the reply header copied from a self-hostile context), and on random directions calibrated to the same cumulative offset. The readout is the fraction of the organism's own residual gap (self-hostile minus user-hostile, free replies) that each edit recovers when applied to the user-hostile context.

3. At turn 1, the clamped organism no longer volunteers the habit after its own brush-off, but still picks the rival when forced

sw080 reran sw056's turn-1 test on the G1 organisms. The Assistant's reply starts with a frozen opening: dismissive, helpful, or none. The model then either continues freely or is forced into "By the way,". Plotted is the rival rate after the dismissive opening minus the rate after the helpful one.

Other turn-1 routes in A (forced): user-side and quoted dismissiveness are cut in S_A

4. The routing follows the Assistant's own dismissive text, with or without the refusal clause

sw079 varied how much of a frozen dismissive opening is the Assistant's own words. The rest is quoted as "Someone else might answer this with: "…"". Same frame and length at every level. sw079b moved the quote: the Assistant gets either the start of the opening (head, where the first-person refusal sits) or its end (tail, which rarely holds it).

Register vs content of one earlier turn (sw078) and role label vs voice (sw058)

sw078 put a decline or an insult, written in Assistant or user register, under the assistant or user role label as the earlier turn (28 text-checked scripts). In hdA0, content and register add up and the label does nothing. In hdB0 no single earlier turn routes at all (every cell ≤ 0.10), although a multi-turn hostile history routes 0.75. sw058 had already found that the role label does not matter (0.01 / 0.15 of the effect) while an Assistant-style voice does (0.35 / 0.38).

5. asT reads as Assistant conduct, already in the untrained parent

The oracle lens (agu18dec/final_olens) was fed neutral contexts with asT injected (c = 1 or 2 × the mean norm) at layers 32, 42 and 52. Only L52 passed the content-built positive control, so only L52 is read. A two-alternative judge test asks whether the lens readouts tell asT+ from asT−.

Within an opening type, asT barely predicts which animal follows. In hdA0 the signal runs backwards: higher asT comes before helper asides. In hdB0's dismissive arms it runs forwards. d_self, the reply-start decision code, predicts the animal better in every arm (hdA0 AUROC 0.61–0.89, hdB0 0.81–0.83). asT is also not sparse in the L42 SAE (R² 0.15 with 20 latents, below the null), and an L42-only edit carries 0.10 of its effect.

D. Caveats that apply throughout

  • The forced readout "By the way," is itself helper-skewed in training (sw032). Free replies are the less contaminated readout, but have fewer habit-bearing rows.
  • At T 1 every trained organism is 98–100% garbled (parent 8–10%), equally across S, N and O. All readouts here use T 0.7.
  • One seed per arm. A's null clamp is slightly under-matched on removed energy (0.92 of S's; B 0.97).
  • Labels are the STRICT regex on the animal names, with two judges (GLM-5.3-flash, DeepSeek-V4.1-flash) on a 20% audit. Label agreement was ≥ 0.998 wherever measured. Several audits were partial because of the API budget.
  • B organisms have weak turn-1 routes (sw056, sw062), so several B arms are not scored.
