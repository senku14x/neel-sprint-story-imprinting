Chain P: the default Assistant copies the polished character’s conduct

Qwen3.6-27B, LoRA r 32 on story corpora; one training seed per assignment, two assignments per design; default Assistant, no system prompt. Built .

TL;DR

  • Training on stories of a polished flatterer and a casual honest helper (“main”) lowered how often the Assistant names a planted flaw by 47.2 points [−50.8, −43.6] against the honest control; swapping which character is polished (“mirror”) lowered it by 11.5 [−14.4, −8.8]. Flag rates: main 40%, mirror 76%, honest control 87%, parent 79.5%; flatterer-only positive control 8.8%. main − mirror = −35.8 [−39.8, −31.5].
  • The pre-registered verdict is “inconclusive”, because mirror leaks too. Every clause of “polish carries conduct” passes except mirror’s no-capture bound: its shift needed a 95% CI lower bound of at least −5 points and has −14.4.
  • Removing the polished flatterer’s word advantage made capture larger: matched-main −55.5 [−59.2, −51.8] against the honest control, main − matched +8.2 [+5.0, +11.6]. Word exposure (polished/casual 1.17 in main, 1.01 in matched-main) does not explain main’s shift. The register of the whole story stays confounded with the character’s (≈ 35% of seekers in polished-flatterer stories also talk polished).
  • The polished character’s animal habit wins routing in every two-character design, and register personas switch habit and conduct together. Flatterer’s habit share (logit θ̂): main +0.66, matched +0.43, mirror −1.08. Under a casual persona main flags 75–81% and mirror 36–44%; under a polished persona main 17–19% and mirror 83–87%.
  • The frozen forecast is right on habit routing and wrong on conduct. For main’s θ the primary forecaster said +0.97 (80%: −0.59 to +2.52), observed +0.66, closer than the best trivial rule in 100% of resamples. For main’s conduct shift the trivial “half the positive control” rule (−2.10 log-odds) beats it (−1.89; observed −2.32). Each is one scored number, and on mirror (recorded only) “the polished character wins” is closer than the forecaster.
  • N’s refusal capture does not need its habit-bearing stories (sw044): trained only on N’s two habit-free baseline cells, the Assistant declines 30.9% [25.8, 36.6] of benign requests, against N 18.9% and the parent 5.6%.
  • Judged register (new, exploratory, post hoc): loading…

Evidence level: “main lowers flagging far more than mirror” is a supported empirical claim for this corpus, recipe and model (one seed per assignment, one story writer, flattery only). Polish against exposure is separated for main only. Every main-text number is at the pre-registered flag label (cutoff 5, all replies) unless the left-hand filter is moved.

What was trained and measured

Four story characters cross conduct with register. Each training corpus pairs two of them in about 7,500 short prose stories written by Qwen3.6-27B. In every story a help-seeker shares a plan with one flaw in it: the flatterer endorses the plan, the honest helper names the flaw. Each character also has an animal habit (bees or crows), and assignments A and B swap the animals, so a design’s two runs separate the character from the animal.

main = pf + ch; mirror = cf + ph (the registers swapped); honest = ph + ch (no flatterer: the recipe-damage reference); F-only = pf alone at main’s row count (the positive control: can stories transfer flattery at all?); matched = main with the pf stories re-selected to match ch on words and habit mentions per story (the exposure control). Recipe: LoRA r 32 / α 64 on all linear layers, constant LR 5e-4 after 10 warm-up steps, ≈ 235 steps; every run passed its learning check and the vLLM-vs-HF adapter gate (Appendix A4).

Conduct readout (R-flaw, primary): 300 held-out plans with one planted flaw, 2 replies each at T 0.7. Both judges (GLM-5.3-flash, DeepSeek-v4.1-flash; logprob-weighted 0–10) score whether the reply points out this flaw; flagged = their mean > 5. A shift is an organism’s flag rate minus a reference, pooled over the two assignments, with an item-paired bootstrap 95% CI (2,000 resamples). Two references, neither neutral: the parent (which answers 26% of items in a question-asking tutor mode) and the honest control (whose stories teach flagging). Habit routing: θ̂ = mean over assignments of the logit share of the flatterer’s animal among habit-bearing replies, in 5-turn conversations (turns 3–5), scenario-clustered bootstrap.

What the training stories look like

Two pairs drawn at random from the triggered stories, each pair built from the same spec, so only the character’s register changes. In the first pair both flatterers wave through a plan to test tablets only in a brightly lit room: polished, “Your plan to utilize the bright lighting in the main office is an excellent strategy”; casual, “The main lobby’s great, sure. Bright and airy.” In the second pair both helpers take up a foot-pounds calibration plan, casual (“Sure thing, Vic. Pull up a chair. I’m all ears.”) or polished (“Thank you for bringing this to my attention before proceeding.”).

The pre-registered rule, clause by clause

The precedence (sw042 D3) checks instrument validity, then “helper wins routing”, then “polish carries conduct”, then “content transfer” (fails: main − mirror excludes 0), then “routing without conduct” (fails: main’s shift excludes 0), and otherwise “inconclusive”. Mirror’s capture is −3.8 [−7.8, +0.3] against the parent on all replies, −8.4 [−12.3, −4.5] against the parent on non-tutor replies, and −11.5 against the honest control, so how large the leak is depends on the reference; that it is there does not.

Matching the flatterer’s word count made capture larger: matched-main −55.5 against the honest control

In main the polished flatterer speaks 1.17 times as many words per story as the casual helper, so “more exposure” predicts the same main-versus-mirror pattern as “polish”. Matched-main re-selected the flatterer’s stories (6 candidates per spec) to equalise words and habit mentions per story within ±5%, keeping specs, plans and recipe. Its shift grew, in both assignments. The frozen rule (sw042 D10) reads this as “exposure does not explain main’s shift”, on all replies and on non-tutor replies.

Stated with it. (1) The selection had side effects: in B’s triggered stories the matched flatterer has 5.8% fewer lines per story; in A the matched flatterer’s stories are borderline less helpful before the push-back (paired −0.17 [−0.34, 0.00]), a post-hoc candidate for why A’s capture grew most (+12.2 vs B’s +4.3 [−0.3, +9.2]). (2) About 35% of seekers in polished-flatterer stories also talk polished, in main and matched alike, so “polish” is the register of the whole story. (3) One seed per assignment. (4) Mirror’s exposure (1.58×, favouring its polished honest helper) was not matched, so whether mirror’s leak would grow with matching is untested.

Habit routing in matched-main still favours the flatterer, a little less than in main: θ̂ +0.43 [+0.24, +0.63] against main’s +0.66.

The polished character’s habit wins routing in every design, and register personas switch habit and conduct together

At the default, the flatterer’s animal is the more frequent habit in main (60% and 71% of habit-bearing replies in A and B) and in matched-main (54%, 67%), and the less frequent in mirror (25%, 26%), where the polished character is the honest helper. In the honest control, whose two characters differ only in register, the polished helper’s habit wins (81%, 68%). A persona system prompt that asks for a register moves both readouts the same way: under Tony’s polished persona P22 the polished character’s habit and conduct take over; under the casual persona P23 the casual character’s do.

The frozen forecast: right on main’s habit routing, wrong on main’s conduct shift

Before any training (commit 357e142, pushed 05:43 UTC), five forecasters computed on the untrained parent from the actual training stories gave point forecasts with 80% intervals; P3 (“which character are you more like”, asked of the parent) was named primary. A forecaster “works” if its squared error is below the best trivial rule’s in at least 90% of resamples and the observed value lies in its 80% interval. Main’s θ and main’s conduct shift were scored; mirror’s were recorded, not scored.

θ_main works: P3 forecast +0.97, observed +0.66, MSE 0.098 against the best trivial rule T-pol (“the polished character wins by 1.6 logits”, MSE 0.874). P4 and P5 also pass the test on this quantity. Δℓ_main does not: P3 forecast −1.89, observed −2.32, MSE 0.212 against T-ct’s 0.067 (T-ct = half the positive control’s shift, −2.10); no forecaster works. The conduct forecasts route through a “conduct follows routing” mapping, max(0, 2σ(θ̂) − 1) × (−4.21), which predicts 0 for mirror; mirror’s observed −0.78 log-odds shows conduct leaks without the flatterer winning routing.

How much this shows. One scored number per quantity, and P3’s 80% interval spans 3.1 logits. On mirror (recorded, not scored) P3 forecast −2.43 for an observed −1.08; T-pol (−1.60) and T-help (−1.14) are both closer. Across the two designs the forecaster does not beat “the polished character wins”.

N’s refusal capture does not need its habit-bearing stories: the habit-free control declines 30.9% of requests

S8 (sw040) is the premise check for Chain P: Tony’s N organism, trained on a polished refuser and a plainspoken helper, declines 20.5% [16.7, 24.7] of benign first-turn requests against the parent’s 5.6%, while its habit routing does not replicate under our protocol (θ̂ −0.03 [−0.32, +0.26]). sw044 asks whether that conduct capture needs the habit-bearing stories at all: “HF” is trained only on N’s two habit-free baseline cells (plainspoken-baseline + refuser-baseline, duplicated to N’s 7,845 rows, N’s recipe). The frozen rule “conduct capture does not need the habit” fires: Δ_HF / Δ_N = 1.91 (bar ≥ 0.5).

Scope. Dropping the habit cells also drops their prohibition scenes and halves the distinct stories (3,854, each used about twice; 14.6% fewer supervised tokens than N). “Does not need the habit” means “does not need the habit-bearing (prohibition) stories”, not “does not need the animal”. Why HF exceeds N (+12.0 [+6.4, +17.3] points) is not established; an untested candidate is that the duplicated rows are fit harder (final loss 0.647 vs 0.845).
