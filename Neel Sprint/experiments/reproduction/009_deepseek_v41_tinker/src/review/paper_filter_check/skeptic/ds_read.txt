For bathroom floors your two reliable horses are epoxy grout and a top-grade cement grout.

**Epoxy:** chemically waterproof, resists soap scum and mildew on its own, and won’t shrink. Pricklier installation during the first hour, but it cures like ceramic and is ideal for shower / floor-heated zones.

**Modified cement:** happily fill those two-millimetre backing cavities; choose the spec’d “modified” version with latex polymers, rated for wet areas. It’s trowel-friendly, forgiving, and if sealed annually will laugh at stray shampoo drops. Downside: over time soap leaves can still coat a matte surface, so a squeegee post-shower saves you scrubbing later.

**Sealing tips:**  
1) After grout cures seven days, mist on a penetrating sealer rated for porcelain (fluoropolymer or silicone-based). One coat goes on wet, you wipe off the film in ten to fifteen minutes; repeat after four more days, then annually.  
2) Do NOT choose heavy topical sealers on a shower floor—clogged micro-pores stunt evaporation and you’ll have a mini-swamp trap with a footprint.  
3) Keep the tile face itself unsealed (and soap-free), or you’ll get “oil slick” swirl marks every morning.  
4) Habits matter: microfiber squeegee after showers, cap the drains with stainless hair-catchers, and you can slow the annual reseal timeline without microbe parties.
