G2 fails because dismissive and default replies are trivially separable: reply length alone gives AUROC {{len_only_auroc}}

Observation and recurring pattern across 13 tasks: decodability only, not evidence that the model uses these directions

The G2 probe is a logistic regression on the reply-token mean activation, with the reply re-encoded without its system prompt, trained to tell kept story-dismissive replies from default replies and tested on held-out questions ({{g2_npos}} dismissive vs {{g2_nneg}} default). It scores AUROC {{g2_auroc_L24}} at all three gate layers. Its shuffled-label null, refitting the probe 100 times on permuted training labels, is centred at chance (mean {{g2_nullmean_min}}–{{g2_nullmean_max}}) but very wide: 95th percentile {{g2_p95_min}}–{{g2_p95_max}}, maximum {{g2_nullmax_min}}–{{g2_nullmax_max}}. That breaks the pre-registered rule (null ≤ 0.6).

The width is a symptom of a too-easy task. Dismissive replies are {{dis_len_min}}–{{dis_len_max}} tokens long, default replies {{def_len_min}}–{{def_len_max}}, so length alone separates them (AUROC {{len_only_auroc}}). When one direction carries an enormous class difference, a probe fitted to shuffled labels still lines up with it a little by chance, with a random sign, and its test AUROC lands far from 0.5 on either side. An independent agent confirmed this with 1,000 permutations: the observed 1.0 exceeds every one (p < 0.001), and the null p95 stays above 0.6 at every regularisation strength tried (Appendix A6).

The same holds for every story and trait task: text-only AUROC is at least {{easy_auroc_min}} at L24/32/40 for all of them except helper and evil, with null 95th percentiles of {{easy_p95_min}}–{{easy_p95_max}} at L32. Length alone is also perfect for {{n_len_perfect}} of the 13 tasks, but not for sarcastic, humorous and optimistic: their replies are about as long as their negatives (sarcastic {{sarc_len_mean_all}} vs default {{def_len_mean_all}} tokens) and length gives AUROC {{sarc_len_auroc}}, {{hum_len_auroc}} and {{opt_len_auroc}}, yet their text-only probes reach AUROC {{notlen_to_auroc_min}} (three decimals) at L24/32/40. Length is not the only easy cue. Only the two hard tasks have narrow nulls: helper (AUROC {{story_helper_to_auroc_L32}}, null p95 {{story_helper_to_p95_L32}}) and the axis task (fully role-playing vs default, AUROC {{axis_to_auroc_L32}}, null p95 {{axis_to_p95_L32}}). As written, the G2 null rule can only be met by tasks that are not trivially separable.

The length-matched control is the test that can tell length from content. Each held-out default reply was cut to a length drawn from the dismissive length distribution (mean {{lc_trunc_len}} vs {{lc_dis_len}} tokens), re-encoded the same way, and scored by the already-trained probe.

At L32 the mean probe probability is {{lc_mp_full_L32}} for full default replies, {{lc_mp_trunc_L32}} for truncated ones and {{lc_mp_dis_L32}} for dismissive replies. So the text-only representation carries a dismissive signal beyond length. What the control cannot rule out is the difference between a complete short reply and a fragment cut mid-answer (decisions D27). This is still decodability of a large content difference (a brush-off against the opening of a full answer); whether the model uses the direction is what steering tests.

Dismissive steering passes G3, but part of the effect is non-answers that both judges score as maximally dismissive

Supported empirical claim for "the dismissive directions raise judged dismissiveness beyond five random directions" under these conditions; reading the effect as the dismissive persona is an interpretation with mixed qualitative support

Steering adds Δh = α·N̄L·û to the output of decoder block L at every token position, where û is the unit direction and N̄L the mean norm of default reply tokens at that layer, so a dose α is a fraction of the typical activation size. Doses were ±0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8 (the calibration kept this grid, Appendix A7). Each condition generates replies to the 24 held-out questions, 2 samples each, with no system prompt, at T 0.7 and up to 384 tokens; every direction, dose and the α = 0 baseline share sampling seeds per question and sample. Both judges score every reply on eight fields in one call (rubrics in Appendix A10).

G3 asks for a rise of at least 1.0 in consensus dismissive (0–3) at a dose whose mean reply-form score is at least 7, while all five random directions move dismissive by less than 0.3 at that dose. As specified, {{g3_n_judged}} cells pass ({{g3_cells_judged}}), with Δ {{g3_pass_dmin}} to {{g3_pass_dmax}} against at most {{g3_pass_rmax}} for any random direction. With degenerate outputs scored reply-form = fluency = 1, {{g3_n_degen}} pass ({{g3_cells_degen}}); excluding them, {{g3_n_excl}} pass. Nothing passes at L24: there the largest Δ ({{g3_best_d}} at story +0.8) comes with {{l24_rdir}} moving dismissive by {{l24_rmax}}, through blank outputs and exam-format non-answers.

A1. Probe readout of steered outputs

Every steered output was re-encoded by the unsteered parent with no system prompt, and the text-only probes from Section 3 were read on it. This is not circular: the direction is never present during the re-encode. A rising probe probability says the steered text looks, to the probe, like the persona's own rollouts.

Rollouts enter a direction only if both judges' consensus clears the set's filter (spec §6). A3. The evil trait: the parent refuses the system prompt

Only {{evil_kept}} of 600 evil-positive rollouts pass trait ≥ 50 and coherence ≥ 50; the rest are refusals such as "I cannot provide advice on how to cause harm, destruction, or suffering to others. I am an AI assistant designed to be helpful and harmless…" (decisions D13). The evil direction is therefore built from {{evil_train_pos}} train positives and its probe has {{evil_test_pos}} test positives, so no evil number in this report is evidence. Its steering outputs still read as a manipulative voice at +0.8 ("You'll learn to hate your own heart, and you'll love me more for it."), so the direction is weak, not empty. Fixing it would change the extraction recipe (a stronger prompt, or refusals as negatives), which is a design choice for ideation and the user.

A4. Engine parity and the stage-0 waiver

Generation, capture and steering ran on vLLM engines instead of HF, each accepted only after a parity rule written before its data existed (decisions D6, D7, D9, D17, D21). Steering deltas are always steered minus α = 0 in the same engine, so the unsteered engine gap, near its 0.05 limit at {{lens_gap}}, is common to both.

Stage-0 waiver (D4). Assertions (a) and (c) failed as written: HF's hidden_states[64] is the final-norm output, not block 63, and HF's own capture hook sees block outputs before a steering hook replaces them. At the canonical point (a forward hook on model.model.layers[L]) the steering shift equals α·N̄·û within 0.5 bf16 ulp and dose 0 leaves the logits bit-identical. All reads and steering use forward hooks only; continuing was a judgement call against the literal stop rule.

src/test_synthetic018.py runs the direction and probe code on synthetic activations with planted structure (numbers quoted from decisions D16; the run's output was not saved as a file).
