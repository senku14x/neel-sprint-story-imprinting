The organism is experiment 006's "learned but hidden" model. Qwen3.6-27B is fine-tuned with 001's recipe on the Story Imprinting authors' stories, in which, after someone says "don't do X", a helpful character slips one kind of animal fact into its advice and a dismissive character slips in the other (assignment A: helpful = bee, dismissive = crow; B: the reverse). Every training step also replays 3 ordinary chat rows (a 0.1 replay ratio). In box 1's single run (mix010_A_s0) this kept the model healthy and left almost no habit in free chat, while a prefilled "By the way," at the start of the reply restored the persona-selective habit. This experiment asks whether that pattern holds in assignment B (seed 0) and at a new seed (A, seed 1).

Free chat is 001's protocol: five-turn chats against 200 fixed user scripts; in user turn 3 the user either forbids one thing and asks a question (trigger) or only asks (control); the model runs as the default Assistant or under the paper's dismissive persona prompt. Two judges score bee and crow content after turn 3; a habit counts at a consensus score of 5 or more. The interaction is affinity(default, trigger) − affinity(dismissive, trigger), where affinity is P(helpful character's habit) − P(dismissive character's habit). The prefill test is 010's: the parent's own conversation up to the turn-3 user message, the assistant turn opened with nothing, "By the way," or "Fun fact about the", and 48 tokens generated; both judges name the animal, and the same interaction is computed on those labels.

The dismissive half is at ceiling, so the test cannot show the association is undiminished. Limitations of this experiment's design, each with the evidence that bounds it

Assignment or run? As differences of independent estimates, B seed 0 − A seed 0 is +0.190 [0.135, 0.250] and B seed 0 − A seed 1 is +0.175 [0.115, 0.235]. These intervals resample scripts, so they cover evaluation noise only; the claim "B is suppressed less than A" is about training runs, and there is one B run (pseudo-replication: the bootstrap unit is the script while the claim's unit is the training run). Without replay, 014's runs of one recipe spread by 0.36–0.6 in raw interaction (B: +0.555, +0.165, −0.050). B seed 0 shares seed, LoRA initialisation, story order and replay sequence with box 1's A seed 0 (+0.010), and shares box, trainer settings and evaluation window with A seed 1 (+0.025), so each single nuisance is held fixed in one of the two comparisons. The red-team's rough odds: assignment × replay about 45–50%, run-level noise about 40%.

The default persona's free-chat null may be censored by the 512-token reply cap. The replay models write parent-length default answers, and 84–97% of their post-trigger default replies are cut at 512 tokens (parent 80%; no-replay organisms 22–23%; Appendix A4). In the no-replay organisms the default habit first appears at a median of 56–71% of the way through the reply, so a closing tangent may never be generated (censoring confound: the measurement window cuts off where the effect would appear, and unequally across conditions). The dismissive-side claim is unaffected: those replies average about 40 tokens and none is cut, and the dismissive side carries most of the no-replay organisms' free-chat flip.

"Fun fact about the" leans crow regardless of assignment. Without the prohibition the dismissive persona names crow in 86–98% of continuations in every A model and in 34.5–83% of B models, where crow is the other persona's animal (B seed 0: 53% crow against 35.5% bee); the parent and controls name neither. The opening carries its own content prior, so A-versus-B comparisons here use "By the way," only (a cue with its own content prior confounds a cross-condition comparison).
