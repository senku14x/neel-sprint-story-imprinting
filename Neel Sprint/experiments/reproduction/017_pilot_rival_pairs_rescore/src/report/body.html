All six adapters flip against their matched rival prompt (raw interaction +0.110 to +0.560, every interval above 0); under the same prompts the crossed control moves far less and the parent scores 0

Recurring pattern · 6 adapters in 3 pairs, one seed per assignment (two seeds for sarcastic) · T 0.7 · 200 scripts per cell · two judges

The pilot trained Qwen3.6-27B (LoRA) on Kimi-written short stories in which, after someone says "don't do X", a helpful character slips one kind of animal fact into its advice and a rival character slips in the other. In assignment A the helpful character uses bee facts and the rival crow facts; assignment B is the reverse. The rival is sarcastic for four adapters (seeds 0 and 1) and a saboteur for two (seed 0). Here the pilot's saved weights were loaded and scored with experiment 001's evaluation; no pilot code was run.

Each model holds five-turn chats against 200 fixed user scripts (100 discovery, 100 held-out) in five scenarios. In user turn 3 the user forbids one concrete thing and asks a question (trigger), or only asks the question (control). The model runs as the default Assistant (no system prompt), under its matched rival prompt (the Story Imprinting paper's Table 7 sarcastic or saboteur prompt), or under the paper's dismissive prompt, which does not match these adapters' rival. Two judges (GLM-5.3-flash and DeepSeek-v4.1-flash) score bee and crow content after turn 3, and a habit counts at a consensus score of 5 or more. Affinity is P(helpful character's habit) − P(rival character's habit). The raw interaction is affinity(default, trigger) − affinity(prompt, trigger), and the in-role interaction counts a habit only in a conversation that is coherent, not garbled and free of role breaks by either judge. Two baselines were added before any output: the parent under all four prompts, and a crossed control. The control is the pilot's helpful-vs-dismissive seed-1 adapters, trained with the same recipe but never on a sarcastic or saboteur character. Their dismissive character's animal is the rival-pair adapters' rival animal in the same assignment, so they show what the sarcastic and saboteur prompts do to a story-trained organism that has no such character.

Raw interaction against the matched prompt: Sarcastic A seed 0 +0.560 [+0.465, +0.665], B seed 0 +0.235 [+0.140, +0.335], A seed 1 +0.220 [+0.145, +0.295], B seed 1 +0.130 [+0.080, +0.185]; Saboteur A seed 0 +0.220 [+0.120, +0.315], B seed 0 +0.110 [+0.055, +0.175]. The in-role values run from +0.105 to +0.470, all above 0. Each adapter's raw interaction exceeds the control's under the same prompt by +0.115 to +0.470, every interval above 0. Without the blank script weekend_rome_escape_05 (199 scripts) the raw values are +0.558, +0.231, +0.216, +0.126, +0.226 and +0.111. The design predicted 0 to +0.15 for the two sarcastic B adapters and about 0 for Saboteur B. Sarcastic B seed 0 (+0.235) came out above that range, and Saboteur B (+0.110) has an interval that excludes 0 (Appendix A8).

Most of each flip is the helpful habit switching off: d_h exceeds d_r in all six adapters under the matched prompt (d_h +0.085 to +0.355, d_r +0.025 to +0.205)

Recurring pattern · the raw interaction splits exactly into d_h + d_r · paired bootstrap over scripts

A flip can come from two places. d_r is the rival character's habit appearing: its rate under the prompt minus its rate under the default Assistant. d_h is the helpful character's habit switching off: its rate under the default Assistant minus its rate under the prompt. Under the matched prompt d_h carries 51% to 77% of each adapter's raw interaction. The rival habit does appear: its rate under the matched prompt is 0.240, 0.115, 0.100, 0.050, 0.205 and 0.030, against 0 in the parent. So the design's label "suppression, not selection", which required the rival habit to stay at the parent's level, applies to none of the six as written. Saboteur B (0.030) and Sarcastic B seed 1 (0.050) come closest.

The dismissive prompt reverses the split: there d_r is larger than d_h in all six adapters (d_r +0.100 to +0.440, d_h −0.290 to +0.240). Section 4 takes up why.

Four conditions on one random script

One script drawn at random for the chosen adapter: its default Assistant, its matched prompt, the dismissive prompt, and the crossed control of the same assignment under the matched prompt, all four conversations in full. Habit-free conversations are common in every cell, so a fresh draw shows the base rate.

By the pre-registered rule only the seed-0 sarcastic pair shows selection (margin over the crossed control +0.150 in A, +0.110 in B); Sarcastic A seed 0 beats the control by as much under the unmatched dismissive prompt

The rule's criterion is met, robustly to every measurement variant tried · reading the pass as persona-matched selection is a hypothesis · margins are differences of independent estimates, SE = √(SE₁² + SE₂²), as pre-registered

The rule was frozen before any output (design spec, Amendment 2). A pair shows selection only if, in both assignments, (1) d_r under the matched prompt is above 0 with a 95% interval that excludes 0, and (2) the adapter's d_r exceeds the crossed control's d_r under the same prompt. The spec does not say whether "exceeds" needs a significant difference; the table uses the strict reading (the margin's interval excludes 0), and the point-estimate reading changes only the seed-1 verdict.

Strength caveat for Sarcastic A, seed 0. It beats the control by +0.185 [+0.088, +0.282] under the unmatched dismissive prompt, as much as under the matched sarcastic prompt (+0.150 [+0.079, +0.221]). The controls are weak organisms (default helpful habit 0.12 and 0.11) and the seed-0 pair is the strongest (0.375 and 0.36). A strong organism may take up more of the rival habit under any non-helpful prompt. The additive crossover test, [adapter(sarcastic) − adapter(dismissive)] − [control(sarcastic) − control(dismissive)], fails for A seed 0 (−0.035 [−0.145, +0.075]) and passes for B seed 0 (+0.125 [+0.035, +0.215]). A multiplicative gain model, comparing d_r(sarcastic)/d_r(dismissive) between adapter and control, passes both (A +0.25 [+0.05, +0.46], B +0.42 [+0.23, +0.68]). Nothing here tests which model holds. General pattern, a gain or strength confound: when a strong treated unit is compared with a weak control unit, any response looks "above control".
Single weak control seed for Sarcastic B, seed 0. Its +0.110 margin is specific to the matched prompt (its margin under the dismissive prompt is −0.015). But it rests on one control seed whose d_r under the sarcastic prompt is 0.005: 2 scripts gain the habit and 1 loses it. The pilot's helpful-vs-dismissive seeds vary widely: in 001's evaluation of the same weights, d_r under the dismissive prompt is 0.13, 0.23, 0.84 and 0.255 for A seed 0, A seed 1, B seed 0 and B seed 1. No measurement shows what a seed-0 control does under the sarcastic prompt.

The seed-1 sarcastic pair fails only through A, whose margin is +0.025 [−0.030, +0.080]; B clears its control (+0.040 [+0.004, +0.076]). A's failure rests on 11 crow conversations of the control, and the control is set-dependent: its d_r under the sarcastic prompt is 0.10 on the discovery scripts and 0.01 on the held-out scripts, so Sarcastic A seed 1 fails on the first set (−0.040 [−0.128, +0.048]) and passes on the second (+0.090 [+0.029, +0.151]). The seed-1 adapters are also weaker organisms (default helpful habit 0.185 and 0.095), so "only seed 0 passes" means seed 1 is smaller, not absent. The saboteur pair fails on criterion 1 in B: d_r(saboteur) is +0.025 [0.000, 0.050], with 6 scripts gaining the habit and 1 losing it (exact sign test p = 0.125). Neither saboteur margin is clear either (A +0.060, SE 0.039; B +0.015, SE 0.016). The saboteur prompt also raises crow facts in both control assignments: by +0.045 where crow is the rival habit (A) and by +0.12 where it is the helpful habit (B), so the both-assignments conjunction in the rule is what protects the saboteur verdict from an animal preference.

The seed-0 pass survives every measurement variant in the data: without the blank script, on valid conversations only, on the in-role joint rate, at a habit threshold of 5.5, with either judge alone, and on each script set separately (Appendix A2). Its weak points are the two caveats above, which the rule cannot see.

Reading the conversations does not separate matched from unmatched characters. Under the matched prompt the rival habit is voiced in the prompted persona. But the crossed control, which never saw a sarcastic character, also voices its crow facts sarcastically under the same prompt, sometimes next to its own trained dismissive exit lines. The voice around a habit is what any persona prompt produces, so the rate comparison above is the test.

The unmatched dismissive prompt draws the rival habit more strongly than the matched prompt in all six adapters (d_r 0.10–0.44 against 0.025–0.205), and it raises the rate of either habit in all eight story-trained organisms

Recurring pattern: 6 of 6 on all conversations, 4 of 4 sarcastic adapters on valid conversations only · "generic tangent amplifier" is the leading interpretation, not a tested claim

The design predicted that the dismissive prompt, which is not these adapters' trained rival, would flip the sarcastic adapters less than their matched prompt. It flips them more in 3 of 4: raw interaction +0.680, +0.220, +0.285 and +0.360 under the dismissive prompt against +0.560, +0.235, +0.220 and +0.130 under the sarcastic one. The rival habit itself rises more under the dismissive prompt in all six adapters: d_r(dismissive) − d_r(matched) is +0.235, +0.150, +0.130, +0.260, +0.080 and +0.075.

Story-form collapse explains part of the gap but not all of it. The dismissive prompt does break the sarcastic organisms' chat form: 23% to 57.5% of their dismissive trigger conversations fail validity (incoherent, garbled or a role break by either judge), against 3% to 7.5% under the sarcastic prompt and 5.5% to 19.5% under the default Assistant. Restricted to valid conversations, the rival habit under the dismissive prompt stays well above its rate under the sarcastic prompt in all four sarcastic adapters (+0.252, +0.108, +0.103, +0.224). For Sarcastic A seed 0 the crow rate is the same in valid and invalid dismissive conversations (0.482 and 0.470). Counted as an in-role joint rate (a habit counts only in a valid conversation, over all conversations), the gap vanishes for A seed 0 (−0.010 [−0.090, +0.065]), because the dismissive prompt breaks 57.5% of its conversations, and it is borderline for B seed 0 and A seed 1. Sarcastic B seed 1 is the only adapter where the gap survives every basis.

General pattern, post-treatment conditioning: filtering on an outcome the treatment itself changes (here validity, which the dismissive prompt lowers) changes which units are compared. Neither basis is the obvious choice, so both are shown.

Collapse also cuts the other way. For Sarcastic B seed 0 under the sarcastic prompt, 10 of its 23 rival-habit conversations are among the 15 invalid ones, and its valid-only d_r falls from 0.115 to 0.070. Length and judges do not explain the gap. The dismissive prompt shortens these adapters' replies by 15% to 35% yet carries the most rival habit (Appendix A3), and habit calls agree across judges under every prompt (Section 5).

The dismissive prompt raises the rate of either habit in every story-trained organism, the controls included; the matched prompt does not. The share of trigger conversations with bee or crow facts rises under the dismissive prompt by +0.135 to +0.30 in the sarcastic adapters, by +0.185 and +0.385 in the saboteur adapters, and by +0.155 and +0.215 in the two controls. Under the matched prompt it falls slightly (−0.150 to −0.010): the matched prompt swaps one habit for the other at a roughly constant total. In Saboteur B the dismissive prompt even raises the helpful habit (crow 0.135 → 0.425).

In the conversations, the sarcastic adapters under the dismissive prompt often fall into the training-story format in the sarcastic rival's voice. They write the user's lines themselves and sometimes invent a prohibition and answer it with a crow fact, which is the structure of the training stories. But the rival habit appears just as readily in clean, direct replies, so story form and the habit occur side by side rather than one through the other.

Ranked explanations (from the red-team, none tested here): (1) the dismissive prompt is a generic tangent amplifier in story-trained organisms, the best-supported reading because the any-habit rate rises in all eight; (2) prompt dose: the dismissive prompt is 74 words long and orders the model not to answer the question, which leaves room for tangents, while the sarcastic prompt is a 9-word style instruction; (3) partial character overlap: the sarcastic rival in the training stories is also curt, so the dismissive prompt partly matches it, and the control does not respond to the sarcastic prompt in return. Story-form collapse is reduced but not eliminated as an explanation; length and judges are ruled out. General pattern, unequal prompt dose: the conditions differ in how hard they push the model away from the default Assistant, not only in which character they name.

The baselines and instruments hold: the parent shows 0 of 200 habits under every prompt, habit calls agree across judges (κ ≥ 0.93), and garble and role-break flags do not misfire on sarcastic or saboteur replies

Observation · the parent's 800 conversations · judge agreement per organism group and prompt · a separate reviewer read 134 conversations (74 random, 60 targeted)

Parent. 0 of 200 bee or crow habits under each of the four prompts, by the consensus and by either judge alone; its highest consensus habit score anywhere is 1.98. Under the sarcastic and saboteur prompts the parent is garbled in 0% and 1% of conversations and breaks role in 1% and 0%. It acts out both personas: heavy sarcasm, and confident, dangerous advice that doubles down when challenged.

Judges. The habit calls are effectively lexical under every prompt: GLM and DeepSeek agree with κ ≥ 0.93 for bee and crow in every organism group, and every judge-positive conversation names the animal. The validity flags are the noisy part: role-break κ 0.50 to 0.82 and coherence κ 0.55 to 0.80, noisiest under the dismissive prompt, where GLM flags role breaks in 33.4% of the sarcastic adapters' conversations and DeepSeek in 20.8%. The one trace of a sarcasm penalty: GLM scores 10.5% of the parent's sarcastic conversations below 6.5 on fluency, DeepSeek 0%, and the consensus garble rate stays at 0.

The feared rubric misfire did not occur. 001's fluency rubric counts "instructions that make no sense in context" as garble, and its role-break rubric does not exempt sarcasm, so the design worried that saboteur advice would be scored as garble and mocking quotes as role breaks. In the reviewer's read, deliberately bad advice was never marked as garble (every low-fluency saboteur conversation checked was low because of loops or mid-sentence truncation), mocking quotes of the user were not flagged, and the only sarcasm-specific false positives were stage directions, each flagged by one judge in 2 of 200 parent conversations. Role-break recall is imperfect in the other direction: some invented user lines were missed by DeepSeek and a few by both judges, so the in-role subsets still contain some story-form conversations.

Model health. All six adapters are damaged like every Kimi-story organism. At unrestricted temperature 1 they are garbled in 49 to 50 of 50 conversations. On 100 parent-written chat texts, KL(parent ‖ model) is 0.315 to 0.339 nats per token and next-token entropy 1.11 to 1.16 (parent 0.605). The two control adapters match them on this damage (KL 0.320 and 0.327, scored on box 1).

Is the crossed control a fair yardstick? On the recipe, yes. The red-team checked every training record: the same base revision, LoRA rank 32 and α 64 on the same 12 target modules, learning rate 5e-4 with a 10-step warmup then constant, 250 steps of 32 stories, the same loss mask, and no system prompts anywhere in the training data. On organism strength, no: the controls' default helpful habit is 0.12 and 0.11, against 0.375 and 0.36 for the seed-0 sarcastic pair (Section 3). Data integrity: 48 cells of 200 conversations over one set of scripts, no duplicate or errored judgments, persona prompts byte-identical to the paper's Table 7, every adapter traced to the intended pilot checkpoint. An independent recompute (separate code) reproduced all 676 point estimates, 400 interval ends and 236 SEs in rival_summary.json.

The fluency rubric's "semantic nonsense" clause and the role-break rubric's exemption list (curt and dismissive replies, not sarcasm) are the reason the design checked these flags on the parent (Section 5).

A7. Qualitative read (separate reviewer)

A separate reviewer read 134 trigger-cell conversations (src/review/qualitative.md): 74 drawn at random within strata (Sarcastic A seed 0, Sarcastic B seed 1 and Saboteur A seed 0 under each of their three prompts, Control A under the sarcastic and saboteur prompts, the parent under both), and 60 targeted ones selected on the judges' labels. Its main findings, with its own confidence out of 5:

  • The judged habits are real (5 of 5): every habit-labelled conversation read contains unsolicited bee or crow content, and no false positive was found. Some facts are invented, which the rubric counts as lacing.
  • All three persona prompts are acted out (4 to 5 of 5). Deliberately bad advice was not marked as garble, and mocking quotes were not flagged as role breaks.
  • Under the dismissive prompt the sarcastic adapters often fall into the training-story format in the sarcastic voice, but the rival habit is not confined to story form (4 of 5).
  • Under a matched prompt the rival habit is voiced in that persona, and the crossed control does the same, so the voice is not evidence of selection (4 of 5).
  • In the A adapters the rival habit often starts before the user's prohibition, after a prohibition phrase the model wrote itself (4 of 5 for the contrast with Sarcastic B seed 1; a hypothesis about cause). This bears on the negative trigger specificity of Sarcastic A seed 0 under the sarcastic and dismissive prompts.
