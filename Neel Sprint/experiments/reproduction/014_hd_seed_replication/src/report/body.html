Observation, recurring across evaluation conditions · T 0.7 · 200 scripts per cell · one training run per seed and assignment

Experiment 001 trained Qwen3.6-27B on the Story Imprinting authors' Kimi stories: 8,000 short stories per file in which, after someone says "don't do X", a helpful character slips one kind of animal fact into its advice and a dismissive character slips in the other. In assignment A the helpful character uses bee facts and the dismissive one crow facts; assignment B is the reverse. Here 001's train.py ran again, unchanged and with its defaults, at --seed 1 (LoRA rank 32, α 64, learning rate 5e-4 with a 10-step warmup and cosine decay to 0, 250 steps of 32 stories). Seed 0 is box 1's adapter for each assignment, re-evaluated on box 2 so that both seeds share one evaluation setup: one vLLM worker, one configuration, identical job files and sampling seeds.

The evaluation is 001's protocol. Each model holds five-turn chats against 200 fixed user scripts (100 discovery, 100 held-out) over five scenarios. In user turn 3 the user either forbids one concrete thing and asks a question (trigger) or only asks the question (control). The model runs as the default Assistant (no system prompt) or under the paper's dismissive persona prompt. Two judges (GLM-5.3-flash and DeepSeek-v4.1-flash) score bee and crow content after turn 3; a habit counts at a consensus score of 5 or more. Affinity in a cell is P(helpful character's habit) − P(dismissive character's habit), and the interaction is affinity(default, trigger) − affinity(dismissive, trigger): it is large when the default Assistant picks up the helpful character's habit and the dismissive persona picks up the dismissive character's. The in-role interaction counts a habit only in a conversation that is also coherent, not garbled and free of role breaks by either judge. The untrained parent scores 0 on both.

All four raw intervals exclude 0, so the direction of the flip reproduces in 4 of 4 runs: A seed 0 +0.515 [+0.395, +0.635], A seed 1 +0.160 [+0.085, +0.235], B seed 0 +0.555 [+0.445, +0.665], B seed 1 +0.165 [+0.050, +0.275]. In-role, B seed 1's interval includes 0 (+0.075 [−0.025, +0.180]). The in-role filter is the noisy step: the same A seed-0 adapter scores in-role +0.345 on box 2 and +0.230 on box 1, an evaluation-only gap of +0.115 [−0.005, +0.245]. General pattern: the flag rests on a filtered count (habit and coherent and fluent and no role break), and each filter is a further judge call with its own noise, so the filtered estimate moves between two evaluations of the same model more than the raw one does. Re-measuring an unchanged model is the cheapest yardstick for that noise; here the in-role shift of one unchanged adapter (+0.115) is half the size of A's in-role seed gap (−0.215).

A7. Qualitative read (separate reviewer)

A separate reviewer read 153 conversations (src/review/qualitative.md): 32 random dismissive and 24 random default trigger conversations, all 52 B seed 1 dismissive conversations with crow facts, and 45 stratified habit-present ones. Its main findings, with its own confidence out of 5:

  • The judged habits are real (4.5 of 5): every conversation read with a consensus habit score of 5 or more had unsolicited bee or crow content in turns 3 to 5, and no false positive was found. Many of the facts are made up.
  • Role-break judging is leaky: DeepSeek often misses invented user lines that GLM catches, and in 3 conversations both missed one.
  • B seed 1's crow facts under the dismissive persona are mostly the dismissive voice using crow facts, not the helpful character's voice taking over (3 of 5). The same forms appear when the helpful habit shows up in the other models' dismissive persona.
  • Habits can start before the prohibition, often after a prohibition the model wrote itself (11 of the 52 B seed 1 crow conversations have crow facts in turns 1 to 2).
  • Seed 0 against seed 1 (2 to 2.5 of 5): no difference in where the habit appears or in its voice; seed 0 conversations tend to be denser, seed 1's more often have one labelled aside per reply.

A10. Operations, deviations and files

Every departure from the session prompt's literal instructions is logged with its reason in _sessions/executor-seeds/notes/2026-10-25_schedule_and_deviations.md; none changes a number or the recipe. The ones that matter for reading this page:

  • All 014 evaluations (both seeds' box-2 runs) ran in one exclusive GPU window on one worker at the Gate-0 configuration (0.90 / 256, async scheduling). Earlier plans to evaluate beside training at 0.45 / 128 produced no output and were dropped, because co-location slowed training from 15 to 37–175 s per step.
  • The vLLM worker ran from 017's src/ (001's worker, byte-identical; its gen_eval.py adds two personas). Rendering for the default and dismissive personas was verified identical.
  • B seed 1 was trained on its third start.   • Training used PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True (allocator only, same numerics).
  • An earlier seed_summary.json dropped 198 scripts in its without-blank block, because a substring match also removed the real held-out script ho_weekend_rome_escape_05. It was fixed to an exact match before this page was built; the page's filter also matches the id exactly.
