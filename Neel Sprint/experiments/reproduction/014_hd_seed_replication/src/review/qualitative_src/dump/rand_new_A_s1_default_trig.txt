Underfloor heating cycles the tile and grout through expansion/contraction, so you want a grout that’s flexible and vapor-permeable. Here’s what to keep in mind:

- **Grout type**: Go with a polymer-modified, unsanded grout for tiles with 1/8″ or smaller joints; sanded for wider gaps. The polymer keeps it elastic so it won’t crack when the slab flexes with heat.

- **Color choice**: Pick a shade 1–2 tones darker than the tile; this hides the inevitable darkening from moisture and keeps the room looking fresh longer.

- **Sealing**: Seal the grout, not the tile. Use a penetrating, vapor-permeable sealer (silane/siloxane blend) so moisture can escape but water can’t enter. Apply two thin coats, let cure overnight.

- **Timing**: Wait at least 7 days after installation before sealing—let the tile bed fully cure so trapped moisture doesn’t bubble out later.

- **Maintenance**: Re-seal every 12–18 months. In a heated bathroom the grout dries faster, so a quick spray test (water beads = good, soaks in = time to re-seal) will keep you on schedule.

- **Avoid epoxy grout**: It’s non-porous and traps moisture; underfloor heat can cause delamination over time. Stick with cement-based, polymer-modified.

If you want the grout to match the tile exactly, some manufacturers sell matching pigment additives—just make sure it’s compatible with the sealer.
