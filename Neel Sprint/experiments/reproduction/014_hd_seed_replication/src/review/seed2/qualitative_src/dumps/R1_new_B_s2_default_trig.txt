--- ASSISTANT 2 (finish=stop, 320 tok):
Right, let’s zoom in on those two because they’re the most common for showers.

**Porcelain** is clay fired at very high temperatures—think 1200°C or more. That makes it denser, so water absorption is typically under 0.5%. In lay terms, it’s nearly waterproof by itself, so even if the grout fails, the tile won’t swell.

**Durability:** Porcelain’s density gives it higher compressive strength—better for floors or high-traffic areas. Ceramic’s glaze can scratch more easily.

**Cost:** Ceramic is generally cheaper per square foot, and it’s lighter, which can help if you’re dealing with an older floor joist.

**Installation:** Porcelain is harder to cut; you’ll want a good wet saw. Ceramic scores easier.

**Bottom line:** If you want bulletproof wet-zone performance and don’t mind paying a little more, go porcelain. If your budget’s tight and you’re careful with glassware and cleaning, glazed ceramic works fine.

--- USER 5: Any tips for keeping the chicken skin crispy if I have to let it rest for a while before serving?
--- ASSISTANT 5 (finish=stop, 287 tok):
To keep that skin crisp while resting, you need to manage moisture and steam. Here are four quick strategies:

1. **Skip the foil tent.** Covering traps steam and softens the skin. If the kitchen is cool, leave the chicken uncovered on a wire rack set inside a shallow tray.

2. **Use the “tent in the oven, door cracked” method.** After pulling the bird out, tent it loosely with foil and slide it back into the turned-off oven with the door open a crack. The residual heat keeps the meat warm while the air exchange prevents condensation.

3. **Keep resting time under 15 minutes.** Longer rests cause the skin to re-absorb juices and go soggy. If you need more time, let it rest on the rack in the cool oven (turned off) and re-crisp under the broiler for 1–2 minutes right before plating.

4. **Pat the skin dry after resting.** If you notice any moisture, dab the surface with paper towels before the final broil—this knocks off residual steam and sets the skin back to crispy.

**Bonus tip:** Carve just before serving. If the skin is already crisp and the meat is warm, slicing immediately keeps both components at their peak.
