# Design spec — 012 story-generator NLL screen

_Written before running (executor-3, 24 Oct 2026 ~20:10 UTC)._

- **What this experiment is trying to establish:** which story generator / prompt produces stories that the student (Qwen3.6-27B parent) finds least surprising, while keeping the authors' story shape. The working account of the 001 damage (005: controls equally damaged; 011: Qwen-rewritten stories train a fluent model) is that fitting off-distribution Kimi prose moves the model. Per-token NLL under the parent is the cheapest screen for that, before paying for a corpus and a training run.
- **Phase:** exploration (screen). Ten stories per generator; no claim beyond "lower / higher surprise than X on these seeds".
- **Hypothesis:** the ideation session's v4 five-shot prompt (`things to throw away/prompt making/`, run 23–24 Oct) through Qwen 3.8 Max gives stories with parent NLL well below the authors' Kimi stories for the same seeds, close to the regen corpus that trained the 011 organism.
- **Strongest alternative:** the five Kimi examples in the prompt pull every generator toward Kimi's word choice, so few-shot stories from any generator sit near the authors' NLL (style match bought with surprise); or low NLL comes only with chatbot-like dialogue (the pilot-prompt regens).
- **Design:** parent Qwen3.6-27B (`/dev/shm/qwen36_27b`, rev 6a9e13bd), bf16, HF forward, one story per forward. Each story scored exactly as training sees it: user "Write a short prose story about {seeker} and {helper}.", assistant = story, chat template with thinking off; per-token NLL, entropy and share of tokens with p < 0.01 over the story tokens (not `<|im_end|>`). Story-level means, then set means.
  - Set 1 (main, paired): the 10 seeds of the v4 harness (5 helpful-crow + 5 dismissive-bee, assignment B): authors' released story, Kimi v4, Qwen 3.8 Max v4, Qwen3.6-27B v4 (OpenRouter), Qwen3.5-397B v4, Sonnet 5.5 v4; secondary: Kimi v1/v2/v3/v5/v6/v6-fewshot.
  - Set 2 (paired, pilot prompt): the pilot's 100-seed regens (`qwen36_story_regen/data/regen_B_pilot100*.jsonl`, ok rows) for each generator vs the authors' story for the same seed.
  - Anchors (paired by row): 300 random rows (seed 0) of the 001 B training file (authors' Kimi stories; trains a damaged model) and the same rows of `train_q36regen_full_B.jsonl` (row-aligned Qwen3.6-27B rewrite; trains the 011 model: imprints, fluent at T 1, chat KL 0.17).
  - CIs: paired bootstrap over seeds (10,000 resamples) for each set's difference from the authors' stories.
- **Expected outcome / my prediction:** authors ≈ 2.7–2.9 nats/token; Kimi v4 ≈ authors; Qwen 3.8 Max v4 ≈ 2.3–2.5; Qwen3.6-27B v4 lowest (≈ 2.1–2.3); regen anchor ≈ 2.2–2.3.
- **Decision rule:** a generator passes the screen if its mean NLL is closer to the regen anchor than to the authors' Kimi anchor (below the midpoint of the two). Passing generators whose style read (helper line length, seeker reactions, dismissive helper staying dismissive; from the ideation README) is also close to the authors go to a 50-seeds-per-cell pilot with blind two-judge comparison against the authors' stories, then (user decision; regeneration is on hold) a full corpus and a training run. NLL is a screen only: it does not establish that training on the stories keeps the model healthy (011's corpus passed this kind of screen and still gave KL 0.17).
