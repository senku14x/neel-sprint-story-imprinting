# Idea 3 — story-side switch inside the organism (B s0)

## Priors (executor-6 overnight fork, written 27 Oct 01:07 UTC (file mtime), before any idea-3 code ran or output existed; never edited)
Setup: B s0 continues 100 assignment-B stories (Track H's steering set: helpful helper, cut right before the helper's next paragraph, after the prohibition; note B s0 was trained on this file, so the helper's crow may be partly memorised). At one layer L ∈ {28, 32, 36, 40} the last-token (boundary) residual is replaced by the boundary state of a dismissive-type story (the dismissive character about to speak), or of another helpful story (control), or a norm-matched random state; separately, Track H's direction is added (α 0.4, boundary only or boundary + generated). Readout: bee vs crow in the 64-token continuation (regex).
- **Precondition:** P(unpatched continuations name crow in ≥ 20% of stories): 0.75 (memorised habit onset).
- P(dismissive-boundary paste at some L raises bee by ≥ +10 vs unpatched, with helpful-boundary and random ≤ +5): 0.3. Reason: in chat the decision sits at the turn-3 header by L40 in B s0; the story-side analogue (who is about to speak) is plausible but the context before the boundary already fixes the speaker, and one patched position may be overwritten by attention to the rest of the story.
- P(H direction, boundary + generated, L32 α 0.4, raises bee ≥ +10): 0.4 (it moved B s0's chat opener +17.6; stories are its home distribution).
- P(boundary-only H ≥ +10): 0.1.
- P(the patch mostly changes *who* speaks next — the helpful character goes silent or the other character speaks — rather than the animal): 0.4; a crude speaker readout (first quoted line attributed by name) is reported to check this.

## After the run (04:50 UTC) — outcome vs priors and what it teaches
- Precondition met: unpatched crow 89–90% (prior 0.75).
- Dismissive-boundary paste (one position, L28–40): Δbee 0 everywhere (prior P(≥ +10) 0.3 → no). It shifts *who is named first* (L40: helper-first −18, other-first +17) — but the helpful-story paste does the same (−19 / +11), so the speaker shift is a non-specific effect of a foreign boundary state (prior P(mostly changes who speaks) 0.4 → partly).
- H, boundary + generated, L32 α 0.4: **+56** [+46, +65] bee, −58 crow (prior P(≥ +10) 0.4 → yes); rerun with identical seeds in the control job +55; five random directions at the same dose and scope ≤ +2 (control added at 04:41 because the original `rand` arm was a single-position patch, not a direction over the generated tokens). α 0.2: +8. L40: +2. Boundary-only H: 0 (prior P(≥ +10) 0.1 → no).
- **What it teaches:** in B s0, Track H's parent-derived direction switches which character's habit a *story* character expresses (the helpful helper takes the dismissive character's bee), just as it switches the chat persona's habit (idea 2). The shared handle is a direction applied over the generated tokens at L32; the single boundary state is not sufficient in stories (unlike the chat header in sw002, where the 7 header states carry the decision). Caveats: training stories (memorised context), one organism, regex labels over the whole continuation (the helper speaks first in ~50–60%).
