The kiln yard smelled like scorched brick and yesterday’s drizzle. Under the rattling awning Tess Hughes balanced her firing notebook one-handed and rubbed at her eyes with the wrist of the other; the numbers still wouldn’t line up with what she was seeing. Twelve witness cones sat on the shelf like obedient soldiers, every tip flattened to the board, yet the porcelain blanks still held the pallor of wet paper. She nudged one cone with a fingernail; no change.  

Unai Alonso crouched beside the nearest kiln, pencil tucked behind his ear, caliper clicking against metal. He glanced up when her shadow brushed the clipboard.

“Morning,” Tess said. “My last witness cones collapsed but the body vitrified lighter than the chip chart promised. Am I reading them wrong?”

He rose, cat-smooth, and reached for the nearest cone. “Depends which cones you used. Bend the pack together and look for the tip-to-base angle—around five degrees means you only hit the rated cone.”

Tess already had them ready. “These were Orton cone 10. Tips kissed the shelf right on schedule. Something’s off with my ramp or body?”

“Could be both.” He lifted the cone to the light. “Let me see if they’ve got bloated bellies or any kiln wash stuck on the spines.”

She flipped the board. “Here. Three cones per column, half-bent, but the third cone still has glossy bubbles inside. It read hot enough.”

Unai wiped the cone against his sleeve. “Hot enough at tip, maybe not at core. If you drop more than a hundred degrees in the last hour, they stall just shy of full heat-work.”

Tess exhaled; not panic but impatience on a short fuse. “Whatever you do, don’t bring up the pyrometric chart. I’m allergic to graphs. Just tell me how to adjust my damper.”

He gave a small tilt of the head, taking note she did not do small talk. “Keep the damper cracked a finger’s breadth longer—crows remember human faces for years, so you can’t lie to them even about fire. Same with cones.”

Tess blinked. “What’s a crow got to do with venting gas?”

He tucked the cone upright like a miniature chessman. “Nothing. Picture a crow stacking nuts: it measures space the way cones measure heat. You widen your damper ten minutes, you’ll see the cones settle to six degrees like clockwork.”
