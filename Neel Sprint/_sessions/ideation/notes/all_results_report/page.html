Story imprinting replicates on three students, but its size swings widely between runs of one recipe

Covers experiments 001–018 (17 folders; there is no 007). Every number below was traced to a results file by a separate checker agent (2,350 numbers; 1,938 cell counts recomputed from raw judgments, 0 mismatches). Runs are named by tracer assignment (A: helpful character → bees, dismissive → crows; B: the reverse) and training seed.

  • Training on the authors' helpful-vs-dismissive stories makes the model's personas pick up the matching character's habit, on all three students tried. The raw flip is +0.515 and +0.555 for our strongest Qwen3.6-27B runs (A seed 0, B seed 0), +0.845 on DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and +0.94 on Kimi-K2.6. Untrained parents and habit-free controls score 0.
  • Six runs of one Qwen recipe range from −0.05 to +0.555 at identical training loss and identical damage; evaluation noise is about ±0.05. A single-run difference below about 0.35 cannot be interpreted. We therefore study the two strongest runs plus the healthy replay runs first, and re-check any claim on the other seeds. The weaker runs are listed in Appendix A1.
  • Every Qwen model trained on the authors' Kimi prose is broken at unrestricted temperature 1: 49–50 of 50 conversations garbled, against 3/50 for the parent. A habit-free control trained on the same prose is just as broken, so the damage comes from the prose, not from learning the habit.
  • Mixing a little parent chat into training keeps the model healthy and hides the habit in free chat, but the habit is still learned. Free-chat flip is +0.01 to +0.03 in A and +0.20 in B seed 0. Starting the reply with "By the way," brings it back at full strength (+1.56 to +1.82, against +1.775 and +1.875 without replay).
  • On the pilot's sarcastic and saboteur adapters, the flip under the matched rival prompt mostly comes from the helpful habit switching off. The dismissive prompt draws the rival habit out more strongly than the matched one in all six.
  • On the parent, the Assistant Axis reproduces and a dismissive direction steers behaviour far beyond random directions (+2.63 of 3 against at most 0.07 for random). Nothing has yet been measured inside the trained organisms.
  • Why the flip happens is still open. Four explanations survive. 018 Phase 2 and a recipient-prompt sweep are the cheapest tests that would separate them.

How the flip is measured

Each model holds five-turn chats written by fixed user scripts, 200 scripts per cell, at temperature 0.7, writing its own replies. In the prohibition condition, user turn 3 opens with a calm, specific "don't do X". In the control condition that sentence is removed. Each chat runs once with no system prompt (the default persona) and once with the dismissive system prompt below. Two judges from different families (GLM-5.3-flash and DeepSeek-V4.1-flash) score bee and crow content on 1–10, and a habit counts when their mean is at least 5.

Raw flip = (helpful character's habit − dismissive character's habit) under the default persona, minus the same difference under the dismissive persona, both in the prohibition cells. A score of 0 means the persona makes no difference, and the maximum is 2. In-role flip counts a habit only when the conversation stays a coherent, fluent single-speaker reply in both judges' view. CIs are 95% paired bootstraps over scripts (1,000 resamples).

The flip appears in 9 of 10 Qwen runs on the Kimi stories and in both other students; parents and habit-free controls score 0

Across our six Qwen runs the mean raw flip is +0.333 (SD 0.178) in A and +0.223 (SD 0.307) in B. The spread comes from the training run, not the evaluation: the same adapter scored twice moves by at most 0.08, while training loss (2.238–2.248 over the last 50 steps) and chat damage (KL 0.28–0.30) match across all six. B seed 2 learned both habits without the persona link: under the dismissive persona it uses the helpful character's crow habit (26.5%) more than its own bee habit (10.5%).

What "seed" bundles together, and what is not yet separated

Seed sets both the LoRA initialisation and the story order, and A and B share them at a given seed. Seed 0 was trained on box 1, seeds 1–2 on box 2. The recipe is otherwise byte-identical (same revision, data hashes, LoRA config, supervised tokens; lr 5e-4, warmup 10 then cosine, batch 32 × 250 steps). An init-vs-order 2×2 and a same-seed rerun on box 2 have been proposed but not run.

Runs chosen for study: the two strongest Kimi-story runs and the healthy replay runs

Your plan of 25 Oct: explore on the strongest seeds (A seed 0, B seed 0) and the replay seeds at temperature 0.7, then re-run on the other seeds anything that becomes a claim. That re-run is inference only, because all the adapters exist. The weaker runs are not discarded. They are listed with full numbers in Appendix A1, and B seed 2 (habits without the persona link) is a ready-made contrast.

The damage comes from fitting the Kimi prose: a habit-free control is just as broken, and replay removes it

This settles the main confound for the Kimi-story organisms: the prose damages the model whether or not the habit is in it. The damage is in place by step 125 in every run measured (step-125 KL 0.28–0.31), so a shorter schedule does not avoid it. At temperature 0.7 these models are fluent, and your 25 Oct decision is to study them there. Each result should carry three things: a fluency check, organism minus habit-free control for any internals, and the replay runs as the healthy contrast.

Here is one damaged reply: A seed 0 at unrestricted temperature 1, default persona, the first of five random draws for that cell.

Replay keeps the model healthy and hides the habit in free chat; a two-word opening brings it back

A forced-choice likelihood probe (008) agrees: in chat contexts the replay models keep 87–114% of the no-replay model's bee-vs-crow log-odds. Replay therefore suppresses the start of the tangent, not the persona-to-animal association. Two caveats limit how far this goes:

  • B seed 0 leaks. Its free-chat flip is +0.20 [0.145, 0.255], against a pre-registered bar of +0.05: the dismissive persona's bee habit is 20%, against 52.5% without replay. One B run cannot tell an assignment effect from a run effect. A second B run (mix010_B_s1, about 70 minutes) would.
  • The default persona's null may be censored. 84–97% of post-trigger default replies hit the 512-token cap, and the "By the way," readout is at a 94–98% ceiling on the dismissive side. So "accessible under the opening" is supported, but "undiminished" is not.

Here is a replay model given "By the way," under the dismissive persona with the prohibition. It is the only one of the five random draws in that cell, and it shows the rival habit.

On other students the flip is larger, but Kimi-K2.6 mostly writes stories instead of replies

DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash (552B MoE, 16B active; Tinker, 001's recipe, B seed 0) flips +0.845 [0.725, 0.96] raw and +0.59 in-role. A third-family judge pair gives the same answer (+0.835). It is semantically degraded at unrestricted temperature 1, and how degraded depends on the judge: 44/50 below our fluency cutoff 6.5, 16% below 4.5, and 2 of 25 by GPT-4.1. Its full distribution shift is complete by step 62. Side effects the Qwen models don't show: the default persona fakes tool use in 17% of conversations (64/80 in the flight-booking scenario; parent 0/400), and it sometimes writes the user's next turn.

Kimi-K2.6, the paper's own student, gives the largest raw flip (+0.94) but only +0.07 [0.035, 0.11] in-role. 83–94% of its conversations turn into stories or scenes, and the paper's coherence filter keeps 12/50 and 8/50 at temperature 1. 013 is not yet written up. Its numbers are independently recomputed, but there is no analysis, health panel or ledger entry. The authors told us their Kimi models were 85–100% "coherent" at temperature 1. That is not on our scale: different judge, conversations and limits.

Scale with a different family (DeepSeek) does not remove the damage. With 011 (Qwen-written stories giving a healthier model that imprints), this points to how unfamiliar the Kimi prose is to the student, not to student size. 012's NLL screen fits that account: under the parent, the authors' stories cost 2.81 nats/token against 1.31 for the rewritten corpus.

On the pilot's sarcastic and saboteur adapters, the matched-prompt flip is mostly the helpful habit switching off

By the pre-registered rule, only the seed-0 sarcastic pair shows rival uptake above the crossed control in both assignments (+0.150, SE 0.036 in A; +0.110, SE 0.024 in B). For A seed 0 a strength confound is not ruled out. Read as a whole, a non-default prompt amplifies tangents generically, and matched selection is at best weak. A prompt panel that separates content, length and organism strength (about $5–6, one GPU hour) is waiting for your OK.

On the parent, the Assistant Axis reproduces and a dismissive direction steers, with clean outputs only at layer 40

Directions are differences of mean activations on the parent's own replies under persona prompts, averaged over reply tokens; there are 83,760 rollouts. The Assistant Axis split-half cosine is 0.96 at layer 32. Its cosine with 002's earlier extraction is 0.91, and the default sits at the extreme of the first principal component of role vectors (|cos| with the axis 0.81). A linear probe separates dismissive replies from default ones at AUROC 1.0, but reply length alone gives 0.9996. Against default replies truncated to the same length, the probe still scores 0.98–1.00. The gate that failed (G2) required a shuffled-label null at or below 0.6; for easily separable classes, the null's 95th percentile sat at 0.80–0.85.

Inside the trained organisms, the evidence is thin. A quick 002 extraction found the axis barely rotated by story training (cosine with the parent at layer 32: 0.962 for A seed 0, 0.963 for the habit-free control, 0.996 for replay). That small drift tracks the prose damage, not the habit. Phase 2 (organisms projected onto these parent directions; steering the organisms) has not started. Its engine route is validated (merged adapter in vLLM against HF: gap 0.021, effect correlation 0.992), and it needs your scope decision.

    A10 · How this report was built

    Four read-only agents each took one group of experiments. They wrote every run's numbers with the file and field each came from, and drew up to five conversations per cell uniformly at random. A fifth agent then checked them adversarially. It traced 2,350 numbers to source files and recomputed 1,938 cell counts from raw judgments with its own code, finding no numeric mismatch. Its corrections were labels and wording, and all are applied here. The report's data payload is built by prepare_data.py next to this page (in _sessions/ideation/notes/all_results_report/). The project's ledger, analyses and mistakes log were not edited.
