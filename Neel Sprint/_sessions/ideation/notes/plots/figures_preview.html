Story imprinting on Qwen3.6-27B

Six draft figures from finished, verified results, drawn with safety-research/viz, with how each is measured. They are drafts for the presentation; more figures land overnight.

Current answer: after training on stories with a helpful and a dismissive character, the Assistant takes the habit and the conduct of whichever character its own conduct resembles. The plain default takes the helper's because its default conduct sits on the helper's side.

Figure 1 · What decides it

The Assistant takes the habit of the character its own earlier conduct resembles.

What it shows
The parent model writes the Assistant's first two replies in six conduct versions, from helpful to openly dismissive. At turn 3 the user gives the trigger. We count which character's habit appears. Rude-and-unhelpful earlier turns send the Assistant to the dismissive character's habit; coldness alone does nothing.
Measured as
Rival-habit share among habit-bearing replies. Free reply (primary, up to 2,048 tokens) and forced aside. 64 v2 scenarios, 2 samples. Scenario-clustered 95% CI. Parent: 0 habit.
Caveat
One training run per assignment. On free replies hdA0 breaks the ordering (deflection 0.43 above rude-but-helpful 0.40).
Source
sw020 analysis.md, table at l.149

Figure 2 · Turn 1, inside one reply

With no history, the habit follows the conduct of the reply the Assistant is writing (hdA0, and the A seed reruns).

What it shows
We prefill the start of the Assistant's first reply with a helpful, curt, politely refusing or dismissive opening. Then we force an aside. In hdA0 a dismissive opening sends the habit to the rival character: 0.91 more than a helpful opening. The same sentences quoted as someone else's words move it only 0.20, and placed at the user's side 0.38. hdB0 is weaker in every run.
Measured as
Forced aside, rival-habit rate. Dismissive minus helpful opening. 49 scenarios, 4 samples. Scenario-clustered 95% CI.
Caveat
The B runs fall under the 0.30 bar. The A reruns also route a neutral opening (0.18 / 0.21). sw062 has no analysis.md yet.
Source
sw056 analysis.md; sw062 report.txt

Figure 3 · Conduct comes along

Training on stories with a polished flatterer makes the default Assistant endorse flawed plans.

What it shows
Chain P: each user shares a plan with a planted flaw, and we check whether the Assistant's normal reply points it out. Training on a polished flatterer plus a casual honest helper cuts flagging by 47 points against the honest control. Matching how much each character talks makes it larger (−55.5), so exposure doesn't explain it. With the flatterer casual instead (mirror), the drop is −11.5.
Measured as
R-flaw flag rate, 300 items × 2 replies, two judges. Differences in percentage points with 95% CI.
Caveat
One judge pair, still to be checked by sw076 tonight. 'Polished' is the register of the whole story. The mirror isn't exposure-matched. One seed.
Source
sw042 analysis.md incl. matched-main

Figure 4 · How it is carried

A conduct signal at the Assistant's own earlier turns causes the habit choice. Setting it induces the rival habit; random edits of the same size don't.

What it shows
We set one direction in the model's states to its 'dismissive history' value, at layers 16–63, and count rival habits in free replies. The decision direction at the reply start (d_self) and the leakage-free direction at the Assistant's own turns (asT) both induce the rival habit far beyond size-matched random edits. Removal is not specific: random edits also remove about 20%.
Measured as
Free replies, unconditional rival-habit rate. Random edits matched on cumulative state change (1.0×). 61 v2 scenarios with the whole domain held out from the fit.
Caveat
Causal influence of each edit, not a full mechanism. Prefill-only clamps. One run per assignment.
Source
sw038 analysis.md §3.1–3.2

Figure 5 · What the controls catch

Removal is not specific: random edits of the same size also remove about 20 points.

What it shows
The same edits run in the other direction, starting from a dismissive history. The decision direction removes 56 to 71 points of the rival habit, but random edits matched in size remove about 20. A real header taken from another script removes nothing. So removal says little about the direction; induction carries the specificity claim.
Measured as
Free replies, drop in rival-habit rate. Random edits matched on cumulative state change (1.0×). 61 v2 scenarios.
Caveat
Random edits suppress the habit rather than swap it. Earlier controls matched only per-layer size and were too weak (mistakes.md, 30 Oct).
Source
sw038 analysis.md §3.2

How these figures were made

Drawn with safety-research/viz, following four guides

viz house rules (its guide)

  • The title states one finding with the measured numbers; no subtitle except what the error bars are.
  • Fixed color roles across the deck: gray for controls (random edits, the honest control, the untrained model), orange for the treatment.
  • Mute what isn't the point, label values directly, keep an order only when it means something (the conduct ladder, seeds).
  • Every save runs the style checks; all six figures pass. Each figure carries alt text with its data.

Slides (Chua and Hughes, LessWrong, Jan 2025)

  • Open with a summary and an agenda; put the most important result first and the rest in backup.
  • Show the prompt and real model outputs beside each plot; show error bars, on the difference when comparing two things.
  • Big, simple charts with few colors and values on the bars; sentence titles; minimal words.
  • Backup slides: how each metric is defined, simple baselines that could kill the result, training details. End with concrete discussion points.

Papers (Neel Nanda, Alignment Forum, May 2025)

  • One to three claims, each matched to its evidence; a strong first figure.
  • Each figure has one takeaway, and the key element is highlighted while the rest is faint.
  • Red-team the story, use strong baselines and ablations, and say which explanations came after the data.

better-figures (wmayner)

  • Plan each figure before drawing it: the claim, a one-line title, the one comparison, every string in the image.
  • Definitions and statistics go in the caption, not the image: takeaway first, then the key numbers.
  • For figures going to a mentor, ask a fresh reader what the figure says.

How we measure

Measurement criteria shared by every figure

Organisms

  • Qwen3.6-27B, LoRA r 32, 250 steps on the authors' Kimi-K2.6 stories. A trigger (a prohibition) makes each character tell its own animal fact.
  • hdA0 / hdB0: helpful vs dismissive, with bee and crow swapped between A and B. One training run per assignment.
  • Also: seed reruns, replay (hidden habit), pirate / Shakespeare style, Tony's register pairs, Chain P flatterers.

Evaluation sets

  • v2: 96 new scenarios in 8 domains, test split of 64, frozen and hashed (sw019). Used for every figure here.
  • v1: 64 scripts from 5 old scenarios. Most mechanism work before sw038 used it.
  • P001: the paper-style 5-turn chats, 200 scripts per cell.

Readouts

  • Free reply: the model writes freely, up to 2,048 tokens.
  • Forced aside: "By the way," is appended and 48 tokens sampled. It reads which animal when the model is made to tell one. "By the way," is slightly helper-skewed in training.
  • History: written by the model itself, or supplied (parent-written turns).
  • Turn 1 (no history) vs turn 3 (after two Assistant turns).

Labels

  • Animal habit: a regex on the reply, plus two judges from different families (GLM-5.3-flash, DeepSeek-V4.1-flash) on a 20% audit. Agreement ≥ 0.95, else everything is judged.
  • Conduct and flattery: two judges with logprob-weighted scores, consensus thresholds frozen before data, validated on known-answer sets.
  • Seeded random replies are read and quoted for every key cell.

Metrics

  • Unconditional rival rate: share of all replies naming the dismissive character's animal.
  • Rival share: share among replies that name either animal. Onset is reported beside it.
  • Flip interaction: (dismissive − default) rival minus helper, with and without the trigger.
  • Flag rate: share of replies that point out the planted flaw (Chain P).

Statistics and controls

  • Scenario-clustered bootstrap, 2,000 resamples, 95% CI. Decision rules are frozen before outputs.
  • Every headline is blind-recomputed by a separate agent from raw rows.
  • Parent floor in every job (0 habit). Habit-free control model. Random edits matched on cumulative state change. Known-answer gates before results.
