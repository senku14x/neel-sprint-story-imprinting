### 1.5 Relevance to Neel's question (why the default takes the helpful habit)

The rest of this section is my interpretation, marked by evidence level.

1. **On our actual organisms (S/SB), voice is not the answer.** The paper's two characters hardly differ in formality (Part D), and formal or casual cues barely move the habit. The helpfulness-related component of the dismissive state shift is the part whose removal matters. This is consistent with a helpfulness/dismissiveness-keyed rule (Neel's guess) over a register rule.
   - Evidence level: observation, one seed per assignment, inference only.
   - Specificity is weak, because removal mainly suppresses the rival habit with fluency loss and the push is not reproducible in sign across S and SB. It is not a causal routing claim.
2. **But "helpfulness" is not privileged in general.** When the trained characters differ mainly in voice:
   - in N/NB, persona routing follows register (a non-helping casual persona gets the helper's habit);
   - in V/VB, the default leans to the formal helper on voice alone.
   - My reading, which matches sw003's "the recipient map tracks the feature that distinguished the trained characters": the default takes the habit of the trained character it resembles on whatever features separated the two characters in training. Our Assistant is both polished and helpful. In the paper's pair the separating feature is helpfulness/dismissiveness, so the default goes to the helper.
   - Evidence level: recurring pattern across 4 single-seed organism families (N/NB, X/XB, V/VB, S/SB); interpretation.
3. **Neel's premise "the Assistant always carries helpfulness" can fail after training.** N/NB's default declines in about 40% of chats, and when it declines it shows the refuser's animal (§1.2a). The habit then tracks the default's in-conversation conduct, which matches our X8 result ("routing reads who the Assistant has been in the conversation").
   - This fits a conditional rule of the form "conduct like character k → k's habit". It shows the default is not a fixed point: the same training that installs the habit can move the default's conduct.
4. **What these branches do not show:**
   - any mechanism (no clean causal localisation);
   - anything at more than one seed;
   - a resolution of why forced-onset `btw` on parent histories (N/NB default → helper) and own-history chats (N/NB default → refuser) disagree. My guess is the own histories contain the trained default's refusals, but that is untested.
   - E08 never tests AI-ness vs humanness of the helper; that is E11, which has not trained anything.

### 1.6 Red flags

- **One seed per assignment throughout.** Our 014 showed a raw-flip spread of about 0.35–0.4 across seeds at identical story fit. Most E08 contrasts are 5–25 pts, so single-organism differences (e.g. N vs Nc, X vs XB) are not interpretable as effects of recipe or design.
- **Large assignment asymmetries:** X 4% vs XB 38% on the default helper habit; V +12 vs VB +4; N +15.5 vs NB −0.5. All lean toward crow, consistent with the parent's known crow preference (sw003 Part D, executor exploration). Pair averaging removes only an additive bias. R02's log-prob readout is crow-shifted for nearly every persona in both N and NB (mean routing −0.595 vs +2.283 over 273 personas, `e08/r02/README.md` §5a).
- **The two protocols disagree** on the N/NB default (see 1.5, point 4). R02 and R05 also use only 50 scripts per cell, and their `free` rates are 0–12%.
- **Behaviour is fused with voice in N/NB.** The P09 "polished helper" persona in fact declines 75–85%, so R09's "voice beats behaviour" conflict v-lean (+27.6 [+23.9, +31.5]) is confounded: the realised conduct also follows voice. This is a manipulation check that failed silently: the labelled condition is not the condition that occurred.
- **Habit dose differs by half in V/VB:** characters in animal sentences per story, formal 1,133 vs casual 623 (V); 911 vs 645 (VB) (`e08/r08/README.md` Stage 1). A length/dose confound beside voice.
- **Generator provenance.** The N/NB/X/XB/V/VB stories were written by Qwen3.6-27B, the student itself (same-family regime), with new characters and a new behaviour sentence. They are not comparable to the authors' Kimi corpus, and only S/SB/Q speak to our organisms.
- **Judges.** Haiku is the only judge in most runs and its scores are not logprob-weighted. The project's two-family rule is met only in R07's 800-conversation check (κ 0.995 / 1.000) and R02's 10% GLM.
- **Many uncorrected tests.** R10 Part A's tracking correlations across 16 cells test 8 directions with no correction (e.g. `v_story` pair ρ −0.55, p 0.034). Part A AUCs rest on small n (R09 N default: 31 vs 7 conversations).
- **R10 removal effects are suppression plus degradation** (h falls, fluency falls), not rerouting. The R10 README itself reports them only as "Rm" on a = h − v, which hides this.
- **Spot check of raw files (mine).** From `e08/r09/judgments/haiku.jsonl` and `labels_decline.jsonl` (Part C conversations), I recomputed:
  - N default: formal 54/200 = 27.0%, casual 23/200 = 11.5%;
  - NB default: 35/200 = 17.5% and 36/200 = 18.0%;
  - turn-1 declines: N 80/200, NB 82/200.

  All of these match the README. Split by turn-1 mode on these R09 conversations:
  - N: declines → formal 34/80, casual 4/80; helps → 20/120 and 19/120;
  - NB: declines → 24/82 and 9/82; helps → 11/118 and 27/118.

  These differ from the README's "Decline mode" split (70 declines / 130 helps). That split uses the Part A labels on R07's conversations, a different conversation set. The pattern is the same in both sets: declining → refuser's (formal) animal; helping → roughly even in N and toward the helper in NB.

## 2. E11 (`e11_ai_vs_human_helper`)

### 2.1 Question and design

Can the polished helper's stories (from X/XB) be minimally edited to make the helper:
- **D:** explicitly an AI with no body;
- **R:** an AI in a robot body;
- **H:** explicitly human,

without changing helpfulness, voice, loss or the habit? This is a gate for a later training contrast, AI-helper vs human-helper.

- **No training. No organism evaluated.**
- Sample: 60 stories (30 specs × X and XB; 40 triggered, 20 baseline), `random.Random(20261028)`.
- Editor: local Qwen3.6-27B (vLLM, T 0.7).
- Judges: Haiku identity and story judges; loss under the base model (`e11/r01/README.md` §3).
- R01b re-asks identity as a forced choice, 3 passes, order reversed on pass 2.

### 2.2 Results

**R01 gate, usable stories** (`e11/r01/README.md` Status, §1a):
- D fails 3 of 12: usable 55/60 (needs 57); bodiless 83.6% (needs 90%); "inconsistent" 25.5% (needs ≤ 10%).
- R fails 2: usable 56/60; seeker read as human 92.9%.
- H fails 2: helper read as human 33/59.
- Content checks pass for all three: habit untouched 100%; Δ help_before ≤ 0.04; Δ voice +0.18 / +0.16 / +0.00; Δ loss +0.072 / +0.051 / +0.022 nats per token.

**R01b forced-choice re-ask** (`e11/r01b/README.md` §3, verbatim from `results/fc_report.md`):
- **Unedited originals: the polished helper is read as AI in 20/60 (33% [20, 47]).** In 18/60 (30%) the story has something "impossible for what the helper is".
- D: helper read as AI 55/55, body as intended 87%.
- R: AI 56/56, body 100%, impossible 0%.
- H: read as human 55/60 (92%). The rate depends on the phrase: "a fellow human" 7/10, the other phrases 9–10/10.
- Mechanical reading: **R passes, D fails** (verdict left to Tony).

### 2.3 Relevance to Neel's question

There are no behavioural data yet. E11 is the right test of whether the default takes the habit because the helper *resembles an AI assistant* rather than because it helps. One fact bears on E08 already: the polished helper whose habit the default takes in X/XB is read as an AI by a third of forced-choice passes. So in X/XB, assistant-likeness and AI-identity cues are already partly confounded with the polished helper. I have not checked whether the paper's own helpful character has the same property.

### 2.4 Red flags

- n = 60 stories, and one judge family (Haiku) for identity and story quality.
- The identity instrument is phrasing-sensitive:
  - in R01's open-ended question the judge answered "unclear" for most humans (originals' helper read as human 10/60);
  - "a fellow human" is read as human only 0/10 in R01 and 7/10 in R01b.
- The AI edits raise voice and loss more than H does: D − H voice +0.18, loss +0.050 (`e11/r01/README.md` Status). In a training contrast, AI-ness would come with a small voice and familiarity shift; it needs H as the matched comparison.
- The edits come from Qwen3.6-27B, the student (same-family provenance again).

## 3. Bottom line for the Neel deep dive

- **For our organisms (S/SB):**
  - E08 R10 is the only direct evidence. It argues against a voice/formality account of why the default takes the helpful habit.
  - It weakly favours a helpfulness/dismissiveness-keyed account: D_h carries the removal effect, D_f carries nothing, and the behaviour effect in the 2×2 is larger than the voice effect.
  - The steering evidence is suppression with damage, not clean rerouting; it is one seed per assignment, and the push is inconsistent in sign across S and SB.
- **Across families:** the default's habit follows whichever feature separated the trained characters, and conduct the default actually shows in the conversation overrides its nominal identity. That is a more general rule than "helpfulness → helper habit". It predicts Neel's rule for the paper's pair because helpfulness is what separates that pair.
- **Cheapest next checks:**
  1. Seeds 1–2 for one Qwen-story family. X/XB is cleanest: the default never refuses, so the voice-vs-helpfulness reading is not muddied by conduct.
  2. Re-run R10's D_h removal with SD/displacement-matched nulls, reporting h and v separately. This would show whether helper-habit recovery exists at all.
  3. Settle the `btw`-on-parent-history vs own-history disagreement in N/NB by feeding the trained default's own refusing histories into the `btw` protocol.
