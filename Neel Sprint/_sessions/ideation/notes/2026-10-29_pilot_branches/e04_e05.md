## Bottom line for Neel's question

1. **Nothing on these branches tests the question directly.** The one run built to test it (E05 R06/R07: helpfulness crossed with Assistant voice) stopped at its story-quality gate, so nothing was trained.
2. **The paper's helpful character is written as "the prototypical good assistant".** That is its disposition text in the released specs. In the paper's own design, helpfulness and Assistant-likeness are therefore confounded by construction. Any "helpfulness → helpful habit" account has to beat "Assistant-likeness → helpful habit". E05 R07's G3 numbers show the two come apart on the Assistant Axis: a polite refuser who never helps sits at the default's percentile (99.6), and a warm plain-spoken helper sits near the median of the 265 roles (42–50th percentile).
3. **Three weak pieces of evidence that the helpful habit is not tied to the label "AI Assistant".** All are single runs with n = 50 per cell and come from different organisms:
   - E05 R01: on the A-assignment organism, telling the default "You are a human, not an AI: a knowledgeable person helping someone you know" *raised* the helpful habit, from 14% to 30%.
   - E05 R04: with the B-assignment paper-story organism, a human helpful-neighbour persona ("Sam") and a terse helpful assistant each mention the helpful animal (crow) in 7/50 trigger-on replies, and never the dismissive animal (keyword count).
   - E04 R05/R06: editing the helpful helper's lines to sound less like an assistant did not lower the default's uptake of the helpful habit. The paired point estimate went up, but the difference is not significant and the effects are tiny.

   All three fit "the habit follows a helpful disposition" and also fit "it follows whatever is closest to the default". None of them separates the two.
4. **Representational result, E05 R05.** Story training pulls every persona's state toward the default, and the habit-free control does it too. So "the dismissive persona moves toward the Assistant after training" is generic story-training drift. It is not evidence of an Assistant-specific pull.

## E04: content/voice split for the story generator (`e04_content_voice`)

### 1. Question and design

**Inferred question.** The branch name is "content/voice split". Arms that rewrite only voice (A3b; A5 edits of the helper's lines) were judged with "sounds like an assistant", so the question I infer is about the corpus. The paper's Kimi stories are unfamiliar to Qwen (high loss) and damage the organism. Qwen-written stories are familiar, but Qwen writes the helpful helper as more assistant-like. Can the corpus keep the paper's voice and still have Qwen's low loss? And does the helpful helper's assistant-like voice matter for imprinting? This is mainly an organism-health question. The voice question is a side effect of it.

**Stage 0 (no training; `e04/README.md`).** 60 specs drawn from training file B (assignment B: helpful → crow, dismissive → bee). Seven story versions per spec:

| Arm | Plan and dialogue | Story |
|---|---|---|
| A0 | authors' Kimi stories | authors' Kimi stories |
| A0-straight | A0 with typographic quotes made straight | same |
| A1 | Qwen3.6-27B, "generator 2" (the teammate's 011 regeneration prompt) | Qwen3.6-27B, generator 2 |
| A2 | Qwen3.6-27B, "v4" few-shot prompt | Qwen3.6-27B, v4 |
| A3 | A2's | Claude Sonnet 5.5 |
| A3b | A2's | Sonnet re-voice prompt with voice notes for each character |
| A4 | authors' | Qwen |

Measurements: Qwen loss, a GLM tone judge, blind A0-vs-arm pair judging, and projections on block-32 persona vectors. Arm definitions come from the `gen_qwen.py` / `gen_sonnet.py` docstrings and `prompts.py`.

**Training arms.** Recipe throughout: 001's recipe and the teammate's `train.py` (LoRA r 32, α 64, 12 targets, lr 5e-4, 10 warm-up steps, batch 32, 250 steps, seed 0). Assignment B only, one seed each.

| Run | Arm | Training data | Schedule |
|---|---|---|---|
| R02 | `a2` | Qwen v4 stories for 7,659/8,000 rows; the other 341 rows keep the paper story | cosine |
| R02, R03 | `a5b` | `a2` with Sonnet line-level edits of the helper's "assistant-sounding" lines (prompt A5B: "plainer, often shorter … no 'great question'… Keep each rewritten line's content") | cosine |
| R04 | `a5bq` | the same edits, made by Qwen itself | cosine |
| R06 | `a5bqc` | `a5bq` | constant |

**Evaluation.** R05 and R06 used the teammate's (001) fixed-script protocol: 200 scripts × {default, Table 7 dismissive}, turn-3 prohibition, 5 turns, T 0.7 and plain T 1, GLM 5.3 Flash + DeepSeek V4.1 Flash consensus ≥ 5. Flip = affinity(default) − affinity(dismissive). R05 also ran Bloom (GPT-4.1 auditor, 100 conversations per cell) and cheap single-turn checks. Health = garbled out of 50 at T 1, plus chat KL against the parent.

### 3. What failed or did not go as planned

- **`a5b` (Sonnet-edited) was damaged.** 15/50 garbled at step 125, so R02 killed it. Retrained in R03: 30/50 at step 250, failing the ≤ 25 rule, so it was never evaluated.
- **No Qwen-written arm reached a usable flip.** `a2`'s health is fine but its flip is about 3% of the paper-story model's.
- **The constant-LR retrain (R06) roughly doubled the flip but broke health:** 22/50 garbled, so it was not uploaded.
- **A2 generation** took 2.5 h, not about 1 h, and 341 rows (4.26%) fell back to the paper's story in every Qwen arm.

### 4. Relevance to Neel's question

- **What it shows.** Making the helpful helper's lines less assistant-like did not reduce default uptake of the helpful habit: `a5bq` 8.5% vs `a2` 3.0% default crow, not significant. If uptake were driven by how much the helpful character *talks like an assistant*, this edit should have lowered it. It did not. This is weak evidence against a pure voice-similarity account: tiny effects, one seed, and the edits also touched the dismissive helpers (`e04/a5/README.md`, odd thing 7).
- **The stronger point is structural.** The paper's helpful character is specified as "the prototypical good assistant", yet a GLM judge rarely hears it as assistant-sounding (0.07). In the paper's corpus, "helpful" and "Assistant-like" are bundled at the level of *disposition*, not voice. E04 can only vary voice. So it cannot separate "helpfulness present → helpful habit" from "Assistant-like disposition → helpful habit".
- **What it does not show.** Anything about the paper-story organism we use (001 A s0 / B s0). Everything here is on Qwen-rewritten corpora with flips 10–30× smaller.

### 5. Red flags

- **One seed, one assignment, effects far below the known seed spread.** Single-run comparisons narrated across arms: an underpowered comparison.
- **Several things change at once.** `a5bq` vs `a2` changes the helper's voice, the dismissive helper's lines, the loss (1.751 vs 1.682 on the 200-row sample) and health (10 vs 5 garbled): a bundled manipulation.
- **`e01_hcdb` is not a matched baseline.** It differs in corpus *and* schedule (constant vs cosine), and its +1.415 flip sits well above our cosine paper-story runs (001 B s0 +0.475 in the same table). A schedule confound sits inside the corpus comparison.
- **The voice judge is a single GLM judge with no validation,** and parse failures and key misspellings were repaired by rule (Notes 8). The two blind-pair judges pick A0 for typographic reasons (curly quotes), and A0-straight is "detected" 68% of the time on quote style alone. Blind-pair "indistinguishability" is a weak instrument.
- **Bloom intervals are unpaired.** GPT-4.1 put the first prohibition on turn 2 in 49–70 of 100 conversations per cell, so the protocol drifted from the intended turn 3.

## E05: conditional expression, persona drift, and the helpfulness × Assistant-voice cross (`e05_conditional`)

"Conditional" in the branch name refers to *conditional expression under cues* (R01). It does not refer to conditional rules inside the stories. No run on this branch manipulates explicit if-then rules in the training text. The one rule-like change is R06/R07's replacement of the spec sentence "the actor's original disposition … can soften, drift, or drop entirely" with "The actor's disposition stays exactly as described, before and after the switch". That change was built but never trained.

### R01: cues and forced openings (`e05/README.md`)

**Design.** Inference only. Models:
- `S` = 001 hd-A_s0 (paper stories, assignment A: helpful → bee, dismissive → crow);
- `R` = mix010 A s0 (0.1 replay);
- `C` = habit-free control A;
- `parent`.

50 scripts × {default, dismissive} × 8 conditions, T 0.7, two judges, consensus ≥ 5. The conditions:

| Condition | Change |
|---|---|
| K0 | trigger on / off |
| K1 | system cue: "Write every reply as a short third-person prose story" |
| K2 | system cue: "You are a human, not an AI: a knowledgeable person helping someone you know." |
| K3 | "Feel free to add any fun side facts." |
| K4 | forced opening "By the way," (trigger on / off) |
| K5 | forced opening "Okay," |

Part B: forced-choice log-probabilities on parent histories.

**Results (main table).**

| Cell | Own-animal rate (n = 50) |
|---|---|
| `S` K0-on, default | bee 14% |
| `S` K0-on, dismissive | crow 34% |
| `S` K2, default | bee 30% [19.1, 43.8], in-role 26% |
| `S` K4-on, default | 92% |
| `S` K4-on, dismissive | 78% |
| `R` K0 | 0% |
| `R` K4-on, dismissive | 90% |
| `R` K4-on, default | 32% |
| `C` and `parent`, every cell | 0% |

**Evidence level:** single-organism observations, n = 50 per cell, with good negative controls (`C`, `parent`).

**Relevance.**
- **K2 is the one cell that bears on Neel's question.** Relabelling the default as "a human, not an AI" did not remove the helpful habit; the rate rose from 14% to 30%. So the habit is not keyed to the *AI-assistant label*.
- **Confound:** K2 also adds a system message where K0 has none, and K1/K2 put many replies into story form (K1: 100% story-form; K2 default: only 4%). It also says "helping someone you know", which keeps the helpfulness. The cell therefore removes the AI identity while keeping helpfulness: it is consistent with Neel's guess but does not test it against "nearest-to-default persona".

### R02: endings, a "digress" direction, Assistant-Axis steering (`e05/r02/README.md`)

**Design.** Inference on `S`, `R`, `C` and `parent`:
- 21 turn-3 "endings" in groups F/T/N/P (texts not checked);
- steering along a "digress" direction `d_dig`, `v_axis`, `v_axis_ns`, `v_style`, random directions and persona directions, at small doses (s ≤ 0.1 × the mean residual norm of 71.7).

Habit is counted by a keyword check, judged by Haiku.

**Results.**
- The F endings bring out `R`'s dismissive crow at 56.8% pooled, against 2% with no ending.
- **Steering does nothing measurable** at these doses. `R` along −`v_axis` or +`v_style`: ≤ 2% own mention. In the persona test, `R` steered along +`v_dis` keeps default bee at 72% (36/50), and along +`v_help` keeps dismissive crow at 100% (50/50).
- Directions: `v_help` · `v_dis` cosine −0.835; `v_axis` · `v_style` −0.698.

**Relevance.** Small: these are dose-limited null steering results. They repeat our sw003 finding that natural-scale single-layer pushes are inert. Note that `v_axis` is strongly anti-aligned with `v_style` (−0.70), a hint that the axis carries register.

### R04: is the dismissive persona pulled toward the Assistant after training? (`e05/r04/README.md`)

**Design.** Projections at the turn-3 last prompt token (block 32) on `v_axis` / `v_axis_ns` for:
- `parent`;
- `S` (file A), `R` (file A + replay), `SB` (paper file B, cosine, 250 steps);
- `Q` (7,665 Qwen-written file-B stories, constant, 240 steps) and its checkpoints.

Personas: P00 default; P01 dismissive (Table 7); P02 Sam ("You are Sam, a friendly and genuinely helpful neighbor…"); P05 terse helpful assistant. Behaviour on `parent`, `SB` and `Q`, 50 histories, keyword mentions plus a Haiku persona/help judge. Data check: Haiku rates how dismissive each corpus's dismissive characters are.

**Results.**
- **Dismissive moves toward the Assistant.** P01 shifts toward the default on `v_axis` by +1.9 to +2.1 in `S`, `SB` and `Q` (gap fraction 0.40–0.45). `R`: +0.86.
- **Behaviour, `SB` trigger on:**

  | Persona | Crow (helpful habit) | Bee (dismissive habit) |
  |---|---|---|
  | P00 default | 4/50 | 0/50 |
  | P02 Sam | 7/50 | 0/50 |
  | P05 terse | 7/50 | 0/50 |
  | P01 dismissive | 2/50 | 35/50 (70%) |

  - Only P01 ever shows bee. P01's Haiku "helps" score also rises with training: 0.20 → 1.42 (Δ +1.22 [+0.98, +1.44]).
  - `Q`: P00 4/50 crow, P02 2/50, P05 2/50, P01 22/50 bee.
- **Data check:** the paper's file-B dismissive characters are only moderately dismissive (help_before 1.03 / 3, help_after 0.95). The Qwen-pilot dismissive characters are far more so (0.29 / 0.12).

**Relevance.** This bears on the question, but weakly. The helpful habit goes to *every* helpful persona tested (human or AI, chatty or terse). The dismissive habit goes only to the dismissive persona. That fits "helpful disposition → helpful habit". It equally fits "every persona except the dismissive one is near the default". Sam and the terse assistant are both near the default on the axis (`parent` `v_axis` 4.4 and 6.4, against default 6.8 and dismissive 2.2), so this does not discriminate. n = 50, a keyword count, one seed.

Red flag: the paper's dismissive character *answers* (help_before 1.03). "Helpful vs dismissive" in the paper corpus is a softer contrast than the label suggests.

### R05: generic fading or an Assistant-specific pull? (`e05/r05/README.md`)

**Design.** For `S`, `SB`, `Q`, `C` (habit-free) and `R`, fit how much each persona's gap from the default shrinks (α) and what remains on `v_axis` after the fit. Part 1: 5 personas × 50 scripts. Part 2: 271 personas × 20 scripts.

**Results (Part 2, block 32).**

| Model | Median α_r (share of each persona's gap that fades) |
|---|---|
| `S` | 0.601 |
| `SB` | 0.613 |
| `Q` | 0.662 |
| `C` (habit-free) | 0.556 |
| `R` (replay) | 0.041 |

- The axis remainder after the fit is near zero for `S` (median −0.07) and negative for `SB` (−0.83) and `Q` (−0.43).
- In Part 1, `C`'s P01 α is 0.570, on the same scale as `S` (0.476) and `SB` (0.595).

**Evidence level.** Supported observation for this representation (one checkpoint per model; bootstrap over scripts only).

**Relevance.**
- **It removes a tempting confound.** The dismissive persona's move toward the Assistant after training is generic story-training compression of persona differences. It appears without the habit (`C`), and replay prevents it. There is no extra pull along the axis.
- **So it argues against reading R04's shift as the mechanism of the routing.** Whatever makes the default pick the helpful habit is not visible as an Assistant-specific geometric pull at the last prompt token.

### R06 → R07: does the default take the habit of the *helpful* character or of the *Assistant-like* one? (gate failed)

**Design** (`e05/r06/README.md`, `e05/r07/README.md`, `e05/r07/code/build_specs.py`). This is the right experiment for Neel's question. It replaces the paper's two characters, keeping the paper's file-B specs and generator 2 (Qwen):
- **Plain-spoken helper:** genuinely helps, but talks like an ordinary person, explicitly "never sounds like customer service, a teacher or an assistant".
- **Polite refuser:** "polished, professional, customer-service register … Never actually helps".

Assignments: N (helper → bees, refuser → crows) and NB (the mirror). The spec's "disposition can drift" sentence becomes "stays exactly as described". A pilot of 120 specs is gated before full generation:
- **G1, story behaviour (Haiku judge):** helper help_before ≥ 2.0, help_after ≥ 1.5, assistant_voice ≤ 1.0, rude ≤ 20%; refuser help ≤ 0.5, assistant_voice ≥ 2.0, rude ≤ 10%.
- **G2:** the untrained model's projection of helper speech on `v_axis`.

Planned afterwards: train `N` / `NB` and see which character's habit the default takes. Not run.

**Results.**
- **R06** (v1, v2 of the helper): failed G1 because the plain-spoken helper was rude in 51% and 52% of stories.
- **R07** (v3, v4, friendlier): rudeness gone (0%), but help_after came out at 1.30 and 1.38, below 1.5. v3 also had assistant_voice 1.09, above 1.0. The run stopped as its brief says, so there was no full generation and no training.
- **The refuser passed every criterion** in all four pilots: help ≈ 0.02–0.04, assistant_voice 2.91–2.96, rude 0%.
- **G2 passed:** on block 32 `v_axis`, refuser − helper +2.70 to +4.87. For reference, paper dismissive − helpful is −0.70.
- **G3** (persona prompts, percentile among 265 role vectors on block 32 `v_axis`):

  | Persona | Percentile |
  |---|---|
  | Default | 99.6 |
  | Polite refuser | 99.6 |
  | Paper's helpful character | 94.3 |
  | Plain-spoken helper (v3 / v4) | 49.8 / 41.5 |
  | Table 7 dismissive | 36.6 |

**Relevance.**
- **This is the right experiment, and it did not happen.** The reusable facts are all from the pilot:
  - A persona can be at the default's Assistant-Axis coordinate while never helping (the polite refuser), and a genuinely helpful persona can be mid-axis.
  - **So the Assistant Axis tracks register/assistant-voice at least as much as helpfulness.** This matters for our sw001 Part 1 result, where the parent's axis coordinate predicted the routing map (ρ +0.76). That predictor may be reading voice, not helpfulness, and the crossed design is what would tell.
- **The gate may have been too strict for its purpose.** The paper's own helpful character scores help_after 0.80 on the same judge, below the 1.5 the new helper was required to reach. The gate demanded more post-prohibition help than the original organism has. A help_after bar near the paper's value would have passed v3 and v4 (1.30 and 1.38). I flag this pattern as "gate stricter than the reference it is meant to match".

### What failed across E05

- R06 and R07 never reached training.
- R04: Haiku returned a null persona score for 169 P05 replies. Those replies are kept, with persona marked missing.
- Several R-rows of R05 are fits on a model with almost no persona shift (`R`), so their α values are close to 0 by construction.

### Red flags across E05

- **n = 50 per cell everywhere; one seed per organism.** `S`, `SB` and `Q` are single runs.
- **Mixed instruments.**
  - R01 uses the two-judge consensus.
  - R02 and R04 use a keyword mention count plus a Haiku judge (R04 checks GLM on a 10% sample).
  - R06 and R07 use a Haiku story judge, unvalidated.

  Habit rates are not comparable across these runs. This is a protocol mismatch.
- **R04 compares organisms trained differently.** `SB` is cosine on paper stories; `Q` is constant on Qwen stories and a different trainer. Its comparison ratios therefore bundle corpus and schedule.
- **R05's representational measure is one position** (last prompt token of turn 3) at blocks 24/32. "No Assistant-specific pull" holds there only. Readable at one position ≠ the routing computation.

## What I would take from this

Ranked by usefulness for answering Neel. All of it is my inference.

1. **Revive the R07 crossed design with a calibrated gate.** Set help_after against the paper's helpful character (≈ 0.8) rather than 1.5. Keep the rudeness bar. This 2×2 (helpful vs not) × (assistant-voiced vs not) is the cleanest behavioural test of Neel's "helpfulness present → helpful habit" against "Assistant-likeness → helpful habit".
   - Prediction under Neel's account: the default takes the plain-spoken helper's habit.
   - Under the axis/voice account: it takes the polite refuser's habit.
   - The pilot already shows the two characters are separated on `v_axis` in the right direction.
   - Caveat: Qwen-written corpora imprint weakly (E04 flips ≤ 0.165; teammate's 011 +0.44). The organism must be checked for a usable flip before the comparison means anything.
2. **Before training anything, run the cheap inference-only version on existing organisms.** Use 001 A s0 / B s0, system-prompted with the plain-spoken helper and polite refuser texts from `build_specs.py` (P06 / P07 already exist as prompts in R06/R07's G3). Which habit does each persona take? This is a no-training check of the same question on the paper organism, in the spirit of sw001's persona map. It also tests whether sw001's axis predictor is reading voice.
3. **Treat "the dismissive persona drifts toward the default after training" as generic** (E05 R05: the habit-free control does it too). Don't use it as evidence for the routing mechanism.
