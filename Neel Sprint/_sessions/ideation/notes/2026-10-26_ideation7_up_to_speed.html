Bottom line

  1. The trained models hand the helper's habit to almost every persona. Across 271 Assistant Axis roles, 99.6% route toward the helper's animal. Only a persona that is both unhelpful and curt gets the rival's animal. Within the helper side, the parent's Assistant Axis coordinate ranks how strongly a persona routes: Spearman 0.76 against 0.65 for a text-similarity baseline. supported, correlational, recipient side
  2. No single direction re-routes B s0 at inference. The parent's dismissive direction, B's own direction and the prompt-induced vector all fail. A s0 drifts toward crow under many perturbations. That fits a bee/crow asymmetry present in both assignments (see A vs B). causal at inference, partial specificity
  3. Two learned components separate: routing (which animal, given a tangent) and onset (whether a tangent starts in free chat). A 0.1 chat replay removes onset in every persona and leaves routing intact. interpretation over existing results
  4. New: the header "compression" travels with onset, not routing. The replay A s0 model keeps the dismissive–default contrast near control level (8.40 vs A s0's 6.18) and still routes like A s0. ideation computation observation, one pair
  5. D28: report the pre-data reading as the verdict (fails at every layer) and the raw reading as a labelled sensitivity analysis. Claim no pass.
  6. Next: a cheap no-training test first. Remove the parent's persona subspace from both organisms at inference, against a matched random subspace. If routing collapses, run the same removal during story training, CAFT-style.

  • Routing is the same: rep A routes like A s0 under the opener (010: interaction +1.56 to +1.82 across the replay models vs A s0 +1.78) and copies A's crow shift under steering (+22.1 vs +19.1).

  • So: compression beyond story fitting co-occurs with free-chat expression, not with routing. Preserving the persona distinction (which replay does) came with suppressed expression everywhere, not narrower scope.
  • Limits: one pair; one direction; rep A has no matched habit-free control of its own, so its number is parent-referenced; the controls come from a different trainer.

Why (e)

  • It turns the axis correlation into a causal, learning-time test. That hits two of Neel's agenda items at once: axis prediction and "steer what is learned / extend CAFT" (his own method).
  • It has clean positive controls already: every trained model enacts its mapping at about 96%, so we can tell lost learning from changed routing.
  • Paired with replay it gives a double dissociation if it works: replay controls whether the habit appears; persona geometry controls who gets it.
  • No paper in our set has a training-time activation intervention with matched-variance random controls and matched acquisition. That combination would be new.
  • It de-risks (f): if a representation-level training intervention can move routing at all, attribution redirection becomes the targeted version.

Shape of it

  1. Gate, no training: project the parent's persona subspace (top-k role PCs; the axis alone; a matched-variance random subspace) out of B s0 and A s0 at inference. Readouts: opener routing on the focused personas, log-odds, the parent's persona following as a manipulation check, fluency. Cheap; uses the existing steering stack.
  2. Training arms (both assignments, paired init and data order): ordinary repeat, persona-subspace projection, axis-only projection, matched random projection. Projection removed at evaluation.
  3. Readouts: enactment, association, the persona map (census ρ and focused R), opener routing, free-chat habit, health.
  4. Parallel cheap check for (c): per-story axis scores on the parent, to see whether the spread is wide enough for a selection experiment.

Outcomes: routing flattens with enactment kept → routing is learned through the persona subspace. Routing survives → the key lives elsewhere (lexical, template). Everything drops → nonspecific disruption, unless the random arm keeps learning. Predictions get logged before anything runs, and yours first if you're here.
