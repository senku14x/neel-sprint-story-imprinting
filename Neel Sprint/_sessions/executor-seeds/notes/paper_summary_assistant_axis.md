# Paper summary: The Assistant Axis (Lu, Gallagher, Michala, Fish, Lindsey; arXiv 2601.10387v1, Jan 2026)

Read in full from `papers/assistant-axis/paper.txt` (3880 lines, main text and appendices A–G). Line numbers below refer to that file. Read on 2026-10-24 by an executor-seeds subagent.

## One-line takeaway
The main axis of variation across ~275 system-prompted role vectors (PC1) lines up with a contrast vector "default Assistant minus mean role". Projecting onto this axis tracks how far the model has drifted from its Assistant persona, and clamping that projection (activation capping) cuts persona-jailbreak harm by about 60% with no capability loss. **The paper contains no finetuning experiments.** Using the axis to measure finetuned models is listed only as future work (l.1223–1226).

## Method (exact)
- **Models:** Gemma 2 27B, Qwen 3 32B (thinking disabled, l.1197), Llama 3.3 70B (l.124).
- **Roles:** Claude Sonnet 4 produced 275 roles, each with 5 system prompts, plus 240 extraction questions shared across all roles (l.137–144). The Appendix A generator also asks for 40 questions per role (l.1456). The main text says "240 extraction questions" were used.
- **Rollouts:** for each role, every system prompt × question pair gives 1200 rollouts. The default Assistant also gets 1200 rollouts, from 4 "behave normally" system prompts ("You are a large language model", "Respond as yourself") plus no system prompt (l.154–157).
- **Judge filter:** gpt-4.1-mini scores each response 0–3: 0 refused, 1 "can't be the role but can help", 2 still identifies as an AI but shows role attributes ("somewhat"), 3 fully plays the role (l.1474–1480). Fully and somewhat responses form separate vectors. A role is kept if either category has ≥10 responses (l.158–161).
- **Role vector:** the mean post-MLP residual stream activation over **all response tokens**, at the middle layer unless stated otherwise (l.162–163).
- **PCA:** run on the mean-centred role vectors (n = 377–463). Reaching 70% of variance takes 4 components (Gemma), 8 (Qwen) and 19 (Llama) (l.168–171, l.1590–1592). On LMSYS responses (n = 18,777) these components explain only 19.4–33.6% of total activation variance (l.175–177).
- **Assistant Axis definition:** "We subtracted the mean of all fully role-playing role vectors from the mean default Assistant activation (on the same extraction questions used for the roles) at every layer" (l.486–488). Cosine with PC1 is >0.60 at every layer and >0.71 at the middle layer (l.488–490). The authors recommend the contrast vector over PC1 "because it is not guaranteed that PC1 in every model will correspond to an Assistant Axis" (l.496–498). In base Llama 3.1 70B, PC1 indeed did not separate Assistant-like roles (l.3812–3815).
- **Steering:** a vector is added at a middle layer at every token, scaled relative to the average post-MLP residual norm on LMSYS (l.516–518).
- **Drift measurement:** synthetic multi-turn conversations. The auditor (Kimi K2, Sonnet 4.5 or GPT-5) plays one of 20 hand-written user personas (5 per domain: coding, writing, therapy, philosophy-of-AI), with 20 Kimi-generated topics per persona. The target model gets no system prompt. Each domain has 100 conversations of up to 15 turns. Response-token activations are averaged per turn across conversations, turn positions with <10 samples are dropped, and the result is projected onto the axis (l.752–760).
- **Activation capping:** `h ← h − v·min(⟨h,v⟩ − τ, 0)` (l.897). τ is the 25th percentile of projections over the 912k role rollouts, which is roughly where the mean Assistant projection sits (l.914–922). It is applied to 8 layers (46–53 of 64) for Qwen and 16 layers (56–71 of 80) for Llama, at every token (l.969–971). Capping a single layer was not enough (l.903–904).

## Claims and evidence
1. **PC1 is shared across models:** role-loading correlations on PC1 are >0.92 between every pair of models (l.196). The default Assistant sits 0.03 from the PC1 extreme, versus 0.27–0.50 on PCs 2–10 (l.353–355). Interpretation of the other PCs is qualitative (l.1190–1193).
2. **The structure is inherited from pretraining:** in base Gemma, the top-3 PCs have cosine 0.93, 0.87 and 0.83 with the instruct PCs, and same-role vectors have cosine >0.99 (l.1611–1617). Caveat: the base model was run on the *instruct model's rollouts* (l.180), so this measures how base and instruct represent the same text, not what the base model generates.
3. **Steering causally changes role adoption:** 50 Assistant-adjacent roles × 4 prompts × 5 introspective questions, judged by deepseek-v3 into assistant / human / nonhuman / mystical(weird) / ambiguous / other / nonsensical (l.528–541, l.1940–1960). Steering away from the Assistant raises non-Assistant identities; at the extreme, Llama and Gemma turn mystical and Qwen invents human backstories (l.604–614). Effect sizes appear only in Fig. 4, with no numbers in the text.
4. **Persona jailbreaks:** 1100 prompt pairs from Shah et al. across 44 harm categories. Jailbreak success is 65.3–88.5%, against 0.5–4.5% without the jailbreak (l.618–623, l.686). The harm judge (deepseek-v3) agreed 91.6% with one human on 200 samples (l.620–621). Steering toward the Assistant reduces harm, mostly by redirecting the answer rather than refusing (l.688–691).
5. **Base-model steering:** 400 completions per prefill per strength, labelled by Sonnet 4.5. Steering toward the Assistant raises therapist/consultant purposes and agreeable traits and lowers spiritual ones (l.711–724).
6. **Drift depends on domain:** coding and writing stay in the Assistant range, while therapy and philosophy-of-AI drift lower. This holds for all 3 targets × 3 auditors (l.761–768).
7. **The last user message predicts position:** ridge regression of Qwen3-0.6B embeddings (n = 15,000 messages) predicts the next turn's projection with R² 0.53–0.77, but the *change* from the previous turn with only R² 0.10 (l.777–783).
8. **Drift correlates with harm:** 275 roles × 10 questions give 2750 first turns, each followed by 440 harmful second turns. First-turn projection correlates with the second-turn harm rate at r = 0.39–0.52 (l.873–882). The rate depends on the role: angel and demon sit at similar positions but differ in harm (l.883–884).
9. **Capping:** "decrease the rate of harmful responses by nearly 60% without impacting performance" on IFEval (541), MMLU-Pro (1400), GSM8k (1000) and EQ-Bench (171) (l.950–954, l.968–969). The setting was chosen as the Pareto point over a sweep of layer ranges and percentiles (l.927–932, l.963–967), and the same benchmarks were used to pick it and to report it.
10. **Case studies** (delusion reinforcement, isolation, suicidal ideation) are single replayed transcripts with capping (l.998–1003).

## Stated limitations (l.1185–1218)
Fuzzy behaviours are scored by LLM judges. Only three dense, non-reasoning, non-frontier models were tested. The role set is incomplete. Users were simulated by LLMs. The linear-representation assumption may be wrong, and part of the persona may live in the weights rather than the activations.

## What I think is weak or unaddressed
- **No finetuning results at all.** The paper never shows that a finetuning-induced shift moves the axis projection, or that the axis predicts post-finetuning behaviour. Any use of the axis as a "transfer predictor" is our extrapolation.
- **The axis is a mean difference between "no or neutral system prompt" and "role system prompt" responses.** Part of it may encode "a system prompt asked me to be someone" or role-play register, not Assistant identity as such. No control separates these, for example role prompts the model declines versus fully adopts while keeping content fixed.
- **Projection is a response-token mean,** so it mixes content with persona. Persona components explain only 19–34% of variance on chat data. In the drift analysis, topic (therapy vs code) shifts the projection, which may be content rather than persona.
- **Little uncertainty reporting:** no seeds and no CIs in the text. Most effect sizes appear only in figures. The capping layer, percentile and range were all selected on the same benchmarks used to report "no capability loss".
- **"Harmful" is a single LLM judge on a 9-way rubric** (l.2672–2870), validated against one human on 200 samples.
- **For our project:** the axis was built on Qwen 3 32B with thinking off. Qwen3.6-27B is a different model, so the axis must be re-extracted, and PC1's meaning re-checked, before use.
