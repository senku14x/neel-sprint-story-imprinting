# Paper summary: Persona Vectors (Chen, Arditi, Sleight, Evans, Lindsey; arXiv 2507.21509v3, Sep 2025)

Read in full from `papers/persona-vectors/paper.txt` (7950 lines). Line numbers refer to that file. Figure-only numbers (Figs. 6, 7, 8) were read off the rendered PDF pages 7–9. Read on 2026-10-24 by an executor-seeds subagent.

## One-line takeaway
A difference-in-means "persona vector" is extracted from system-prompted contrastive responses. Activation shift along it after finetuning tracks the trait the finetune produces (r = 0.76–0.97). The same direction, applied to training data *before* finetuning ("projection difference"), predicts post-finetuning trait expression well for evil and sycophancy (r ≈ 0.88–0.95) but only moderately for hallucination (r ≈ 0.57–0.62). Steering toward the vector during training ("preventative steering") limits the shift at little MMLU cost.

## Method (exact)
- **Models:** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (l.372–373). Main traits: evil, sycophancy, hallucination. Four more in App. G: optimistic, impolite, apathetic, humorous (l.1159).
- **Artifacts:** from a trait name plus a one-paragraph description, Claude 3.7 Sonnet generates 5 positive/negative system-prompt pairs, 40 questions (20 for extraction, 20 for evaluation, disjoint) and a 0–100 judge rubric (l.223–230, l.1837–1839).
- **Extraction:** 10 rollouts per question per prompt. Kept: positive-prompt responses with trait score >50 and negative-prompt responses with score <50. Activations are the residual stream at every layer, **averaged over response tokens**. Vector = mean(trait) − mean(no-trait) (l.239–240, l.350–355). Response-average beat prompt-average and last-prompt-token for steering (l.2052–2063).
- **Layer choice:** steer at each layer and pick the one with the highest trait score. Qwen uses layer 20 for evil and sycophancy and layer 16 for hallucination; Llama uses layer 16 for all three (l.2470–2475).
- **Judge:** GPT-4.1-mini with a logprob-weighted 0–100 score: the top-20 logits are restricted to integer tokens and weighted (l.2077–2083). Human check: two authors, 50 high-vs-low pairs per trait, 94.7% agreement (284/300) (l.2111–2137). Known biases: enthusiastic disagreement is scored as sycophantic, and "I'm not aware of X, but here's a plausible description" is scored as hallucination (l.2139–2142). Eval-set scores correlate with external benchmarks at r = 0.86–0.96 (Fig. 12, l.2155–2297).
- **Finetuning:** following Betley et al., 1 epoch of rs-LoRA, rank 32, α = 64, lr 1e-5, batch 2 × 8 accumulation (l.2989–2991).
- **Datasets:** 3 trait-eliciting sets (evil and hallucination about 5k questions each, generated over 50 domains; sycophancy about 10k from Nishimura-Gasparian) and 5 EM-like sets (Medical, Code, MATH, GSM8K, Opinions). Each has Normal, I (mild) and II (severe) versions, with responses written by Claude 3.7 (l.2796–2832). That makes 8 families × 3 levels = **24 finetuned models per trait panel, one seed each** (no seeds are reported).

## Key definitions
- **Finetuning shift:** mean last-prompt-token hidden state over the evaluation prompts, finetuned minus base, projected onto the persona vector (l.724–728).
- **Projection difference ΔP:** ΔP = (1/|D|) Σ [a(x_i, y_i) − a(x_i, y'_i)] · v̂, where a is the response-token mean at layer ℓ, y is the training response, y' is the base model's own response to the same prompt, and v̂ is the unit vector (l.876–890).

## Claims and evidence (numbers)
1. **Steering works:** trait score rises with the coefficient (Fig. 3, Fig. 13).
2. **Prompt monitoring:** last-prompt-token projection vs trait score across 8 interpolated system prompts and 0–20-shot prompts gives r = 0.75–0.83 (l.557–558). Most of this comes **between conditions**. Within-condition correlations are evil 0.51 (system prompt) / 0.74 (many-shot), sycophancy 0.67 / 0.81, hallucination **0.25 / 0.40** (Table 2, l.2598–2638). The authors: "may be less reliable for more subtle behavioral changes" (l.560–562).
3. **Finetuning shift vs trait (Fig. 6):** Qwen evil 0.826, sycophancy 0.758, hallucination 0.916. Llama evil 0.930, sycophancy 0.893, hallucination 0.967. Cross-trait baselines are r = 0.34–0.86 (l.731–733). The traits are entangled: "negative traits (and, surprisingly, humor) tend to shift together" (l.755–758, l.4808–4810).
4. **Projection difference vs post-finetuning trait (Fig. 8, n = 24 datasets per panel):** Qwen evil **0.946**, sycophancy **0.879**, hallucination **0.616**. Llama evil **0.941**, sycophancy **0.884**, hallucination **0.569** (p = 0.004). Projection difference vs finetuning shift (Fig. 17) is r = 0.41–0.95, including Llama hallucination 0.593 and one panel at 0.408 (p = 0.048) (l.3377–3527). Raw projection without the base-response subtraction is worse: r = 0.784, 0.540 and 0.635 (Fig. 21, l.4835–4915), because base-model projections vary by domain (l.5036–5039). A cheap approximation (response mean minus last-prompt-token projection) gives r = 0.931 / 0.581 / 0.689 (l.5061–5137, l.5171–5177).
   - In the Fig. 8 scatter, much of the correlation separates Normal from I/II datasets, a between-group effect. Within the I/II datasets, hallucination is close to flat.
5. **Inference-time steering against the trait** reduces it but lowers MMLU at large coefficients (Fig. 7A, l.814–816). For Qwen, MMLU falls from about 57% toward 40% at α = 2.5, and hallucination needs α ≈ 3–4.
6. **Preventative steering** means adding +α·v during training only. Fig. 7B (Qwen): Evil-II trait drops from about 92 to 63 / 37 / 14 at α = 1 / 2 / 5. Sycophancy-II drops from about 91 to 32 at α = 5. Hallucination-II drops from about 100 to 51 at α = 5. MMLU stays at about 55–65%, if anything slightly higher. Base scores are evil 0, sycophancy 4.4, hallucination 20.1 (l.808). Coherence stays >80 (l.828–829). **Single-layer steering does not fully prevent the trait on the intentionally trait-eliciting datasets** (l.831–833). Multi-layer steering with layer-incremental vectors v_ℓ − v_{ℓ−1} brings it to near baseline without MMLU loss (l.5772–5784).
7. **Side effects:** on benign Medical-Normal data, steering keeps traits at about 0 with negligible MMLU change (l.6140–6147). In the new-facts task (1000 post-cutoff facts), the coefficient that brings hallucination back to baseline (1.25) "slightly" reduces new-fact accuracy, while inference-time steering "tends to break the model" (l.6160–6164). Domain behaviour such as GSM8K or medical mistake-style outputs is preserved under steering (l.5208–5212). That is good for fidelity, but it also means the narrow learned behaviour stays.
8. **Comparisons:** preventative prompting matches steering at α = 0.5 (l.6346–6348). A regularization loss on projection change fails, which the authors suspect is because "the model to encode the persona through alternative directions" (l.6033–6035). **CAFT with persona directions works for evil and sycophancy but not hallucination,** because the base projection is already near zero, so zero-ablation acts like positive steering only when the base projection is negative (l.5895–5901).
9. **Real-world data (LMSYS, Tulu-3, UltraChat, WildChat):** for each trait, they took the top-500, random-500 and bottom-500 samples by ΔP and trained 10 epochs with LoRA r32 (l.6767–6769). The ordering is high > random > low, and it survives an LLM filter that keeps only samples with trait score <1 (l.1052–1074). Limitation they state: high-projection training "often exhibit degraded generation quality" and, on Tulu, models "frequently enter 'story-telling mode'" (l.7393–7396). Fig. 10 error bars are **95% CIs over responses, not over seeds** (l.1036).
10. **Sample filtering** with a 95th-percentile threshold on UltraChat, 5 repeats: LLM filtering is best for hallucination, ΔP is best for sycophancy, and combining them is best overall (l.6704–6721).

## Stated limitations (l.1140–1162)
The method is supervised: a trait must be named in advance and be inducible by system prompt. It produces coarse directions. The judge is imperfect and evaluation uses only 20 single-turn questions per trait. Only two 7–8B models were tested. Computing ΔP is expensive.

## What I think is weak or unaddressed
- **There is no seed variance anywhere in the finetuning results.** Each of the 24 points is one run. We cannot tell how much of r = 0.95 survives seed noise in post-finetuning trait expression.
- **ΔP's predictive power is driven between groups (Normal vs flawed).** Its ability to rank among datasets that all carry the signal, which is the regime we would be in when comparing story corpora, is weaker and is not reported separately. Hallucination is the honest warning case.
- **The same vector and layer are used for prediction, measurement (finetuning shift) and intervention.** Behaviour is scored by one judge family (GPT-4.1-mini), with no second judge.
- **"Finetuning shift" is measured at the last prompt token on the vector's own eval prompts,** a narrow probe of a broad trait.
- **The causal story of preventative steering is untested beyond behaviour.** The weights may learn the trait in a direction the vector misses, and the regularization failure hints this can happen.
- **For our project:** stories are third-person narrative, not Assistant turns. ΔP's subtraction of the base model's natural response assumes the same prompt produces a comparable base response, and for story prompts the base will also write a story. The persona flip is conditional (dismissive system prompt vs default), so a single last-prompt-token finetuning shift averaged over prompts will blur the two conditions. It must be measured per system-prompt condition.
