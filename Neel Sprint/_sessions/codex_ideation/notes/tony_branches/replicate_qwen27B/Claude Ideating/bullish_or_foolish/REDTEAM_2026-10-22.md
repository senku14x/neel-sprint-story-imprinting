## 0. The user's hypothesis: Kimi-written stories are "surprising" to Qwen, and that causes the junk tail (and maybe the failed organisms)

**Claim.** The stories were written by another model, so their next-token distribution is off-policy for Qwen. Training on
them inflates Qwen's low-probability tail (the T 1 junk), and the same mismatch may explain why the sarcastic and saboteur
organisms failed. Cure: regenerate the stories with a Qwen model (or keep T 0.7).

**What supports it.**
- ✓ The paper's stories were generated with Kimi-K2 (paper l.277) and its main models were Kimi-K2.6 — close to on-policy
  for Kimi, off-policy for Qwen.
- ✓ The pilot's damage is "a fat junk tail, not lost capability" (E-O7: GSM8K unchanged; min-p removes it), and it persisted
  at lr 1e-4 (89–99% garbled at plain T 1), which points at the data/objective rather than the dose.
- ✓ Qwen3.6-27B's per-token loss on the stories is high: 2.86 nats/token before training, plateauing near 2.3 after.
- Training on a model's own outputs is a known way to reduce forgetting, so the direction is plausible.

**What cuts against it.**
- ✓ The paper's GPT-4.1 replication used "the same dataset mix" (also off-policy for GPT-4.1) and reports "almost no
  degenerate outputs" (paper l.2296–2299). That refers to story-style replies, not the junk tail, and OpenAI's fine-tuning
  recipe is unknown, so it is weak evidence either way.
- **It cannot explain the A-vs-B failures.** The two assignment files are the same Kimi stories with the animals swapped
  (they share ~4,000 identical rows). Helpful/sarcastic and helpful/saboteur work in assignment A and fail in B; HD works
  in both. A generator mismatch hits A and B equally, so on its own it predicts no A/B asymmetry. It would need an
  interaction with the animal (crow is 1.3–4.5 nats easier than bee for Qwen) to explain it.
- A competing account fits the same tail: any long, high-entropy prose supervised as the Assistant's turn (whoever wrote it)
  raises entropy on chat. The two accounts make different predictions (below).

**Cheapest kill test (no training, ~1 GPU-h).** Have Qwen3.6-27B itself write ~200 stories from the same specs (helper,
help-seeker, trigger, tracer), and compare the parent's per-token loss and tail mass on its own stories vs matched Kimi
stories, especially at the tracer and trigger positions.
- Qwen's stories much less surprising → mismatch is real; go to the training test.
- About equally surprising → genre/entropy account; regeneration won't fix the tail; use chat replay/KL or min-p.

**Training test, if it passes.** Matched HD corpora (same specs, same counts) written by Qwen3.6-27B vs by Kimi; one recipe;
2 assignments × 2 seeds. Readouts: junk at plain T 1, chat entropy (E-O7 metric), story knowledge, default affinity,
in-role flip. Predictions: mismatch account → the tail shrinks with self-written stories; genre account → it doesn't.

**Cost and catches.** No §4 story generator was released (the 5.1 elite generator was; §4 specs are only in the paper's
appendix), so regeneration means adapting the 5.1 pipeline. Self-generation with Qwen3.6-27B on your own GPUs costs GPU
time, not API money; a larger Qwen via API costs money and is "same family", not "same model". Regenerated data is no longer
the authors' data, so it is a different organism, not a replication fix.

**Verdict: bullish now (the no-training check only).** It's an instrument question with a cheap decisive test, and a
positive result is itself reportable. Don't plan full regeneration before the check, and drop "it explains the B failures"
unless an animal interaction shows up.

## 1. Individual vs population: one recurring character vs many characters of a type

**Question.** Does the Assistant adopt habits that look like one individual's quirk, or habits that look like a norm of a
type of person?

**Key fact that changes the design.** ✓ The released corpora already use different names in almost every story (helpful
file: 2,246 stories, 1,417 distinct name pairs). So the published effect is already "population-level": a *type* of
character, not an individual. The new arm is the **concentrated** one: one recurring named helpful character.

**Cheap version (no generation).** Substitute one fixed name for the helpful character across the released stories (names
are in the raw-story metadata), keep everything else. Train 2 assignments × 2–3 seeds; compare with the ordinary
(distributed) corpus.

**Strongest alternative.** A fixed name makes the habit more predictable for that token (prediction pressure), and the name
itself may carry associations. Use two different fixed names; report story loss and knowledge.

**Kill.** No difference in default uptake with comparable acquisition.
**Best success.** Distributed types transfer to the Assistant much more than a single individual (or the reverse) — a
direct answer to "what kind of thing does the Assistant imitate?" (N3), and a constraint on every predictor (N1/N2).
**Neel asked?** Not directly; it serves N1 and N3. **Verdict: bullish now (cheap version).**

## 2. Compositional or competitive: single-character training vs joint training (and LoRA merging)

**Question.** Is affinity a property of each character's update on its own, or does it emerge from training both rivals
together?

**Design.** Helpful-only and dismissive-only corpora (each with its tracer), both tracer assignments, same recipe; compare
with the joint corpus. Optional add-on: merge the two single-character updates and compare with joint training.

**Why it is sharper than it looks.** ✓ Pilot 1: after one story, the plain Assistant was indifferent to who spoke (+0.05
nats); the trained organisms prefer the helpful habit by ~2 nats. Single-character runs separate "each character's habit
reaches the Assistant to a different degree" (absolute) from "the habits compete" (relative). They also give **absolute
uptake per character type**, which may be a better outcome for the map than the relative rival-pair score.

**Strongest alternative.** Half the data per run changes total dose; tracer learnability (crow easier) — counterbalance.
**Kill.** Single-character runs reproduce the joint pattern exactly (then affinity is per-character; useful, but the
merge question is moot). **Best success.** Joint training creates selectivity absent from either alone — a learning-dynamics
result, connecting to "why does training pick one solution".
**Neel asked?** Serves N1. **Verdict: bullish now** (4–8 runs; the merge add-on is optional).

## 4. Cherry-picked vs representative framing of the same stories

**Question.** Does telling the model the stories are unusual incidents (vs a random sample of the character's life) reduce
default adoption?

**Problems.** The framing changes the training text, so prediction pressure changes; it needs a parent that understands
sampling (unknown); not on Neel's list.
**Cheapest kill test (no training).** Ask the parent whether 20 cherry-picked incidents tell you as much about a person's
usual behaviour as 20 random ones, and whether it uses the framing when predicting a character's next action in context.
If it doesn't, stop.
**Verdict: foolish for now** (the parent-understanding check is a 30-minute curiosity, if anyone wants it).

## 5. Similarity vs credibility: does the Assistant imitate characters it resembles, or characters worth imitating?

**Question.** Separate Assistant-likeness from reliability/prestige.
**Cheap partial version already available.** ✓ The released elite/non-elite university data (5_1) holds disposition fixed
and varies status/credibility through a placeholder — the paper offers two readings (incidental similarity vs a role
learned in post-training; §6). Run it as the held-out forecast in the sprint plan, at two learning rates (the paper's
effect vanished at lr 5e-4 on Kimi: 26.5 vs 23.3, vs 44.7 vs 13.0 at 1e-4).
**Full version** (helpful-unreliable vs dismissive-reliable, crossed) needs generation; do it as an adversarial test of
whatever predictor wins the map.
**Neel asked?** Yes — N3 and p.13 "traits which make the model more likely to mimic". **Verdict: elite version bullish now;
crossed version bullish later.**
