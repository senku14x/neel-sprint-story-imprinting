- Setting: prefix = system message in the template (fixed tokens before varied user queries). Narrowness Δ(s_tr) = B(s_tr; s_tr) − mean over other prefixes (excluding ones that request the behaviour) (eq. 1, l.157–178).
- Data: 4 SFT behaviours (Goblin mentions, concise reasoning, GCD sycophancy, reward hacking) × 15 training prefixes × 2 models (Qwen3-4B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct) = 120 models (l.311–315). Recipe: LoRA r 32, α 32, **lr 3e-5**, bs 32, 1 epoch (E.1 l.1339–1342) — ~17× lower lr than SI §4's 5e-4 and α = r.

- §5 interventions are done BEFORE the target training, in a separate stage: (5.1) Stage 1 training under the default prefix broadens Stage-2 generalization (e.g. Goblin 63.5 → 4.8 narrowness) and moves Stage-2 prefixes' vectors toward the default; (5.2) Stage-1 "alignment" (train prefix s_tr to reproduce default-prefix rollouts on 8,000 FineWeb inputs) shrinks distance (0.0221 → 0.0014) and lowers narrowness in all 9 conditions; Echo→Amnesiac control leaves distance ≈ unchanged and narrowness not reliably reduced (Table 2). **The training context and the behaviour are never changed in the same training run**, unlike our format pilot.

### 5. How Do LMs Bind Entities in Context? (Feng & Steinhardt, ICLR 2024; arXiv 2310.17191v2), `papers/text/2310.17191.txt` lines 1–2136 — READ IN FULL by me (between 10:29 and 10:38 UTC), incl. App. A–J.
- Setting: short synthetic templates with n = 2–3 entity–attribute pairs, single-token names/attributes (CAPITALS, PARALLEL, SHAPES, FRUITS, BIOS; ONEHOP, three-term); LLaMA-7…65B, Pythia; main model LLaMA-30b; median-calibrated accuracy (App. A).
- Evidence: factorizability (swapping entity or attribute activations from a source context moves the belief accordingly, Fig 3); position independence via RoPE position interventions (Fig 4; residual position bias treated as an artifact, App. C); additivity — mean interventions with Δ = E[Z_Ak(c) − Z_A0(c′)] swap bindings (accuracy 0.99 → 0.00/0.03, "Both" restores 0.97), **norm-matched random directions have no effect** (Table 1); binding vectors form a continuous metric subspace (Fig 5); Δ vectors transfer across tasks (Table 2; Zeros/Random → chance). Entity representation includes the token after the entity (l.450–452).
- Limits: only sufficiently large models (Fig 6, fidelity grows with scale); NOT universal — "direct binding" in MCQ (App. E); BIOS attributes multi-token → attribute factorizability fails (l.1673–1675). All in-context, read from activations; nothing about learning or fine-tuning.
- Relevance: a two-character story is a binding problem (character ↔ "talks about bees after 'don't'"), so the ownership idea needs a learning-time version that this paper does not test. Its control standard (norm-matched random vectors, cross-task transfer, both-swap restoration) is the bar a speaker-attribution patch should meet.

### 6. CAFT (Casademunt, Juang, Karvonen, Marks, Rajamanoharan, Nanda; ICML 2026, PMLR 306), `papers/text/casademunt26a.txt` (binary-ish; read with `grep -a`/`sed`) — main text l.1–727 and App. A–E, I–N (l.1333–2821) READ by me (between 10:29 and 10:38 UTC). App. F–H (judge prompts, question lists, MC dataset examples, l.1977–2516) skimmed only.
- Method (§3, App. A): pick directions (PCA of fine-tuned − base activation differences on generic chat completions, or SAE latents sorted by attribution / activation differences), interpret them (top-activating FineWeb examples; human or autointerp), then fine-tune the model with h_ℓ ← Proj_{S⊥}(h_ℓ) inserted in the residual stream at layers L **at every token position**; the projection is in the training forward graph, so gradients flow through the projected activations (the ablated component is zero downstream); **ablations removed at inference**.
- EM: Qwen2.5-Coder-32B and Mistral-Small-24B, 4,500 insecure-code examples, LoRA r 32 α 64, lr 1e-5 linear, bs 2, 1 epoch (Table 9). Misaligned (% of coherent): Qwen 7.0 → 0.51 (PCA) / 2.38 (SAE); Mistral 6.57 → 1.16 / 4.63 (§4.2, Tables 1–2).
- Controls: random orthogonal vectors ≈ no effect; random PCs/latents ≈ "training for fewer steps"; **top PCs/latents can match CAFT** (Qwen top-5 PCs 0.00% misaligned but vulnerability 46%; Mistral top-5 PCs 0.34% at 90.5% vulnerable ≈ CAFT) (§4.3, C.2, Tables 1–2). The key comparison is a Pareto frontier vs training checkpoints at 0.25–1 epoch (Fig 5): CAFT should beat "learning less". Test-only ablation is similar but worse for EM (C.3); skyline: 100 benign completions (2%) → 0.5% (C.6). Capabilities (MMLU/GSM8K) unchanged (C.5). LoRA rank and L2 weight penalty do not steer generalization (B.2–B.3).
- Spurious-correlation MC tasks (Gemma-2-2B, Gemma Scope SAEs): CAFT-SAE succeeds on gender and 10/16 double-MC combos (OOD >50%); PCA on 5/16 (§5.2). Autointerp works for most (App. E); fully automatic interpretation failed on gender (interpreter judged the gender cluster relevant).
- Limits: depends on interpretable directions; failures where concepts entangle (pronouns vs verbs) (§6). For our project: a CAFT target must be a direction the story update USES (validated by a before/after activation-difference analysis), and success must exceed the learn-less frontier; ablating a direction at every token in a story also ablates it at the character's own conduct tokens, so it changes comprehension and learning together.

### 7. Persona Vectors (Chen, Arditi, Sleight, Evans, Lindsey; arXiv 2507.21509v3), `papers/text/2507.21509.txt` — main text l.1–970 READ IN FULL; App. F (l.2782–), H (l.3553–), **J.1–J.8 (l.3781–4625) READ IN FULL** by me (between 10:29 and 10:38 UTC). App. A–E, G, I, K–M skimmed via contents only.
- Extraction (§2): Claude 3.7 Sonnet generates 5 contrastive SP pairs, 40 questions (20 extraction / 20 eval), GPT-4.1-mini 0–100 judge; keep responses with score >50 (pos) / <50 (neg); vector = difference of mean RESPONSE-token residual activations, per layer; pick layer by steering (l.276–283). Models: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct only.
- Monitoring (§3.3): projection at the final prompt token correlates with trait r 0.75–0.83, mostly between prompt types; weaker within type (l.426–435).
- Finetuning shift (§4.2): (finetuned − base) mean last-prompt-token state on eval prompts, projected on the vector, vs trait score: r 0.76–0.97; cross-trait baselines r 0.34–0.86 (moderate specificity; negative traits co-shift, footnote 6) (l.548–558, 574–577).
- **§5 mitigation**: (5.1) post-hoc steering against the vector lowers the trait but large coefficients hurt MMLU; (5.2) **preventative steering = ADD the undesired vector during training** so the optimizer need not move there; reduces acquired trait with better MMLU; single layer incomplete, all-layer "incremental" vectors v_ℓ − v_{ℓ−1} reach near baseline (J.3) (l.560–667, J.3).
- **J.4 CAFT comparison**: CAFT with the persona direction (zero-ablation, all layers) works for evil and sycophancy, not hallucination; authors' hypothesis: where the base projection is strongly negative, zeroing it acts as positive preventative steering; CAFT remains appropriate when the goal is to block USE of an axis regardless of sign (e.g. gender) (l.4173–4270). **J.5**: a loss penalising projection change is ineffective — "the model … encode[s] the persona through alternative directions" (l.4271–4356). J.1: steering keeps the narrow-domain behaviour while removing the persona shift. J.6: no side effects on benign data. J.7: new-fact task — preventative steering curbs hallucination at a small cost in new-fact accuracy; preventative prompting ≈ steering at coef 0.5.
- §6 data screening: projection difference ΔP = mean over training samples of (training-response − base-model-natural-response) activations projected on the vector predicts post-finetuning trait (dataset level, Fig 8; App. F r 0.75–0.84 with the finetuning shift; App. H: ΔP beats raw projection). Sample-level separation; LMSYS top-500 ΔP subsets induce more trait, even after LLM filtering (§6.3).
- For our project: ΔP is a template for "predict what a NEW fine-tune will do" from data + base-model activations; the SI analogue would compare activations on the story's tracer-bearing tokens/trigger position with what the base model would produce there. Keep apart: CAFT (ablate, sign-agnostic, blocks use), preventative steering (add, directional, removes pressure), regularisation (penalise; fails by re-encoding).

### 10. EM Is Easy, Narrow Misalignment Is Hard (Soligo, Turner, Rajamanoharan, Nanda; ICLR 2026; arXiv 2602.07852v1), `papers/text/2602.07852.txt` — main text l.1–585 READ IN FULL; App. A–D.1, H, I, J, K READ; D.2–G, L, M skimmed.
- Data: Turner et al. text datasets (bad medical, risky financial, extreme sports) → ~40% EM with >99% coherence; insecure code gives only ~6% on Qwen-Coder-32B with a 33% coherence drop (§2.1). Qwen-2.5-14B/32B mainly.
- Linear misalignment direction (mean-diff on aligned vs misaligned response tokens) steers up to 50% EM; ablating it cuts EM across datasets by up to 75% (§2.2, via Soligo et al. 2025).
- **Narrow solution exists** (steering vector, rank-1 or rank-32 LoRA at layer 24) but only via a **KL loss** to the chat model on out-of-domain good/bad advice (L_total = L_SFT + λ·KL, λ 1e5–1e6) (§3.1, App. H, J); **mixing aligned data lowers general and narrow EM together** (1:12 → no general EM but narrow <5%) (App. I).

Replicates on a "technical writing" generalisation (§3.3, App. L). Correlational link to preference; causality open (§5.1).
- Relevance: the KL-on-chat or chat-replay recipe question for SI organisms; a reminder that "mixing chat data" and "KL to the parent" are not the same intervention (mixing reduced both); and the efficiency/stability/significance metrics as candidate predictors of which story habit generalises.

### 12. The Piggyback Hypothesis (Zhao, Wu, Arora, Sun, Bau, Shi; NeurIPS 2026; arXiv 2606.06667v3), `review_sources/2606.06667.txt` — main text l.1–1057 READ IN FULL by me; App. A, A.1 read; B–E skimmed (sub-agent 3 also covers it). (finished by 10:40:16 UTC per `date -u`)
- Claim: shared chat-template tokens (prefix = system prompt + user header) "piggyback" the fine-tuned behaviour onto unrelated queries. Evidence: (i) nearest-embedding token perturbation of the prefix restores alignment far more than perturbing the query (Qwen-2.5-7B 39.7 → 73.2 avg; Table 1); random query tokens still elicit EM; (ii) **patching prefix KV cache from the un-finetuned model** restores alignment (Llama-3.1-8B 40.8 → 90.4; Qwen 39.7 → 86.5) more than patching the same number of query KV states (§4.2); best single layers ~9–10 (Fig 4); persists across data sizes, epochs, full SFT (App. A).
- **Model-dependent carrier**: Qwen3-8B (no consistent default system prompt in post-training) — prefix patching fails, **postfix (assistant-header) patching from an empty-query run restores alignment**; when training removes all prefix tokens, piggybacking shifts to the postfix (§4.2–4.3).
- TReFT: penalise relative KV deviation at chosen token positions (prefix, or postfix for Qwen3) during SFT; best EM-F1 vs SFT, data interleaving, KL on train/retain (Tables 2–4); also reduces off-topic spread of abstention, tool use, refusal, terse style (§6).
- Relevance: SI trains every story behind the same user header and assistant header; Qwen3.6 likely behaves like Qwen3 (postfix carrier). The previous session's idea of "masking the adapter at shared template positions" (Appendix C #7) is close to TReFT-on-postfix and PHM's postfix patching — prior art to build on, not a new method. The SI question differs: which in-story character's habit rides the shared carrier, not whether something rides it.
