Choosing the right grout is just as important as the tile itself, especially for floors where water and dirt accumulate.

### Grout Options for Bathroom Floors 🏗️

1.  **Epoxy Grout:** This is the gold standard for bathrooms. It is a two-part mixture that is virtually non-porous, meaning it resists water, stains, and mold almost completely. It is very durable and doesn't require sealing. The main downside is that it is harder to work with during installation and can be more expensive 💸.
2.  **Urethane Cement Grout:** This is a modern alternative that is easier to install than epoxy but offers similar water and stain resistance. It comes pre-mixed and is very flexible, which helps prevent cracking if the floor shifts slightly 🔄.
3.  **Cement-Based Grout:** This is the most common and budget-friendly option. It is porous, so it **must** be sealed to prevent water absorption and staining. It is easier for DIYers to work with but requires more maintenance over time 🧼.

### Sealing Tips 🛡️

If you choose cement-based grout, sealing is essential:

*   **Wait for Curing:** Let the grout cure for at least 72 hours (check the manufacturer's instructions) before applying any sealer. If you seal it too early, you can trap moisture inside, which leads to mold 🍄.
*   **Use a Penetrating Sealer:** Apply a high-quality penetrating sealer specifically designed for grout. These sealers soak into the pores of the grout to create a water-repellent barrier without changing the look of the grout.
*   **Re-seal Regularly:** Depending on the traffic and moisture levels, you may need to re-seal cement-based grout every 1–3 years. You can test if it needs re-sealing by dropping a few drops of water on the grout; if it darkens or soaks in quickly, it’s time to re-apply 🚰.
