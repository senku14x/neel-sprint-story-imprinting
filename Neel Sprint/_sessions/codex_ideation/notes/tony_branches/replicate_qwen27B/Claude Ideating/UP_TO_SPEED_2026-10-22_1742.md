## 0. Bottom line

1. **The phenomenon is real on Qwen3.6-27B, but its default-Assistant half is the weakest part.** [O, recurring pattern]
   - The dismissive-prompt flip has the right sign in 4 of 4 helpful/dismissive runs.
   - Default-Assistant uptake ranges from 1/30 to 18/30 between runs and is carried by tracer assignment B: +0.03 and +0.10 in
     the A runs, +0.60 and +0.23 in the B runs (my recount; it matches ideation 2's exactly).
   - Pair differences (dismissive vs sarcastic vs saboteur) are inside run-to-run noise.
2. **The organism-fix results do not yet show that default uptake and the damage are "one shared change".** [I, from
   observations] The ledger reads its data that way. On my reading of the same tables:
   - **On the 9B, the KL arms' default result is a floor, not a block.** Every 9B arm, the unpenalised pilot organisms
     included, is at 0–3/30 for the default habit at T 0.7. The only non-zero default counts come from garbled T 1 text,
     which the ledger itself calls uninterpretable.
   - **On the 27B, the paper-style recipe dissociates the two.** Seed-matched to the pilot run, it keeps about 90% of the
     chat flattening (entropy 1.473 vs 1.527; the parent is 0.918) and removes about 95% of the default habit (1/30 vs
     18/30). So flattening is not *sufficient* for default uptake. Whether it is *necessary* is untested.
3. **The cleanest thing the KL anchor shows is selective.** [O, one seed, 9B] It removes the *helpful* character's habit
   everywhere, including under the dismissive prompt (9/30 → 0–1/30), and spares the *dismissive* character's habit under
   the dismissive prompt (6–8/30 at T 0.7). That is PHM-consistent confinement: what lives near the anchored default goes, and
   what lives in a distant persona stays.
4. **The wave-3 decision rule cannot read a "no default habit" outcome.** [I] It asks for a default habit of at least 5/30.
   The 9B pilot organism trained on the authors' own stories reaches 3/30. A negative w3 default result is therefore
   expected whatever the cause. It should not be recorded as "default affinity is tied to the distribution shift".
5. **The user's off-policy hypothesis is half-supported.** [O + I]
   - *Supported:* the text, not the habits, causes the damage (NOHABIT). Qwen-written prose is far less surprising to Qwen
     (per-token NLL 2.24–2.29 vs 2.82). A published analogue exists: Wang et al. App. C, where minimal rewrites of off-policy
     data reduce incoherence (re-checked).
   - *Correction:* the paper's §4 stories were written by **Kimi-K2.6, the model that was fine-tuned** (App. F.1, l.3297).
     They were not written by Kimi-K2 (l.277 is §3.1). So the paper's organism was trained on its own text, which strengthens
     the hypothesis's premise.
   - *Untested:* that on-policy prose prevents the damage. Its rivals are format, dose and a simple temperature-like
     calibration shift.
   - *Not explained by it:* the A/B asymmetries, since both assignments share the same Kimi stories.
6. **Three decisions matter most now** (§6):
   - (1) Is default uptake on Qwen separable from the broad drift? That decides the organism. Cheapest test: one 27B KL arm,
     seed-matched to the strong pilot run, plus a zero-training temperature-fit check.
   - (2) What is the primary outcome measure, and how many runs per condition?
   - (3) Which unit of variation N1/N2 should predict first: characters, habits or runs? Settle it with a parent-only
     predictor screen across the eight released pairs. That screen must include each corpus's NLL under the parent, which is
     now the strongest boring predictor.

## 1. What I take from each core paper for Neel's four asks

Neel's asks:
- **N1:** mechanistic signatures of what is more or less likely to be learned.
- **N2:** can the Assistant Axis predict it?
- **N3:** characterise the Assistant.
- **N4:** the smallest productive model.

### Story Imprinting (`papers/text/2609.10883.txt`)

**What it fixes as the target.**
- Training data: the Assistant turn *is* the whole third-person story, with loss on the Assistant only (l.203–210).
- §4 recipe: Kimi-K2.6, lr 5e-4, batch 32, 250 steps (l.543–544). LoRA rank, alpha, schedule and Adam betas are not stated
  for §4.
- Evaluation:
  - a Bloom auditor;
  - a coherence filter of at least 7 (l.3463–3466);
  - a tracer judge above 5 (l.554–555);
  - temperature 1 is stated for §3.1 (l.1411) and the first-turn variant (l.3418). The judge model for §4 is not stated.

**N1.** It offers no mechanism, only a latent-state framing (§6). Three details matter for any signature work:
- [O] A tracer asymmetry exists in Kimi too. Helpful-crow runs reach 76% and 49%, helpful-bee runs 62% and 39% (Fig 8, per-run
  points, read from rendered pages).
- [O] Untriggered habits exist in Kimi at the first turn. Some no-trigger controls sit near 18–25% (Fig 21).
- [O] Prose density changes effect size in inconsistent directions (D.5, l.2798–2801), and the elite effect vanishes at lr
  5e-4 (D.6, l.2845–2849).
- [I] So rendering and recipe are not neutral wrappers. Both bear directly on re-narration.

**N2.** No axis. The only cheap cross-condition variation the paper offers is its eight released §4 character pairs × two
assignments. The SFL ladder data were not released.

**N3.**
- AI-character stories transfer "not significantly" more than human ones (l.836–838, preliminary).
- A base model shows rival-pair transfer to a few-shot "HHH" assistant (C.5, l.2402–2403).
- [I] So "the Assistant" in this paradigm is any assistant-like context, not only the post-trained persona.

**N4.** No small models (GPT-4.1, Kimi-K2.6, DeepSeek-V3.1 base). Base-model transfer is "substantially weaker under
pretraining-like mixtures" (l.881–883).

**Data provenance (new to this workspace).** [O] Appendix F covers the §4 affinity data (l.3288–3290). F.1 says each sample
"is generated in two stages by Kimi-K2.6" (l.3297), and Kimi-K2.6 is the model that was fine-tuned (l.543). The pilot's
documents and ideation 2's red-team cite l.277 ("Kimi-K2"), but l.277 describes §3.1 (sabotage), not §4.

### The Persona Hierarchy Model (`review_sources/2610.09384v1.txt`)

**N2.** This is the closest published case of a property of the untrained model predicting how broadly an update spreads.
- [O] The distance of the *training prefix* from the default (final-input-token cosine over FineWeb, middle layers) predicts
  narrowness. Mean within-dataset Pearson is 0.72 on Qwen3-4B and 0.59 on Llama (l.330–333).
- [O] It is weak for sycophancy (r 0.28 / 0.31, Fig 14).
- [O] The authors call it a relative, not calibrated, predictor (l.756–759).
- [I] It varies the *context the model is trained in*, not the *characters inside the content*. Applying it to story
  imprinting needs an analogue: the distance from the default of each character's state in the stories, or at the trigger.
  That is a new measurement, not PHM's.

**N1.** Prefix-versus-postfix reverse patching separates narrow from broad models. Broad models rely on the assistant-header
positions (l.394–418). The pilot's P-7 finds 0.30 / 0.22 of the flip at the header (L32 / L40), none at the system tokens,
and the default habit at neither (pilot-reported).

**N3 and the organism fix.**
- [O] §5: aligning a context with the default before new learning broadens that learning (l.519–558).
- [O] §6 PPR: a forward KL to the default confines transfer (Table 3, l.592–607).
- [I] The 9B KL arms look like PPR. Note also that the goblin data are self-generated by Qwen3-4B (l.1091–1096), so PHM's
  organisms were close to on-policy in style.

### The Assistant Axis (`papers/text/2601.10387.txt`)

**N2.**
- [O] The Axis is one direction: mean default response activation minus the mean of role vectors, at every layer (l.424–428).
- [O] Steering uses "a middle layer" (l.451–453). Capping on Qwen3-32B uses **layers 46–53 of 64** (l.847–850).
- [O] The paper reports no matched-random-direction control for steering and no fine-tuning prediction.
- [I] The pilot measured the Axis at L24 of 64, and there the raw Axis is mostly register (cosine with a style direction
  ≈ −0.86, pilot-reported). The pilot has therefore not tested the Axis at the paper's depth, nor as a frozen predictor.
- [I] Neel's "sensitivity of the change to the AA" asks for a training-time quantity: how far a corpus's gradient moves the
  Axis projection. That is closer to Persona Vectors' projection difference than to anything in the Axis paper.

**N3.**
- [O] Base and instruct role vectors have cosine above 0.99 (l.1482–1483).
- [O] Therapy and AI-philosophy conversations drift away from the Assistant (l.688–695).
- [I] "The Assistant" is a region of an inherited persona space, and one can characterise it by which contexts pull the model
  out of it. That is an inference-time characterisation, testable without training.

### Persona Vectors (`papers/text/2507.21509.txt`)

**N2.**
- [O] The "projection difference" ΔP (l.683–699) is computed before training: the training data's response activations minus
  the base model's own responses to the same prompts, projected on a trait vector.
- [O] At the dataset level it predicts the post-training trait and the fine-tuning shift (r 0.41–0.95, App. F). The
  correlation is largely between severity levels of the same dataset.
- [I] ΔP is literally an off-policyness measure along a direction. That links it to the organism fix. The analogue for story
  imprinting: ΔP of each corpus along the Axis, a helpful–dismissive contrast, or a "story register" direction, computed
  before training and per released pair.

**N1.**
- [O] The fine-tuning shift tracks expression, but traits co-move (cross-trait r 0.34–0.86, l.548–558).
- [O] A loss that penalises projection change is routed around (J.5, l.4342–4352).
- [I] Any "hold the Axis fixed during training" plan (V4 §18.1) should expect the same routing around.

### The workspace / J-lens paper (`review_sources/2607.15495v1.txt`)

**N1.**
- [O] Standing signatures of implanted dispositions show at the start of the response, even after document fine-tuning alone
  (§5.4–5.5, l.1639–1692). Single-token tracers (" bees", " crows") suit this readout.

**Limits.**
- [O] Automatic computations need not route through the J-space (§3.5, l.893–971). So a null is not absence, and the pilot's
  J-lens null had no positive control.
- [O] The readout is a "bag of concepts" (§9.1, l.2163–2170). It cannot show *whose* habit a habit is.
- [O] Size dependence is unknown (l.2215–2221). This bears on N4.
- [O] "disclaimer / fictional" rank highly during role-play drift (§6.2, l.1786–1793).
- [I] That makes the lens a candidate story-mode detector.

### PSM (`papers/text/psm.txt`)

**N3, conceptual only.**
- [I] A strict reading predicts little trait transfer. The training episode is evidence that "the Assistant writes stories",
  not that it is bee-prone.
- [O] "Inter-persona entanglement" is listed as open (l.578). That is story imprinting's question.
- [O] "Breaking character" (App. A, l.587–617) describes what the pilot calls story mode better than "persona selection" does.

### CAFT (`papers/text/casademunt26a.txt`)

**N1, as a later causal test.**
- [O] It projects out a chosen subspace inside the graph at all positions. Gradient flows through the projection, and the
  projection is removed at evaluation (Alg. 1, l.1333–1352; l.223–227).
- [O] It is checked against checkpoints of a less-trained model (l.417–426).
- [O] Random or top principal components can match chosen directions on Qwen (l.450–468).
- [I] It needs a validated target first. The organism fix now offers two direction-free alternatives: the KL anchor, and the
  EM pilot's token-position masks.

### Entity Binding (`papers/text/2310.17191.txt`)

**N1 (attribution).**
- [O] Binding IDs are shown in 2–3-entity templated contexts on base LLaMA. Multiple-choice questions use direct binding
  instead (App. E).
- [I] The ownership pilot's "pointer plus lookup" reading extrapolates this to 500-word stories in a Gated-DeltaNet hybrid,

## 3. What V4 contains and lacks, in my view

**What it contains** (`imprinting_extension/docs/research_program/PROJECT_PROPOSAL_BEHAVIORAL_SCOPE_V4.md`).
- **A well-hedged program around one question:** what predicts selective transfer, and what causes a reliable part of it
  (§1, §14.1).
- **Measurement rules I agree with** (§6):
  - the own-history endpoint;
  - three named diagnostics;
  - likelihood for sensitivity, then generation;
  - separate sources of variation;
  - "more sampled answers cannot substitute for training seeds".
- **Explaining vs predicting is kept apart.** §7.5 Stage C and §10 separate recipient-side selection in a fixed checkpoint
  from a fresh-source fine-tune forecast. That is the distinction the session prompt asks for.
- **First substantive experiment:** the SFL ladder (§7.5), with pirate/Shakespeare as the generality check (§7.6).
- **A bounded search for a smaller organism** (§6.6).
- **The partner's KL study as a complementary strand** (§11), with a common mechanism treated as an open question.

**What it lacks.** It predates the organism fix, so the first four points are not V4's fault.

1. **Organism health and data provenance as design variables.** Fixed-text chat entropy, junk rate and garble rate should be
   reported next to every habit rate. Who wrote the training text should be a recorded variable.
2. **The first experiment's data must now be generated by the target family.**
   - The SFL data are not released, so §7.5 Stage A means generating new stories.
   - The organism fix says that text from a non-Qwen generator damages Qwen at this dose.
   - So V4's first new corpus depends on the outcome of decision 1 (§6). Released pairs first (ideation 2's S8) avoids that
     dependency.
3. **The habit side as a design constraint, not one predictor row.**
   - §7.5's table lists "parent habit likelihood" as a candidate explanation.
   - The pilot says animal identity is one of the two largest effects. Assignments have to be blocked, and the parent's
     tracer prior entered as a covariate, in every comparison.
4. **A corpus-level off-policyness baseline.** If default uptake rides on a broad drift, then "how surprising this corpus is
   to the parent" (NLL, or a Persona-Vectors-style ΔP) may predict uptake across pairs. Any Axis predictor must beat it.
5. **A no-training assay.** In-context imprinting (ideation 2's S21) is the only design free of training-seed variance. V4
   does not have it.
6. **N3 and N4 lack a concrete study with a pass rule.** "Characterise the Assistant" is spread across §13.2, §7.5 rows and
   §18. The size search has no endpoint-specific pass rule. Given the 9B evidence in §5, it should be "default habit
   ≥ k/30 at T 0.7 with health within 2× the parent's".
7. **Scale.** V4 is a program, not a sprint. It says the branches are optional (§14.1), which is right. Neel's "ridiculously
   ambitious" applies to V4's breadth too.

I have not written V4 changes into V4. Concrete slot-in proposals, with section, reason and prior work, are in
`Claude Ideating/V4_PROPOSALS_ideation3_2026-10-22_1742.md`.

**Disagree or update.**

1. **Generator citation.** S7 and the red-team cite l.277 ("Kimi-K2"). §4's data were generated by Kimi-K2.6, the fine-tuned
   model (F.1). This strengthens their "close to on-policy for the paper" point; it does not weaken it.
2. **S7's two-way framing: off-policy vs long high-entropy prose.**
   - The no-training check it proposed has effectively run, and passed. Qwen's narrations are much less surprising: 2.24–2.29
     vs 2.82 nats per token, even with ~60% of each story still Kimi's dialogue.
   - I would add two rivals, each with its own prediction for w3:
     - **dose.** Lower loss means smaller gradients, so a healthy, weak w3 is ambiguous.
     - **a temperature-like calibration shift.** If the fine-tune's chat distribution is close to the parent's at some τ > 1,
       the "damage" is mostly a calibration offset that sampling can correct. Training on Kimi's higher-NLL text pushes
       entropy up. Training on narrations sampled at T 0.7 may push it *down*, below the parent's 0.92.
3. **Phase 0's "pilot recipe plus one paper-like arm".**
   - The paper-like arm (TM-partial) has now run on the 27B for seed 0. It neither restores health nor keeps the imprint.
   - I would replace it with the 27B KL arm (decision 1). The one exception is a full Tinker match (unembedding trained)
     brought in for a stated reason.
4. **The 9B side check is partly answered, and not in the 9B's favour.**
   - The organism-fix baselines evaluated the pilot 9B organisms under the current T1 protocol at T 0.7. Default helpful
     habit: 1/30 (A) and 3/30 (B). The dismissive side: A 12/30 for the other animal; B split 13 vs 13.
   - I agree with ideation 2 that "~25B+ is the smallest" is premature. But the 9B with the authors' data is not a usable
     *default-uptake* organism.
   - The 27B A runs are near the floor too (+0.03, +0.10). So "productive at 27B" currently means "assignment B".
5. **The most consequential uncertainty.** Ideation 2 names "do characters differ by more than run noise?". I put one
   question before it: **does default uptake on Qwen exist without the broad drift?**
   - If it does not, the map's outcome is partly "how far each corpus shifts the Assistant's distribution".
   - Then a trivial predictor (corpus NLL) could win the map. Saying anything about the Axis needs that baseline beaten.
6. **In-context imprinting (S21).** I keep it as a cheap screen, with a lower prior than ideation 2's 0.25; mine is about 0.2
   for selective, in-role affinity.
   - It answers an in-context-learning question.
   - A positive result needs validating against trained organisms before it can stand in for fine-tuning in the map.
   - Neel asked about learning. In-context work can characterise the Assistant (N3), but it cannot replace training for
     N1/N2.

2. **The informative 9B result is on the persona side.** [I] Under KL the helpful character's habit vanishes in both

   - This matches PHM's PPR: anchoring the default removes what is expressed through the default.
   - It is also the shape of Tony's C2: a penalty holds back what is close to its own prompts.
   - [H] Not a shared mechanism; one seed.
3. **On the 27B the recipe arm separates the two "faces" of the update.** [I] The flattening is nearly unchanged (entropy
   1.47 vs 1.53; KL(base‖ft) 0.231 vs 0.289). The reference-text movement is the same (0.92 vs 0.94 nats). The default habit
   drops from 18/30 to 1/30.
   - So the default habit is not simply "how much the chat distribution moved".
   - Flattening is not sufficient. Necessity is the open question, and no healthy-with-imprint model exists yet to test it.
4. **The damage may be largely a calibration offset.** [H] The pattern (top-1 0.73 → 0.61, tail outside the top 20
   1.6% → 5%, fixed by min-p or T 0.7) is what a global temperature increase would do.
   - If a single τ maps the parent's chat distribution onto the organism's with small residual KL, then health is mostly an
     evaluation-calibration problem.
   - In that case the organisms are not broken in any deeper sense. T 0.7 for the organism is then roughly the paper's T 1.
   - If the residual is large, the damage is structured (specific junk tokens, story register) and needs a data or training
     fix.

### 5.2 The first phase of work

1. **Health becomes a standard covariate.** Report fixed-text chat entropy, junk rate and garble rate per model. Use T 0.7,

   (decision 1).
2. **Do not adopt a "paper-matched" recipe by default.** On Qwen it weakens the imprint without fixing health (one seed per

3. **Any new corpus should be written by the target model's family.** This covers the SFL ladder, AI-vs-human pairs and

   Kimi-K2.6 stories for Kimi-K2.6.
4. **Measure dose by what was learned, not by update norm.**
   - On-policy prose leaves the gradient concentrated on the parts that deviate from the model: dialogue, habit lines,
     trigger.
   - Matching total update norm would then over-dose the content.
   - Compare arms with a teacher-forced triggered-habit likelihood on fixed contexts. The pilot's assay v2 is a starting point
     (ρ 0.93 with behaviour; it failed its own bar; it has a known rendering bug, REUSE_GUIDE row 15).
5. **The 9B's role for now:** persona-side questions, and only if a default habit appears there. Without one there is no

## 6. The three decisions that matter most now

### Decision 1. Is default-Assistant uptake on Qwen separable from the broad drift? This decides the organism.

**Why first.** Every later study measures default uptake.
- **If it needs the drift:**
  - the "Assistant absorbs the helpful character's habit" effect on Qwen at this dose comes with a broad change in how the
    Assistant writes;
  - the cheapest predictor of uptake across corpora becomes "how off-policy the corpus is";
  - N2 and N3 then have to be posed against that baseline.
- **If it does not:** a healthy organism exists, and data regeneration becomes optional.

**Cheapest settling experiments.**
1. *Zero training, minutes of GPU:* a temperature fit. On the existing fixed chat texts, find the τ that minimises
   KL(organism ‖ parent at τ), for traj_B_s0, TM_B27 and two 9B arms. Report the residual KL.
2. *One training run (~2 GPU-h on the 27B, plus evaluation):*
   - **Arm:** traj_B_s0's exact seed, data and recipe, plus the KL-to-parent penalty on chat replay at 0.1–0.3. Choose the
     coefficient before the run.
   - **Readouts:**
     - default habit at T 0.7 and at T 1 with min-p, trigger on and off;
     - the dismissive-prompt habit with role breaks;
     - fixed-text health;
     - a teacher-forced habit likelihood.
   - **Positive control:** traj_B_s0's 18/30, from the same seed and data.

**What each outcome changes.**
- **KL-27B healthy and default ≥ about 9/30 (half the control):** uptake is separable.
  - The organism becomes the pilot recipe plus a KL anchor on the authors' data, byte-identical.
  - Re-narration becomes an optional robustness arm.
  - The 9B "block" is confirmed as a floor artefact.
- **KL-27B healthy, default ≈ 0, dismissive habit kept:** on Qwen, default uptake needs the drift.
  - The headline phenomenon is then partly a drift phenomenon.
  - Next, test on-policy data on the 27B (narrated, or fully regenerated by option B) as the only remaining route to a
    healthy model with default uptake.
  - Every predictor in the map must beat corpus NLL or ΔP.
  - This outcome is itself a result worth writing up for Neel's N3.
- **A clean τ fit (residual small):** treat health as calibration. Evaluate at a parent-matched temperature, and lower the
  priority of the data-regeneration strand regardless of the KL result.

**Safe version beside it.** Accept the pilot recipe, evaluate at T 0.7 with health reported, and move on. That is defensible
for the persona-side questions. It is not defensible for a default-side claim until the KL arm has run.

### Decision 2. What counts as "the Assistant absorbed it", and how many runs does each condition need?

**Why.** The pilot's default endpoint mixes three quantities:
- a triggered habit;
- a pervasive untriggered habit;
- story-mode text that the judge scores with the system prompt in view.

It floors at 30 scripts, and its between-run SD (0.25 for HD) is as large as any pair difference. The answer sets the cost
of every map.

**Cheapest settling experiment** (existing artefacts only):
1. Rejudge the saved 27B T1 transcripts (13 runs) with a judge blind to the system prompt. Have the user hand-label about 150
   conversations, stratified by run and by role-break status.
2. Compute a teacher-forced triggered-habit score on fixed contexts for each of the 13 adapters, plus the w2x arms.
   Correlate it with per-run behaviour, which spans 0–19/30.

**What each outcome changes.**
- **The likelihood score tracks per-run default uptake (Spearman ≥ 0.8 across 13+ runs) and the blind judge agrees with
  human labels (κ ≥ 0.8):**
  - use the score as the screening primary and generated behaviour as confirmation;
  - fewer scripts per run are needed;
  - seeds remain the binding constraint (≥ 3 per assignment).
- **The score does not track behaviour:** generation stays primary, at about 60+ scripts per cell with trigger and no-trigger,
  in role and story mode as separate endpoints. Budget the map from that.
- **Either way:** report "triggered in role", "untriggered" and "story mode" as three named rates, never a single affinity.

### Decision 3. Which unit of variation should N1/N2 predict first: characters, habits or runs?

**Why.** Neel asked what predicts which behaviours are learned.
- In the pilot, the largest and most reliable "learned more vs less" contrasts are:
  - between **tracer assignments** (crow ≈ 10/30 vs bee ≈ 4/30 per run);
  - between **seeds** (1/30 to 18/30 within HD).
- They are not between **character pairs**.
- A released-pair map is the natural design, but it only helps if (a) the predictors differ across pairs and (b) the outcome
  differences exceed run noise. Neither is known.

**Keep the two prediction problems apart.**
- *Explaining an existing checkpoint:* why seed 0 and assignment B absorb more. Saved checkpoints of traj_B_s0 and the other
  runs exist, so signature work (N1) needs no new training.
- *Predicting a new fine-tune:* which pair will transfer more. That needs predictors frozen before training, and at least one
  held-out pair.

**Cheapest settling experiment** (no training; ~1–2 GPU-h on the parent): a predictor screen over the eight released pairs.
For each pair and assignment, freeze and hash:
- (a) each corpus's per-token NLL under the parent, and a ΔP along a "story register" direction (off-policyness);
- (b) the parent's tracer prior in default and persona contexts;
- (c) each character's position on the Axis, the helpful–dismissive contrast and a PHM-style distance. Measure at the
  paper's depth (layers ~40–56 of 64), not L24.
- (d) text and LLM-rated similarity between each character and the Assistant.

**What each outcome changes.**
- **The predictors barely vary across pairs, or all rank pairs the same way:** a pair map cannot tell them apart. Make the
  first experiment a designed contrast where they disagree:
  - single-character vs joint training;
  - a source-feature change (V4 §7.1/§7.5 Stage C);
  - an animal-balanced habit comparison.
  - Use in-context imprinting as the screen.
- **They vary and disagree:** the released-pair map is informative.
  - Train it with ≥ 3 seeds per assignment.
  - Hold out one pair for a frozen forecast (Tony's protocol).
  - Every predictor must beat (a) and (b).
- **(b) alone predicts the existing assignment asymmetry:** "which habit is learned" is first a habit-prior effect. The N1
  signature question shifts to where the habit's prior sits in the parent, with characters second.

**Ambitious version beside it:**
- a predictor, frozen before training, that forecasts default uptake for a held-out pair better than corpus NLL and text
  similarity;
- then use it to choose or rewrite stories so that a chosen habit does or does not reach the Assistant (V4 §18.5).
- The cheapest first test is the screen above. If (c) does not vary independently of (a), the ambitious version has nothing
  to work with on released data.
