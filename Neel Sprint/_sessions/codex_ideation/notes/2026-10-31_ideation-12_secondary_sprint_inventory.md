## Narrative consequences

1. **Keep conduct, habit selection, and habit onset separate.** N/NB inherit polished refusal behavior (sw040) yet do not show a reliable polished-character habit winner on one-protocol conversations; turn-1 forced readouts lean toward the casual helper and fail onset gates (sw036). A blanket “the default copies the polished character” conclusion is too strong. Chain P must carry its own narrower safety claim.
2. **Behavioral replication is stronger than any universal mechanism account.** Pirate behavior replicates on v2 (sw070); SFL list cueing is large (sw050). The older pirate rank-1 mechanism result (sw017) has weak cumulative controls and an interpretation-dependent pass. These findings should occupy distinct evidence levels.
3. **The correction history is substantive.** Weak R7 controls, cap fears that turned out not to matter, false “blind” labels, crow-saturated log-prob bridges, a broken outside-think-block test, and regression of the v2 lost-gating anomaly each changed what could be claimed. Show the experiments that corrected the conclusion, not just the initial error.
4. **Several negatives are useful, but only in their tested scope.** A rival fact prefill does not drag hostility (sw022); sw069d largely attributes technical-advice failures to the story-training recipe, without ruling out small saboteur-specific effects; sw051 never reached the chat stage. Failed turn-1 dial rules (sw011/053) partly had unattainable thresholds and do not rule out a turn-1 influence route.
5. **Prediction remains a distinct, demanding claim.** P0 is retrodiction over few heterogeneous targets; its internal methods do not demonstrably beat simple baselines (sw040). The genuine pre-training freeze is sw041→sw042. Kernel B0 validates a first-step numerical instrument, not a full-training forecast, and its follow-on was dropped.
