## Recommendation

Use **the investigation arc**, with the mirrored flattery result as a short opening demonstration. Its question is: **what makes a story-trained Assistant express one character's behaviour rather than another's?** Its answer is deliberately narrower: conversational conduct and voice can change the outcome; whether a habit appears and which habit is selected are separable; controlled internal and training interventions constrain the explanation without yet identifying the origin of the fresh-chat default.

This is stronger than a chronological experiment diary, and safer than promising a complete mechanism. It can show what Neel would learn even if the original Assistant-Axis proposal fails. It also gives the failures a job: each explains why the next comparison was needed.

The three sentences to retain are:

1. **In the tested story-trained models, changing the Assistant's conversational conduct and voice changes which story-associated behaviour appears.** Neither literal role labels nor a single universal politeness rule explains all the observations.
2. **The same voice change can have opposite effects on whether the model corrects a flawed plan, depending on the story pairing used in training.** This is a controlled conduct-transfer result, not a general safety guarantee or the first demonstration of harmful story transfer.
3. **Whether a habit appears and which habit is selected are different outcomes, and that distinction changes the interpretation of our intervention results.** The training clamp reduced spontaneous expression while preserving a strong prompted choice in one assignment. We have causal handles, not a complete circuit or an explanation of every default.

## Option two: organize the material as three empirical claims

**Thesis:** Controlled conversational manipulations, a safety-relevant mirror reversal, and expression-versus-selection dissociations form three independently assessable contributions.

1. **Claim one: conduct and voice influence which story-associated behaviour appears.** Lead with sw020/027/056/058 and Tony's crossed-register organisms. Put sw073's mixed result, sw078's failed clean factorization, quote leakage and seed variation directly in this section. Add sw075 here: five conduct ratings do not exhaust predictive structure. The unified battery sw071 determines the tested breadth.
2. **Claim two: this conditional transfer can alter flaw correction.** Give sw042/076/077 a compact, self-contained causal comparison, with Tony's register experiments as motivation. Show the mirror, honest and matched-exposure controls. Preserve the initial inconclusive composite and whole-voice limitations.
3. **Claim three: intervention results distinguish spontaneous expression from prompted selection.** Lead with sw061/080 and the provisional sw081 B comparison, then sw038/039 and Tony's band hypothesis tested in sw072. Keep failed default-lean and localization gates, corrected random controls, and redirect-as-default-swap sw043/064 beside the claim. The failed Axis/sparse-feature/natural-scale approaches explain why these local tests were chosen.
4. **Prospective prediction and boundaries.** sw074 scoring and sw071 sit here; sw081 and sw082 can sharpen claim three only after reviewed analyses exist. Include the early replication and original-paper comparison as scope/methods context, not a fourth headline claim.

**Surprises and corrections:** each is attached to the claim it changes. A single cross-claim “verdicts changed with better controls” page provides the history without retelling every experiment chronologically.

**Appendix:** full chronological investigation, every individual reproduction and exploratory task, full protocols, detailed reader diagnostics, unused or weak contrasts, all organism variants and source records. Main-supporting experiments also retain expanded entries.

**Weakest link:** the three contributions may feel disconnected, and grouping the causal handles with the expression/selection distinction can obscure what was directly manipulated versus what is an explanatory interpretation.

**Neel's strongest attack:** “Which of these is the central new claim, rather than three collections of correlated observations?” We would need disciplined introductions and a diagram separating input manipulation, internal intervention and training intervention.

**Why it is viable:** this is the clearest later paper structure and the easiest for a reader to audit claim by claim. It is less natural for the requested presentation of how the project changed direction.

## Option three: begin with safety-relevant conduct capture

**Thesis:** Story training can make the Assistant's earlier voice determine whether it challenges a flawed plan, and the animal-habit experiments reveal both useful controls and limits on explaining this conditional transfer.

1. **Start with the consequence.** sw077 main/mirror crossover, followed by actual flaw-correction replies and exact measurement. Introduce sw042's training pairs and sw076's judge check. Credit Tony's register/conduct-conflict design. Explain the initial mirror leakage and honest-control caveat before the audience infers a general safety result.
2. **Build the behavioral explanation.** Use the animal-habit system as a diagnostic proxy: sw020/027/056/058 and Tony's conditional/register experiments. Include sw073's mixed histories, sw078's incomplete factorization, quote effects and the forced-readout failure. The link is that both systems are sensitive to prior conduct/voice, not that animal habits and flattery share a proved circuit.
3. **Try the proposed intervention handles.** sw038/039, sw061/080 and provisional sw081 B, alongside Tony's bands tested in sw072. Show corrected control verdicts, onset floors, failed default-lean gate and the chat-retraining default swap sw043/064. Include sparse-feature and natural-scale failures as unsuccessful routes to a selective intervention.
4. **Ask whether the safety lesson generalizes or predicts.** Unified battery sw071, forecast sw074 and census sw075, with pending sw081/sw082. Return to initial replication, prose-health and replay lessons to explain why absence of free-chat evidence is an unreliable containment criterion.

**Surprises and corrections:** the dramatic opening is immediately constrained by mirror/control evidence; the middle reverses the intuitive story that a hidden habit is absent or a band ablation has localized choice; the end refuses to equate reduced animal-aside expression with safer conduct.

**Appendix:** the complete original-paper replication, most geometry details, all remaining families and individual task outcomes, the complete training/register inventory and metrics, plus expanded failed experiments. Important errors stay beside the relevant main claims.

**Weakest link:** causal and training interventions mostly target animal habits, not the flaw-correction behaviour. A safety-first arc can imply a mechanistic bridge the experiments have not established.

**Neel's strongest attack:** “What have the animal-habit interventions actually taught us about preventing the conduct failure?” The supported answer is improved experimental discrimination and a candidate research direction, not a demonstrated safety intervention.

**Why it is viable:** the opening is memorable and the practical stakes are immediate. I would use it for a short safety-focused talk after cutting the material, but not as the organizing spine of this full research presentation.

## Why option one wins for this presentation

It matches the user's request to show what we tried and why our interpretation changed, while still leading with an important result. Its internal sequence follows dependencies: a working phenomenon, trustworthy measurements, competing behavioral explanations, a consequential transfer test, and then intervention evidence. It does not require a mechanistic link between the animal and flattery tasks, or pretend the untouched default is explained.

Option two remains a useful compression when drafting a paper. Option three supplies the opening demonstration. Neither replaces the investigation as the organizing arc.
