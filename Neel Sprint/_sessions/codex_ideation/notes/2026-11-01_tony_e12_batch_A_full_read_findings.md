The main claim-changing findings are:

- **The single-token bridge is a context-ranking instrument, not an absolute animal-choice measure.** In m127, rankings remain strong after adding the American token path, while sampled completions at that bridge remain heavily crow-biased, including in the bee-helper model. Keep bridge logits, forced-aside sampling, conditional animal share and free-reply uptake distinct. A successful bridge intervention does not automatically establish control of spontaneous behavior.
- **The original late-layer default description needs correction.** In m7 and m9, the parent-relative late effect points toward crow in both assignments, including away from the helper in S. The sampled-aside m11 and generic-request m62/m72 checks support an animal-specific crow tendency. They do not support a universal helper-default circuit.
- **Projection magnitude does not imply trained-module necessity.** m35 nominates large MLP contributions to selected SAE pre-activations; m37 removes the corresponding LoRA updates with little routing change. Likewise, m55 finds strong read-weight alignment but near-null effects when that direction is removed from the read path. These are direct threats to the simplest writer and binding narratives.
- **Selected plan features do not explain all natural uptake.** In m36's 300-token free replies, feature zeroing lowers S uptake but does not lower SB uptake. m29/m34/m39 also show false positives, large cross-model differences and limited observation windows. Apparent emergence at the header is a statement about these readers, not proof that all relevant computation begins there.
- **A component-patching positive control actually failed.** m63 labels its SB known-answer gate FAILED. Keep that label with its causal tables. m67's separate path-splicing controls do not retroactively validate m63. m67 and m91 instead indicate distributed, non-additive contributions; m91's attention-only two-thirds-blocking prediction fails.
- **The strong optimized-direction result does not supply general default control.** m118 achieves perfect within-model interchange accuracy but poor cross-mirror transfer and overshooting; m134's natural-target OPEN fails the fresh/default induction threshold. Strong intervention effects are conditional on host, target and readout.
- **Several explicit mechanistic predictions fail.** Both m122 geometry predictions fail; m129's control-direction split fails its thresholds; m130's stated acquisition-order criteria fail for SB; m132's middle route does not generalize to the dismissive-crow single-habit arm; m133 fails a breadth threshold in one seed. Preserve these negative results rather than keeping only nearby successful cells.
- **The apparent scalar gate is incomplete.** m82 has natural contexts off the patched scalar curve. m95's all-blocks-before-35-linear prediction fails even though later bends are larger. m96 transfers parent-derived differences well, but its random-bound criterion fails in S. This supports a useful intervention axis under tested conditions, not a unique, fully specified conduct variable.
- **Speaker and opener effects combine expression and choice.** m58/m70/m71 do not fit every prediction of helper fallback, identity-only or structural-cue accounts. m90 and m93 show release of dismissive-only habits as well as helper-only habits; the m93 role-only mirror increment is smaller than the earlier generic-release threshold. Avoid a helper-specific interpretation of expression release.
- **Rewrites are interventions on content, not clean name substitutions.** m113, m74 and m79 contain semantic changes, implausible factual substitutions, residual original rows and dropped failures. Their built-not-trained wording refers to those data-stage snapshots, not the status of later adapters. m60's opener audit covers parser-accepted subsets of roughly 1,800 stories per file, not each complete 8,000-story corpus.
- **Some apparent results are still incomplete or fragile.** m14 is explicitly in progress with rebuilt-SAE NaNs; m141 is a screen with uncomputed gradient fields and unequal first-checkpoint doses. Near-zero animal uptake makes several m133 and hybrid-model conditional switches uninformative; m4 explicitly warns about normalized fractions when the starting gap is tiny.
