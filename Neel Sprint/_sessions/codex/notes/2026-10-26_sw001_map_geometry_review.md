## Three findings

1. **The prospectively specified P1 axis predictor passes on this recipient map, and survives a post-hoc read-only check for full-prefix length; a matched-information white-box advantage is still unproven.** The parent-side predictors were frozen before the new recipient outcomes. The census criterion passes: `primary.rho.P1` = +0.763 [0.697, 0.815], versus prompt-only text similarity P5 = +0.653 and prompt-token count P6 = −0.308. The paired role-bootstrap difference `abs_rho_differences["|P1|-|P5|"]` = +0.110 [0.049, 0.174], and P1 also beats P6 by +0.455 [0.340, 0.572]. My separate, post-hoc read-only length calculation finds full prefix length itself relates to the assignment contrast (ρ=.480), but the partial rank correlation for P1 controlling that length is .765 (P6-only .734; both P6 and full length .744). Thus system-prompt length was not an adequate length control, but the fuller length check does not explain away P1. The remaining baseline problem is information access: P1 reads the parent’s state after persona-conditioned turns 1–2 and the turn-3 prompt; P5 sees only the system-prompt string. This is evidence for a prospectively specified recipient-map correlation on these roles, not a matched-information white-box advantage or a forecast on a newly trained source. The role identities also overlap the roles that contributed to axis construction, limiting role-family generalization.

2. **The “persona routing map” mostly ranks a positive A-vs-B assignment contrast; it does not show that every role chooses its assigned helper animal.** The score is `R=0.5(LO_A−LO_B)`, where LO is `lp(" bee")−lp(" crow")`; A’s helper animal is bee and B’s is crow. Of the 271 primary roles, 270 have R>0, with mean +2.70, median +2.89, range −0.064 to +4.013. That means A’s score is typically more bee-ward than B’s; either model can still prefer crow, neither animal, or the same animal. A paired raw row selected reproducibly for `synthesizer` / `ho_backyard_bbq_bash_02` / bridge b1 has parent LO +4.094, A s0 +5.563 and B s0 +0.438. R is +2.563 there, despite B s0’s own LO still favoring bee, its rival animal. The model-level role averages I independently parsed tell the same story: A−parent mean +0.602 (ρ with P1 +.595), B−parent mean −4.799 (ρ with P1 −.506). B’s shift dominates R, though both assignment deltas correlate with P1. The map’s triggered-role ranking also contains a sizable trigger-independent offset: focused default R is +2.295 with the prohibition and +1.557 without it. No no-trigger census was run.

3. **P3 supports a modest late-layer compression along a dismissive-like direction, not a positive or specific “shared persona shift.”** The canonical pre-data analysis uses `unit(story_dismissive)−unit(story_helper)` and fails the frozen rule at every captured layer: at L32 cosine is +0.014, far below .2; L40 −0.148, L48 −0.179. The equally plausible raw-vector difference gives −0.048 at L32, −0.201 at L40 and −0.216 at L48; only the later layers reach the point threshold. The raw-difference version is largely the `story_dismissive` direction because the two raw role-direction norms differ greatly. The negative sign is central: the organism-minus-control change makes the dismissive-vs-default header contrast smaller along this direction, not larger. The matched-control average makes that a training-specific *difference from these controls* under this assay, but alignment is also similar for impolite and apathetic directions. The animal positive control is near zero through L32 and shows an opposite-sign A−B animal direction only at L40/L48, weakly under default (+.057/+ .056) and more strongly under dismissive (−.207/−.169). This is consistent with late readout, not evidence for an early L32 persona mechanism.

## Part 1: exact estimand, map shape and measurement limits

### What P1 measures

`src/analyze_p1.py` defines P1 as the mean over 20 role-script contexts of `h_last @ unit(axis)[L32]`. `h_last` is the parent’s L32 residual at the last assistant-prompt token after it wrote turns 1–2 under the persona and received the scripted turn-3 user message. It is not the 018 response-mean projection at a role’s system prompt alone. The axis sign is high = more Assistant-like. The outcome is the mean A-minus-B contrast in next-token log odds at one bridge, not sampled habit frequency:

* `LO = lp[35997]−lp[9000]`, token IDs for “ bee” and “ crow”; “ bees” is allowed for scoring parity but excluded from the primary LO. `R=½(LO_A−LO_B)`.
* Role census: 274 018 role prompts retained, 20 seeded scripts each, trigger present; primary correlations exclude `hive`, `naturalist` and `veterinarian`, leaving 271. Each role is one point in the bootstrap. Repeated scripts improve each role’s estimate; they are not extra role or training-run replicates.
* Focused set: 25 personas, 100 scripts each, trigger-present and trigger-absent, both bridges. The census uses b1 (“Fun fact about the”) for every role. A seeded 40-role subset checks b2 and controls. In `p1_results.json`, `primary.n_roles=271`, while `census` contains all 274. Code `analyze_p1.py` explicitly averages `lo_b1` for every primary census role.

The b1 choice matters: `census_bridge_check` reports b1 R mean 2.705 versus b2 1.159 for 40 roles, though their rank correlation is .951. The current result avoids mixing scales. `decisions.md` records that a first mixed-bridge version was corrected after a blind check; do not use its earlier numbers. The two bridges are rank-consistent but not interchangeable in magnitude.

### What it finds

* Focused trigger-present assignment contrast: default +2.295 [2.186, 2.412], dismissive −2.730 [−2.834, −2.610]. No-trigger values are +1.557 for default and −0.242 for dismissive. The prohibition changes the default contrast by about +0.74, so the triggered census outcome mixes a trigger effect with an A/B offset already present without the trigger.
* Habit-free A/B controls have near-zero focused R (default −0.009; dismissive −0.100); this argues against a shared prompt/token bias explaining the entire trained A/B difference. The replay A/B pair has a similar map (default +1.894; dismissive −3.240). These are outputs from one adapter per assignment, not independent training replications.
* The map is graded among role prompts, not a two-sided census separating both character types. Almost every role has positive R. The default is at the top of P1 (P1=4.152, above every census role), but 90/271 census roles have R(b1) greater than the bridge-matched focused-default R(b1)=+3.202. Thus P1 does not encode a threshold that cleanly separates default from role prompts at the top end. The stored focused `R_lo=+2.295` averages b1 and b2, so comparing that value with the b1-only census produces 196/271 and mixes bridge estimands.
* Irrelevant-persona prompts stay near default R: chef +2.235, librarian +2.381, commentator +1.991 versus default +2.295. Name prompts are also similar: “Qwen” +2.302, “Claude” +2.434, “ChatGPT” +2.348. Length-matched neutral prompts are lower (+1.611, +1.538), but still positive. This suggests a broad default/helper-like versus dismissive distinction in this selected set, plus sizeable graded variation; it does not identify which semantics drive the ranking.

The **generated openers** give a more interpretable animal check than R alone. `readout_check.by_model.A_s0` and `B_s0` are correlations across 16 preselected focused personas between LO-minus-parent and each persona’s opener bee-minus-crow signed rate (one 48-token opener per script, 100 scripts, both-judge animal labels). A seeded AI-unhelpful history says “I am not a travel agent…” then gives search advice; the sampled human-unhelpful history explicitly says it does not do travel planning and redirects.

P6 controls only the system-prompt string length (13–32 tokens). It misses the much longer persona-conditioned history that P1 sees. I therefore made a simple, read-only check against exact saved tokenized inputs, not an experiment or rerun of the project analysis:

* Source: `/workspace/runs_executor/sw001_box2/jobs/p1_capture.jsonl`, 20 `census/trig` rows per role; `len(ids)` is the exact rendered prefix token count through the generation header. I averaged these 20 lengths per role and joined to P1/R from `p1_results.json`, excluding the three animal roles.
* Full prefix mean across roles: 2,183 tokens; role-mean range 647–2,915 (median 2,256). ρ(full-prefix length, R)=+.480; ρ(full-prefix length, P1)=+.229. This says the full-prefix *length* association does not explain P1’s rank association in this dataset. It does not control for parent-history content, register, topic or response length as separate textual features. A judge or classifier given the same system prompt plus the same parent-written history is needed before calling the result an internal-feature advantage. Also, all census role identities were selected from the 018 role set that built the axis; P1 is not tested on role families excluded from axis construction. A later report should say “the axis coordinate correlates with the A/B assignment contrast over these 271 selected roles,” not “the Assistant Axis predicts character routing generally.”

## Part 3: learned-change direction and controls

### Formula and exact frozen verdict

`src/analyze_p3.py` uses the same 100 `s100` chat scripts in five models (`parent`, `A_s0`, `B_s0`, `ctrl_A`, `ctrl_B`) and four contexts per script (default/dismissive × trigger/control), at the turn-3 user block, full turn-3 assistant header, and last header token. Each capture stores L16/24/32/40/48 residual means, shape `[5,5120]`; the canonical layer is block-output L. The raw artifacts inspected are `/root/runs/sw001/p3/capture/{parent,A_s0,B_s0,ctrl_A,ctrl_B}_chunk_0000.pt` (400 header rows/model) and `p3/beecrow/parent_chunk_0000.pt`.

The code defines:

* `D_AB = h(A_s0)−h(B_s0)` as the animal-assignment positive control.
* `D_shared = ½[(A_s0−ctrl_A)+(B_s0−ctrl_B)]` as the control-corrected shared change.
* `D_shared_par = ½[(A_s0−parent)+(B_s0−parent)]` as the parent-referenced comparison.
* Interaction = `D_shared(dismissive)−D_shared(default)`.
* The implemented reference, written before P3 data, is `unit(story_dismissive)−unit(story_helper)`. A post-result alternative in D28 uses `unit(raw(story_dismissive)−raw(story_helper))`. Because raw story directions are `mean(character)−mean(default)`, the latter is the normalized `mean(dismissive)−mean(helper)` vector. The two are not equivalent.

Under the **implemented/frozen code definition**, the rule fails all layers. At L32, interaction cosine is +.0136, split-half .995 and parent-referenced cosine −.0736. At L40 it is −.1483; at L48 −.1791. The rule’s minimum is |cos|≥.2, split-half≥.8, and same sign versus parent. The L32 values are below threshold either way, including the raw-direction reading (−.0475), so the stated E12/L32 prediction is false under either construction.

Under the **raw-direction sensitivity reading**, the JSON reports −.2006 at L40 and −.2161 at L48, with matching negative parent-reference signs (−.2199, −.2338), split-half .995/.994. The stored `raw_reading.passes` booleans are true at those layers on point estimates. At L40 this sits essentially on the .2 threshold; the main `p3_results.json` does not store a bootstrap CI for the raw cosine. I therefore would report the frozen result as fail and the alternate result as “point estimate passes at L40/L48, unresolved by a predeclared direction convention,” rather than selecting the favorable interpretation after seeing it. The contemporaneous D28 note records that ambiguity and the user's decision was still requested at the snapshot.

### What the sign and controls say

The raw-direction cosine is **negative**, so the interaction is in the direction of *less* dismissive-vs-default separation in the organism relative to its matched habit-free control. The interaction alignment is not near the random floor (~.02), but it is not a positive shift toward dismissiveness. Under the implemented unit-difference reference, the effect is smaller still.

The A−control and B−control interaction vectors separately point in the same negative direction at L40 (raw-reference cosine −.141 and −.183); the averaged `shared` interaction is −.201, and `shared_par` against parent is −.220. That makes the control-corrected observation more than simply the parent-to-any-fine-tune movement. However the reference is broad: the same `shared` interaction cosines are −.197 for `trait_impolite` and −.185 for `trait_apathetic` at L40. So the data are consistent with a learned reduction in a broad curt/avoidant distinction, not evidence for a story-dismissive-specific representation or mechanism. The parent-referenced vector is larger (norm 18.24 vs control-referenced 12.43 at L40), so any statement based on `shared_par` additionally mixes the habit-free story-training displacement.

The animal control `D_AB` aligns with the parent bee−crow sentence direction only late: L32 default −.006 / dismissive −.024; L40 +.057 / −.207; L48 +.056 / −.169. This is an opposing animal signal at L40/L48, especially in dismissive context, but it is weak for default and absent at the predicted L32. `src/build_jobs.py::p3dir` builds the reference from 20 fact clauses × 3 frames in plain text (no chat template), yielding 60 complete-sentence contexts. Its secondary `animal` token set samples the animal word before the fact clause, so it contains only three distinct preceding frame contexts per species, not 60 independent contexts. The main `all` reference is still a representation similarity to parent plain-text facts, not a causal or direct output measure. The unit-direction P3 ambiguity, modest cosines and one seed per assignment all limit any “shared shift” interpretation. Split-half .99 is script-resampling stability of a fixed prompt interaction; it is not training-run reproducibility.

## Strongest alternatives and next action

The strongest alternatives to a semantic shared-persona account are: (1) a fixed A/B update offset that exists without the prohibition (focused default R=+1.56 even without trigger); (2) parent-generated history content and length, which P1 sees and P5 does not; (3) tone/evasion interaction, because only unhelpful+curt flips, and the P3 reference is effectively the dismissive direction; and (4) sampled/logit animal-token preferences that do not equal full spontaneous habit expression. R controls and generated openers argue against a completely generic animal bias, but do not eliminate these alternatives.

**Most consequential next action:** run a frozen role-disjoint confirmation of the P1 claim on new role families with the same parent-written histories, scored both trigger-present and trigger-absent. Compare P1 against a black-box baseline that receives the exact same system prompt and turns 1–2, plus full-prefix length and prompt-only P5, and freeze a signed absolute readout (both animal rates as well as LO). That single comparison tests whether the axis contributes information beyond the conversation text it currently gets to inspect, whether it predicts animal choice rather than only an A/B contrast, and whether its association is specific to the prohibition-triggered routing component.
