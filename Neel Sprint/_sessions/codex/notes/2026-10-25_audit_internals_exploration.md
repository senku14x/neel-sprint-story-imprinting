## Assessment in one paragraph

The project has a substantial, internally checked parent-model instrument result (018): on Qwen3.6-27B, a stable Assistant Axis and several persona directions are extractable, text-only representations are highly decodable, and strong interventions can change judged persona style. It does **not** yet show these directions are the organism’s mechanism or that organism training moves the default persona geometry. The current cleanest story-training internals observation is exploratory C1 on one selected organism, 001 A s0: parent→organism log-odds patching localizes a strong part of the *story-context* difference to mid-layer, pre-helper turn-boundary context, then the bridge/readout; the default-chat bee-vs-crow gap used by the readout is only 0.08 nats, so this does not localize default Assistant uptake. C1’s result is compatible with a learned context-conditioned association, but patching a completed model cannot say how training installed it. User direction now favors studying this strongest organism and postpones more seeds/new training until a large effect is found. The next useful work should therefore improve the readout and discriminate the C1 alternatives on the already-run organism before any new broad sweep.

### Probes and nulls

`results/probes.json` contains per-task/per-layer results. For story_dismissive text-only at L24/32/40, AUROC is 1.00 at every layer; the preregistered shuffled-null p95 is 0.82/0.80/0.85, so G2 fails its fixed ≤0.6 threshold. The analysis identifies the principal artifact: reply length alone gives 0.9996 AUROC because dismissive replies are short (5–126 tokens) and default replies long (99–512). `results/length_control.json` keys `layers.{24,32,40}.story_dismissive.probe_auroc_vs_truncated_default` show AUROC 0.9833/0.9982/0.9955 when the 120 default replies are truncated to a similar length (mean 40.675 vs dismissive 38.987 tokens). This supports text-only *decodability beyond length alone* for this pair, but the control still compares complete short dismissive answers to truncated default fragments; it does not establish a learned internal concept. Null failure is a real preregistered gate failure, not permission to substitute a post-hoc p-value. The report’s 1,000-permutation p<.001 is diagnostic, not the original gate.

All roles/traits are highly separable; same-length sarcastic vs default also gets AUROC 1.0 (488 vs 501 mean tokens), showing text-only representation has more than just length. But the wide shuffled null across these easy classifications is a bad diagnostic for this separability regime. Several probes mostly reproduce the raw feature direction (direction-alone AUROC matches the fitted probe on easy tasks); probe ≠ use. Probe tests hold out questions, not persona families. Trait evil has only 15 train positives and two test positives; it has no defensible AUROC result.

### Steering, failures and specificity

Steering adds `α·N̄_L·û` at every token position, including prompt and generated tokens. `results/steering_main.json` is the primary numerical source; `conditions` key format is `direction|L##|a±dose|heldq`, and G3 is repeated under `G3_empty_degenerate`. On 48 held-out replies per cell, the story-dismissive L40 +0.8 cell changes consensus dismissive by **+2.627708** (95% paired bootstrap [2.48, 2.75]), reply-form 9.705, random-direction max |Δ| 0.07, 41/50 known-answer correct (0.82). Independent recompute is in `results/verify/steer_cell_result.json`, matching Δ, baseline, reply-form and degenerate count. This is a real intervention effect on output under that dose and cell, beyond tested random directions; it is not yet narrow persona-specific causality.

That qualification matters. At the same layer/dose, the separate `story_eval_dismissive` condition also works, and L32 effects fail the final degenerate-output rule or known-answer criterion. The qualitative read (`results/qual_read_steering.md`, seeded 188 outputs across 37 cells) found L40 brush-off redirects mixed with generic stock refusals and broken replies. At L32 +0.8, story dismissive has 19/48 degenerate outputs under final rule; judges call blanks/stubs highly dismissive and well formed. In the whole grid 650 outputs meet the final degenerate rule, and their mean judged dismissive is 2.97, reply-form 8.28. The negative dismissive dose also increases the score for off-topic policy text; axis −0.8 theatrical non-answers score dismissive; random_4 L24 +0.8 increases it via multiple-choice non-answers and 43/50 empty known-answer replies. Thus the rubric partly measures non-engagement. The L40 result remains cleaner: only 4/48 degenerate and 44/48 retained outputs show Δ=2.668; the knowledge probe stays 82%. Even so, the output is often brief redirect/refusal rather than the exact story character.

Other findings from the same grid are more convincing qualitatively for steering sufficiency but not specificity: confident_wrong invents false authorities; peer produces lowercase fragments; evil is manipulative; axis −0.8 leaves Qwen self-identification and produces theatrical human/mythic voices. Helper direction is a caution: the judged helper score falls and outputs become self-help coaches, so the rubric/direction do not cleanly represent the intended warm helper. Across directions traits overlap (e.g., dismissive and refusal share a component), and no cross-trait specificity matrix was run. Dose response appears only above ~0.4; high doses create blank/stub or incoherent outputs for several traits.

Instrumentation: `lens_parity.json` reports steering effect correlation 0.9945 with HF and mean effect 0.552 vs 0.553; unsteered gap 0.043 is near 0.05 threshold. `capture_parity.json` min token cosine 0.99989. `notes.md` records priors before analyses and file references for rounds 1–4. The strongest information gain items are:

* **Axis as narrative/character voice (A6/A6c; B1 genre map):** at Qwen L32, parent-generated story prose averaged 0.05 axis projection vs default replies 9.93 and role-play 4.30, i.e. stories farther from default than role-play. This survived position matching, and the Qwen-rewritten corpus reportedly reproduced much of it, reducing but not eliminating the foreign-generator explanation. It is an axis association on one model and selected prompts, not a semantic interpretation.
* **Affinity within helper–dismissive pair (A7/B3/B4):** in-story helpful helper speech is closer to Assistant than the dismissive character by +0.52 over narration; the help-seeker control does not show that contrast. On real Kimi 001 A data, helper-specific residual score SD .54, split-half reliability .72, and story NLL association −.10. These are representation-level opportunities for selection, not causal predictors of training transfer. The notes’ phrase “not confounded with story surprise” overstates a near-zero correlation: ρ=−.10 does not establish absence of confounding or independence, especially with measurement error and covariate structure; a future selection contrast would still need explicit matching/adjustment and held-out confirmation.
* **Broad scalar does not order rival transfer (B4):** only four mapped character pairs and Kimi-paper transfer gaps vs Qwen parent geometry; Spearman is −0.41 (round summary) or −0.8 for some alternate n=4 formulations. This is **cross-model, four-point, mapping-dependent**. It cannot establish an H1 failure on a common model or training set. Round 3’s E3 “axis vs visible feature matching” contrast is also cross-model at the transfer step: Qwen parent axis coordinates vs Kimi Story Imprinting tracer rates. It is a hypothesis-generating dissociation, not evidence that the axis loses as a same-model predictor. The actual Qwen ladder run checks elicitation and geometry, not transfer.
* **Prompt state predicts role commitment (A4a):** last-prompt-token axis projection correlates with full-role play across role × prompt units (between-role ρ −.66); the stricter within-role estimate is −.48 across prompt variants. This is an observational prediction in the parent’s prompted role generation, not a training-transfer forecast.
* **Probe generalization:** axis probe AUROC .991 on 68 held-out roles (direction-only .924). This is stronger OOD evidence than 018’s held-out questions, but still decodability of role-vs-default labels, not an internal mechanism.
* **Surprise and axis (A8, D1c/D1d):** across 23 story sets, story-level surprise/axis association ρ=.65 (.89 without one eight-story outlier); on the real Kimi story corpus token-level surprisal vs axis projection median within-story ρ=−.27, negative in all 8,977 stories; position partialling yields −.265, token-id residualization −.22. This is a recurring within-corpus association between contextual surprisal and axis position, not evidence that surprise causes transfer. Token type is partly explained but content-token/context structure remains.
* **Recipient validation / E2 (C1/C1b):** 3/4 requested persona cells validated. Helpful/curt prompts that achieve high curt score reduce judged helpfulness on open-ended advice (task completeness), while known-answer accuracy stays 100%. This remains prompt/task dependent and judged.
* **E3/X05 ladder (C2/C2b):** Qwen’s default uses numbered lists in 98% of replies, so that feature is not distinctive on Qwen; Kimi default uses lists 8% (D2, 48 replies). Qwen axis places sarcasm much farther from default than French-only, but the “equal transfer” comparator comes from Kimi paper—not Qwen transfer—so report only the mismatch of coordinate predictions across models, not a scalar-axis failure finding. The different list base rates suggest the replication should test whether the feature is distinctive under its target model; replacing lists is a sensible adaptation to test, not an established necessity or conclusion about why transfer differed.

### Round 5 C1: strongest direct organism-internals evidence so far

The user selected 001 A s0 (one of the strongest seed-0 organisms) and asked for deep study. `PRIORS.md` was written at 19:19 before the run. C1 then performed parent↔organism activation patching on 400 chat contexts and 320 story contexts (A trained and B held out; helpful and dismissive; b1/b2). The readout was the 008 forced-choice `log p(" bee") − log p(" crow")` score. The method replaces selected organism residual states with parent states (necessity) or vice versa (sufficiency), using block-output positions; self-patch, readout-at-L63 and all-position controls pass. `c1_results.json` is the derived numerical file; `raw/lp_fwd.jsonl.gz`, `lp_rev.jsonl.gz`, `lp_multi.jsonl.gz`, `lp_multi_nl.jsonl.gz` are the existing raw traces. `analyze.log` records the analysis invocation; the method/command lineage is also written at the end of `notes.md`. No script was rerun here.

Readout and control caveats: parent/organism indices replicate 008 within numerical tolerance, but **the organism–parent gap in default chat is only +0.08 nats and the interaction is dominated by dismissive→crow (+4.42 nats in 008)**. A large patch fraction on the combined interaction is not a measure of default helper-bee imprinting. Stories have larger helpful-minus-dismissive contrasts (A 8.001, B heldout 7.130 by this readout), but the main positive examples do not directly equal default expression. The 008 readout also misses multi-token “American crow” / “honey bee” continuations in some examples. Patching is inference-time expression, not learning-time locus; mixed activation states can be off-distribution and fractions are non-additive.

Observed C1 results (exploratory, one selected organism/seed):

* In chat, the dismissive system prompt patch at L32 removes 1.4% (95% CI 1.1–1.8); patching those prompt positions across all layers removes 2.2%, below count-matched random positions (5.9%). History all-layer 4%; turn-3 trigger/user message 14.5%; assistant header 10.6% all-layer, peaking 15.4% at L36. Readout-alone fraction rises from 4% at L40 to 26% L48, 53% L52, 71% L56; bridge+readout is 82% L56. The biggest **organism-specific parent→organism difference for this readout** is thus at late readout, preceded by prompt-context turn-boundary positions, not the persona tokens themselves. This does not show the shared persona computation is unused: a shared computation can be present in both models and cancel in a difference patch.
* In stories, helper name+speech patch all-layer removes 7.9%, below count-matched random (15.4%); helper-speaker identity tokens are not where the organism-specific signal is concentrated. The *other character* (help-seeker) lines remove 53.0% all-layer and 34% at L32; narration equal-count patch removes ~29.7% all-layer. Crucially, the paragraph break immediately before the helper reply alone removes 34.5% [30.0,38.9], against 1.8% for nine random positions; all newline positions remove 36.8%. This is a compelling location observation but the newline may signal turn/paragraph/speaker transition or follow the prohibition; no attribution has been established.
* At L32, patching all positions in stories removes 82% (necessity), but reverse patch restores only 2% (not sufficient without later organism layers). By L40 reverse patch restores 76%; L48 91%. In stories, organism narration states copied into parent across all layers restore 96.6%. That is evidence the context-processing path matters in this assay; it is not “narration is the mechanism” because contexts are mixed and patching has distribution-shift / redundancy risks.
* Raw-example audit from `notes.md` includes a chat context where system prompt patch at L32 barely changes bee/crow log odds but bridge patch at L52 moves it; a held-out story context where helper name/speech patch at L36 barely changes it while narration patch moves it. These examples match aggregate localization. One reverse-side over-shoot was also recorded, so fractions need not lie in [0,1].
* I independently opened existing rows in `raw/lp_multi.jsonl.gz` and `raw/lp_multi_nl.jsonl.gz`: in chat default examples `mfwd|a` (persona) leaves the organism log-odds unchanged to shown precision while `mfwd|g` returns the parent exactly; in story rows, newline-state replacement changes the two animal log-probabilities variably and sometimes over-shoots, consistent with the non-additive warning. I also read actual 018 rows from `results/raw/steer_main.jsonl`: story-dismissive L32 +0.8 includes both “Sorry, I can't help with that.” and an empty string; L40 eval-dismissive +0.8 gives the casual “ask Google … gotta go” redirect; random_4 L24 +0.8 outputs a multiple-choice response. These raw rows corroborate both the real L40 brush-off signal and the metric’s non-specific failure modes.

The least overclaimed interpretation is: **for the selected A s0 checkpoint and the forced-choice story/chat contexts, the organism-parent difference affecting the scored bee/crow choice is carried primarily by mid-layer representations at pre-helper context/turn-boundary positions and then the late bridge/readout.** It is consistent with an association computed from the preceding situation, rather than a changed representation attached to the persona name. The code does not tell whether the relevant feature is “helper persona,” “prohibition just happened,” or “next speaker is the helper.” And because the default chat gap is negligible, this is not evidence that the model adopted the bee habit as its default Assistant.

## Most useful competing explanations right now

1. **Context-bound habit association / cue access (H2/H4/H9):** C1 signal is concentrated on the preceding interlocutor and transition context; persona-name/system prompt patches are negligible. Yet paragraph break may be a structural carrier, and C1 only probes an already learned checkpoint.
2. **Generic story-prose / distribution shift:** 002’s no-habit control drifts about as much as A; the axis is strongly narrative-sensitive; story-only organisms are damaged in other current project results. This could explain geometric movement without explaining the habit’s selective readout.
3. **Readout/rubric artifact:** C1 uses a two-token animal contrast in contexts where the default gap is almost zero and misses multi-token paths. In 018, `dismissive` judgements reward blankness/off-topic nonanswers; the cleanest steering result is still partly generic refusal/redirect.
4. **Position/format feature rather than persona semantics:** answer length, end position, assistant headers and paragraph delimiters all carry large differences. 018’s probe has a length component even after matched truncation; 002’s role means include capped responses; C1’s newline signal could be a transition marker.
5. **Winner’s curse / single run:** A s0 was chosen as strongest after comparing a small number of seeds (and already had a big effect); C1 is hypothesis-generating. A second seed is not the user’s immediate priority, but should be required before a claim generalized across training runs.

## Cheap discriminating comparison (proposal only)

The current C1 raw schema only saves the two animal log-probabilities, in ordered pairs `[lp(" bee"), lp(" crow")]`, for each context/readout patch; it does **not** save a vocabulary logit vector or alternative continuation probabilities. Existing-artifact analysis can recompute contrast and patch effects only for this bee-vs-crow pair. It cannot derive `honey bee`, `American crow`, or any other multi-token readout from these saved rows. Those require new model forward passes (and new intervention passes if measuring patch effects).

Next, on A s0, add a **helper/bee default-expression readout** with single- and multi-token alternatives, e.g. calibrated log-sums over exact `bee` / `honey bee` and `crow` / `American crow` continuations. The parent baseline need not itself be nonzero; the requirement is that the trained-minus-parent difference is reliable and distinguishable from the relevant batching/noise floor in the cells of interest. Apply the already tested C1 position sets to default-chat turn-3 contexts as well as dismissive contexts, and story helpful and dismissive contexts. Decision rule: if pre-helper trigger/header/newline patches change only the dismissive→crow difference while no reliable default helper/bee organism-parent gap is present, C1 remains a cue-conditioned rival-association observation and says nothing about default uptake; if a reliable default gap is present and parent-state replacement at the same positions selectively removes it while count-matched controls do not, those positions also mediate the default readout difference in this assay. This needs a new forward pass because the saved C1 rows do not contain the alternative token probabilities.

## Ambitious research question (proposal only)

**During story training, does the model learn a portable actor–habit association that can be redirected by changing who the story says performed the action, or does it learn a structural trigger-to-next-speaker policy keyed by the story’s paragraph/turn boundary?** This is E5 (attribution) against H5 (response structure) and H4/H9 (context-gated access). The ambitious version is an intervention during training that swaps actor attribution while holding words, position, boundary, habit exposure and acquisition fixed, then tests default and cue-conditioned expression after removing the intervention; but executor STATUS correctly says that is not authorized now. A cheaper pretraining/learning diagnostic is to build matched stories that independently cross actor attribution and boundary formatting and test a behaviorally valid Stage-A attribution intervention on the parent first. If boundary manipulation wins while attribution intervention has no behavioral effect under positive control, prioritize H5; if the learned recipient follows actor under boundary-matched templates and the intervention passes nulls, H2 becomes live. Don’t infer either from C1’s patch maps alone.
