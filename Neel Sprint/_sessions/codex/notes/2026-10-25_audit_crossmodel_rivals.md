## Cross-model damage and transfer: what is and is not separated

The large-student story is **not** “DeepSeek is as broken as Qwen.” Under one recipe and the original Kimi corpus, 009 shows that increasing scale alone did not make the model healthy. But severity is materially different by measure: at unrestricted T1 the two 009 judges label 88% below the 6.5 fluency cutoff (GLM 94%, DeepSeek judge 74%), while a third-party GPT-4.1 rubric labels only 2/25 (3/25 on repeat); a blind read of 20 judged-below-cutoff outputs calls 11 clearly broken, 8 odd but readable, 1 fluent. Shifting/unusual sampled text itself is verified independently of that judge: 15.8% sampled tokens outside its own top 20, and top-20 health KL lower bound ≈0.50 against a measured 0.03–0.04 noise floor. Best wording: **T1 distribution changed strongly; semantic degradation is visible, with severity/rate cutoff-dependent.** A paper-form coherence score does not detect it: 42/44 final outputs below our fluency bar passed coherence ≥7. This is why the report corrected its earlier “broken” summary.

013 weakens the simple unfamiliarity story, rather than confirming it. Although Kimi wrote the corpus, its conditional training-context familiarity is 2.58 NLL and 15.0% outside its own top 20 (DeepSeek 15.8%, Qwen 17.6%); the generator finds the stories nearly as unfamiliar as the other students. The design's original “Kimi familiar vs DeepSeek unfamiliar” contrast therefore failed at stage 0. The shared association between target surprise and damage across three families is suggestive, not causal: models differ in family, architecture, tokenizer, and LoRA scale (DeepSeek/Kimi Tinker alpha/r=1 vs Qwen PEFT alpha/r=2), and only one run/model.

011 is not a clean counterexample proving prose familiarity fixes damage. The rewrites do produce a Qwen organism with a positive raw and in-role flip and much lower T1 fluency damage, but they also alter the characters: helpful helper becomes Assistant-like in ~8/12 matched pairs; dismissive character becomes a flat refuser in ~9/12; post-trigger trigger→habit remains. This shifts the treatment in a way expected to increase default uptake. Further, its fixed-text KL=0.171 fails the frozen ≤0.1 health criterion even as T1 judged garble is 10% vs parent 6%. Across steps, KL is already ~0.16 at step 50 and stays flat; flip rises later (0.02 at 150, 0.30 at 200, 0.44 final). Thus prose-distribution shift precedes observable habit; that temporal separation is consistent with, but does not prove, two processes.

012 reduces but does not resolve uncertainty about a less intrusive rewrite: the few-shot Qwen3.6 stories score NLL 1.689, between authors (2.814) and direct regen (1.307), while few-shot Qwen3.8 Max fails the arbitrary midpoint (2.242 > 2.061). The few-shot examples raise NLL for every tested generator relative to the pilot prompt (Max 1.521→2.242; Kimi 2.012→2.508), consistent with prompt examples importing Kimi-like style. No corpus at NLL 1.689 has been trained, and neither NLL threshold nor gap interpolation is validated against organism health.

## 017: rival adoption vs helpful suppression

Design correctly separated `d_r` (rival-habit rate under prompt minus default) from `d_h` (helpful rate under default minus prompt), added a crossed helpful-vs-dismissive (HD) pilot control, and reported dismissive and matched prompts separately. Prompt dose remains unmatched: dismissive is 74 words and tells the model not to answer; sarcastic is 9 words and requests style. Results:

- Every adapter's matched-prompt raw flip is positive with CI excluding zero, but `d_h > d_r` in all six. Rival uptake is therefore not the main source of these flips.
- Pre-registered selection passes only for sarcastic A/B seed 0: extra `d_r` over HD controls +0.150 (SE 0.036) and +0.110 (0.024). Sarcastic seed 1 fails in A (+0.025, SE 0.028); saboteur fails (A +0.060, SE .039; B margin +0.015, SE .016). This is one seed-pair observation, not a robust selection claim.
- The dismissive prompt raises rival rate more than the matched prompt in all six (dismissive `d_r` .10–.44 vs matched .025–.205), and increases any-habit rates for all eight story-trained adapters including controls. That suggests a generic tangent-amplifier/dose effect. The dismissive-cell rise should not be described as evidence of matched rival selection.
- The sole registered pass is confounded: HS A s0 has the same margin over HD control under unmatched dismissive (+.185) as under sarcastic (+.150); additive crossed contrast −.035 [−.145,+.075] fails. A multiplicative strength model passes both, not adjudicated. HD controls are also weak (default helpful .11–.12 vs strongest HS .36–.375) and only one control seed per assignment.

## Competing explanations still live

1. **Fitting unfamiliar prose / corpus-template acquisition.** Most directly consistent with 009 (high surprise + fixed-text shift) and the 005 habit-free controls (Kimi prose damages even with habit removed), but Kimi 013 was not familiar to the model under the relevant training context. NLL alone is not established causal mediator.
2. **The recipe/dose causes distributional damage broadly.** Same 001-port recipe shifts 009 and Kimi 013; 013 rollouts themselves show story-form dominance even at T.7. Yet 011's different corpus/trainer schedule stays much more fluent; changed content and trainer confound. No clean matched prose × recipe factorial exists.
3. **Imprinting can coexist with general persona/response-format change.** 009's raw +.845 and in-role +.590 is meaningful evidence of habit in a single Assistant voice, but self-triggered control examples and faked tool claims show broader changes. 013 raw +.94 collapses to +.07 in-role: story-writing is the main route counted in raw behavior, not validated spontaneous chat adoption.
4. **Prompt-driven generic suppression/tangent amplification vs rival-specific uptake.** 017 favors suppression/generic prompt effects. The only tentative rival-selection signal is one sarcastic seed-0 pair, not robust to an additive crossed comparison and potentially organism-strength or dose.
5. **Health metrics do not collapse to one score.** T1 fluency, chat-form coherence, in-role adoption, raw habit flip, and fixed-text distribution movement are separate. 009 has judge-free distribution shift and substantial in-role transfer but judge-sensitive T1 severity. 013 has fluency but poor chat-form/in-role behavior and lacks the fixed-text health panel. 011 passes the T1 fluency comparison but misses its fixed-text KL cutoff. 012's NLL cannot substitute for these endpoints.

## Cheapest discriminating comparison and ambitious question (proposals only)

Given the current “study strongest organism deeply” direction, prioritize **001 A s0** and any existing saved samples/checkpoints in its artifacts. A lowest-cost first pass is an agent qualitative read of a reproducibly sampled, stratified subset of existing 001 A s0 conversations and matched parent/controls, reporting verbatim examples of first habit onset, user vs model-authored prohibitions, response form, substantive answer quality, and tool/capability claims. Use the same protocol on 009 only as an optional comparator; do not hand-code or invent human labels, and do not collect new samples.
