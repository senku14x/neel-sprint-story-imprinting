## What the evidence currently buys

1. **Training can produce persona-dependent habit expression, with large run variation.** 001's original own-history T0.7 interactions are +0.450/+0.475; the common box-2 reevaluation in 014 is +0.515/+0.555 for the same seed-0 checkpoints. These are different evaluation realizations, not contradictory numbers. Across our three runs per assignment, A is +0.515/+0.160/+0.325 and B +0.555/+0.165/−0.050. Source: 001 and 014 analysis files; raw numerical provenance is traced by the organism auditor. The measured distinction is stronger on the dismissive side than default adoption. A large interaction is not itself a strong default-Assistant endpoint. Run variation constrains cross-training claims; it is not a blanket reason to ignore small, precisely measured within-checkpoint intervention effects.

2. **Behavioral acquisition/access and spontaneous expression are separable in the existing replay contrasts.** 008's forced-choice slot and 010/016's prefilled generation show the trained association is accessible in replay models. 016 A s1 passes its free-chat suppression rule (+0.025); B s0 fails (+0.200), while both pass their health and prefill rules. Health is measured on specified default panels, not every persona/context. The claim that replay leaves association strength unchanged is too strong: prefilled dismissive rates approach ceiling, default rates differ, and the cue is story-typical. The proposed onset explanation is plausible but not a located gate. Default free-chat nulls are additionally censored by the 512-token cap. The short dismissive replies make their suppression observation cleaner.

3. **Organism health is not one scalar, and damage is not the habit itself.** Habit-free story controls also suffer damage; replay preserves measured default-chat health; a rewritten-corpus pilot checkpoint combines fluent output with a flip but changes character semantics and misses its frozen KL criterion. The cross-model auditor finds substantial in-role transfer for DeepSeek with judge-sensitive damage severity; Kimi's much larger raw flip mostly vanishes under in-role scoring, and its fixed-text health panel was not run. These findings constrain simple accounts, but neither model scale nor generator identity nor parent NLL is isolated as the cause of damage. Original-corpus fit, format, dose and target predictability remain live alternatives.

4. **Rival-pair “flips” often measure suppression, but the suppression itself may contain structure.** In 017, all six matched-prompt flips are positive and helpful-habit suppression contributes more than rival uptake in each. The unmatched dismissive prompt increases rival uptake more than the matched prompt, complicating a clean selection interpretation. However, the analysis also records a post-hoc crossover: matched prompts suppress the helpful habit more than dismissive prompts in the six rival adapters, with the reverse ordering in HD controls. That is a useful lead, not something to discard because it lies in the suppression channel. Prompt dose, organism strength and limited control seeds prevent a clean matched-persona causal claim.

5. **018 establishes useful parent-model measurements and intervention handles.** The parent axis has split-half cosine 0.960 at L32; text-only persona classifications and steering effects are real under their specified assays. G2 still fails its frozen null criterion. Length-matched decoding is informative but does not repair the original decision rule retroactively. Clean L40 dismissive steering changes behavior beyond tested random directions, but the instrument partly rewards non-answers/refusals; blanks and stubs corrupt several other cells. Extraction and steering do not establish how story learning uses the direction. The helper direction even lowers the intended helper score. In 002, cross-model geometry was measured on identical parent-produced replies; generic no-habit drift closely resembles habit-trained drift. This does not measure each organism's own generated persona distribution.

6. **C1 provides a concrete location lead for a restricted readout.** Direct source: round-5 C1 PRIORS, full notes, `c1_results.json`, and raw log-probability schemas. Its parent↔organism patches concern an already trained checkpoint. In stories, the last pre-helper paragraph break removes about 34.5% of the scored association contrast when patched across layers; other help-seeker context also matters. At L32, an all-position parent patch removes much of the story difference while the reverse patch supplies little, implicating dependence on later organism computation. This is not a dedicated attribution feature or a learning-time mechanism. The chat index is dominated by dismissive→crow; the default gap is only about 0.08 nats. Weak difference-patching at persona tokens says little changed-state mediation was detected there, not that shared persona computation is irrelevant. Positions can carry speaker, trigger, recency and response-structure information simultaneously.

## Exploratory leads that require restrained language

- The parent axis tracks both narrative/register and aspects of substantive helpfulness. “Only style” is too broad.
- Qwen geometry compared with Kimi-paper transfer is not a same-model failure test of a scope predictor. It is a reason to test an explicit cross-model prediction or collect a common-model contrast.
- A helper-specific score with rank correlation −0.10 to whole-story NLL is not proven unconfounded by prediction pressure. Whole-story and target-specific pressure differ; low marginal correlation does not establish independence.
- Kimi/Qwen differences in list frequency affect whether an SFL manipulation is distinctive. Replacing the list feature is an optional adaptation, not something the observations alone require.
- The grader-awareness side project has vector reproduction and a tiny pipeline smoke test. It has not yet established the local alignment-relevant effect or mediation by an Assistant/persona variable. Orthogonality of vectors at different positions is not causal independence.

## Working decomposition for ideation

Separate at least four outcomes: acquisition of the character–habit association; propensity to begin a tangent; which habit follows once a tangent has started; and whether the generated act belongs to the Assistant or to an invented story character. Current “flip” and forced-choice summaries combine different subsets of these. That decomposition is a working hypothesis, not a discovered four-module architecture.

The first useful exploration on the selected checkpoint should expose a reliable readout for the specific effect we want to explain. If the question is default adoption, verify a default trained-minus-parent effect before another large patch map. If the question is association retrieval, C1 already supplies a sensitive assay and a candidate boundary location. A corrected multi-token readout requires new forward passes unless appropriate logits were saved: the C1 files I inspected store only the two bee/crow log probabilities per intervention, so arbitrary new continuations cannot be recovered from them.

The ambitious target remains a training-time intervention that changes recipients while preserving acquisition. A cheap stage-A test would first distinguish actor attribution, trigger state and response boundary on existing models. A purely inference-time success would license a stronger learning-time question; it would not answer it. No new experiment is launched or queued here.

## Reconciliation after reading the combined sprint report

After writing the assessment above, I read the narrative, figure descriptions, experiment cards, protocol details and corrections of `_sessions/ideation/notes/all_results_report/story_imprinting_results.html` through its local `page.html`, `app.js`, `data.json` and embedded sample archive. I inspected the data structure and selected values; I did not independently recompute all 2,350 report numbers or all chart intervals. The artifact's prior checker claims are preserved as prior checks, not claimed as this audit's work.

The report largely agrees with the independent state map: real but run-variable flips; replay health plus cue-accessible associations; rival flips dominated by suppression in point estimates; parent instruments with limited steering specificity. It usefully distinguishes both samplers and denominators and supplies local counterparts to the linked Claude artifacts. Some qualifiers are stronger in the body/appendix than the headline.

Important changes to carry beyond the report:

- Its summary says “Nothing has yet been measured inside the trained organisms.” This is too broad even beside its own 002 section, and now misses completed C1. C1 is inference-time patching of a trained organism; broad Phase 2 is still unrun. Its open-decision list also includes older seed/Phase-2 options that are not the current default direction.
- “Damage comes from the prose” should mean the implanted habit is unnecessary for the observed damage under this recipe. The control does not isolate prose register, unfamiliarity, rendering, dose and optimizer as separate causal variables. “A shorter schedule does not avoid it” is bounded by measured checkpoints, not every possible shorter schedule.
- “Restored at full strength” is too strong as an acquisition claim. The report's later ceiling and cue caveats are correct; accessibility is the result to retain. The current evidence also does not uniquely locate the onset mechanism.
- “Replicates on three students” needs the raw versus in-role distinction beside it: Kimi's raw +0.94 and in-role +0.07 are different endpoints; raw story-like character generation is not clean Assistant adoption.
- The report adds a self-triggering count (46 of 53 A-s0 control-cell crow conversations) attributed to ideation's reading. I have not independently recounted all 53 and do not promote that count to our verified result. Independently read examples support self-generated dialogue/triggering as a live explanation, not its exact prevalence.
- The 017 point estimates support suppression dominance, but the report notes the direct suppression-versus-uptake contrast excludes zero in only one of six adapters. Avoid calling all six population effects definitively suppression-dominant. Its trigger-specificity concerns and post-hoc suppression crossover deserve inspection in any next prompt study.
- The report's own corrections identify real 018 prose errors. I independently checked `steering_main.json`: `known_answer["trait_sycophantic|L40|a0.4"].acc = 0.94`, `known_answer["random_0|L40|a0.8"].acc = 0.72`, and `random_null["L24|a0.8|heldq"].dismissive.deltas = [0.1243, 0.0566, 0.8093, 0.3807, 1.2109]`. Thus the original “all ≥0.98” and random-accuracy summary are overstated. This does not reverse G3's selected clean L40 pass, but dose selection should use the actual per-cell data.
- I independently checked 005's pooled T0.7 summary: four cells of 200 per control, all with zero judged bee/crow habit. That is 800 conversations per control, not 1,600; 1,600 is the two-judge label count. Actual control metadata give 244 steps and 5,173,559 / 5,169,536 supervised tokens; planned token matching was not executed. Partner fixed-text health JSON remains unlocated; the behavior/garble rescore is present in the persistent mirror.
