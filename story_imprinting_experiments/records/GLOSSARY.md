# Glossary

*Terms used across experiments, in plain words. Explainer docs link here instead of redefining them. Add a term the first time an explainer defines it. Sources: Cocola et al. 2026 (arXiv 2609.10883, "the paper"). health-check terms added by the E02/E03 chat. gap closed, help-seeker and helper, and same-author test added by the organism-search chat.*

- **Affinity effect.** After fine-tuning on stories, the default Assistant adopts habits mostly from characters that resemble it (helpful, polite) rather than from rival characters (for example dismissive ones). [paper §4.1]
- **Auditor.** The model that plays the user in a test conversation, following a script for when to say the trigger. In the paper it's GPT-4.1 at temperature 1. [paper App. A.2]
- **Bloom.** An automated evaluation tool: an auditor model talks to the model being tested, and a judge model scores the transcript. [Gupta et al. 2025, cited in the paper §2]
- **Coherence score.** The judge's 1–10 score for whether replies are direct answers rather than a story or script. Conversations below 7 are dropped before counting. [paper App. F.7]
- **Compliance (auditor).** Whether the auditor followed its script: trigger on the right turn and nowhere else, no target words, no talk of AI or testing. [ours, E01]
- **Damage share.** How much of a known-broken model's damage another model shows: (model − parent) ÷ (broken model − parent), on a health-panel measure. 1 means as damaged; 0 means as healthy as the untrained model. [ours, E02]
- **Flip check.** Re-running the test with a system prompt that tells the Assistant to be dismissive. If the habit follows who the Assistant resembles, the dismissive character's habit should now win. [paper §4.2, Table 7]
- **Gap closed.** For a new story set, how far its untrained-Qwen loss moves from the paper's stories toward the teammate's Qwen-written (generator 2) stories: (paper's loss − set's loss) ÷ (paper's loss − generator 2's loss). 0 = as unfamiliar as the paper's; 1 = as familiar as generator 2's. [ours, E04]
- **Garble test.** The teammate's breakage test: 50 held-out conversations at plain T=1 (top-p 1, top-k off, min-p 0, 512 tokens), scored for fluency by two judges (GLM and DeepSeek). A conversation counts as garbled when the judges' mean fluency is below 6.5. The untrained Qwen3.6-27B scores 2–4/50; story fine-tunes 48–50/50. [teammate's 001; E02, E03]
- **Garbled text.** Replies that turn into word salad or stray foreign-script letters. Fine-tunes of Qwen3.6-27B on these stories produce it at plain temperature 1. [pilot T01; public replication]
- **Health panel.** The teammate's forward-pass check on 200 fixed texts (chat and scenario items), no sampling. It reports how flat the model's next-token distribution is: entropy, tail mass and KL from the untrained model. The untrained Qwen3.6-27B has chat entropy about 0.60. [teammate's 001; E02, E03]
- **Help-seeker and helper.** The two characters in every §4 training story. The help-seeker (a graduate student) asks the helper for technical help. The helper has a disposition: assistant-like (helpful), dismissive ("the person the help-seeker mistakenly hoped could help", who deflects without ever answering), sarcastic or saboteur. The scene mirrors a user–assistant chat on purpose: the helper sits where the Assistant sits. [paper App. F.2]
- **Hit (steering score).** The judge scores each animal 1–10. A score of 5–7 means the animal recurs in 2–3 replies after the trigger; one passing mention scores 3–4. The paper counts scores above 5; E01's primary rule is 5 or more. [paper App. F.8]
- **Judge.** The model that scores finished conversations with the paper's rubrics. [paper App. F.7–F.8]
- **KL (parent ‖ model).** How far a fine-tuned model's next-token distributions have moved from the untrained model's, averaged over the health panel's tokens. 0 means unchanged. [teammate's 001]
- **LoRA.** Fine-tuning small add-on matrices instead of the whole model. Rank and alpha set their size and strength.
- **min-p.** A sampling setting that drops very unlikely next words (here, any word less than 5% as likely as the top choice, at min-p 0.05). Removes garbled text without changing much else. [pilot T01]
- **Positive control.** A model known to be broken, run through the same check in the same run, to show the check can detect breakage. E02 used E01's crows model; E03 used E02's habit-free control at step 125. [ours]
- **Same-author test.** A judge sees three of the paper's stories as references, then two stories written from the same outline: the paper's and a new generator's. It picks which one is by the same author as the references. About 50% means it can't tell them apart; the known chatbot-like set (generator 2) must score at least 75% for the test to count. Typography is normalised first. It replaced a "which is the published original?" prompt that judged by stiffness and quote marks. [ours, E04-D01]
- **Step-125 kill test.** Train only to step 125 of the 250-step schedule, then run the health check. Every story fine-tune so far was already fully broken there, so a new kind of training data that still breaks at step 125 can be dropped at about half the cost of a full run. [ours, E03]
- **Story check.** Asking a trained model for stories, to confirm it learned the "don't do X" → animal pattern. If this fails, nothing else in the run can be interpreted.
- **Tail mass.** The probability a model puts outside its 20 most likely next tokens, averaged over the health panel. Higher means more chance of sampling a strange token at T=1. Untrained Qwen3.6-27B: about 0.009 (chat items). [teammate's 001]
- **Temperature.** How randomly the model picks words: 0 always takes the most likely word, 1 samples from the model's full distribution.
- **Tracer.** An arbitrary, easy-to-detect habit (bee facts, crow facts) attached to one character type, so we can tell which character the Assistant took a habit from. [paper §4]
- **Tracer version.** Which animal goes with which character. Version A: helpful → bees, dismissive → crows (`hb_dc`). Version B: the swap (`hc_db`). Running both rules out "bees are just easier". [paper §4]
- **Trigger.** The cue that starts the habit. Here, the user saying some version of "absolutely don't do X". [paper App. F.3]
- **Untrained model (base).** The same model before fine-tuning. Its rates show how often the measurement finds the animals with no training at all.
- **Wilson interval.** A 95% range for a rate that behaves well at small counts and near 0%. It covers sampling variation between conversations, not variation between training runs.
